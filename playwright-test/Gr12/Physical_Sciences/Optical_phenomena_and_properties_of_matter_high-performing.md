> In a photoelectric experiment, ultraviolet radiation of frequency **$8.99e+14\text{ Hz}$** is shone onto the clean surface of a **Potassium (K)** cathode.
> The work function of Potassium (K) is **$3.68e-19\text{ J}$**.
> 
> 1. Define the term *work function* ($W_0$) of a metal.
> 2. Calculate the threshold frequency ($f_0$) of Potassium (K).
> 3. Calculate the maximum kinetic energy ($E_{k,\text{max}}$) of the ejected photoelectrons.
> 4. Calculate the maximum speed ($v_{\text{max}}$) with which photoelectrons leave the cathode surface.
> 5. State the effect on $E_{k,\text{max}}$ if the intensity of the incident radiation is doubled while keeping the frequency constant.

- **Student Inputted Answer (canonical):** `f0=5.55e+14 Hz, Ek_max=2.28e-19 J, vmax=7.08e+05 m/s`

- **Official Worked Solution:** 1. **Work function:** The minimum amount of energy needed to emit an electron from the surface of a metal.

2. $W_0 = h f_0$
   $f_0 = \frac{W_0}{h} = \frac{3.68e-19}{6{,}63 \times 10^{-34}} = 5.55e+14\text{ Hz}$

3. $E = W_0 + E_{k,\text{max}}$

   $(6{,}63 \times 10^{-34})(8.99e+14) = 3.68e-19 + E_{k,\text{max}}$
   $5.96e-19 = 3.68e-19 + E_{k,\text{max}}$
   $E_{k,\text{max}} = 5.96e-19 - 3.68e-19 = 2.28e-19\text{ J}$

4. $E_{k,\text{max}} = \frac{1}{2} m v_{\text{max}}^2$

   $v_{\text{max}}^2 = \frac{2 \times 2.28e-19}{9{,}11 \times 10^{-31}} = 5.01e+11$
   $v_{\text{max}} = 7.08e+05\text{ m}\cdot\text{s}^{-1}$

5. **No effect / Remains unchanged.** Increasing the intensity increases the number of photoelectrons emitted per second, but does not alter the energy of individual photons or $E_{k,\text{max}}$.
