**Question Prompt:**
> A stationary pedestrian stands beside a long straight urban road as a commuter train sounding its horn approaches a stationary listener at a constant speed of 35 m·s⁻¹. The siren emits sound waves with a frequency of 440 Hz. Take the speed of sound in air as 340 m·s⁻¹.
> 
> 1. State the Doppler Effect in words.
> 2. Explain in terms of wavefronts why the frequency detected by the listener is higher than the emitted frequency.
> 3. Calculate the frequency (fL) heard by the stationary listener as the vehicle approaches in Hz.
> 4. State one practical application of the Doppler Effect in medical diagnosis.

- **Student Inputted Answer (canonical):** `1. The apparent change in frequency of a wave detected by an observer when there is relative motion between the sourc...`
- **Expected / Memo Answer:** `1. The apparent change in frequency of a wave detected by an observer when there is relative motion between the source and observer; 2. Wave...`
- **Official Worked Solution:** 1. Doppler Effect: The apparent change in frequency (or pitch) of a wave detected by an observer because the source and the observer have different velocities relative to the medium of propagation. [2]
2. As the source moves towards the stationary observer, each successive crest is emitted closer to the previous crest. This compresses the wavefronts, decreasing the observed wavelength (λ), which results in a higher observed frequency (fL > fs) because wave speed in the medium remains constant. [2]
3. fL = [v / (v - vs)] · fs [1M]
   fL = [340 / (340 - 35)] · 440 = [340 / 305] · 440 [1M]
   fL = 490,49 Hz [1A]
4. Application: Measuring blood flow velocity in arteries using ultrasound (Doppler flow meter) / echocardiogram. [1]
