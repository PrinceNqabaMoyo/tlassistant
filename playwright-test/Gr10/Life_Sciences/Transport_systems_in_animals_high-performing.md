**Question Prompt:**
> During a clinical physiology investigation at a South African academic hospital, pulmonary gaseous exchange is measured in athletes training at an altitude of 1400 m above sea level.
> In human lungs, gaseous exchange occurs between the alveoli and pulmonary blood capillaries.
> 
> 1. State Fick's Law of diffusion and identify three structural features of human alveoli that satisfy this law.
> 2. Explain how oxygen and carbon dioxide are transported in human blood.

- **Student Inputted Answer (canonical):** `1. Rate of diffusion proportional to surface area and concentration gradient, inversely proportional to thickness; la...`
- **Expected / Memo Answer:** `1. Rate of diffusion proportional to surface area and concentration gradient, inversely proportional to thickness; large surface area, 1-cel...`
- **Official Worked Solution:** 1. Fick's Law: Diffusion rate is proportional to surface area and concentration gradient, and inversely proportional to membrane thickness [2].

**Question Prompt:**
> During a clinical physiology investigation at a South African academic hospital, renal excretion is evaluated in a patient with a glomerular filtration rate of 120 cm³·min⁻¹.
> The human kidneys contain millions of microscopic functional units called nephrons.
> 
> 1. Name the two components that make up the Malpighian body (renal corpuscle).
> 2. Describe the process of ultrafiltration that occurs in the Malpighian body, explaining how high filtration pressure is generated.
> 3. State which major blood components are normally prevented from passing into the glomerular filtrate.

- **Student Inputted Answer (canonical):** `1. Glomerulus and Bowman's capsule; 2. Afferent arteriole wider than efferent arteriole, high pressure forces water/s...`
- **Expected / Memo Answer:** `1. Glomerulus and Bowman's capsule; 2. Afferent arteriole wider than efferent arteriole, high pressure forces water/solutes through podocyte...`
- **Official Worked Solution:** 1. Glomerulus (capillary network) and Bowman's capsule. [2]
2. Ultrafiltration [3]: The afferent arteriole has a wider diameter than the efferent arteriole, creating high hydrostatic pressure in the glomerulus [1]. This pressure forces small molecules (water, glucose, amino acids, urea, mineral salts) through the endothelial pores and podocyte filtration slits into Bowman's capsule [2].
3. Blood cells (erythrocytes, leukocytes) and large plasma proteins (e.g. albumin, fibrinogen) are too large to pass through the filtration slits [2].
