> 
> 1. Calculate the estimated population size of the riverine rabbits using the Lincoln-Petersen index.
> 2. State two validity conditions/assumptions that must be met for this capture-mark-recapture estimate to be reliable.
> 3. The carrying capacity ($K$) of this reserve for this species is estimated at $410$. Explain what will happen to the population growth rate as the population size approaches $K$.

> - $120$ individuals were captured, marked with waterproof paint/tags, and released ($M$).
> - Two weeks later, a second sample of $30$ individuals was captured ($S$), of which $6$ were found to be marked ($R$).
> 
> 1. Calculate the estimated population size of the riverine rabbits using the Lincoln-Petersen index.
> 2. State two validity conditions/assumptions that must be met for this capture-mark-recapture estimate to be reliable.
> 3. The carrying capacity ($K$) of this reserve for this species is estimated at $700$. Explain what will happen to the population growth rate as the population size approaches $K$.

- **Student Inputted Answer (canonical):** `1. P = 600; 2. Assumptions: closed population (no births/deaths/migration), marking does not affect survival/predatio...`
- **Expected / Memo Answer:** `1. P = 600; 2. Assumptions: closed population (no births/deaths/migration), marking does not affect survival/predation, marks do not fade, r...`
- **Official Worked Solution:** 1. Lincoln Index [3]:
   $$P = \frac{M \times S}{R} = \frac{120 \times 30}{6} = 600\text{ individuals}$$ [A]
2. Validity assumptions (any 2) [2]:
   - The population must be closed (no births, deaths, immigration, or emigration during the sampling interval).
   - The mark must not harm the animal, restrict its movement, or make it more vulnerable to predators.
   - Marked individuals must mix randomly back into the general population before the second sample.
   - The mark must not wash off or fade between captures.
3. Carrying capacity explanation [3]: As population approaches $K = 700$, environmental resistance increases (competition for food, shelter, disease increases). The growth rate decelerates (enters decelerating phase) until birth rate equals death rate, fluctuating in dynamic equilibrium around $K$ (logistic/S-shaped growth).
