# Analytical Geometry

* Analytical geometry is the study of geometric properties, relationships and measurement of points, lines and angles in the Cartesian plane.
* Geometrical shapes are defined using a coordinate system and algebraic principles.
* Some consider the introduction of analytical geometry, also called coordinate or Cartesian geometry, to be the beginning of modern mathematics.





## Drawing figures on the Cartesian plane



* If we are given the coordinates of the vertices of a figure, we can draw the figure on the Cartesian plane. For example, quadrilateral ABCD with coordinates A(1; 1), B(3; 1), C(3; 3) and D(1; 3).
* The order of the letters for naming a figure is important. It indicates the order in which points must be joined:
* A to B, B to C, C to D and D back to A. So the above quadrilateral can be referred to as quadrilateral ABCD or CBAD or BADC. However it is conventional to write the letters in alphabetical order and so we only refer to the quadrilateral as ABCD.

## Distance between two points

### Definition of a point

It is a simple geometric object having location as its only property. In 2 dimensional geometry, its location is described by an ordered pair of numbers written as (x; y) where x is the x-coordinate and y is the y-coordinate.



### Definition of distance

It is a measure of length between two points.



### Derivation of formula for distance using Pythagoras' theorem.

* Diagram of the below description required for visualization
* For two points A(x\_1; y\_1) and B(x\_2; y\_2) we can plot a third point C as C(x\_2; y\_1) or C(x\_1; y\_2)
* The distance between A and B becomes the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle with A, B, AND C as vertices.



AB^2 = AC^2 + BC^2

∴ AB = √ (AC^2 + BC^2)



\-And:



AC = x\_2 - x\_1

BC = y\_2 - y\_1



\-Therefore:



AB = √(AC^2 + BC^2)

= √\[(x\_2 - x\_1)^2 + (y\_2 - y\_1)^2]



\-Therefore to calculate the distance between any two points, (x\_1; y\_1) and (x\_2; y\_2), we use:



distance (d) = √\[(x\_2 - x\_1)^2 + (y\_2 - y\_1)^2]



Note that: (x\_2 - x\_1)^2 = (x\_1 - x\_2)^2.

## Gradient of a line

* The gradient of a line is determined by the ratio of vertical change to horizontal change.
* Gradient (m) describes the slope or steepness of the line joining two points.
* To derive the formula for gradient, we consider any right-angled triangle formed from A (x\_1; y\_1) and B (x\_2; y\_2) with hypotenuse AB as shown in the diagram alongside.
* The gradient is determined by the ratio of the length of the vertical side of the triangle to the length of the horizontal side of the triangle.
* The length of the vertical side of the triangle is the difference in y-values of points A and B.
* The length of the horizontal side of the triangle is the difference in x-values of points A and B.



* Therefore, gradient is determined using the following formula:
* Gradient (m) = (y\_2 - y\_1)/(x\_2 - x\_1) or (y\_1 - y\_2)/(x\_1 - x\_2)
* We need a simulation that shows why those to forms of the formular are equivalent



* Remember to be consistent: m ≠ (y\_1 - y\_2)/(x\_2 - x\_1)

## Straight lines

* A straight line is a set of points with a constant gradient between any two of the points.
* Consider the diagram below with points A (x; y), B (x\_1; y\_1) and C (x\_2; y\_2).



* We have m\_AB = *m*\_BC = *m*\_AC and *m* = (y-y\_1)/(x-x\_1) = (y\_2 -y\_1)/(x\_2 - x\_1), where (x; y) is any point on the line.



* This formula can also be written as y - y\_1 = m(x - x\_1).



* The standard form of the straight line equation is y = mx + c where m is the gradient and c is the y-intercept.

## Parallel and perpendicular lines

* Two lines that run parallel to each other are always the same distance apart and have equal gradients.
* If two lines intersect perpendicularly, then the product of their gradients is equal to −1.
* If line WX is perpendicular to YZ (WX ⊥ YZ), then m\_WX × m\_YZ = -1.
* Perpendicular lines have gradients that are the negative inverses of each other.

## Horizontal and vertical lines

A line that runs parallel to the x-axis is called a horizontal line and has a gradient of zero. This is because there is no vertical change:



m = (change in y)/(change in x) = 0/(change in x) = 0



A line that runs parallel to the y-axis is called a vertical line and its gradient is undefined. This is because there is no horizontal change:



m = (change in y)/(change in x) = (change in y)/0 = undefined



## Points on a line

## Mid-point of a line

To calculate the coordinates of the mid-point M (x; y) of any line between the points A (x\_1; y\_1) and B (x\_2; y\_2), we use the following formulae:



x = (x\_1 + x\_2)/2



y = (y\_1 + y\_2)/2



From this we obtain the mid-point of a line:



Midpoint M (x; y) = ((x\_1 + x\_2)/2 ; (y\_1 + y\_2)/2)

DIAGRAM\[

Cartesian plane

\####### Approach 1:

the diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other, therefore the mid-point of EG will be the same as the mid-point of F H. We must first find the mid-point of EG. We can then use it to determine the coordinates

of point H.



\####### Approach 2:

in a parallelogram EH will have the same gradient and length as FG. Finding that gradient and length of FG and using the formulae to work backwards by inputting E(-1; 0).

###### Step 4: Substitute values calculate the coordinates of M

M(x; y) = ((-1 + 8)/2 ; (0 + 11)/2) = (7/2 ; 11/2)



###### Step 5: Use the coordinates of M to determine H
