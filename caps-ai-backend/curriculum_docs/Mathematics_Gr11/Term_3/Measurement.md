## Area of a polygon

### EXAMPLES

\-ABCD is a parallelogram with DC = 15 cm, h = 8 cm and BF = 9 cm.



>DIAGRAM\[

\-Parallelogram ABCD

\-A dashed line from A perpendicular to DC is labelled h

\-F lies on BC such that AF is perpendicular to BC and labelled H

]



\-Calculate:



#### a. 

the area of ABCD



#### b. 

the perimeter of ABCD





#### SOLUTION



##### Step 1: Determine the area

The area of a parallelogram ABCD = base × height:

Area = 15 ×8

= 120 cm^2



##### Step 2: Determine the perimeter

\-The perimeter of a parallelogram ABCD = 2DC + 2BC.

\-To find the length of BC, we use AF ⊥ BC and the theorem of Pythagoras.

In △ABF: AF^2 = AB^2 − BF^2

= 15^2 − 9^2

= 144

∴ AF = 12 cm



Area ABCD = BC ×AF

120 = BC ×12

∴ BC =10 cm

∴ Perimeter ABCD = 2(15) + 2(10)

= 50 cm

## Right prisms and cylinders

\-Rectangular, square and triangle prisms and cylinders



### EXAMPLE - SURFACE AREA

\-A box of chocolates has the following dimensions:

length = 25 cm

width = 20 cm

height = 4 cm



\-And a cylindrical tin of biscuits has the following dimensions:

diameter = 20 cm

height = 20 cm



#### a. 

Calculate the area of the wrapping paper needed to cover the entire box (assume no overlapping at the corners).



#### b. 

Determine if this same sheet of wrapping paper would be enough to cover the tin of biscuits.





#### SOLUTION

##### Step 1: Determine the area of the rectangular box

Surface area = 2 ×(25 ×20) + 2×(20×4) + 2×(25×4)

= 1360 cm^2



##### Step 2: Determine the area of the cylindrical tin

The radius of the cylinder = 20/2 = 10cm.



Surface area = 2×π(10)^2 + 2π(10)(20)

&#x20;= 1885cm^2



##### Step 3: Write the final answer

No, the area of the sheet of wrapping paper used to cover the box is not big enough to cover the tin.

### EXAMPLE -VOLUME

\-A rectangular glass vase with dimensions 28 cm × 18 cm × 8 cm is used for flower arrangements. 

\-A florist uses a plastic cylindrical jug to pour water into the glass vase.

\-The jug has a diameter of 142 mm and a height of 28 cm.



a. 

Will the plastic jug hold 5 of water?



b. 

Will a full jug of water be enough to fill the glass vase?





#### SOLUTION

##### Step 1: Determine the volume of the plastic jug

\-The diameter of the jug is 142 mm, therefore the radius = 142/ 2×10 = 7,1 cm.

\-Volume of a cylinder = area of the base × height



Volume of the jug = πr^2 ×h

= π × (7,1)^2 × 28

= 4434 cm^3



And 1000 cm^3 = 1l



∴ Volume of the jug = 4434/1000

= 4,434 l

No, the capacity of the jug is not enough to hold 5 of water.





##### Step 2: Determine the volume of the glass vase

Volume of a rectangular prism = area of the base × height

Volume of the vase = l × b × h

= 28 × 18 × 8

= 4032 cm^3

∴Volume of the vase = 4032/1000

= 4,032 l



Yes, the volume of the jug is greater than the volume of the vase.

## Multiplying a dimension by a constant factor



### EXAMPLE

\-The Nash family wants to build a television room onto their house. 

\-The dad draws up the plans for the new square room of length k metres. 

\-The mum looks at the plans and decides that the area of the room needs to be doubled. To achieve this:



* the mum suggests doubling the length of the sides of the room
* the dad recommends adding 2 m to the length of the sides
* the daughter suggests multiplying the length of the sides by a factor of √2
* the son suggests doubling only the width of the room



\-Who’s suggestion will double the area of the square room? Show all calculations.



#### SOLUTION

##### Step 1: Draw a sketch

>DIAGRAM\[

\-Squares of sides k; sides 2k; sides k + 2; sides √2k; and rectangle 2k by k.

]



##### Step 2: Calculate and compare

\-First calculate the area of the square room in the original plan:



Area O = length × length

= k^2

\-Therefore, double the area of the room would be 2k^2.



\-Consider the mum’s suggestion of doubling the length of the sides of the room:

Area M = length × length

= 2k × 2k

= 4k^2

\-This area would be 4 times the original area.



\-The dad suggests adding 2m to the length of the sides of the room:

Area D = length × length

= (k + 2) × (k + 2)

= k^2 + 4k + 2

≠ 2k^2



\-This is not double the original area.



\-The daughter suggests multiplying the length of the sides by a factor of √2:

Area d = length × length

= √2k × √2k

= 2k^2



\-The daughter’s suggestion would double the area of the room. 

\-Practically, the length of the room could be multiplied by √2 ≈ 1,41 which would given an area of 1,96m^2.



\-The son suggests doubling only the width of the room:

Area d = length × length

= √2k × k

= 2k^2

\-The son’s suggestion would double the area of the room, however the room would no longer be a square.



##### Step 3: Write the final answer

\-The daughter’s suggestion of multiplying the length of the sides of the room by a factor of √2 would keep the shape of the room a square and would double the area of the room.
