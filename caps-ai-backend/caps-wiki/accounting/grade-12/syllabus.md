# Accounting Curriculum Specifications (CAPS)

## 1. Curriculum Overview
Accounting focuses on measuring performance and processing/communicating financial information about economic sectors. The discipline emphasizes ethical behavior, transparency, and accountability through the logical, systematic, and accurate recording of financial transactions.

## 3. Educational Objectives & Learner Competencies
Upon completion of the curriculum, learners will be able to:
*   **Record, analyze, and interpret** financial and other relevant data to facilitate informed decision-making.
*   **Communicate** financial information effectively, adhering to generally accepted accounting practices, current developments, and legislation.
*   **Demonstrate** a comprehensive understanding of fundamental accounting concepts.
*   **Apply** skills, knowledge, and values to real-world situations, bridging the gap between theory and practice for career readiness or higher education.
*   **Manage** personal finances and activities with responsibility and effectiveness.

## 4. Mathematical Foundations
The core of the accounting equation, which serves as the foundation for the bookkeeping and financial statement topics, is expressed as:

$$Assets = Equity + Liabilities$$

Where:
*   **Assets:** Resources controlled by the entity.
*   **Equity:** The residual interest in the assets of the entity after deducting all its liabilities.
*   **Liabilities:** Present obligations of the entity arising from past events.

#### 2. COGNITIVE SCOPE & DEPTH OF TREATMENT
The curriculum is structured to transition learners through the following cognitive levels:

*   **Knowledge:** Recall of GAAP principles, accounting concepts, and basic definitions.
*   **Routine Procedures:** Application of standard bookkeeping processes, ledger entries, and basic calculations (e.g., VAT, depreciation).
*   **Complex Procedures:** Preparation and analysis of financial statements, reconciliation statements, and production cost statements.
*   **Problem Solving:** Interpretation of financial data, age-analysis, and valuation of inventory using methods such as:
    *   **Specific Identification:** $Cost = \sum (Units_i \times Price_i)$
    *   **First-In-First-Out (FIFO):** Valuation based on the oldest stock items.
    *   **Weighted Average:** $\text{Weighted Average Cost} = \frac{\text{Total Cost of Goods Available}}{\text{Total Units Available}}$

#### 2. Cognitive Scope & Depth of Treatment

The curriculum follows a developmental trajectory from foundational knowledge to critical evaluation:

*   **Grade 10 (Foundational):** Focuses on the acquisition of basic knowledge regarding internal control processes.
*   **Grade 11 (Procedural/Application):** Focuses on the identification of ethical scenarios and the demonstration of specific internal audit mechanisms (e.g., division of duties, physical controls).
*   **Grade 12 (Complex/Evaluative):** Focuses on high-level cognitive skills including:
    *   **Analysis:** Examining the role of professional bodies and the King Code.
    *   **Evaluation:** Critiquing existing internal control and audit systems.
    *   **Synthesis:** Understanding the consequences of non-compliance (disciplinary and punitive measures).

---

#### 3. Instructional Planning Notes

*   **Pacing:** Teachers should ensure that Grade 12 learners have mastered the Grade 11 foundational concepts of "division of duties" and "documentation" before attempting the "critiquing" of internal control systems.
*   **Assessment Focus:** 
    *   **Ethics:** Assessment should move from scenario-based identification (Grade 11) to the application of governance frameworks like the King Code (Grade 12).
    *   **Internal Control:** Assessment should transition from defining processes (Grade 10/11) to providing recommendations for improvement based on identified weaknesses in a business scenario (Grade 12).

### 3. Mathematics & Formulas (Financial Indicators)

The following formulas are required for the analysis and interpretation of financial statements:

**Profitability & Efficiency:**
*   Gross profit on sales: $\frac{\text{Gross Profit}}{\text{Sales}} \times 100$
*   Net profit on sales: $\frac{\text{Net Profit}}{\text{Sales}} \times 100$
*   Operating profit on sales: $\frac{\text{Operating Profit}}{\text{Sales}} \times 100$

**Liquidity:**
*   Current ratio: $\text{Current Assets} : \text{Current Liabilities}$
*   Acid test ratio: $(\text{Current Assets} - \text{Inventory}) : \text{Current Liabilities}$

**Solvency & Gearing:**
*   Solvency ratio: $\text{Total Assets} : \text{Total Liabilities}$
*   Debt equity ratio: $\frac{\text{Non-current Liabilities}}{\text{Shareholders' Equity}}$

**Returns & Shareholder Value:**
*   Return on shareholders' equity: $\frac{\text{Net Profit after Tax}}{\text{Average Shareholders' Equity}} \times 100$
*   Return on total capital employed: $\frac{\text{Net Profit before Interest and Tax}}{\text{Average Capital Employed}} \times 100$
*   Net asset value per share: $\frac{\text{Shareholders' Equity}}{\text{Number of shares in issue}}$
*   Earnings per share: $\frac{\text{Net Profit after Tax}}{\text{Number of shares in issue}}$
*   Dividends per share: $\frac{\text{Total Dividends}}{\text{Number of shares in issue}}$

**Activity Ratios:**
*   Stock turnover rate: $\frac{\text{Cost of Sales}}{\text{Average Inventory}}$
*   Stock holding period: $\frac{\text{Average Inventory}}{\text{Cost of Sales}} \times 365$
*   Average debtors' collection period: $\frac{\text{Average Debtors}}{\text{Credit Sales}} \times 365$
*   Average creditors' payment period: $\frac{\text{Average Creditors}}{\text{Credit Purchases}} \times 365$

*While specific algebraic formulas are not provided in this excerpt, the analysis of fixed assets requires the application of the following standard accounting relationships:*

*   **Carrying Value Calculation:**
    $$\text{Carrying Value} = \text{Cost} - \text{Accumulated Depreciation}$$

    $$\text{Replacement Rate} = \frac{\text{Cost of New Assets}}{\text{Total Cost of Fixed Assets}}$$

### 3. MATHEMATICAL FORMULAS & CALCULATIONS

The following mathematical relationships are central to the Cost Accounting module:

*   **Total Cost of Production:**
    $$\text{Total Cost} = \text{Fixed Costs} + \text{Variable Costs}$$

*   **Cost per Unit:**
    $$\text{Cost per Unit} = \frac{\text{Total Production Costs}}{\text{Number of Units Produced}}$$

*   **Contribution per Unit:**
    $$\text{Contribution per Unit} = \text{Selling Price per Unit} - \text{Variable Cost per Unit}$$

*   **Break-even Point (Units):**
    $$\text{Break-even Point} = \frac{\text{Total Fixed Costs}}{\text{Contribution per Unit}}$$

# SECTION 4: Assessment Specifications

## 1. Definition and Purpose of Assessment
Assessment is defined as a process that measures individual learners' attainment of knowledge (content, concepts, and skills). The data and information obtained serve two primary functions:
*   **Learner-centric:** Informing learners about their strengths, weaknesses, and progress.
*   **Stakeholder-centric:** Assisting teachers, parents, and other stakeholders in making informed decisions regarding the learning process and learner progress.

Assessment must be mapped directly against the content and intended objectives of the subject.

## 2. Assessment Categories

### A. Informal (Daily) Assessment
*   **Nature:** Planned teaching and learning activities integrated into daily lessons.
*   **Timing:** Occurs at the beginning, during, or at the end of a lesson.
*   **Methods:** Question and answer sessions, short written classwork, homework exercises, and open-book tests.
*   **Feedback:** Essential for improving learning and teaching; involves self-assessment and peer assessment to encourage reflection.
*   **Scaffolding:** Acts as a stepping stone to formal tasks in the Programme of Assessment.
*   **Recording:** Not formally recorded for promotion or certification purposes. Teachers may use simple checklists for tracking progress.

### B. Formal Assessment
*   **Nature:** A systematic evaluation of learner progress within a grade and subject.
*   **Examples:** Projects, oral presentations, demonstrations, performances, tests, examinations, and practical tasks.
*   **Programme of Assessment:** All formal tasks must form part of a year-long, school-based Programme of Assessment.

### 2. Formal Assessment: Mandatory Project Specifications

In the FET (Further Education and Training) band, a project is a mandatory component of the Programme of Assessment.

#### Instructional Guidelines
*   **Guidance & Monitoring:** Teachers must provide necessary guidance prior to commencement and monitor progress throughout the project lifecycle.
*   **Classroom Integration:** Aspects of the project should be completed in class to facilitate teacher supervision and progress tracking.
*   **Transparency:** All assessment criteria must be discussed with learners and the assessment instrument (rubric/checklist) must be provided to learners alongside project instructions.
*   **Nature of Work:** Projects generally involve research, consolidation of information, and the preparation of a written document. In specific subjects (e.g., Computerised Accounting), the focus may shift to the development of technical skills rather than traditional research.
*   **Timeline:** Projects are typically initiated towards the end of Term 1 for submission during Term 2.

#### Technical Formatting Requirements
*   **Structure:** Must include a cover page and a table of contents (where applicable).
*   **Content Organization:** Text must be divided into logical paragraphs or sections.
*   **Visuals:** Inclusion of pictures, photos, diagrams, and graphs is encouraged.
*   **Referencing:** All text references and a bibliography must be clearly indicated.
*   **Length:** Between 4 and 8 pages (typed) or 6 and 10 pages (written). This excludes the cover page, table of contents, graphs, and pictures.
*   **Typography:** Font size 12 with $1.5$ line spacing.
*   **Presentation:** The final document must be bound or stapled neatly.

### Assessment Task Specifications: Presentation, Case Study, and Report

This document outlines the instructional and assessment requirements for three core task types within the CAPS curriculum framework.

#### 1. Presentation
Presentations may be conducted in written or oral formats, provided that tangible evidence of the presentation is maintained.

**Instructional Guidelines:**
*   **Transparency:** All assessment criteria must be communicated to learners prior to the commencement of the task.
*   **Technology:** The use of electronic presentation tools is encouraged where resources permit.
*   **Structural Requirements:**
    *   Include a cover page and appropriate headings.
    *   Include a table of contents (where applicable).
    *   Organize text into logical paragraphs or sections.
    *   Integrate visual aids (pictures, photos, diagrams, graphs).
    *   Ensure physical evidence is bound or stapled appropriately.
    *   Provide the assessment instrument (rubric/checklist) to learners alongside task instructions.

#### 2. Case Study
Case studies utilize real-life situations, problems, or incidents to allow learners to assume specific roles and apply prior knowledge to interpret, analyze, and solve problems.

**Instructional Guidelines:**
*   **Source Material:** Utilize newspaper articles, magazine articles, and TV or radio presentations to ensure subject relevance.
*   **Cognitive Scope:** Questions must span the cognitive hierarchy:
    *   **Lower-order:** Direct extraction of information/quotes from the source.
    *   **Higher-order:** Analysis of comments and formulation of informed suggestions/decisions.
*   **Structural Requirements:**
    *   Include a cover page and appropriate headings.
    *   Include a table of contents (where applicable).
    *   Organize text into logical paragraphs or sections.
    *   Integrate visual aids (pictures, photos, diagrams, graphs).
    *   Ensure physical evidence is bound or stapled appropriately.
    *   Provide the assessment instrument to learners alongside task instructions.

#### 3. Report
A report serves as written evidence of a survey, analysis, or investigation. It is typically shorter in duration than a project and focuses on specific problem-solving scenarios (e.g., advising a business on liquidity problems).

**Instructional Guidelines:**
*   **Focus:** Strong emphasis on problem-solving and, where applicable, consulting with experts.
*   **Structural Requirements:**
    *   Include a cover page and appropriate headings.
    *   Include a table of contents (where applicable).
