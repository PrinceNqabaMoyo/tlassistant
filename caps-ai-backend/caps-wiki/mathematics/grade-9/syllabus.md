### 2. CONTENT & COGNITIVE SCOPE

#### Core Concepts
*   **Number Systems:** Recognition and distinction between natural numbers, whole numbers, integers, rational numbers, and irrational numbers.
*   **Calculation Techniques:** Proficiency in mental and written calculations, estimation, rounding off, compensating, and long division.
*   **Number Theory:** Prime factorisation, Lowest Common Multiple (LCM), and Highest Common Factor (HCF).

#### Depth of Treatment & Teaching Guidelines
*   **Consolidation:** Grade 9 builds upon Grade 8 knowledge of whole numbers.
*   **Number System Hierarchy:** Learners must understand that:
    *   Natural numbers $\subset$ Whole numbers $\subset$ Integers $\subset$ Rational numbers $\subset$ Real numbers.
    *   Integers include the operation $a - b$ where $a < b$.
    *   Rational numbers are defined as $\frac{a}{b}$ where $a, b \in \mathbb{Z}$ and $b \neq 0$.
*   **Calculator Policy:** Calculators should be used routinely for complex calculations, but learners must be guarded against over-dependence; mental and written competency remains essential for algebraic manipulation.
*   **Contextual Application:** Emphasis on financial contexts to enrich the understanding of number systems.

---

### 3. MATHEMATICAL SPECIFICATIONS & FORMULAS

*   **Rational Number Definition:**
    A number is rational if it can be expressed in the form:
    $$\frac{a}{b} \text{ where } a, b \in \mathbb{Z} \text{ and } b \neq 0$$

*   **Integer Operation Constraint:**
    The extension of number systems to include integers allows for the operation:
    $$a - b \text{ where } a < b$$

*   **Cognitive Focus:**
    *   **Knowledge:** Identifying and classifying number sets.
    *   **Routine Procedures:** Performing standard operations (addition, subtraction, multiplication, division) and prime factorisation.
    *   **Complex Procedures:** Applying LCM and HCF in problem-solving contexts.
    *   **Problem Solving:** Using estimation and calculator-assisted verification to solve multi-step numerical problems.

#### 2. Content & Cognitive Scope
The curriculum requires learners to move beyond rote calculation into conceptual application.

**Core Concepts:**
*   **Rational vs. Irrational Numbers:** Understanding that irrational numbers cannot be expressed as $\frac{a}{b}$.
*   **Pi ($\pi$):** Recognition of $\pi$ as an irrational number, utilizing approximations like $\frac{22}{7}$ or $3,14$.
*   **Ratio and Rate:** Application of speed, distance, and time relationships.
*   **Proportionality:** Mastery of direct and indirect (inverse) relationships.

**Mathematical Formulas:**
*   Speed: $speed = \frac{distance}{time}$
*   Distance: $distance = speed \times time$
*   Time: $time = \frac{distance}{speed}$
*   Direct Proportion: $y = kx$ (where $k$ is a constant)
*   Indirect Proportion: $y = \frac{k}{x}$ or $x \times y = k$ (where $k$ is a constant)

**Cognitive Depth:**
*   **Knowledge:** Identifying units of measurement and definitions of constants.
*   **Routine Procedures:** Converting between units of time and distance; applying standard formulas.
*   **Complex Procedures:** Solving multi-step word problems (e.g., calculating travel time for varying speeds over specific distances).
*   **Problem Solving:** Analyzing relationships between variables to determine if a scenario represents direct or indirect proportion.

#### 3. Teaching Guidelines & Clarifications
*   **Unit Conversion:** Educators must ensure learners are proficient in converting between units (e.g., minutes to hours) before applying speed/distance formulas.
*   **Direct Proportion:** Characterized by $x$ and $y$ increasing or decreasing in the same proportion. Represented graphically as a straight line.
*   **Indirect Proportion:** Characterized by $x$ increasing as $y$ decreases (or vice versa). Represented graphically as a non-linear curve.
*   **Contextual Application:** Problems should be grounded in real-world scenarios, such as vehicle travel times and constant speed calculations.

#### Laws of Exponents (Depth of Treatment)
Learners must master the following laws where $a \neq 0$ and $m, n$ are integers:

*   Product rule: $a^m \times a^n = a^{m+n}$
*   Quotient rule: $a^m \div a^n = a^{m-n}$ (for $m > n$)
*   Power of a power rule: $(a^m)^n = a^{m \times n}$
*   Power of a product rule: $(a \times t)^n = a^n \times t^n$
*   Zero exponent rule: $a^0 = 1$
*   Negative exponent rule: $a^{-m} = \frac{1}{a^m}$

#### Cognitive Guidelines
*   **Progression:** Builds upon Grade 8 foundations.
*   **Methodology:** Introduce laws through a range of numeric examples first, then transition to variables.
*   **Verification:** Ensure learners understand the equivalence of expressions by verifying that the Left-Hand Side (LHS) equals the Right-Hand Side (RHS).

#### Cognitive Levels & Depth of Treatment

*   **Routine Procedures:** Using a provided rule to calculate a specific term.
*   **Complex Procedures:** Deriving a rule from a sequence where the relationship is non-linear (e.g., quadratic patterns).
*   **Problem Solving:** Working backwards from a term value to find its position (e.g., solving $626 = n^2 + 1$ to find $n = 25$).

---

### 3. MATHEMATICAL FORMULAS & EXAMPLES

#### Key Examples and Rules
1.  **Sequence:** $2; 5; 10; 17; \dots$
    *   **Rule:** The term value is the square of the position plus one.
    *   **Formula:** $T_n = n^2 + 1$
    *   **Application:** To find the 10th term ($n=10$):
        $$T_{10} = 10^2 + 1 = 100 + 1 = 101$$
    *   **Inverse Application:** To find the position of the term $626$:
        $$626 = n^2 + 1$$
        $$625 = n^2$$
        $$n = \sqrt{625} = 25$$

2.  **Sequence:** $1; 0; -2; -5; -9; -14; \dots$
    *   **Pattern Analysis:** The difference between terms increases by 1 each time ($-1, -2, -3, -4, -5$).
    *   **Extension:** To find the next 3 terms:
        *   $-14 - 6 = -20$
        *   $-20 - 7 = -27$
        *   $-27 - 8 = -35$

3.  **Sequence:** $-2; -5; -8; -11; \dots$
    *   **Pattern Analysis:** Constant difference of $-3$.
    *   **Rule:** Subtract 3 from the previous term.

#### 2. Teaching Guidelines & Clarifications

**Numeric Patterns:**
*   Learners must identify the relationship between the position of a term and the value of the term.
*   **Example sequence:** $-2, -5, -8, -11, \dots$
    *   1st term: $-3(1) + 1 = -2$
    *   2nd term: $-3(2) + 1 = -5$
    *   3rd term: $-3(3) + 1 = -8$
    *   4th term: $-3(4) + 1 = -11$
*   **Generalization:** The rule can be expressed as $-3n + 1$, where $n$ represents the position of the term.
*   **Application:** To find the 20th term: $-3(20) + 1 = -59$.

**Geometric Patterns:**
*   Patterns are represented diagrammatically to reveal structure.
*   **Example: Hexagon Matchstick Pattern**
    *   Structure: Successive hexagonal shapes.
    *   Rule derivation:
        1.  Each hexagon has 6 sides.
        2.  Each additional hexagon adds 5 matches (sharing one side).
    *   **Calculation:**
        *   1st hexagon: $1 \times 6 = 6$ matches.
        *   2nd hexagon: $2 \times 6 = 12$ matches (or $6 + 5$).
        *   10th hexagon: $10 \times 6 = 60$ matches (if treated as individual units) or specific sequence logic based on shared sides.

---

#### 3. Cognitive Scope & Pedagogical Focus
*   **Conceptual Understanding:** Developing an understanding of functional relationships where an independent variable (position) determines a dependent variable (term value).
*   **Skills to be Mastered:**
    *   Translating between diagrammatic representations and numeric sequences.
    *   Formulating algebraic expressions for the $n^{th}$ term.
    *   Applying rules to calculate specific terms in a sequence.
*   **Cognitive Levels:**
    *   **Knowledge:** Identifying the next term in a simple sequence.
    *   **Routine Procedures:** Calculating the $n^{th}$ term using a provided rule.
    *   **Complex Procedures:** Deriving the general rule from a geometric pattern.
    *   **Problem Solving:** Applying pattern logic to solve real-world or abstract structural problems.

#### Linear Equation Solving
To solve $3(x - 2) = x + 2$:
1.  **Expand:** $3x - 6 = x + 2$
2.  **Isolate variables:** $3x - x - 6 = 2$ (add $-x$ to both sides)
3.  **Simplify:** $2x - 6 = 2$
4.  **Isolate constant:** $2x = 8$ (add $6$ to both sides)
5.  **Solve:** $x = \frac{8}{2} = 4$

#### Zero Product Property
To solve $(x - 1)(x + 3) = 0$:
*   Apply the principle: $x - 1 = 0$ or $x + 3 = 0$.
*   **Solution 1:** $x = 1$ (add $1$ to both sides).
*   **Solution 2:** $x = -3$ (add $-3$ to both sides).

### 3. Mathematical Representations & Formulas

**Two-Way Table Example (Birth Order):**

| 1st birth | 2nd birth (Boy) | 2nd birth (Girl) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Boy** | BB | BG |
| **Girl** | GB | GG |

**Probability Calculation:**
The probability of a specific outcome (e.g., two boys) is calculated as:
$$P(\text{Event}) = \frac{\text{Number of favorable outcomes}}{\text{Total number of possible outcomes}}$$

For the event of two boys (BB) in two births:
$$P(BB) = \frac{1}{4} = 25\%$$

**Tree Diagram Structure (Three Births):**
*   **1st Birth:** Boy / Girl
*   **2nd Birth:** Boy / Girl (branching from each 1st birth outcome)
*   **3rd Birth:** Boy / Girl (branching from each 2nd birth outcome)
*   **Sample Space:** $\{BBB, BBG, BGB, BGG, GBB, GBG, GGB, GGG\}$

At this stage, learners must be assessed on the following core competencies:
*   **Transformation Geometry:** Understanding and applying geometric transformations.
*   **Geometry of 3D Objects:** Properties and spatial reasoning of three-dimensional shapes.
*   **Data Handling:** Collecting, organizing, representing, analyzing, summarizing, interpreting, and reporting data.
*   **Probability:** Application of probability theory to solve problems.

**Pacing Guidelines:**
*   The 12-hour allocation includes time for both formal/informal assessment and revision of the term's work.
