#### 2. CONTENT & COGNITIVE SCOPE

**Core Concepts & Depth of Treatment:**

*   **Microorganisms:**
    *   Basic structure and general characteristics of:
        *   Viruses
        *   Bacteria
        *   Protista
        *   Fungi
    *   *Note:* Macroscopic organisms in the protista and fungi groups should only be mentioned (not studied in detail).
*   **Ecological Roles:**
    *   Roles in maintaining balance in the environment and web of life.
    *   Symbiotic relationships, including nitrogen-fixing bacteria in plants and *E. coli* in the human intestine.
*   **Disease Management:**
    *   Effect and management of one disease from each group:
        *   **Viruses:** Rabies, HIV/AIDS, influenza.
        *   **Bacteria:** Blight, cholera, tuberculosis, anthrax.
        *   **Protists:** Malaria.
        *   **Fungi:** Rusts, thrush, ringworm, athlete's foot.
*   **Immunity & Biotechnology:**
    *   Immunity: Plants and animals' immune responses against infecting microorganisms.
    *   Vaccinations (briefly).
    *   Use of drugs (e.g., antibiotics) and their effect on microorganisms.
    *   Use of microorganisms to produce medicines (e.g., insulin and antibiotics).
    *   Traditional technology: Production of beer, wine, and cheese.

**Cognitive Levels:**
*   **Knowledge:** Recall of structural characteristics of microorganisms.
*   **Routine Procedures:** Culturing microorganisms on agar/bread.
*   **Complex Procedures:** Mapping disease distribution in the study area.
*   **Problem Solving:** Analyzing the impact of antibiotics and the role of biotechnology in medicine and food production.

#### 4. MATHEMATICAL APPLICATIONS
While this section is primarily biological, the study of microbial growth involves exponential concepts:
*   **Bacterial Growth:** Represented by the exponential growth formula:
    $$N_t = N_0 \times 2^n$$
    Where:
    *   $N_t$ = Number of bacteria at time $t$
    *   $N_0$ = Initial number of bacteria
    *   $n$ = Number of generations (divisions)

### LIFE SCIENCES: TERM 1 CURRICULUM SPECIFICATIONS (PLANT BIODIVERSITY & REPRODUCTION)

#### 1. ANNUAL TEACHING PLAN (ATP) & TIMELINES
| Topic | Duration | Pacing Guidelines |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Biodiversity of Plants** | 3 Weeks (12 Hours) | Focus on developmental lines, in-depth life cycles, and evolutionary pathways. |

#### 2. CONTENT & COGNITIVE SCOPE

**A. Biodiversity of Plants (Developmental Lines)**
*   **Core Concepts:** Grouping of Bryophytes, Pteridophytes, Gymnosperms, and Angiosperms.
*   **Key Differentiators:**
    *   Presence/absence of vascular tissue (xylem and phloem).
    *   Presence/absence of true leaves and roots.
    *   Reproductive structures: seeds vs. spores.
    *   Presence of fruit.
*   **Evolutionary Trends:** Decreasing dependence on water for reproduction from Bryophytes to Angiosperms.
*   **Skills:** 
    *   Understanding phylogenetic trees and cladograms.
    *   Observing and drawing macroscopic parts of plant specimens.
*   **Cognitive Depth:** 
    *   *Knowledge:* Identification of plant groups.
    *   *Complex Procedures:* Constructing/interpreting phylogenetic trees showing evolutionary history and structural changes.

**B. Reproduction in Plants**
*   **Core Concepts:**
    *   Asexual vs. Sexual reproduction (advantages and disadvantages).
    *   Flowers as reproductive structures.
    *   Pollination adaptations (wind, insects, birds) using South African examples.
    *   The Significance of Seeds (seed banks, food source, endemic species).
*   **Skills:**
    *   Dissection of flowers (wind, insect, and bird-pollinated).
    *   Recording observations in comparative tables.
    *   Optional: Germination process recording.

#### 3. RESOURCE REQUIREMENTS
To achieve the required depth of treatment, the following resources are mandatory:
*   **Specimens:** Plant specimens, various flowers.
*   **Tools:** Hand lenses, scalpels or blades, microscopes, prepared slides.
*   **Reference:** Textbooks, identification guides/keys, micrographs, charts, models.

#### 1. Annual Teaching Plan (ATP) & Timelines

#### 2. Content & Cognitive Scope

**Core Concepts & Skills:**
*   **Dentition:** Comparative analysis of herbivorous, carnivorous, and omnivorous lifestyles; nutritional requirements and energy relationships (linked to ecology/food chains).
*   **Human Nutrition (Alimentary Canal):** Macro-structure and functional analysis of organs.
*   **Processes of Nutrition:** Ingestion, digestion, absorption, assimilation, and egestion.
*   **Mechanical Digestion:** Types and functions of teeth, chewing processes, and peristalsis.
*   **Chemical Digestion:** Enzyme functions (carbohydrases, proteases, lipases); substrate, pH, and end-products.
*   **Absorption:** Small intestine structure (tissue level) and adaptations for surface area (villi); hepatic portal system transport.
*   **Assimilation:** Glucose incorporation, amino acid metabolism, deamination, and breakdown of alcohol, drugs, and hormones.
*   **Homeostatic Control:** Hormonal regulation of blood sugar levels; incidence of diabetes.

**Cognitive Depth & Practical Requirements:**
*   **Practical Application:** Dissection of a mammal (sheep intestines/stomach) to observe structural adaptations.
*   **Cognitive Levels:** 
    *   *Knowledge:* Identifying structures and definitions.
    *   *Routine Procedures:* Describing digestive processes.
    *   *Complex Procedures:* Linking structure to function (e.g., villi adaptations).
    *   *Problem Solving:* Analyzing hormonal control and health implications (diabetes).

#### 3. Resource Requirements
*   **Media:** Textbooks, newspapers, popular magazines.
*   **Biological Specimens:** Sheep intestines (obtained from a butcher).
*   **Laboratory Equipment:** Scalpels or sharp knives, hand lenses, DVD/video for dissection demonstration.

#### 4. Mathematical & Scientific Context
While the curriculum focuses on biological processes, the study of nutrition involves quantitative analysis of energy and metabolic rates. 

*   **Metabolic Rate/Energy Balance:**
    $$\text{Energy Balance} = \text{Energy Intake} - \text{Energy Expenditure}$$
*   **Blood Glucose Regulation:**
    The homeostatic feedback loop is represented by the relationship between insulin and glucagon:
    $$\text{High Blood Glucose} \xrightarrow{\text{Insulin}} \text{Glycogen Storage}$$
    $$\text{Low Blood Glucose} \xrightarrow{\text{Glucagon}} \text{Glucose Release}$$

#### 4. Mathematical Application in Nutrition
Students are required to perform calculations regarding energy and nutritional intake. The fundamental relationship for energy balance is:

$$\text{Net Energy} = \text{Energy Intake} - \text{Energy Expenditure}$$

Where:
*   **Energy Intake:** Sum of calories from macronutrients (Carbohydrates, Proteins, Fats).
*   **Energy Expenditure:** Sum of Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR) and energy used during physical activity.

*Note: When calculating the nutritional value of a meal, students must utilize the standard energy density values:*
*   Carbohydrates: $\approx 17 \text{ kJ/g}$
*   Proteins: $\approx 17 \text{ kJ/g}$
*   Fats: $\approx 37 \text{ kJ/g}$

### 4. MATHEMATICAL & BIOLOGICAL FORMULAS
While specific chemical equations for metabolic waste are implied in the syllabus, the homeostatic control mechanisms involve the following regulatory relationships:

*   **Water Balance Regulation:**
    $$\text{High ADH} \rightarrow \text{Increased water re-absorption} \rightarrow \text{Concentrated urine}$$
    $$\text{Low ADH} \rightarrow \text{Decreased water re-absorption} \rightarrow \text{Dilute urine}$$

*   **Homeostatic Feedback Loop:**
    $$\text{Stimulus (e.g., low blood pressure/high salt)} \rightarrow \text{Receptor} \rightarrow \text{Control Center (Hypothalamus/Pituitary)} \rightarrow \text{Effector (Kidney)} \rightarrow \text{Response (Hormonal adjustment)}$$

### Life Sciences Grade 10-12: Environmental Studies (Term 3)

#### 1. Annual Teaching Plan (ATP) & Timelines

#### 2. Content & Cognitive Scope
The curriculum focuses on the interaction between organisms and their environments, encouraging learners to identify local environmental problems and propose solutions to modify human impact.

**Core Concepts:**
*   **Population Size:**
    *   Factors: Immigration, emigration, mortality, births.
    *   Dynamics: Fluctuations, limiting factors, and carrying capacity.
    *   Growth Models: Logistic and geometric growth curves.
*   **Interactions in the Environment:**
    *   **Predation:** Two South African examples of predator-prey relationships (represented via graphs).
    *   **Competition:**
        *   *Interspecific:* Competition for light, space, water, shelter, and food.
        *   *Intraspecific:* Competition for food, access to mates, water, space, and shelter; survival determined by access to ecological niches.
    *   **Specialisation:** Competitive exclusion and resource partitioning; discussion of one example of coexistence in animals and one in plants.

**Investigations & Skills:**
*   **Field/Practical Skills:** Determine population size using quadrant or simple sampling (e.g., simulated mark/recapture).
*   **Data Handling:** Collect, record, and interpret data; calculate/estimate population size.
*   **Case Study:** Rationale for culling (e.g., elephants in Kruger National Park) as an application of estimating population size.
*   **Social/Communication Skills:** Draw up a public survey to test public opinion on culling; present results in a pie graph.

*   Posters and Charts
*   Brochures
*   DVDs, Newspapers, and Magazines

#### 4. Mathematical & Analytical Requirements
Learners are expected to master the following quantitative concepts:
*   **Population Estimation:** Using the mark-recapture method, typically represented by the Lincoln-Petersen index formula:
    $$N = \frac{M \times C}{R}$$
    *Where:*
    *   $N$ = Total population estimate
    *   $M$ = Number of individuals marked in the first sample
    *   $C$ = Total number of individuals captured in the second sample
    *   $R$ = Number of marked individuals recaptured in the second sample
*   **Graphical Representation:**
    *   Plotting and interpreting logistic and geometric growth curves.
    *   Constructing pie graphs for survey data.
    *   Interpreting predator-prey relationship graphs.
