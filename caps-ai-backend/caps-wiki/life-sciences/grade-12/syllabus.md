#### 2. CONTENT & COGNITIVE SCOPE

**Core Concepts:**
*   **Molecular Basis of Life:** Understanding that organisms are composed of atoms forming molecules.
*   **Genetic Information:** DNA as the carrier of the genetic code, determining organismal traits and functions.
*   **Cellular Organization:** The progression from DNA/genes to cell specialization, tissues, and organ systems.
*   **Biological Processes:** Replication, Transcription, and Translation (Protein Synthesis).

**Skills to be Mastered:**
*   **Analytical:** Interpreting the structure of DNA and RNA molecules.
*   **Procedural:** Extraction of DNA (practical investigation).
*   **Critical Thinking:** Understanding the necessity of DNA replication for the cell cycle.
*   **Application:** Applying knowledge of the genetic code to protein synthesis sequences.

**Cognitive Levels:**
*   **Knowledge:** Identifying locations of DNA/RNA, naming key scientists, and defining basic terminology.
*   **Routine Procedures:** Describing the sequence of events in transcription and translation.
*   **Complex Procedures:** Explaining the role of DNA in protein synthesis and the significance of non-coding DNA.
*   **Problem Solving:** Analyzing case studies related to DNA profiling and genetic code applications.

---

#### 3. MATHEMATICS & FORMULAS (Conceptual Context)
While specific mathematical formulas are not provided in this excerpt, the curriculum requires understanding the molecular ratios and base-pairing rules inherent in the structure of nucleic acids:

*   **Base Pairing Rules:**
    *   In DNA: Adenine ($A$) pairs with Thymine ($T$); Guanine ($G$) pairs with Cytosine ($C$).
    *   In RNA: Adenine ($A$) pairs with Uracil ($U$); Guanine ($G$) pairs with Cytosine ($C$).

*   **Genetic Code:**
    *   The relationship between codons (triplets of nucleotides) and amino acids:
        $$\text{Codon} \rightarrow \text{Amino Acid}$$

#### 2. Content & Cognitive Scope

**Strand 1: Meiosis**
*   **Core Concepts:**
    *   Process of reduction division.
    *   Purposes of gametogenesis (including exceptions: mosses, ferns).
    *   Importance of meiosis: Diploid to haploid transition, production of gametes.
    *   Introduction of genetic variation: Random segregation and crossing over.
    *   Consequences of abnormal meiosis: e.g., Down's syndrome.
    *   Comparison: Similarities and differences between mitosis and meiosis (linking to Grade 10 knowledge).
*   **Skills:** Observation and biological drawing of microscopic cell structures.
*   **Depth of Treatment:** Learners must be able to identify stages and explain the biological significance of genetic variation and chromosomal errors.

**Strand 2: Life Processes in Plants and Animals**
*   **Core Concepts:**
    *   Diversity of reproductive strategies in the animal kingdom.
    *   Mechanisms for reproductive success in varying environments.
    *   Fertilisation types: External vs. Internal.
    *   Developmental strategies: Ovipary, ovovivipary, vivipary.
    *   Structural adaptations: Amniotic egg.
    *   Offspring survival: Precocial vs. altricial development and parental care.
*   **Depth of Treatment:** Conceptual understanding of how reproductive strategies ensure survival in specific environments.

### 2. CONTENT & COGNITIVE SCOPE

#### Core Concepts & Depth of Treatment
*   **Foundations of Genetics:**
    *   Genes: Dominant and recessive genes and alleles.
    *   Historical context: Gregor Mendel (Father of Genetics).
*   **Inheritance and Variation:**
    *   **Monohybrid crosses:** Phenotype and genotype; homozygous and heterozygous (pure bred and hybrid).
    *   **Patterns of Inheritance:** Complete, incomplete, and partial dominance; codominance.
    *   **Dihybrid crosses:** Phenotypes and genotypes.
*   **Chromosomal Basis:**
    *   Sex chromosomes.
    *   Sex-linked alleles and sex-linked diseases (e.g., haemophilia, colour blindness).
*   **Mutations:**
    *   Harmless vs. harmful mutations.
    *   Examples of diseases/disorders.
    *   Gene mutations vs. chromosomal aberrations.
    *   Link between mutations and natural selection.
*   **Biotechnology & Applications:**
    *   Genetic engineering: Stem cell research, genetically modified organisms (GMOs), biotechnology, and cloning.
    *   Mitochondrial DNA and tracing genetic links.
    *   Paternity testing and DNA fingerprinting (forensics).

#### Skills to be Mastered
*   **Problem Solving:** Calculating and predicting outcomes of genetic crosses.
*   **Analytical:** Interpreting genetic lineages (pedigree charts).
*   **Investigative:** Applying knowledge of DNA to forensic and medical scenarios.

### 2. CONTENT & COGNITIVE SCOPE

#### Responding to the Environment: Plants
*   **Core Concepts:** Hormonal regulation in plants, tropisms, and structural/chemical defence mechanisms.
*   **Skills:** Designing investigations, identifying variables, controlling variables, recording and interpreting results.
*   **Depth of Treatment:** Understanding the role of auxins in growth regulation and the practical application of plant hormones in agriculture (weed control).

#### Evolution by Natural Selection
*   **Core Concepts:** 
    *   **Evidence:** Fossil record, biogeography, and genetics.
    *   **Theories:** Comparison of Lamarckism, Darwinism, and Punctuated Equilibrium.
    *   **Mechanisms:** Artificial selection vs. Natural selection.
    *   **Genetics Link:** Evolution as a change in the gene pool of inherited characteristics.
    *   **Population Dynamics:** Overproduction of offspring, environmental pressure, extinction, and adaptation.
    *   **Variation:** Continuous vs. discontinuous variation.
*   **Skills:** Debating scientific ideas, demonstrating natural selection through simulation, and researching/reporting on specific examples of artificial selection.
*   **Cognitive Levels:** 
    *   *Knowledge:* Defining terms like Lamarckism and Punctuated Equilibrium.
    *   *Complex Procedures:* Analyzing the link between genetics and evolutionary change.
    *   *Problem Solving:* Evaluating how environmental pressures lead to adaptation or extinction.

### 4. RESOURCES & TECHNICAL SPECIFICATIONS

*   **Required Equipment:** Seedlings, clinostat (if available).
*   **Reference Materials:** Textbooks, biographies of Charles Darwin.
*   **Mathematical/Conceptual Framework:** 
    *   Evolutionary change is defined as a shift in the gene pool frequency of inherited characteristics:
    $$\Delta P = \frac{\text{Number of specific alleles}}{\text{Total number of alleles in population}}$$
    *   *Note:* While the syllabus focuses on qualitative understanding, the mechanism of natural selection relies on the differential reproductive success of individuals:
    $$R = \frac{N_{offspring}}{N_{parents}}$$
    *   Where $R$ represents the reproductive rate, which determines the pressure on the gene pool in a given environment.

#### 2. Content & Cognitive Scope

**Core Concepts:**
*   **Evidence of common ancestors for living hominids:**
    *   Anatomical differences and similarities between African apes and humans.
    *   **Fossil evidence:** Key features including bipedalism (spine and pelvic girdle), brain size, teeth (dentition), prognathism, and palate shape, cranial and brow ridges.
    *   **Context:** Understanding the significance of thousands of fossil fragments.
    *   **Genetic evidence:** Mitochondrial DNA.
    *   **Cultural evidence:** Tool-making.
*   **Out of Africa Hypothesis:**
    *   Evidence for African origins of all modern humans.
    *   Genetic links and mitochondrial DNA.
    *   **Geographic sites:** Rift valley fossil sites in East Africa (Kenya and Tanzania) and Ethiopia.
    *   **Key figures:** Johansen, the Leaky family.
    *   **Fossil discoveries:** *Ardipithecus*, *Australopithecus*, *Homo*.
    *   **South African sites:** Specific fossils discovered at these sites.

**Instructional Requirements:**
*   **Poster Presentation:** Map out the three major phases in hominid evolution from $6 \text{ mya}$ to the present.
*   **Genera Mapping:** Show diagnostic features and approximate timelines for *Ardipithecus* (Ethiopia), *Australopithecus* (East and South Africa), and *Homo* (various sites).
*   **Depth of Treatment:** Students must understand that scientists may interpret relationships between genera differently as new evidence is discovered.
