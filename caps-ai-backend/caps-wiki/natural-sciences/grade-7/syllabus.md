## 2. Content & Cognitive Scope

### Core Concepts
*   **The Biosphere:** Understanding the interconnectedness of the lithosphere, hydrosphere, and atmosphere as the foundation for life.
*   **Life Processes:** Mastery of the seven biological functions required for an organism to be classified as "living."
*   **Biodiversity & Classification:** 
    *   Understanding the necessity of classification systems.
    *   Distinguishing between major biological kingdoms.
    *   Understanding the hierarchical structure of biological classification:
        $$\text{Kingdom} \rightarrow \text{Phylum/Division} \rightarrow \text{Class} \rightarrow \text{Order} \rightarrow \text{Family} \rightarrow \text{Genus} \rightarrow \text{Species}$$

### Skills to be Mastered
*   **Observational Skills:** Identifying observable features (shape, colour, size, use) to categorize objects and organisms.
*   **Analytical Skills:** Comparing and contrasting vertebrates and invertebrates.
*   **Practical Investigation:** Designing and conducting experiments to test environmental requirements for life (e.g., seed germination under varying conditions).

### Cognitive Levels
*   **Knowledge:** Recalling definitions of the biosphere and the seven life processes.
*   **Routine Procedures:** Sorting organisms into established classification groups.
*   **Complex Procedures:** Investigating the growth of seedlings under different environmental variables.
*   **Problem Solving:** Applying classification logic to identify unknown specimens based on observable characteristics.

### Natural Sciences: Sexual Reproduction in Angiosperms (CAPS Curriculum)

#### 1. Annual Teaching Plan (ATP) & Timelines
| Time | Topic |
| :--- | :--- |
| 3 ½ weeks | Sexual reproduction in Angiosperms |

#### 2. Content & Cognitive Scope

**Core Concepts:**
*   **Sexual reproduction in Angiosperms:**
    *   Seeds produced in flowers (sexual organs of Angiosperms).
    *   **Flower components:**
        *   Stamens (male structures for producing pollen containing male sex cells).
        *   Stigma (female structure for receiving pollen).
        *   Ovary (female structure for producing female sex cells).
        *   Petals (attracting pollinators).
        *   Sepals (protecting the flower bud).
    *   **Processes:**
        *   Pollination and fertilisation as essential processes for seed production.
        *   Pollination mechanisms: Wind, water, and pollinators (insects, birds, mammals).
        *   Adaptations: Large colourful petals, scent, and sweet nectar.
        *   Role of pollinators in food crop production (e.g., maize).
    *   **Fertilisation:**
        *   Fusion of male and female sex cells.
        *   Pollen grain process: Pollen attaches to stigma $\rightarrow$ pollen tube grows down style $\rightarrow$ male sex cells reach ovule.
        *   Double fertilisation: One male cell fertilizes the egg (forming a seed); the other unites with two cells in the embryo sac (forming endosperm/starchy food).
        *   Post-fertilisation: Ovary enlarges to become fruit; seeds contained within.
    *   **Dispersal:** Fruits and seeds dispersed in various ways.

**Skills to be Mastered:**
*   Observing life cycle stages.
*   Measuring plant growth (height) over time.
*   Recording data in diagrams, tables, and graphs.
*   Identifying, drawing, and describing flower components.
*   Comparing flower structures and pollination methods.
*   Describing adaptations for pollination and seed dispersal.

#### 3. Suggested Practical Work & Resources

| Activity Type | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Investigations** | Growing plants (beans/maize) to observe life cycle stages; measuring height; recording data. |
| **Practical Work** | Identifying, drawing, and describing flower components; comparing structures and pollination methods. |
| **Demonstrations** | Describing different fruit, seeds, and methods of seed dispersal. |

**Required Equipment & Resources:**
*   Variety of plant specimens.
*   Soil.
*   Containers for growing plants.
*   Seeds (e.g., beans and maize).
*   Rulers or measuring tapes.

#### 4. Cognitive Levels & Depth of Treatment
*   **Knowledge:** Recall of flower parts (stamen, stigma, ovary, petals, sepals) and definitions of pollination/fertilisation.
*   **Routine Procedures:** Measuring plant growth, recording observations in tables/graphs, drawing biological structures.
*   **Complex Procedures:** Comparing pollination adaptations across different species; explaining the biological process of double fertilisation.
*   **Problem Solving:** Analyzing the relationship between pollinators and food security; evaluating how specific seed structures facilitate dispersal in different environments.

### Natural Sciences: Acids, Bases, and Neutrals (Curriculum Specification)

#### 1. Content & Cognitive Scope

| Category | Specifications |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Core Concepts** | Properties of acids, bases, and neutrals; Acid-base indicators (litmus paper). |
| **Depth of Treatment** | Identification of substances based on physical properties (taste, feel, corrosiveness) and chemical testing using indicators. |
| **Skills to be Mastered** | Observation, classification, experimental testing, data recording, and drawing conclusions. |
| **Cognitive Levels** | **Knowledge:** Identifying properties of acids/bases. **Routine Procedures:** Using litmus paper to test substances. **Complex Procedures:** Analyzing results to classify household substances. |

#### 2. Content Detail

**Properties of Acids, Bases, and Neutrals**

*   **Acids:**
    *   Taste: Sour.
    *   Feel: Rough on the skin.
    *   Safety: Many are dangerous to taste or feel (corrosive).
    *   Examples: Lemon juice, other fruit juices, vinegar, tartaric acid, swimming pool acid.
*   **Bases:**
    *   Taste: Bitter.
    *   Feel: Slippery on the skin.
    *   Safety: Many are dangerous to taste or feel (corrosive).
    *   Terminology: Soluble bases are called alkaline.
    *   Examples: Bicarbonate of soda, washing powder, most soaps, bleach, household cleaners.
*   **Neutrals:**
    *   Examples: Pure water, salt solution, sugar solution, cooking oil.
    *   Classification: Neither acids nor bases.

**Acid-Base Indicators**

*   **Tool:** Red and blue litmus paper.
*   **Mechanism:**
    *   Red litmus paper: Remains red in an acid or neutral; turns blue in a base.
    *   Blue litmus paper: Remains blue in a base or neutral; turns red in an acid.
*   **Protocol:** Always use both red and blue litmus paper to test a substance.

#### 3. Suggested Activities: Investigations & Practical Work

| Activity | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Household Substance Investigation** | Test a range of substances (vinegar, tartaric acid, aspirin, antacids, shampoo, soap, bicarbonate of soda, salt water, sugar water, liquid soap). |
| **Methodology** | Test whether substances are acids, bases, or neutrals using red and blue litmus paper. |
| **Data Handling** | Record results in a table and draw conclusions. |
| **Note on Detergents/Soaps** | Detergents/soaps are expected to test basic, but some additives (e.g., lemon juice) may cause acidic results. Check packaging labels for verification. |

#### 2. Content & Cognitive Scope

**Core Concepts:**
*   **Definition of an Element:** A pure substance which cannot be broken down further.
*   **The Periodic Table:** A classification system for elements that make up matter and materials in the world.
*   **Historical Context:** Devised by Dmitri Mendeleev in the 1860s, arranging elements by properties in a table format.
*   **Structural Arrangement:**
    *   Left-hand side: Metals.
    *   Far right-hand side: Non-metals.
    *   Region between metals and non-metals: Semi-metals.
*   **Element Specifications:** Each element has a unique name, symbol, atomic number, and specific position on the table.
*   **Properties:**
    *   **Metals:** Usually shiny, ductile, malleable, solid (except mercury), high melting/boiling points.
    *   **Non-metals:** Variety of properties depending on state (solids or gases).
    *   **Semi-metals:** Solids with properties of both metals and non-metals.

**Skills to be Mastered:**
*   **Identification:** Learning names and symbols of the first 20 elements.
*   **Categorization:** Classifying elements as metals, semi-metals, or non-metals.
*   **Practical Application:** Measuring boiling points, separating mixtures (sand, iron filings, salt, ethanol, water), and testing substances with indicators.

## 2. Content & Cognitive Scope

### Core Concepts
*   **Energy Definition:** Energy is required to perform work, move, or live.
*   **Energy Sources:**
    *   *Non-renewable:* Cannot be replenished once used (e.g., coal, oil, natural gas, uranium).
    *   *Renewable:* Continually replenished (e.g., hydro, wind, sunlight, wood/biofuel).
*   **Energy Types:**
    *   **Potential Energy ($E_p$):** Stored energy in a system (e.g., stretched rubber band, weight on a table, chemical energy in a cell/battery or food).
    *   **Kinetic Energy ($E_k$):** Energy of a body in motion (e.g., falling weight, wind, moving vehicle).
*   **Systems:** A system is defined as a set of parts working together.
    *   *Mechanical systems:* e.g., scissors, rulers, cricket balls.
    *   *Thermal systems:* e.g., heating cold water.
    *   *Electrical systems:* e.g., circuits with motors, buzzers, or bulbs.
    *   *Biological systems:* e.g., food chains.

### Cognitive Depth & Skills
*   **Knowledge:** Identifying and defining energy sources and types.
*   **Routine Procedures:** Reading energy labels on food packaging; setting up simple electrical circuits.
*   **Complex Procedures:** Investigating energy transfers across different systems (mechanical, thermal, electrical, biological).
*   **Problem Solving:** Explaining why specific sources are classified as renewable or non-renewable; analyzing energy flow in a food chain.

### 4. MATHEMATICAL REPRESENTATION
*While the provided curriculum page focuses on conceptual physics, the underlying energy principles are governed by the following relationships:*

*   **Conservation of Energy:**
    $$\Delta E_{total} = 0$$
    $$E_{input} = E_{output} + E_{lost}$$

*   **Thermal Conduction (Conceptual Basis):**
    The rate of heat transfer $Q$ is dependent on the material's thermal conductivity $k$:
    $$Q \propto k \cdot A \cdot \frac{\Delta T}{d}$$
    *(Where $A$ is cross-sectional area, $\Delta T$ is temperature difference, and $d$ is distance/thickness).*

    *   Mechanical to electrical energy conversion.
    *   Dynamos: Small generators converting mechanical movement to electricity (e.g., bicycle lights, mine helmets, wind-up torches).

### 4. MATHEMATICAL & SCIENTIFIC PRINCIPLES
The curriculum emphasizes the principle of energy conservation and loss within a system, represented by the relationship:

$$E_{input} = E_{useful} + E_{wasted}$$

Where:
*   $E_{input}$ is the total energy supplied to the system.
*   $E_{useful}$ is the energy converted into the desired output.
*   $E_{wasted}$ is the energy dissipated into the surroundings (usually as heat or sound).

Efficiency ($\eta$) can be inferred from the curriculum's focus on waste:
$$\eta = \left( \frac{E_{useful}}{E_{input}} \right) \times 100\%$$

#### 4. Mathematical & Scientific Principles
The curriculum requires understanding the qualitative relationships of gravity, which can be represented by the universal law of gravitation:

$$F = G \frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2}$$

Where:
*   $F$ is the gravitational force.
*   $G$ is the gravitational constant.
*   $m_1, m_2$ are the masses of the objects (e.g., Earth and Moon).
*   $r$ is the distance between the centers of the masses.

**Key Instructional Note:** Students must master the inverse-square relationship between distance ($r$) and force ($F$), and the direct relationship between mass ($m$) and force ($F$).
