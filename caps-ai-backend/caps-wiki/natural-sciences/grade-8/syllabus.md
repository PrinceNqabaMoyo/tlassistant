## 2. Content & Cognitive Scope

### Core Concepts
*   **Photosynthesis:**
    *   Interactions and interdependence in an ecosystem driven by the need for energy.
    *   The Sun as the primary energy source (light and heat).
    *   Chemical process: Plants use carbon dioxide (from air) and water (from soil) to produce glucose (food).
    *   Chemical Equation: 
        $$\text{carbon dioxide} + \text{water} \xrightarrow[\text{sunlight}]{\text{chlorophyll}} \text{glucose} + \text{oxygen}$$
    *   Conversion of glucose into starch, cellulose, and other chemical compounds for growth and reproduction.
*   **Respiration:**
    *   Food as a source of potential energy.
    *   Process by which energy is released from food via chemical reactions.
    *   Chemical Equation:
        $$\text{glucose} + \text{oxygen} \rightarrow \text{energy} + \text{carbon dioxide} + \text{water}$$

### Skills & Practical Work
*   **Investigation 1 (Photosynthesis):** Testing leaves for starch production.
    *   Method: Boiling leaves, extracting chlorophyll using ethanol/methylated spirits, applying iodine solution.
    *   Reporting: Structure report with headings (Aim, Hypothesis, Method, Results, Conclusion, Discussion).
*   **Investigation 2 (Respiration):** Testing for carbon dioxide in exhaled air.
    *   Method: Using clear lime water and drinking straws.

#### 3. Mathematical & Scientific Principles

*   **Energy Transfer Rule:** Energy transfer between trophic levels follows the $10\%$ efficiency principle.
    *   If $E_{in}$ is the energy at a lower level, the energy available to the next level ($E_{out}$) is approximately:
    $$E_{out} \approx 0.10 \times E_{in}$$
*   **Ecosystem Dynamics:** Ecosystem balance is defined by the relationship between resources ($R$) and population capacity ($C$). If $R < C$, the system fails to remain in balance.

*   Field observation tools: Notebooks for recording local ecosystem interactions.
*   Analytical tools: Diagrams for mapping food webs and energy pyramids.

### 2. CONTENT & COGNITIVE SCOPE

#### **Core Concepts**
*   **Atoms as Building Blocks:**
    *   All matter is composed of atoms.
    *   Definition of an element: Substance consisting of identical atoms.
    *   Chemical indivisibility: Elements cannot be broken down into simpler substances by chemical means.
    *   Elements cannot be transformed into other elements via chemical reactions.
    *   Periodic Table: Listing of all known elements.
*   **Sub-atomic Particles:**
    *   Composition: Protons, neutrons, and electrons.
    *   Structure: Central nucleus containing protons and neutrons; electrons move in the space surrounding the nucleus.
    *   Electrical Charge: Atoms are electrically neutral because the number of negatively charged electrons equals the number of positively charged protons ($n_{e^-} = n_{p^+}$).

#### **Skills to be Mastered**
*   **Modeling:** Constructing 2D representations of atomic structures.
*   **Identification:** Using the Periodic Table to identify the first 20 elements.
*   **Conceptualization:** Visualizing the spatial arrangement of sub-atomic particles.

#### **Cognitive Levels & Depth of Treatment**
*   **Knowledge:** Recall definitions of atoms, elements, and sub-atomic particles.
*   **Routine Procedures:** Identifying elements on the Periodic Table and calculating particle counts for neutral atoms.
*   **Complex Procedures:** Constructing physical models to represent abstract atomic structures.

### 4. MATHEMATICAL REPRESENTATION OF ATOMIC NEUTRALITY

For any neutral atom, the relationship between sub-atomic particles is defined as:

$$N_{\text{protons}} = N_{\text{electrons}}$$

Where:
*   $N_{\text{protons}}$ is the total count of positively charged particles in the nucleus.
*   $N_{\text{electrons}}$ is the total count of negatively charged particles in the electron cloud.

### Natural Sciences: Particle Model of Matter (CAPS Specification)

#### 1. Annual Teaching Plan (ATP) & Timelines
| Time Allocation | Topic | Pacing Guideline |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 5 Weeks | Particle model of matter | Intensive conceptual development of atomic/molecular theory |

#### 2. Content & Cognitive Scope

**Core Concepts:**
*   **The Particle Model of Matter:** Understanding that all matter (solids, liquids, gases) is composed of particles (atoms and molecules).
*   **Scientific Theory:** The particle model as a framework to explain the physical state and behavior of matter.
*   **Scale and Vacuity:** Recognition that particles are sub-microscopic; spaces between particles are empty (vacuum), not containing air.
*   **Comparative States of Matter:**
    *   **Solids:** Regular arrangement, strong forces, vibration only, small inter-particle spaces.
    *   **Liquids:** Loose arrangement, weaker forces, sliding motion, small inter-particle spaces.
    *   **Gases:** No regular arrangement, extremely weak forces, rapid movement, large inter-particle spaces.

**Cognitive Levels & Skills:**
*   **Knowledge:** Defining atoms, molecules, and the particle model.
*   **Routine Procedures:** Drawing diagrams to represent particle arrangements in different states.
*   **Complex Procedures:** Comparing and contrasting the physical properties of solids, liquids, and gases based on particle behavior.
*   **Problem Solving:** Applying the particle model to explain observations of matter.

#### 3. Suggested Practical Work & Investigations
| Activity Type | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Diagrammatic Representation** | Drawing diagrams to represent particles in a solid, liquid, and gas; explaining arrangement, movement, forces, and spacing. |
| **Comparative Analysis** | Constructing a table comparing the particles of gases, liquids, and solids. |

#### 4. Mathematical & Scientific Notation
The particle model relies on the understanding of spatial relationships and force interactions. While specific quantitative formulas are not provided in this section, the conceptual framework follows:

*   **Force Interaction:** $F_{solid} > F_{liquid} > F_{gas}$ (where $F$ represents the relative strength of inter-particle forces).
*   **Spacing/Volume:** $S_{gas} \gg S_{liquid} \approx S_{solid}$ (where $S$ represents the relative magnitude of empty space between particles).

#### 2. Instructional Planning: Content Details

| Topic | Key Concepts |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Density of different materials** | - Materials have varying densities (e.g., bread vs. clay brick).<br>- Density depends on particle type and inter-particle spacing.<br>- Lower density materials float on higher density liquids (e.g., oil on water). |
| **Expansion and contraction** | - Solids, liquids, and gases expand when heated and contract when cooled.<br>- Heating increases particle movement, pushing particles further apart (expansion).<br>- Cooling decreases particle movement, bringing particles closer together (contraction).<br>- **Crucial distinction:** Individual particles do not change size; only the spaces between them change. |

#### 3. Suggested Practical Work & Resources

| Topic | Suggested Activities | Equipment & Resources |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |

| **Density** | Mixing oil and water; reading about oil pollution on water surfaces. | Beakers, oil, water. |
| **Expansion** | Demonstrating expansion and contraction using thermal energy. | Ball and ring apparatus. |

#### 4. Scientific Principles & Mathematical Context
While the provided text focuses on conceptual modeling, the underlying physical relationships are governed by the following principles:

*   **Density Formula:**
    $$\rho = \frac{m}{V}$$
    *Where $\rho$ is density, $m$ is mass, and $V$ is volume.*

*   **Particle Behavior:**
    *   **Expansion:** As temperature ($T$) increases, the average kinetic energy of particles increases, leading to an increase in the average distance ($d$) between particles.
    *   **Contraction:** As temperature ($T$) decreases, the average kinetic energy of particles decreases, leading to a decrease in the average distance ($d$) between particles.

*   **Note on Particle Size:**
    The curriculum explicitly emphasizes that for any substance:
    $$r_{particle} \text{ (heated)} = r_{particle} \text{ (cooled)}$$
    *The radius ($r$) of the particle remains constant during thermal expansion/contraction.*

#### 3. Technical Specifications & Conceptual Framework

**Reflection Principles:**
*   The angle of incidence ($\theta_i$) is equal to the angle of reflection ($\theta_r$):
    $$\theta_i = \theta_r$$
*   The normal is defined as the line perpendicular to the surface at the point of incidence.

**Refraction Principles:**
*   Light changes direction when entering a medium of different optical density.
*   Light traveling from a denser medium to a less dense medium bends away from the normal.
*   Dispersion occurs when white light is separated into its constituent spectral colors via a triangular prism.

**Biological Mechanism:**
*   Light $\rightarrow$ Retina (specialized receptor cells) $\rightarrow$ Electrical impulses $\rightarrow$ Brain interpretation.
