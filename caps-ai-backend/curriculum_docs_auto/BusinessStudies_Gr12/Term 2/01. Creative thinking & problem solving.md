## Routine Thinking vs. Creative Thinking

### Routine Thinking
*   **Brain Function:** Associated with the LEFT brain.
*   **Focus:** Logical thought and a methodical way of doing things.
*   **Nature:** Consistent, normal thinking applied to specific situations.
*   **Outcome:** Doing things in the same way leads to the same results; no new ideas or thought patterns are generated.

### Creative Thinking
*   **Brain Function:** Associated with the RIGHT brain.
*   **Focus:** Uses imagination, insight, art, and music to find solutions to unique challenges.
*   **Nature:** Generates new ideas and approaches problems in an unusual way.
*   **Outcome:** Explores **innovative** ideas to obtain better results.

---

## Conventional vs. Non-conventional Solutions

### Conventional Solutions
*   Believed and followed by everyone.
*   What most people expect; they appear "normal."
*   Focus on being relevant to a specific situation.
*   Usually, there is only one accepted solution to each problem.

### Non-conventional Solutions
*   Not created as part of the norm; do not follow traditional beliefs.
*   Creative, unique, and usually the result of creative thinking.
*   Often interrupted or changed by even better ideas.
*   Diverse solutions may be applied to a single problem or challenge.

4.  **Communication:** Keep channels open by placing suggestion boxes around the workplace.

## 8.2 Problem-solving techniques

Businesses use problem-solving as an instrument to determine if there are aspects within the business environment that need to be changed. This ensures better control over the business environment and allows for the quick identification and implementation of solutions.

### The Meaning of Problem-solving
*   **Definition:** The process of studying a situation to find ways to bring about change.
*   **Process:** It involves collecting facts, including problem finding and problem shaping.
*   **Skills:** Creative and logical skills must be applied to find solutions to identified problems.
*   **Evaluation:** Alternative solutions should be generated and evaluated in response to problems.
*   **Participants:** Problems can be solved by an individual, a group, or a team member.

---

### Key Definitions
*   **Change management:** A process used by organisations to facilitate and implement change through internal and external processes.
*   **Experts:** People who are very knowledgeable and skillful in a specific area.
*   **Questionnaires:** An instrument with a series of questions and answers used for research purposes.

---

### Problem-solving Techniques

#### 1. Delphi Technique
*   **Purpose:** Used to solve new and complex problems.
*   **Method:** A panel of experts who never meet face-to-face provides creative solutions, avoiding the need for long, drawn-out group meetings.

#### 2. Force Field Analysis
*   **Purpose:** Used for decision-making, especially when implementing change management.
*   **Method:** The analysis involves identifying the advantages (driving forces) and disadvantages (restraining forces) of a decision for change.

### Application of the Delphi Technique
To apply the Delphi Technique effectively, businesses should follow these steps:

1.  **Invite Experts:** Invite a panel of experts to study complaints from customers.
2.  **Individual Contact:** Experts do not need to be in the same location and are contacted individually.
3.  **Design Questionnaire:** A questionnaire containing questions on how to improve the quality of products or services is designed and distributed to the panel members.
4.  **Individual Response:** The panel members respond to the questionnaire individually to suggest improvements and return them to the business.
5.  **Feedback Report:** The responses from the experts are summarized into a final feedback report.

# Unit 8.2: Problem-Solving Techniques (The Delphi Technique)

This section outlines the iterative process used in the Delphi technique to reach a consensus among a panel of experts.

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

## The Delphi Technique Process

The Delphi technique follows a structured, cyclical approach to gather expert opinion and reach a final decision. The process is broken down into the following steps:

### Step-by-Step Procedure

1.  **Preparation:** Prepare the questionnaire for Round 1.
2.  **Selection:** Select respondents (panel members) based on clearly defined criteria.
3.  **Initial Engagement:** Invite participants to the Delphi process, send out the questionnaire, and collect responses for Round 1.
4.  **Analysis & Feedback:** Analyse the responses and provide feedback to the participants.
5.  **Second Round:** Prepare and send out the questionnaire for Round 2.
6.  **Iterative Analysis:** Analyse the responses and provide feedback again.
7.  **Consensus Building:** Continue steps 5 and 6 until consensus is reached.

### Post-Process Requirements

Once the iterative rounds are complete, the following actions are taken to finalise the solution:

*   **Feedback Reporting:** A feedback report and a second set of questions or questionnaires based on the feedback report must be sent to the panel members.
*   **Expert Input:** The panel members are requested to provide further input or ideas on how to improve the quality of products or services after they have studied the results and documentation.
*   **Refinement:** A third questionnaire based on previous feedback from the second round is distributed to panel members and experts.
*   **Finalisation:** A final summary or feedback report with all the methods to improve the quality of products or services is prepared.
*   **Conclusion:** After consensus has been reached, the best solution is chosen.

# Term 2: Force Field Analysis

## The Application of Force Field Analysis
Force Field Analysis is a decision-making tool used to evaluate the forces for and against a proposed change. To apply this technique, follow these steps:

1.  **Define the Situation:** Describe the current situation (problem) and the desired situation (solution).
2.  **Analyze Inaction:** Identify the consequences if no action is taken.
3.  **List Forces:** Identify all driving forces (supporting change) and restraining forces (resisting change).
4.  **Discuss Strengths:** Evaluate the key restraining and driving forces.
5.  **Numerical Scoring:** Allocate a score to each force using a scale of $1$ to $5$, where $1$ is weak and $5$ is strong.
6.  **Evaluate Viability:** Weigh the positives against the negatives to determine if the project is viable.
7.  **Select Solution:** Choose the force with the highest score as the primary solution.
8.  **Mitigate Resistance:** Analyze restraining forces to determine the best strategy for moving forward.
9.  **Action Plan:** Identify priorities and develop a formal action plan.

# 8.3 Solutions to a Business Problem

In the business environment, employees must work collaboratively to successfully identify and implement solutions to business problems. Working together builds positive employee morale, which encourages "buy-in" when the time comes to implement those solutions.

## Advantages of Working with Others to Solve Problems

Collaborative problem-solving offers several strategic benefits to an organization:

*   **Diverse Perspectives:** It is often difficult to be creative and innovative in isolation. Working with others allows for a variety of viewpoints to be considered.
*   **Broader Skill Base:** Access to a wider range of skills, knowledge, and experiences allows for more comprehensive solutions.
*   **Idea Generation:** Ideas are often **triggered** by the contributions of others, which accelerates the problem-solving process.
*   **Knowledge Sharing:** It facilitates peer-to-peer learning, where colleagues can teach and explain concepts to one another.
*   **Improved Results:** Working together is encouraged to produce higher-quality outcomes and greater results.
*   **Shared Workload:** The burden of the problem is distributed, as everyone shares the responsibility and their unique experiences.

### Application of Force Field Analysis
To effectively apply Force Field Analysis, follow these steps:
1. Describe the current situation (problem) and the desired situation.

### 3.5 Applying Force Field Analysis to Solve Business Problems

Force Field Analysis is a decision-making tool used to evaluate the forces for and against a change. The process involves:

1.  **Define the Situation:** Clearly describe the current problem or the desired future state.
2.  **Predict Consequences:** Identify the potential outcomes if no action is taken.
3.  **List Forces:** Identify all driving forces (supporting change) and restraining forces (resisting change).
4.  **Discuss Strengths:** Analyze the intensity and impact of the identified restraining forces.
5.  **Discuss Driving Forces:** Analyze the impact of the forces pushing for change.
6.  **Numerical Scoring:** Assign a score to each force on a scale (e.g., $1 = \text{weak}$, $5 = \text{strong}$).
7.  **Evaluation:** Weigh the positives against the negatives to determine if the project is viable.
8.  **Strategic Planning:** Analyze the restraining forces to develop a strategy to overcome them and move forward.
