## 14.1 Solving by Inspection

### Number Puzzles
Solve the following puzzles to practice identifying unknown values:

1. I am thinking of a certain number. If I add $3$ to that number, the answer is $13$. What is the number?
2. I am thinking of a certain number. If I multiply that number by $5$, the answer is $30$. What is the number?
3. I am thinking of a certain number. If I multiply that number by $3$ and then add $4$ to the result, the answer is $19$.
    * (a) Is the number $3$? Give a reason for your answer.
    * (b) Is the number $4$? Give a reason for your answer.
    * (c) Is the number $5$? Give a reason for your answer.
    * (d) Is the number $6$? Give a reason for your answer.

---

### Key Concepts and Definitions

*   **Algebraic Representation:** Number puzzles can be shortened by using letter symbols (variables) as placeholders for unknown numbers. For example, "a certain number plus $3$ equals $13$" is written as:
    $$x + 3 = 13$$
*   **Equation:** A mathematical statement such as $x + 3 = 13$ that could be true or false depending on the value of $x$ is called an **open number sentence** or an **equation**.
*   **Solution:** The value of the unknown that makes the equation true is called the **solution** of the number sentence.
    *   *Example:* For $x + 3 = 13$, the solution is $x = 10$ because $10 + 3 = 13$ makes the statement true.
*   **Solving by Inspection:** When the solution to a number sentence can be seen at once (e.g., for $x + 4 = 20$, we know $x = 16$ because $16 + 4 = 20$), we say we solve the number sentence **by inspection**.

### 14.2 Solving by the Trial and Improvement Method

When a solution to an equation is not immediately obvious, use the **trial and improvement** method. This involves testing various possible values until the correct solution is identified.

**Example:** Solve $6 \times x - 11 = 43$

| Possible solution | Test | Conclusion |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Try 5 | $6 \times 5 - 11 = 30 - 11 = 19$ | 5 is too small |

| Try 8 | $6 \times 8 - 11 = 48 - 11 = 37$ | 8 is too small |
| Try 9 | $6 \times 9 - 11 = 54 - 11 = 43$ | 9 is the solution |

### Solving Equations by Inspection or Trial and Improvement

To solve an equation by inspection, we look at the relationship between the numbers and the variable to determine what value makes the statement true. In trial and improvement, we guess a value, test it in the equation, and adjust our guess until we find the correct solution.

#### Worked Examples

**4. Solve $4 \times x + 7 = 31$**

| Possible solution | Test | Conclusion |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $x = 6$ | $4(6) + 7 = 24 + 7 = 31$ | Correct |

**5. Solve $10 \times x + 11 = 141$**

| Possible solution | Test | Conclusion |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $x = 13$ | $10(13) + 11 = 130 + 11 = 141$ | Correct |

## Mathematical Background

### Definition of an Equation
An equation expresses equality. Expressions written on either side of the equal sign have the same value.
*   **Example:** $15 = 18 - x$ or $3 + x = 9$

### Solving an Equation
Solving an equation means finding the value of the unknown (e.g., $x$) that makes the equation true. While there are various methods depending on complexity, we primarily use **inverse operations** to build algebraic reasoning.

### Methods of Solving

#### 1. Solving by Inspection
This method is used when the solution can be identified immediately by asking, "Which value makes this equation true?"
*   **Example:** For the question "what plus $7$ is $12$?", we write the equation as $x + 7 = 12$. By inspection, we know $x = 5$ because $5 + 7 = 12$.
*   **Note:** When dealing with subtractions like $24 - x = 14$, asking "24 minus what is 14?" helps avoid common errors made when applying inverse operations incorrectly.

#### 2. Solving by Trial and Improvement
This method involves substituting different values for the unknown until the value that makes the equation true is found.

# Chapter 14: Algebraic Equations 1

## 14.1 Solving by Inspection

### Key Concepts
*   **Equation:** A mathematical statement that shows two expressions are equal.
*   **Variable:** A letter symbol (placeholder) used to represent an unknown number.
*   **Open Number Sentence:** A mathematical statement that can be true or false depending on the value of the variable.
*   **Solution:** The specific value of the variable that makes the equation true.
*   **Solving by Inspection:** Determining the value of the unknown by looking at the equation and identifying the number that satisfies the equality.

### Understanding Equations
An equation uses an equal sign ($=$) to relate two sides, indicating that they must be equal in value. 

**Example:**
*   "A certain number plus 3 equals 13" can be written as:
    $$x + 3 = 13$$
*   To solve this, we look for the value of $x$ that makes the statement true. Since $10 + 3 = 13$, the solution is $x = 10$.

---

### Exercises: Number Puzzles

1. I am thinking of a certain number. If I add 3 to that number, the answer is 13. What is the number?
2. I am thinking of a certain number. If I multiply that number by 5, the answer is 30. What is the number?
3. I am thinking of a certain number. If I multiply that number by 3 and then add 4 to the result, the answer is 19.
    *   (a) Is the number 3? Give a reason.
    *   (b) Is the number 4? Give a reason.
    *   (c) Is the number 5? Give a reason.
    *   (d) Is the number 6? Give a reason.

---

### Answers and Worked Examples

| Question | Solution | Reasoning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | $10$ | $10 + 3 = 13$ |
| 2 | $6$ | $6 \times 5 = 30$ |
| 3 (a) | No | $3 \times 3 + 4 = 9 + 4 = 13$ |
| 3 (b) | No | $3 \times 4 + 4 = 12 + 4 = 16$ |
| 3 (c) | Yes | $3 \times 5 + 4 = 15 + 4 = 19$ |
| 3 (d) | No | $3 \times 6 + 4 = 18 + 4 = 22$ |

---

### Teacher's Note: Misconceptions
Many learners view the equal sign ($=$) as an instruction to "do something" (e.g., $13 + 8 = ?$). It is important to teach learners that the equal sign represents a balance, where the left-hand side must equal the right-hand side.

### 1. Solving Equations by Inspection

Solving by inspection means looking at an equation and determining the value of the variable ($x$) that makes the left-hand side equal to the right-hand side.

**Practice Exercises: Solve by inspection**

| Question | Equation | Solution |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 (a) | $x - 8 = 8$ | $x = 16$ |
| 1 (b) | $x + 7 = 20$ | $x = 13$ |
| 1 (c) | $\frac{16}{x} = 8$ | $x = 2$ |
| 1 (d) | $\frac{x}{16} = 2$ | $x = 32$ |
| 1 (e) | $5 \times x = 40$ | $x = 8$ |
| 1 (f) | $8 \times x = 40$ | $x = 5$ |
| 2 (a) | $84 \div x = 7$ | $x = 12$ |
| 2 (b) | $36 \div x = 4$ | $x = 9$ |
| 2 (c) | $x + 56 = 100$ | $x = 44$ |
| 2 (d) | $100 - x = 56$ | $x = 44$ |

---

### 2. Solving by Trial and Improvement

When an equation is too complex to solve by inspection, use the **trial and improvement** method. This involves testing different values for the variable until you find the one that satisfies the equation.

**Worked Example:**
Solve: $6 \times x - 11 = 43$

| Possible solution | Test | Conclusion |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Try 5 | $6 \times 5 - 11 = 30 - 11 = 19$ | 5 is too small |
| Try 10 | $6 \times 10 - 11 = 60 - 11 = 49$ | 10 is too big |
| Try 8 | $6 \times 8 - 11 = 48 - 11 = 37$ | 8 is too small |
| Try 9 | $6 \times 9 - 11 = 54 - 11 = 43$ | 9 is the solution |

---

### 3. Important Concepts & Misconceptions

*   **Equation vs. Expression:** An **equation** must have an equal sign ($=$) to compare two sides. An **expression** (e.g., $2x + 12$) does not have an equal sign and cannot be "solved" in the same way.
*   **The Goal:** When solving, always look for the value of the variable ($x$), not the final value of the expression.

**Teaching Guidelines:**
*   Learners should work individually and then discuss their methods and answers in pairs or small groups.
*   **Misconception Alert:** Learners often assign different values to the same variable on either side of an equation. Emphasize that in a specific equation, all instances of the same variable must represent the same value.

**Worked Examples:**

| Equation | Possible Solution | Test | Conclusion |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |

| $10 \times x + 11 = 141$ | $x = 13$ | $10(13) + 11 = 130 + 11 = 141$ | The solution is $x = 13$ |

### 14.3 Describing Problem Situations with Equations

**Steps to Solve Word Problems:**
1.  Read the problem and identify what needs to be found.
2.  Assign a variable to represent the unknown quantity.
3.  Write an equation using the quantities provided in the problem.
4.  Solve the equation.
5.  Check your solution.

**Exercises: Write Number Sentences**

1.  There are 30 learners in a class. $x$ learners are absent and 19 are present.
    *   Equation: $30 - x = 19$ or $19 + x = 30$ or $30 - 19 = x$
2.  There are 70 passengers on a bus. At a bus stop $m$ passengers get off. There are now 23 passengers on the bus.
    *   Equation: $70 - m = 23$ or $m + 23 = 70$ or $70 - 23 = m$
3.  A boy buys a bicycle for R1 260 on lay-by. How many payments of R90 each must he make to pay for the bicycle? Let $x$ be the number of payments.
    *   Equation: $90 \times x = 1 260$ or $x = 1 260 \div 90$
4.  Five people share a total cost of R240 equally amongst themselves. Let $c$ be the cost per person.
    *   Equation: $5 \times c = 240$ or $c = 240 \div 5$
5.  A school charges R100 a day for the use of its training facilities plus R30 per athlete per day for food and equipment. A team of athletes paid R400 for a day's practice. Let $x$ be the number of athletes.
    *   Equation: $30 \times x + 100 = 400$ or $x = (400 - 100) \div 30$
6.  Bennie has R54 to buy chocolates. Each chocolate costs R6. How many chocolates can he buy? Let $x$ be the number of chocolates.
    *   Equation: $6 \times x = 54$ or $x = 54 \div 6$

### Making Sense of Equations

The primary objective is to analyze and interpret number sentences that describe real-life situations. It is essential to understand what each variable represents and what the constants signify within an equation.

#### Teaching Guidelines
*   Equations are tools used to describe real-life scenarios.
*   The specific choice of letters (variables) is not critical; any symbol can be used to represent an unknown value.
*   Focus on the relationship between the numbers and the context of the problem.

---

### Exercises: Making Sense of Equations

**1. Rajbansi Taxi Service**
Rajbansi Taxi Service charges R10 per kilometre travelled and a standard charge of R30 per trip. The equation for a trip is:
$$10 \times t + 30 = 80$$

*   **(a)** Explain what each number and letter symbol stands for in the equation.
*   **(b)** Why is $t$ multiplied by $10$ in the equation?

**2. Music Concert Tickets**
The cost of an adult's ticket for a music concert is four times the cost of a child's ticket. An adult's ticket costs R240. The equation is:
$$4 \times x = 240$$

*   **(a)** What does $x$ represent?
*   **(b)** Why is $x$ multiplied by $4$?
*   **(c)** Solve the equation by inspection.
*   **(d)** How much does a child's ticket cost?

**3. Egg Cartons**
There are 12 eggs in a carton. The equation is:
$$12 \times c = 72$$

*   **(a)** What does the letter symbol $c$ represent in the equation?
*   **(b)** What value of $c$ makes the equation true?
*   **(c)** What does the number $72$ represent?

---

### Answers and Explanations

| Question | Answer / Explanation |
| :--- | :--- |
| **1 (a)** | $80$ is the total amount charged; $t$ is the number of kilometres travelled; $10$ is the cost per kilometre; $30$ is the standard charge. |
| **1 (b)** | Because each kilometre travelled costs R10. |
| **2 (a)** | The cost of a child's ticket. |
| **2 (b)** | Because the cost of an adult's ticket is four times that of a child's ticket. |
| **2 (c)** | $4 \times x = 240 \implies 4 \times 60 = 240$, so $x = 60$. |
| **2 (d)** | R60. |
| **3 (a)** | The number of cartons. |
| **3 (b)** | $c = 6$. |
| **3 (c)** | The total number of eggs in $c$ cartons. |
