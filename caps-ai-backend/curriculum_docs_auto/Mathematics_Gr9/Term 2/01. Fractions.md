### Activity: A strange measuring unit
In this activity, we use a "greystick" as a unit of measurement to understand how fractions represent parts of a whole.

*   **Measuring with fractions:** When an object is longer than one unit but shorter than two, we divide the unit into equal parts to measure it accurately.

*   **One sixth ($\frac{1}{6}$):** When one greystick is divided into six equal parts, each part represents $\frac{1}{6}$ of the greystick.
*   **One seventh ($\frac{1}{7}$):** When one greystick is divided into seven equal parts, each part represents $\frac{1}{7}$ of the greystick.

### Understanding Fractions: Core Concepts

A fraction represents a part of a whole. It consists of two main components:

*   **Numerator:** The number of parts being considered.
*   **Denominator:** The total number of equal parts that make up the whole (the "name" of the part).

For example, in the fraction $\frac{5}{6}$:
*   The **numerator** is $5$.
*   The **denominator** is $6$.
*   We read this as "five sixths".

---

### Worked Examples: Addition of Fractions

To add fractions, they must have a common denominator. If they do not, convert them to equivalent fractions first.

**(a) Five twelfths plus three twelfths**
$$\frac{5}{12} + \frac{3}{12} = \frac{5+3}{12} = \frac{8}{12} = \frac{2}{3}$$

**(b) Five twelfths plus one quarter**
Convert $\frac{1}{4}$ to twelfths: $\frac{1 \times 3}{4 \times 3} = \frac{3}{12}$.
$$\frac{5}{12} + \frac{3}{12} = \frac{8}{12} = \frac{2}{3}$$

**(c) Five twelfths plus three quarters**
Convert $\frac{3}{4}$ to twelfths: $\frac{3 \times 3}{4 \times 3} = \frac{9}{12}$.
$$\frac{5}{12} + \frac{9}{12} = \frac{14}{12} = 1\frac{2}{12} = 1\frac{1}{6}$$

**(d) One third plus one quarter**
Find a common denominator (12):
$\frac{1}{3} = \frac{4}{12}$ and $\frac{1}{4} = \frac{3}{12}$.
$$\frac{4}{12} + \frac{3}{12} = \frac{7}{12}$$

A fraction represents parts of a whole. It is written as:
$$\frac{\text{numerator}}{\text{denominator}}$$

*   **Denominator:** The total number of equal parts the whole is divided into (written below the line).

### 6.3 Combining Fractions

When denominators are the same, you can add the numerators while keeping the denominator constant.

#### Worked Example 1: Adding fractions with the same denominator
**Problem:** A team builds $\frac{8}{12}$ km of road in one week and $\frac{10}{12}$ km in the next. What is the total length?

1.  **Add the numerators:** $8 + 10 = 18$
2.  **Keep the denominator:** $\frac{18}{12}$
3.  **Simplify:** Since $\frac{12}{12} = 1$ whole, then $\frac{18}{12} = \frac{12}{12} + \frac{6}{12} = 1 \frac{6}{12}$
4.  **Final result:** $1 \frac{6}{12}$ km, which is the same as $1 \frac{1}{2}$ km.

#### Worked Example 2: Adding mixed numbers
**Problem:** Calculate $4 \frac{5}{9} + 2 \frac{7}{9}$

1.  **Add the whole numbers:** $4 + 2 = 6$
2.  **Add the fractions:** $\frac{5}{9} + \frac{7}{9} = \frac{12}{9}$
3.  **Convert the improper fraction:** $\frac{12}{9} = \frac{9}{9} + \frac{3}{9} = 1 \frac{3}{9}$
4.  **Combine the results:** $6 + 1 \frac{3}{9} = 7 \frac{3}{9}$

## 1. Operations with Mixed Numbers
When working with mixed numbers, you can either convert them to improper fractions or work with the whole numbers and fractions separately.

### Worked Example: Subtraction of Mixed Numbers
To solve $4\frac{7}{12} - 1\frac{5}{12}$:
1. Subtract the whole numbers: $4 - 1 = 3$
2. Subtract the fractions: $\frac{7}{12} - \frac{5}{12} = \frac{2}{12}$
3. Simplify the fraction: $\frac{2}{12} = \frac{1}{6}$
4. Combine: $3\frac{1}{6}$

# 6.6 Fraction of a Fraction

To calculate a "fraction of a fraction" or a "fraction of an amount," you multiply the fraction by the total value. The word "of" in mathematics indicates multiplication.

### Core Concept: Calculating Parts of a Whole
To find a fraction of an amount, divide the amount by the denominator (bottom number) and multiply by the numerator (top number).

**Example:** To find $\frac{3}{5}$ of $R60$:
1. Find $\frac{1}{5}$ of $R60$: $R60 \div 5 = R12$
2. Find $\frac{3}{5}$ of $R60$: $R12 \times 3 = R36$

### Key Concepts: Equivalent Fractions and Multiplication
When calculating a fraction of a fraction, it is often easier to use equivalent fractions to simplify the calculation.

**Example:**
To calculate $\frac{3}{8}$ of $\frac{2}{3}$:
1. Finding one-eighth of $\frac{2}{3}$ is difficult.
2. We look for an equivalent fraction for $\frac{2}{3}$ where the numerator is divisible by $8$.
3. Since $\frac{2}{3} = \frac{8}{12}$, we can calculate $\frac{3}{8}$ of $\frac{8}{12}$ instead.
4. One-twelfth of $8$ is $\frac{8}{12} = \frac{2}{3}$. Therefore, $\frac{3}{8}$ of $\frac{8}{12} = \frac{3}{12} = \frac{1}{4}$.

### 11. Calculating Fractions of Fractions
To calculate a fraction of a fraction, multiply the numerators together and the denominators together.

**Worked Examples:**
(a) $\frac{1}{2}$ of $\frac{1}{3}$ of R60
$= (\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{3}) \times 60 = \frac{1}{6} \times 60 = R10$

(b) $\frac{2}{7}$ of $\frac{2}{9}$ of R63
$= (\frac{2}{7} \times \frac{2}{9}) \times 63 = \frac{4}{63} \times 63 = R4$

(c) $\frac{4}{3}$ of $\frac{2}{5}$ of R45
$= (\frac{4}{3} \times \frac{2}{5}) \times 45 = \frac{8}{15} \times 45 = 8 \times 3 = R24$

### 12. Practical Applications
(a) John practises for $\frac{1}{2}$ of $\frac{3}{4}$ of an hour.
$= \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{3}{4} = \frac{3}{8}$ of an hour.

(b) A bag weighs $\frac{3}{8}$ kg. Find $\frac{3}{4}$ of a bag.
$= \frac{3}{4} \times \frac{3}{8} = \frac{9}{32}$ kg.

(c) Mass of $7\frac{1}{2}$ packets if one packet is $\frac{3}{4}$ kg.

## Mathematical Background

Fractions were historically developed to facilitate accurate measurement of objects smaller than standard units. For example, the Latin roots of our current measurement system reflect this:
*   **Centimetres:** Hundredths of a metre.
*   **Millimetres:** Thousandths of a metre.

### Definition of a Fraction
A **fraction** is a number representing equal parts of the same whole, collection, quantity, or unit of measurement.

### Uses of Fractions
1.  **As Measures:** Used to express lengths or quantities (e.g., a strip measured as $3 \frac{3}{5}$ units).
2.  **Parts of Whole Objects:** Used for physical items (e.g., "a quarter of an apple").
3.  **Parts of Collections:** Used for groups (e.g., "three eighths of the learners in a school").
4.  **Parts of Non-physical Quantities:** Used for abstract values (e.g., "63 hundredths of the available marks").
5.  **Explaining Decimals:** Fractions help explain decimal place value.
    *   Example: $34,56 = 30 + 4 + \frac{5}{10} + \frac{6}{100}$
    *   This represents: $3 \text{ tens} + 4 \text{ units} + 5 \text{ tenths} + 6 \text{ hundredths}$.

## 6.1 Measuring accurately with parts of a unit

### A Strange Measuring Unit
In mathematics, we use fractions to measure lengths accurately when whole units are not sufficient. To understand this, we use a hypothetical unit called a **greystick**.



![Textbook_Mathematics_Gr9_Geometry-of-straight-lines.pdf-0018-20.png](images/Textbook_Mathematics_Gr9_Geometry-of-straight-lines.pdf-0018-20.png)

It serves to help Grade 9 learners identify and apply the properties of co-interior, corresponding, and alternate angles formed by parallel lines to solve algebraic and geometric problems.



### Key Concepts
*   **Reading Fractions:** A fraction like $\frac{5}{8}$ should be read as "five eighths," not "five over eight." This ensures a conceptual understanding of the parts of a whole.
*   **The Need for Fractions:** When measuring, objects often fall between whole units. To measure accurately, we must subdivide the unit into smaller, equal parts (e.g., sixths or tenths).
*   **Quantities in Fractions:** When a whole is divided into equal parts, there are three related quantities:
    1.  The size of the whole.
    2.  The number of equal parts.
    3.  The size of each individual part.

### Developmental Phases of Fractions
1.  **Awareness:** Understanding that the same part of a whole or collection can be described using different fractions.
2.  **Specification:** The ability to identify and name equivalent fractions.
3.  **Production:** The ability to generate equivalent fractions using a mathematical formula.

### Mathematical Relationships
*   **Sharing Situations:** Finding the size of each part when the size of the whole and the number of parts are known.
*   **Grouping Situations:** Finding the number of equal parts when the size of the whole and the size of each part are known.

### Understanding Fractions: Concepts and Misconceptions

#### Common Misconception
Learners often mistakenly view a fraction like $\frac{2}{5}$ as two separate whole numbers stacked on top of each other. 
*   **Correction:** In the term "two fifths," the number $2$ indicates a count of objects, but the objects themselves are no longer "wholes." They are "fifths." The fraction represents a specific type of object being counted.
*   **Pedagogical Note:** It is essential for learners to develop the concept of fractions by using verbal descriptions (e.g., "two fifths") before introducing symbolic notation.

# Lesson: Understanding Fraction Notation

## 1. Core Concepts

### The Anatomy of a Fraction
A fraction represents a part of a whole. It consists of two main components:

*   **Numerator:** The number of parts being referred to. It "enumerates" (counts) the parts.
*   **Denominator:** The name/size of the parts. It "denominates" (names) the parts based on how many equal pieces the whole is divided into.

**Example:** In the fraction $\frac{5}{6}$:
*   The numerator is $5$.
*   The denominator is $6$.
*   We read this as "five sixths."

### Equivalent Fractions
Equivalent fractions represent the same value even though they have different numerators and denominators. You can visualize these using a "fraction wall."

**Examples:**
*   $\frac{2}{3} = \frac{4}{6}$
*   $\frac{6}{9} = \frac{2}{3}$

---

## 2. Addition of Fractions with Same Denominators
When denominators are the same, you simply add the numerators while keeping the denominator constant.

**Formula:**
$$\frac{a}{c} + \frac{b}{c} = \frac{a + b}{c}$$

**Worked Examples:**

| Problem | Calculation | Result |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Five twelfths plus three twelfths | $\frac{5}{12} + \frac{3}{12}$ | $\frac{8}{12}$ (eight twelfths) |
| Five twelfths plus one quarter | $\frac{5}{12} + \frac{3}{12}$ | $\frac{8}{12}$ (eight twelfths) |
| Five twelfths plus three quarters | $\frac{5}{12} + \frac{9}{12}$ | $\frac{14}{12}$ (fourteen twelfths) |

*Note: To add fractions with different denominators (like quarters and twelfths), first convert them to equivalent fractions with the same denominator.*

When adding fractions with the same denominator, do not add the denominators together.

# Fractions: Operations and Percentages

## 1. Operations with Mixed Numbers
To add or subtract mixed numbers, ensure the fractions have a common denominator. If the fractional part of the first number is smaller than the second, you may need to "borrow" from the whole number.

### Worked Examples
*   **Addition:** $3\frac{3}{10} + 1\frac{4}{5}$
    *   Find a common denominator: $1\frac{4}{5} = 1\frac{8}{10}$
    *   Add whole numbers and fractions: $(3+1) + (\frac{3}{10} + \frac{8}{10}) = 4 + \frac{11}{10} = 4 + 1\frac{1}{10} = 5\frac{1}{10}$
*   **Subtraction:** $5\frac{5}{8} - 3\frac{7}{8}$
    *   Since $\frac{5}{8} < \frac{7}{8}$, borrow from the 5: $4\frac{13}{8} - 3\frac{7}{8} = 1\frac{6}{8} = 1\frac{3}{4}$

# 6.6 Fraction of a Fraction

## Concept: Form Parts of Parts
To calculate a fraction of a whole, collection, quantity, or measurement, remember that the word **"of"** acts as a mathematical operation meaning **multiply**.

### Key Rules for Calculation
When calculating a fraction of an amount:
1. To find $\frac{1}{5}$ of R60, divide R60 by 5:
   $$\frac{1}{5} \times 60 = 60 \div 5 = 12$$
2. To find $\frac{2}{5}$ of R60, divide by 5 and multiply the result by 2:
   $$\frac{2}{5} \times 60 = (60 \div 5) \times 2 = 12 \times 2 = 24$$
3. To find $\frac{3}{5}$ of R60, divide by 5 and multiply the result by 3:
   $$\frac{3}{5} \times 60 = (60 \div 5) \times 3 = 12 \times 3 = 36$$

### Fractions: Multiplication and Equivalent Fractions

#### Key Concept: Calculating Fractions of Fractions
To calculate a fraction of a fraction (e.g., $\frac{7}{12}$ of $\frac{3}{5}$), it is often easier to find an equivalent fraction for the second part so that the numerator is divisible by the denominator of the first part.

**Example:** Calculate $\frac{7}{12}$ of $\frac{3}{5}$.
1. Find an equivalent fraction for $\frac{3}{5}$ that can be divided by $12$:
   $$\frac{3}{5} \times \frac{4}{4} = \frac{12}{20}$$
2. We know that one-twelfth ($\frac{1}{12}$) of $\frac{12}{20}$ is $\frac{1}{20}$.
3. Therefore, seven-twelfths ($\frac{7}{12}$) of $\frac{12}{20}$ is:
   $$7 \times \frac{1}{20} = \frac{7}{20}$$
4. Thus, $\frac{7}{12}$ of $\frac{3}{5} = \frac{7}{20}$.

## 2. Ordering and Comparing Fractions

### Core Concept: The Common Denominator
Fractions can only be compared accurately when their denominators are the same. To achieve this, find the **Least Common Multiple (LCM)** of the denominators.

*   **To compare halves and quarters:** Use $4$ (LCM of $2$ and $4$).
*   **To compare halves, quarters, and eighths:** Use $8$ (LCM of $2, 4,$ and $8$).
*   **To compare sixteenths, eighths, twenty-fourths, and twelfths:** Use $48$ (LCM of $16, 8, 24,$ and $12$).

### Rules for Ordering
Once denominators are identical:
1.  **Ascending Order:** Arrange fractions from smallest to largest based on the size of their numerators.
2.  **Descending Order:** Arrange fractions from largest to smallest based on the size of their numerators.

### Worked Examples

#### Ordering from Smallest to Biggest
**Set:** $\frac{7}{16}, \frac{3}{8}, \frac{11}{24}, \frac{5}{12}, \frac{23}{48}$
1.  Convert to common denominator ($48$):
    $$\frac{21}{48}, \frac{18}{48}, \frac{22}{48}, \frac{20}{48}, \frac{23}{48}$$
2.  Order numerators: $18, 20, 21, 22, 23$
3.  **Result:** $\frac{3}{8}, \frac{5}{12}, \frac{7}{16}, \frac{11}{24}, \frac{23}{48}$

#### Ordering from Biggest to Smallest
**Set:** $\frac{41}{60}, \frac{19}{30}, \frac{42}{60}, \frac{44}{60}, \frac{51}{60}$
1.  Convert to common denominator ($60$):
    $$\frac{41}{60}, \frac{38}{60}, \frac{42}{60}, \frac{44}{60}, \frac{51}{60}$$
2.  Order numerators (descending): $51, 44, 42, 41, 38$
3.  **Result:** $\frac{51}{60}, \frac{44}{60}, \frac{42}{60}, \frac{41}{60}, \frac{19}{30}$

## 3. Mixed Operations with Fractions
Convert to common denominators before adding or subtracting.

(a) $\frac{3}{10} + \frac{7}{30} = \frac{9}{30} + \frac{7}{30} = \frac{16}{30} = \frac{8}{15}$
(b) $\frac{2}{5} + \frac{7}{12} = \frac{24}{60} + \frac{35}{60} = \frac{59}{60}$
(c) $\frac{1}{100} + \frac{7}{10} = \frac{1}{100} + \frac{70}{100} = \frac{71}{100}$
(d) $\frac{3}{5} - \frac{3}{8} = \frac{24}{40} - \frac{15}{40} = \frac{9}{40}$
(e) $2\frac{3}{10} + 5\frac{9}{10} = 7\frac{12}{10} = 8\frac{2}{10} = 8\frac{1}{5}$

## 7 & 8. Multiplication of Fractions

**Rule:** Multiply numerators together and denominators together. Simplify where possible.

(7a) $\frac{1}{2} \times 9 = \frac{9}{2} = 4\frac{1}{2}$
(7b) $\frac{3}{5} \times \frac{10}{27} = \frac{30}{135} = \frac{2}{9}$
(7c) $\frac{2}{3} \times 15 = \frac{30}{3} = 10$
(7d) $\frac{2}{3} \times \frac{3}{4} = \frac{6}{12} = \frac{1}{2}$

(8a) $2\frac{2}{3} \times 2\frac{2}{3} = \frac{8}{3} \times \frac{8}{3} = \frac{64}{9} = 7\frac{1}{9}$
(8b) $8\frac{2}{5} \times 3\frac{1}{3} = \frac{42}{5} \times \frac{10}{3} = \frac{420}{15} = 28$
(8c) $(\frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{2}) \times \frac{6}{7} = (\frac{2}{6} + \frac{3}{6}) \times \frac{6}{7} = \frac{5}{6} \times \frac{6}{7} = \frac{30}{42} = \frac{5}{7}$
(8d) $\frac{2}{3} \times \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{3}{4} = \frac{6}{24} = \frac{1}{4}$
(8e) $\frac{5}{6} + \frac{2}{3} \times \frac{1}{5} = \frac{5}{6} + \frac{2}{15} = \frac{25}{30} + \frac{4}{30} = \frac{29}{30}$
(8f) $\frac{3}{4} - \frac{2}{5} \times \frac{5}{6} = \frac{3}{4} - \frac{10}{30} = \frac{3}{4} - \frac{1}{3} = \frac{9}{12} - \frac{4}{12} = \frac{5}{12}$
