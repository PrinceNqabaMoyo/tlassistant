## Mathematical background

* **Line segments, lines and rays** can be thought of as a collection of points that are so close together that there are no spaces in between.
* We make a pencil (or pen) mark to indicate the position of a **point**. We use letters like $A, B, P, Q$ (all upper-case) to name a point.
* A **line segment** has a beginning and an end. When we name a line segment, we need only name the two endpoints. We can measure the length of a line segment.
* A **line** extends indefinitely in both directions and has no beginning or end. We cannot measure the length of a line. We cannot draw a line on paper, but we add arrows to a line segment to represent a line. We name a line by choosing any two points on the line and labelling them with chosen letters, for example line $AB$, or line $BA$, etc. Sometimes a line can be named by a single letter (lower case), for example $p$.
* A line that has a definite starting point but no definite endpoint is called a **ray** and is named by the starting point first and any other point on the ray, for example $AB$. We cannot measure the length of a ray.
* Learners should understand the difference between lines and line segments. In the work that learners will encounter in the future, a line segment or a ray is often simply called a line.

#### Teaching guidelines
* Learners will measure the line segments of the quadrilateral in question 1 more accurately if they measure in millimetres.
* Talk about the meaning of the word **segment** as part of something. A line segment is a set of points with a definite starting and end point.

* Learners often confuse lines with line segments and lines with rays. Let learners draw points on a sheet of paper and join them two by two with a ruler to create line segments.

* We usually mention the endpoints of the line segments in alphabetical order, for example $AB$ and not $BA$, although $BA$ is not incorrect.

# LINES AND RAYS

## Key Concepts & Definitions

* **Line Segment:** A line segment has a definite starting point and a definite endpoint. Because of this, we can draw and measure line segments.
* **Line:** We can think of lines as having no ends, although we cannot draw them completely. A line goes on indefinitely in both directions. We use arrows at both ends of a drawing to show that it goes on indefinitely. A line cannot be measured.
* **Ray (Half-line):** A line that has a definite starting point but goes on indefinitely at the other end. We can draw the starting point and part of a ray, using an arrow to indicate that it goes on at the one end.
* **Orientation:** A line, line segment, or ray can be horizontal, vertical, or slanted.

---

## Teaching Guidelines

* **Extending Line Segments:** Talk about extending a line segment indefinitely in both directions, which we show by an arrowhead at each end. Explain that it is impossible to show a line completely for obvious reasons: it has no beginning and no end. This is why we cannot measure the length of a line. Therefore, we show only a part of a line in a drawing, while the arrows indicate that it is a line.
* **Modeling Rays:** Let learners think of a ray of light that starts at a source (for example, the sun) and travels on indefinitely in one direction. Explain that we can represent a ray on paper as beginning at a point and continuing indefinitely in a direction. A ray has a point at the beginning and an arrowhead on the other end.
* **Naming Rays:** Circulate and make sure that all learners draw the rays correctly with the first letter as the starting point and the other letter at the arrowhead (for example, ray $EF$ with $E$ at the start, or ray $FT$ with $F$ at the starting point).
* **Intersecting Lines:** Make sure that learners understand that lines could meet somewhere if they are extended (i.e., if they are not parallel).

---

## Common Misconceptions

Learners often confuse lines and rays. You can use a physical explanation to help them remember:
* **Line Segment:** Represent a point by a closed fist; make a fist with each hand and extend your arms. The distance between the two fixed points (fists) represents a line segment.
* **Line:** Now open one fist and point your fingers; this represents a ray. With both hands opened and fingers pointed in opposite directions, we can represent the idea of a line.
* **Ray Direction:** Explain that a ray can point in any direction, but we always mention the starting point of the line first.

## Geometry of Straight Lines

### Key Concepts & Definitions

* **Line Segments:** Line segments have both starting points and endpoints. Line segments that do not cross each other (intersect) will not meet.
* **Rays:** A ray has a starting point and extends infinitely in one direction.
