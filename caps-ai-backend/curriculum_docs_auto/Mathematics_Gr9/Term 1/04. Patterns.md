## 2.1 Introduction and key concepts (EMG35)

Graphs and diagrams are used to display information about the relationship between two variables (e.g., the price of petrol over time). These relationships often follow specific patterns and rules.

**In this chapter, you will learn to:**
* Interpret graphs found in media (newspapers and magazines).
* Identify relationships between quantities that follow specific patterns.
* Distinguish between linear patterns (which form straight lines) and inverse proportions (which form curved graphs).
* Determine the mathematical rules or formulae for patterns presented in tables and graphs.

### Continuous and Discrete Graphs

In mathematics, the nature of the data determines how a graph should be drawn:

*   **Discrete Values:** These are values that can only be whole numbers (e.g., counting people). When drawing graphs for discrete values, points are connected by **dotted lines** because the values between the whole numbers do not exist.
*   **Continuous Values:** These are values that can include decimal fractions (e.g., measurements like length or time). When drawing graphs for continuous values, points are connected by **solid lines** to show that all values between the points are included.

# 2.2. Making Sense of Graphs That Tell a Story

## Key Concepts: Discrete vs. Continuous Data

Graphs are used to represent relationships between variables. Depending on the nature of the data, we distinguish between **discrete** and **continuous** variables.

### 1. Discrete Variables
Discrete data consists of values that can only be counted as whole numbers. You cannot have a fraction of these items.

*   **Example:** The number of people on a bus. You cannot have $2.5$ people on a bus; therefore, the data points are distinct and separate.
*   **Graph Representation:** Usually shown as a series of individual points (often connected by lines to show a trend, but the spaces between the points do not represent valid data).



![Textbook_Mathematics_Gr9_Collect-organise-and-summarise-data.pdf-0005-02.png](images/Textbook_Mathematics_Gr9_Collect-organise-and-summarise-data.pdf-0005-02.png)
> **[Diagram Context - Textbook_Mathematics_Gr9_Collect-organise-and-summarise-data.pdf-0005-02.png]**
> This graphic displays two dot plots (or frequency histograms) representing numerical data sets, with each cell containing a specific integer value. By organizing these values into columns, the diagram allows students to visually compare the distribution, frequency, and central tendency of two distinct data sets.



### 2. Continuous Variables
Continuous data consists of values that can be measured. These values can include fractions or decimals, and they exist along a scale without gaps.

*   **Example:** Time and distance. A bus travels over a period of time, and at any specific moment (e.g., $1.5$ minutes), there is a corresponding distance covered.
*   **Graph Representation:** Shown as a solid, unbroken line. This indicates that every point along the line represents a valid measurement.



![Textbook_Mathematics_Gr9_Collect-organise-and-summarise-data.pdf-0005-11.png](images/Textbook_Mathematics_Gr9_Collect-organise-and-summarise-data.pdf-0005-11.png)

## Summary of Differences

| Feature | Discrete Data | Continuous Data |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nature** | Counted (Whole numbers) | Measured (Any value) |
| **Graph Style** | Individual points | Solid, unbroken line |
| **Example** | Number of passengers | Distance vs. Time |

## Key Concepts for Interpreting Graphs

*   **Constant Distance (Resting):** On a distance-time graph, a horizontal line (gradient = $0$) indicates that no distance is being covered, meaning the subjects are stationary or resting.
*   **Steepness (Rate of Change):** The steepness of the line represents the rate of change.

*   **Vertical Jumps:** On a quantity-time graph (like the petrol tank), a sudden vertical line indicates an instantaneous change in the quantity, such as refilling the tank.
*   **Downward Slopes:** A downward slope indicates a decrease in the quantity over time (consumption).

### Mathematical Concepts: Continuous vs. Discrete Data

*   **Continuous Data:** Data that can take any value within a range. It is represented by a solid line on a graph because the values change smoothly over time (e.g., temperature).

### Calculations Example (Reference for 3d)
To calculate the difference between two values:
$$\text{Difference} = \text{Maximum Value} - \text{Minimum Value}$$

If the maximum temperature is $T_{max}$ and the minimum is $T_{min}$, the calculation is:
$$\Delta T = T_{max} - T_{min}$$

### Dependent and Independent Variables

#### Definition: Variable
A **variable** is the quantity you are measuring or calculating.

#### Key Concepts

*   **Independent Variable:** A variable that stands alone and is not changed by the other variables you are trying to measure. 
    *   *Example:* A person's age is an independent variable because factors like what they eat or how much television they watch do not change their age.
*   **Dependent Variable:** A variable that depends on other factors. Its value changes based on the influence of other variables.
    *   *Example:* A test score is a dependent variable because it changes based on factors like how much you studied, how much sleep you had, or how hungry you were.

#### How to Identify Variables
To determine which variable is dependent and which is independent, construct a sentence that makes logical sense. 

*   *Example:* "Time causes a change in distance travelled." 
    *   Since it is not possible for distance travelled to cause a change in time, **time** is the independent variable and **distance** is the dependent variable.

#### Plotting Variables on a Graph
When representing variables on a Cartesian plane:
*   The **independent variable** is placed on the **horizontal axis** ($x$-axis).
*   The **dependent variable** is placed on the **vertical axis** ($y$-axis).

# Touching the Axes

## Key Concepts
When interpreting graphs, understanding where the line intersects the axes provides critical information about the relationship between variables.

*   **Vertical Axis ($y$-axis) Intercept:** If a graph touches the vertical axis, it indicates that the quantity on the horizontal axis is $0$.
*   **Horizontal Axis ($x$-axis) Intercept:** If a graph touches the horizontal axis, it indicates that the quantity on the vertical axis is $0$.

> **NOTE:** 
> *   The **horizontal axis** is also known as the $x$-axis. We plot the **independent variable** on this axis.
> *   The **vertical axis** is also known as the $y$-axis. We plot the **dependent variable** on this axis.

### Plotting points on a grid

To plot points, we use an **ordered pair**. An ordered pair provides the exact position of a point on a Cartesian plane (grid).

*   **Definition:** An ordered pair is written as $(x; y)$.
    *   The first number is the **horizontal coordinate** ($x$-axis).
    *   The second number is the **vertical coordinate** ($y$-axis).

#### Method: How to plot the point $(5; 4)$

1.  **Start at the origin:** Begin at the point $(0; 0)$.
2.  **Horizontal movement:** Move along the horizontal $x$-axis until you reach $5$.
3.  **Vertical movement:** Move upwards until you are in line with $4$ on the vertical $y$-axis.
4.  **Mark the point:** Draw a dot where the grid lines cross at this position.

### 2.4 Inverse Proportion Patterns, Relationships and Graphs

#### Concept: Inverse Proportion
An inverse proportion relationship exists when two quantities have an opposite effect on each other:
*   As one quantity **decreases**, the other **increases**.
*   As one quantity **increases**, the other **decreases**.

### Writing a General Formula

To describe the value of any term in a sequence, we use the position of the term, denoted by $n$. Since $n$ can represent different positions, it is called a **variable**.

#### Example: Deriving a Formula
If a sequence follows the general formula $(10n) - 5$:
*   To find the 100th term, substitute $n = 100$:
    $$(10 \times 100) - 5 = 1000 - 5 = 995$$

#### Simplifying Expressions
Sometimes a pattern can be written in different ways. For example:
$$5 + [(n - 1) \times 10]$$

To simplify this expression:
1.  Distribute the 10: $5 + 10n - 10$
2.  Combine like terms: $10n - 5$

*   $c$ is the $y$-intercept.

$$y = \frac{k}{x} \quad \text{or} \quad xy = k$$
Where $k$ is the constant of proportionality.

# 2. Patterns, Relationships and Representations

## 2.2 Making sense of graphs that tell a story

Graphs are visual representations of the relationship between two quantities. Learning to interpret these graphs allows us to understand information presented in the media and real-world contexts.

### Key Concepts in Graph Interpretation

To understand how a graph "tells a story," focus on the following features:

*   **Increasing:** The graph goes upwards (the value of the dependent variable increases as the independent variable increases).
*   **Decreasing:** The graph goes downwards (the value of the dependent variable decreases as the independent variable increases).
*   **Constant:** The graph remains flat (horizontal), indicating no change in the dependent variable over time.
*   **Gradients:** The steepness of the line. A steeper line indicates a faster rate of change.
*   **Maximum and Minimum points:** The highest and lowest points reached on the graph.

### Continuous vs. Discrete Graphs

*   **Continuous Graphs:** Represent data that can take any value within a range. These are usually drawn with solid lines.
*   **Discrete Graphs:** Represent data that consists of separate, distinct points. Dotted lines are sometimes used in discrete graphs to help visualize the trend or the steepness between points, rather than implying a continuous connection.

### Mathematical Relationships: Independent and Dependent Variables

#### Key Concepts
*   **Independent Variable:** The variable that is changed or controlled in a scientific experiment or mathematical relationship to test the effects on the dependent variable. In this context, it is the number of games hired.
*   **Dependent Variable:** The variable being tested and measured. It "depends" on the independent variable. In this context, it is the total cost of hiring the games.
*   **Slope:** In a linear relationship, the slope represents the rate of change. A positive slope indicates that as the independent variable increases, the dependent variable also increases.
