### Algebraic Expressions: Concepts and Definitions

An **algebraic expression** is a mathematical phrase that uses numbers, variables, and operation symbols to represent a computational instruction.

*   **Variable:** A letter symbol (such as $x$) used to represent an input number that can change or vary.
*   **Constant:** A fixed number in an expression that does not change (e.g., in $(x + 4) \times 5$, the numbers $4$ and $5$ are constants).
*   **Unknown Value:** When an algebraic expression is set equal to a specific result (forming a number sentence), the variable represents a specific unknown value that must be solved.

## Mathematical Background

### 1. Algebraic Expressions
An **algebraic expression** is a symbolic description of a set of calculations that can be performed on different values of a variable.

*   **Example:** The expression $3x + 5$ means "multiply the value of $x$ by three and add five to the answer."

**Rules for Calculation:**
To find the value of an expression for particular values of a variable, follow the standard order of operations:
1. Calculations in brackets.
2. Multiplication and division (in the order they appear).
3. Addition and subtraction (in the order they appear).

### 2. Relationships and Patterns
Relationships can be represented in words, tables, or algebraic expressions.

**Example Table:**
| Input | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Output | 7 | 11 | 15 | 19 | 23 | 27 |

*   **In words:** "Three more than a number multiplied by 4."
*   **Algebraically:** $3 + 4 \times x$ or $4 \times x + 3$.

### 3. Equations
An **equation** is a statement about an unknown number. It states that certain calculations performed on an unknown result in a specific value.

*   **Example:** The equation $2x + 3 = 15$ states that "when a certain unknown number is multiplied by two and three is added to the result, the answer is fifteen."
*   **Solving an equation:** This means finding the value of the unknown number.

### 4. Substitution and Equivalence
*   **Substitution:** Used to find out when expressions are equal in value.
*   **Equivalent expressions:** Algebraic expressions that have the same numerical value for the same values of $x$, even if they look different.

### Algebraic Expressions: Sequences and Flow Diagrams

#### 1. Understanding Sequences
A sequence is a list of numbers that follow a specific pattern. For example, the sequence of odd numbers is: $1; 3; 5; 7; 9; \dots$

We can derive a rule for this sequence based on the position of the number:
*   First odd number: $2 \times 1 - 1 = 1$
*   Second odd number: $2 \times 2 - 1 = 3$
*   Third odd number: $2 \times 3 - 1 = 5$
*   $n^{th}$ odd number: $2 \times n - 1$

**Key Definitions:**
*   **Constants:** Numbers that remain the same in an expression (e.g., $2$ and $-1$ in the rule $2n - 1$).
*   **Variables:** Symbols (usually letters like $n, x, y, z$) that represent changing values or positions in a sequence.

### Algebraic Expressions and Computational Instructions

#### Key Concepts
*   **Algebraic Expression:** A mathematical phrase that can contain numbers, variables, and operators (e.g., $(x + 4) \times 5$).
*   **Variable:** A letter symbol (like $x$) that can be replaced by many different input numbers. It represents a **variable quantity**.
*   **Unknown:** A specific value that makes a number sentence true.
*   **Constant:** A fixed number in an expression that does not change (e.g., in $(x + 4) \times 5$, the numbers $4$ and $5$ are constants).
*   **Computational Instruction:** A sequence of operations performed on an input number.

#### Worked Example: Perimeter and Area of Rectangles
Chris uses the following formulas for rectangles:
*   Perimeter: $P = 2 \times l + 2 \times b$
*   Area: $A = l \times b$

**Completed Table:**

| Rectangle | $1$ | $2$ | $3$ | $4$ |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Length ($l$) | $24$ | $6$ | $8$ | $12$ |
| Breadth ($b$) | $1$ | $4$ | $3$ | $2$ |
| Perimeter ($P = 2 \times l + 2 \times b$) | $50$ | $20$ | $22$ | $28$ |
| Area ($A = l \times b$) | $24$ | $24$ | $24$ | $24$ |

**Analysis of the Formula:**
1.  **Rita's Formula:** Rita calculates the perimeter by adding length and breadth first, then multiplying by $2$. Her formula is: $P = 2 \times (l + b)$.
2.  **Testing the Formula:** For Rectangle $1$: $2 \times (24 + 1) = 2 \times 25 = 50$. This produces the same result as Chris's formula.
3.  **Constants:** The number $2$ is a constant because it represents the two lengths and two breadths of a rectangle.
4.  **Variables:** $P$, $l$, and $b$ are variables because they have changing values depending on the specific rectangle.
5.  **Constant Area:** The area of all these rectangles is $24$, which remains constant in this specific set.

### Teaching Guidelines: Constants and Variables

*   **Constants:** These are values in a formula or expression that do not change.
*   **Variables:** These are quantities that do change their value and are usually represented by letter symbols (e.g., $x, y, w$).

---

### Worked Example 1: Calculating Age
**Problem:** Sindi calculates her father's age using the formula $F = x + 37$, where $x$ is Sindi's age. How old was her father when Sindi was 43 years old?

**Solution:**
Substitute Sindi's age ($x = 43$) into the formula:
$$F = 43 + 37$$
$$\therefore F = 80$$
Sindi's father was 80 years old.

### Worked Example 2: Savings Formula
**Problem:** Jacob saves R5 per week after starting with R45. The formula for his total savings ($y$) after $w$ weeks is $y = 45 + 5 \times w$.

**(a) Savings Table:**

| Number of weeks ($w$) | How to calculate $45 + 5 \times w$ | Amount saved ($y$) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 0 | $45 + 5 \times 0 = 45 + 0$ | 45 |
| 1 | $45 + 5 \times 1 = 45 + 5$ | 50 |
| 2 | $45 + 5 \times 2 = 45 + 10$ | 55 |
| 4 | $45 + 5 \times 4 = 45 + 20$ | 65 |
| 5 | $45 + 5 \times 5 = 45 + 25$ | 70 |

**(b) Goal Analysis:**
The cell phone costs R90. Will Jacob have enough money by the eighth week?
Calculation: $45 + 5 \times 8 = 45 + 40 = 85$.
**Answer:** No, he will only have saved R85 by the eighth week.

**(c) Constants vs. Variables:**

| Question | Formula: $y = 45 + 5 \times w$ | Explanation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Which are constants? | 45 and 5 | These numbers remain the same all the time. |
| Which are variables? | $y$ and $w$ | The number of weeks and the total savings change each week. |
