# CHAPTER 6: CELLULAR RESPIRATION

## Introduction

By now we know that all living things require energy to live. In this chapter, we will study the process by which this energy is transformed so that organisms can make use of it.

## Key Terminology

| Term | Definition |
| :--- | :--- |
| **metabolism** | Chemical processes in organisms which are controlled by enzymes |
| **catabolic** | A metabolic process in which complex molecules are broken down into simple ones to release energy |
| **anabolic** | The synthesis of more complex substances from simple molecules |

## Definition of Cellular Respiration

Cellular respiration is the chemical process where glucose is broken down gradually, in the presence of oxygen (aerobic respiration) or in the absence of oxygen (anaerobic respiration), to release energy.

## Importance of Energy

Energy is used by organisms in various ways. Some of the main ways in which energy is used include the following:
* growth
* cell division
* movement
* transport of substances
* active transport

## Aerobic respiration (needs oxygen)

Aerobic respiration takes place:
* in the presence of oxygen;
* inside the cytoplasm and mitochondria of cells.

Figure 1 shows the internal structure of a mitochondrion.

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

Figure 1: The internal structure of a mitochondrion

The requirements for the process are oxygen and glucose and the by-products released are carbon dioxide and water as well as ATP energy. This can be shown in the following word equation:

$$\text{Glucose} + \text{Oxygen} \longrightarrow \text{Carbon Dioxide} + \text{Water} + \text{ATP}$$

## The stages of aerobic respiration

Aerobic respiration takes place in three stages:
1. Glycolysis – takes place in the cytoplasm
2. Krebs Cycle – takes place inside the mitochondrion

---

3. Oxidative Phosphorylation – takes place inside the mitochondrion

## Glycolysis

* takes place outside the mitochondrion, in the cytoplasm of the cell
* no oxygen is required during this stage
* glucose is broken down into smaller molecules, releasing a small amount of energy that is stored in energy-rich ATP molecules
* releases high energy hydrogen ions ($H^+$) that are used in the third stage of cellular respiration (oxidative phosphorylation)

> **Note:** No biochemical detail is required in the exam.

## Krebs Cycle

* can only take place if oxygen is present
* occurs inside of the mitochondrion
* releases carbon dioxide and high energy hydrogen ions ($H^+$)
* transports hydrogen atoms to the third stage (oxidative phosphorylation) via hydrogen carrier enzymes

## Oxidative Phosphorylation

* takes place inside of the mitochondrion and requires oxygen
* passes high energy hydrogen atoms from one hydrogen carrier enzyme to the next, releasing energy in the process
* uses released energy to combine a phosphate molecule to an $ADP$ (Adenosine Di-phosphate) molecule to form $ATP$ – called **phosphorylation**
* is represented in the formula: $\text{ADP} + \text{P} \longrightarrow \text{ATP}$
* oxygen acts as a final hydrogen acceptor binding with the hydrogen forming water which is released as a waste product of cellular respiration

### Alcoholic Fermentation
Breaking down of glucose in the absence of oxygen, to give rise to the production of alcohol in plant cells.

### Lactic Acid Fermentation
Breaking down of glucose in the absence of oxygen to form lactic acid in animal cells.

### Lactic Acid
Acid formed in muscle cells, during anaerobic respiration; leads to muscle exhaustion / cramping.

---

### Key Characteristics of Anaerobic Respiration
* Without oxygen being present.
* It occurs for short periods of time, mainly during physical exercise.
* Occurs differently in plants and animals.
* Produces less ATP than aerobic respiration.

---

### Anaerobic Respiration in Animals
* Is known as lactic acid fermentation.
* Occurs in muscles during intense exercise.
* Enables glycolysis in the cytoplasm of animal cells.
* Results in the accumulation of lactic acid causing the muscles to become tired and painful.
* Produces only a small amount of ATP.

> **Note:** The lactic acid can be converted back to pyruvic acid when the muscles receive oxygen.

---

### Anaerobic Respiration in Plants
* Is known as alcoholic fermentation.
* Enables glycolysis in the cytoplasm of plant cells.
* Results in the accumulation of pyruvic acid.
* Breaks down pyruvic acid forming ethanol (alcohol) and releasing carbon dioxide in the process.
* Can be summarised as:

$$\text{glucose} \longrightarrow \text{ATP} + \text{pyruvic acid} \longrightarrow \text{ethanol} + \text{carbon dioxide}$$

| :--- | :--- |

# Revision Notes: Cellular Respiration Investigations

## Core Concepts

There are several investigations used to demonstrate the requirements and products of cellular respiration.

### Testing for Gases

1. **To test for oxygen:**
   * **Test:** A glowing splint test.
   * **Procedure:** A wooden splint or stick is lit and then extinguished while still glowing.
   * **Result:** In the presence of oxygen, the glowing end of the splint will re-ignite or glow brighter.

2. **To test for carbon dioxide:**
   * **Test:** Lime water test.
   * **Result:** A clear lime water solution will turn milky (cloudy) in the presence of carbon dioxide.

# Revision Notes: Investigating Carbon Dioxide Production During Aerobic Respiration

## Core Concept
Aerobic respiration is a metabolic process in which living organisms use oxygen to break down organic compounds (like glucose), releasing energy, water, and carbon dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$). 

This experiment demonstrates that **living organisms (such as a snail) produce carbon dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$) during aerobic respiration**.

---

## Experimental Setup (Figure 3)

The experimental apparatus is set up in a continuous train of interconnected chambers and test tubes (labeled A, B, C, and D) to control the gas entering and leaving the organism's chamber:

* **Incoming Air & Soda Lime:** Incoming air passes through soda lime and sodium hydroxide solution (in container A) to scrub and remove any atmospheric carbon dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$) before it reaches the living organism.
* **Test Tube B (Control Check):** Contains lime water. The lime water remains clear, confirming that the soda lime and sodium hydroxide successfully removed all $\text{CO}_2$ from the incoming air.
* **Chamber C (Living Organism):** Contains a small organism (a snail) undergoing aerobic respiration.
* **Test Tube D (Detection):** Contains lime water. As air passes out of chamber C and bubbles through test tube D, any $\text{CO}_2$ produced by the snail reacts with the lime water.

---

## Method

1. Use a small living organism (e.g., a snail).
2. Sterilize the equipment to ensure no micro-organisms influence the experimental results.
3. Place the snail into a large sealed jar (Chamber C).
4. Set up the apparatus as shown in Figure 3, ensuring all test tubes and connections are tightly sealed to prevent gas leaks.
5. Leave the apparatus set up, examine the test tubes the following day, and record the results.

---

## Results

| Location / Test Tube | Observation | Explanation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Test Tube B** | The lime water remains **clear**. | Carbon dioxide from the incoming air was completely removed by the soda lime and sodium hydroxide in container A. |
| **Test Tube D** | The lime water turns **milky** (cloudy). | Carbon dioxide was produced by the snail during aerobic respiration in chamber C and passed into test tube D. |

---

## Conclusion

Carbon dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$) is produced as a waste product during aerobic respiration by living organisms (such as the snail).

## Introduction

It is important to distinguish between breathing, gaseous exchange and cellular respiration.

* **Breathing** is a mechanical process of taking oxygen into the lungs. Why do we need to breathe? All organisms, from simple, unicellular to more advanced multicellular organisms need oxygen ($\text{O}_2$) to sustain cellular functions.

# Revision Notes: Cellular Respiration and Gaseous Exchange

## Core Definitions

* **Cellular respiration:** A chemical process that takes place within cells in order to release energy. Both plants and animals need cellular energy for survival.
* **Gaseous exchange:** A physical process that involves the exchange of gases between the air and the blood in the lungs. Organisms are adapted to ensure that this exchange process is optimised. Mammals have a mechanism of breathing that allows gases to enter their bodies, facilitating both gaseous exchange and cellular respiration.

---

## Key Considerations in Gaseous Exchange

When studying aspects of gaseous exchange, keep the following in mind:

1. **Structure to function:** How the organism is designed to carry out gaseous exchange.
2. **Regulation and control:** The control of breathing in humans.
3. **Gas management:** How the amounts of gases are kept at manageable levels in the blood.
4. **Disease and life choices:** How certain diseases and lifestyle choices can negatively impact effective gaseous exchange, impairing breathing and affecting overall health.

---

## Key Concepts Summary

* **Energy production:** All living organisms need cellular respiration to obtain energy for cellular functions.
* **Diffusion of gases:** Important gases diffuse across surfaces in a process called gaseous exchange.
* **Breathing mechanism:** Air moves in and out of respiratory organs through the mechanical process of breathing.
* **Surface dependency:** Effective gaseous exchange depends on the surface across which gases diffuse, as well as the external environment where the gases are found.
* **Habitat adaptations:** Plants and animals have gas exchange systems designed to suit their specific habitats (whether aquatic or terrestrial).
* **Neural regulation:** Breathing in humans is regulated by a respiratory center in the brain to ensure the correct balance of gases in the blood.
* **Tuberculosis (TB):** A treatable bacterial disease that is highly prevalent in South Africa.
* **Infections:** Bronchitis and pneumonia are bacterial infections that impact respiratory function.
* **Disorders:** Asthma, hay fever, and lung cancer are disorders of the respiratory system that are on the increase globally due to rising pollution levels.
* **Harmful substances:** Smoke from both cigarettes and marijuana is harmful to lung health.
