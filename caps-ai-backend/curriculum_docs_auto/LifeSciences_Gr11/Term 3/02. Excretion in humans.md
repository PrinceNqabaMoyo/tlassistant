# CHAPTER 8: EXCRETION IN HUMANS

## Introduction

An accumulation of waste is dangerous to the cells, tissues, organs, systems and the body as a whole. The human body is designed to effectively remove waste. 

This chapter will briefly look at excretion in various organs, the substances excreted by each and the origins of these substances. There will be a detailed focus on the human urinary system and the structure and functioning of the human kidneys. It is these organs that filter the blood, regulate water and salt levels and play an important role in the control of blood pH levels. 

Kidney functioning can be weakened by diseases, lifestyle choices and accidental injuries. Renal or kidney failure can be effectively treated with dialysis or kidney transplants which are successfully carried out in many hospitals in South Africa.

* A bladder

* Osmoregulation – regulation of levels of $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ in body fluids
* Excretion – removal of nitrogenous waste e.g. urea
* Regulation of pH of body fluids
* Regulation of salt concentration of body fluids

## The Structure of the Kidney

* The kidneys are bean-shaped structures (Figure 2) that are found half-way down the back just under the ribcage.
* They weigh between 115 and 170 grams each depending on the age and gender of the person and are about 11 cm long.
* The kidneys are protected by adipose (fat) tissue and each kidney is covered by a renal capsule which protects the kidney and its internal structures from infections.
* Blood carrying waste products but rich in oxygen, is taken to the kidneys by the renal artery which branches off the aorta.
* The blood is filtered by the kidney.
* Deoxygenated blood with the waste products removed, leaves the kidney through the renal vein.

1. **The Malpighian Body (Renal Corpuscle)**
   - **Location:** Occurs in the cortex region of the kidney (refer to Figure 6).
   - **Components:** 
     - **Bowman’s capsule:** A cup-shaped structure.
     - **Glomerulus:** A dense capillary network located in the hollowed-out region of the capsule.
   - **Podocytes:** Special cells on the inner lining of the Bowman's capsule. They feature finger-like extensions that wrap around the capillaries of the glomerulus, creating slits between these extensions to allow substances to pass through.

2. **The Renal Tubule**
   - **Structure and Path:**
     - **Proximal convoluted tubule:** The first segment, located close to the capsule in the cortex.
     - **Loop of Henle:** Runs down into the medulla and loops back.
     - **Distal convoluted tubule:** The second segment located back in the cortex.
     - **Collecting ducts:** Fed by the distal tubule, leading down to the pelvic region of the kidney.
   - **Peritubular Capillary Network:** A secondary capillary network surrounding the renal tubule.
   - **Cellular Adaptations:** Cuboidal epithelial cells line the renal tubule, featuring microvilli extensions on their surface. Each cell contains a rich supply of mitochondria to provide energy via cellular respiration, which is used to move substances against a gradient.

# Main Processes in the Nephron and Composition of Human Blood

## 1. The Nephron and Processes of Kidney Functioning



![Textbook_LifeSciences_Gr11_Gaseous_exchange.pdf-0009-17.png](images/Textbook_LifeSciences_Gr11_Gaseous_exchange.pdf-0009-17.png)


**Figure 6:** Diagram of the location of the main processes as they occur in the nephron.

### Key Structures of the Nephron
* **Afferent arteriole:** Brings blood into the glomerulus under high pressure.
* **Efferent arteriole:** Carries blood away from the glomerulus.
* **Malpighian body / Renal corpuscle:** Consists of the glomerulus enclosed within Bowman's capsule.
* **Renal tubule:** The long, twisted tube leading away from Bowman's capsule where re-absorption and secretion take place.
* **Peritubular capillaries:** A network of capillaries surrounding the renal tubule that facilitates re-absorption and secretion.
* **Nephron:** The functional unit of the kidney, comprising the Malpighian body and the renal tubule.

### The Four Main Processes of Urine Formation
1. **Filtration ($1$):** Blood flows into the glomerulus via the afferent arteriole. Due to high pressure, water, small molecules, and dissolved wastes are filtered out of the blood and into Bowman's capsule.
2. **Re-absorption ($2$):** As the filtrate moves along the renal tubule, essential substances, useful solutes, and water are re-absorbed back into the blood via the peritubular capillaries.
3. **Secretion ($3$):** Additional waste products and excess ions are actively secreted from the peritubular capillaries into the renal tubule.
4. **Excretion ($4$):** The remaining fluid (urine) travels down to be eliminated from the body.

# Revision Notes: Renal Filtration and Re-absorption

## 1. Glomerular Filtration

*   **Location:** Takes place in the Malpighian body of the nephron.
*   **Path of Blood:** Blood enters the glomerulus from the renal artery via the **afferent arteriole** and leaves the glomerulus via the **efferent arteriole**.

### Adaptations of the Malpighian Body for Filtration
*   **Vessel Diameter:** The afferent arteriole is wider than the efferent arteriole. This creates **high blood pressure**, forcing plasma with dissolved substances into the capsular space of Bowman's capsule.
*   **Capillary Walls:** The walls of the glomerular capillaries are thin, consisting of a single layer of squamous epithelial cells. This, combined with the **podocytes** on the inner wall of Bowman's capsule, makes ultra-filtration possible.
*   **Selective Permeability by Size:** Only smaller dissolved substances travel through the filtration slits between the podocytes. Larger proteins remain in the blood.
*   **Surface Area:** Bowman's capsule is cup-shaped to enlarge the contact area with the glomerulus.

> **Key Concept:** The formation of the glomerular filtrate is a **non-selective process**. Both useful substances (e.g., glucose, amino acids, vitamins, minerals, and water) and waste substances (e.g., urea and uric acid) are filtered through into the capsule.

---

## 2. Tubular Re-absorption

*   **Location:** Takes place in the **proximal convoluted tubule**.
*   **Process:** Involves the **active re-absorption** of glucose, amino acids, vitamins, and other important substances present in the glomerular filtrate.
*   **Water Re-absorption:** About $65\%$ of the water moves back into the blood of the peritubular capillaries by **osmosis**, preventing dehydration and the loss of vital nutrients.

### Why is Tubular Re-absorption Efficient?
*   **Energy Supply:** Active transport requires energy. Cuboidal epithelial cells lining the tubules contain many **mitochondria** (sites for cellular respiration).
*   **Increased Surface Area:** **Microvilli** on these cells maximize the surface area for re-absorption.
*   **Fluid Movement:** Water movement occurs via the passive process of osmosis. The fluid inside the renal tubule at this stage is called **tubular filtrate**.

### Role of the Loop of Henle
*   Conserves and recovers water from the filtrate to be returned to the blood.
*   The cells lining the ascending loop of Henle are impermeable to water.
*   Salt is actively pumped out of the loop into the medulla tissue of the kidney, making the medulla **hypertonic** (very salty).

## 3. Tubular secretion

**Tubular secretion** involves the **active removal** of unnecessary substances from the blood in the peritubular capillaries into the tubular filtrate in the distal convoluted tubule. The substances removed include:

* creatinine
* ammonia
* potassium ions ($\text{K}^+$)
* hydrogen ions ($\text{H}^+$)
* sodium ions ($\text{Na}^+$)
* bicarbonate ions
* drugs e.g. penicillin

## 4. Excretion of urine

The filtrate that enters the collecting duct is now called **urine**. Urine consists of urea, excess water and salts. Useful substances should not be excreted in the urine.

Urine collects from all the collecting ducts in the **medulla region** and empties into the **pelvic region** of the kidney. Urine passes down the **ureter** and into the bladder. The bladder has muscles that control the release of urine into the urethra and **urination** occurs.

# Homeostatic regulation by the kidneys

The human body has the ability to maintain a stable internal environment – this is **homeostasis**. It is important that the body's temperature is kept within a narrow range of around $37^\circ\text{C}$. The $\text{pH}$ of the body fluids needs to be regulated and the composition of these fluids need to be kept within certain limits for effective metabolism.

The kidney is involved in $3$ homeostatic mechanisms:
* the regulation of $\text{pH}$ of the blood (considered above)
* the regulation of **water** levels (osmoregulation)
* the regulation of **salt** levels in the blood

---

# Osmoregulation

The homeostatic control of water and salt levels in blood and tissue fluid is known as osmoregulation. $\text{ADH}$ is produced by the hypothalamus and secreted from the pituitary gland and helps to limit water loss in the urine and prevent dehydration (see Table 2 and Figure $10$).

Table 2: Osmoregulation

| Too little water in the blood | Too much water in the_blood |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Dehydration** is when the blood and tissue fluid are short of water | **Overhydration** occurs when the blood and tissue fluids are very dilute. |
| This can be brought about by excessive exercise, hot temperatures, increased sweating or decreased water intake. | This can be because of cooler temperatures, little exercise with no sweating and an excessive intake of water. |

# Homeostatic Control of Water and Regulation of Salt Levels in the Blood

## 1. Concepts & Structure

### Homeostatic Control of Water Balance (Osmoregulation)
The body maintains water balance through negative feedback mechanisms involving the hormone **ADH (Anti-Diuretic Hormone)**, which acts on the collecting ducts and distal convoluted tubules of the kidneys.

| Increased Water Retention (High ADH) | Decreased Water Retention (Low ADH) |
| :--- | :--- |
| The hormone is transported in the blood to the kidney. The permeability of the collecting duct and the distal convoluted tubule is increased. | Collecting ducts and distal convoluted tubules in the kidney become less permeable. |
| More $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ is absorbed and passed into the blood. | Less $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ is absorbed into the blood. |
| The blood becomes more dilute and less concentrated urine is excreted. | Less water leaves the collecting duct and more, dilute urine is excreted. |

---

### Homeostatic Feedback Mechanisms

#### Summary of the Pathways:
1. **When water level decreases (Dehydration / Concentration increases):**
   * Water level in blood decreases $\rightarrow$ Pituitary secretes more ADH.
   * Permeability of collecting ducts and distal convoluted tubules increases.
   * More water is reabsorbed into the blood $\rightarrow$ Returns to normal water levels.

2. **When water level increases (Overhydration / Dilution increases):**
   * Water level in blood increases $\rightarrow$ Pituitary stops secreting ADH / less ADH secreted.
   * Permeability of collecting ducts and distal convoluted tubules decreases.
   * Less water is reabsorbed into the blood / more water is lost $\rightarrow$ Returns to normal water levels.

### Notes on Diuretics
* **Alcoholic and caffeinated drinks** act as **diuretics**, causing an increase in urination frequency and loss of water.
* **ADH** functions in the exact opposite manner by helping the body retain water.

# Revision Notes: Dialysis and Kidney Transplants

## Disadvantages of Dialysis

Dialysis serves as a life-saving medical procedure for individuals experiencing kidney failure, but it comes with several notable disadvantages:

* **Time Consuming and Expensive:** The procedure requires regular, lengthy sessions at medical facilities, leading to significant financial and time commitments.
* **Fatigue:** Patients frequently experience physical exhaustion following dialysis treatments, which limits their ability to work and maintain normal daily activities.
* **Incomplete Waste Removal:** While dialysis filters toxins, it cannot completely replicate all functions of a healthy biological kidney to remove all waste products from the blood.
* **Temporary Solution:** Dialysis is not a cure; the only long-term solution for total kidney failure is a kidney transplant.

---

## Kidney Transplants

### Overview
A patient with both kidneys seriously damaged may be considered for a kidney transplant. In this procedure, a healthy donor donates one of their kidneys to the patient experiencing kidney failure (the recipient).

### Donor Criteria
To be considered a suitable donor, an individual must meet specific biological compatibility requirements:
* **Blood Group Compatibility:** The donor must have a compatible blood group ($A$, $B$, $AB$, or $O$) matching the intended recipient.
* **Tissue Match:** There must be a very close tissue match between the donor and recipient.
* **Ideal Donor:** The most suitable donor is typically a blood relative of the patient.

### Challenges and Complications
* **Organ Rejection:** The recipient's immune system may recognize the donated kidney as a foreign object and attempt to reject it. 
* **Immunosuppressive Drugs:** To reduce the chances of organ rejection, recipients must take immunosuppressive drugs. However, these medications can cause harmful side-effects.
* **Donor Shortage:** There is a persistent shortage of available organ donors.
* **Legal and Ethical Considerations:** Organ donation involves complex legal and ethical frameworks that must be strictly navigated by both donors and recipients (such as regulations governing organ donation in South Africa).
