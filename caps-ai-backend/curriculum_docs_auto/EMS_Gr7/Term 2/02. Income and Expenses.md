# Financial Literacy: Personal and Business Finance

## 1. Personal Finance
Personal finance involves managing your own money, understanding your income, and tracking your financial position.

### Key Concepts
*   **Personal Income:** Money received by an individual from various sources (e.g., wages, salaries, investments).
*   **Personal Expenses:** Money spent by an individual on goods, services, or obligations.
*   **Assets:** Items of value that you own (e.g., cash, property, vehicles).
*   **Liabilities:** Debts or financial obligations you owe to others (e.g., loans, credit card debt).

### The Statement of Net Worth
A Statement of Net Worth provides a snapshot of your financial health at a specific point in time. It is calculated using the following formula:

$$\text{Net Worth} = \text{Total Assets} - \text{Total Liabilities}$$

*Note: Financial value is not a measure of your worth as a person.*

---

## 2. Business Finance
Businesses generate income and incur expenses differently than individuals.

### Sources of Business Income
Businesses earn revenue through various channels:
*   **Sales:** Revenue from selling goods or services.
*   **Interest:** Income earned on cash held in interest-bearing accounts.
*   **Rental:** Income from leasing property or equipment.
*   **Royalties:** Payments for the use of intellectual property (e.g., books, music, patents).
*   **Dividends:** A portion of a company's profit paid to shareholders.

### Business Expenses
Common costs incurred by businesses include:
*   **Salaries and Wages:** Payments to employees.
*   **Tax:** Mandatory payments to the government.
*   **Cost of Sales:** Costs associated with buying stock or raw materials to produce goods.

---

## 3. Savings and Investments
Businesses must manage profits carefully to ensure long-term sustainability.

*   **Savings:** Setting aside money for emergencies or future needs. Businesses should seek professional advice on the best savings accounts offered by banks.
*   **Investments:** Using money to generate further income. 
    *   **Unit Trusts:** A collective investment scheme where money from many investors is pooled and managed by professionals.
    *   **Risks:** All investments carry a degree of risk; it is essential to understand these before committing capital.

### Personal Finance: Income, Expenses, and Net Worth

*   **Net Worth:** The value of an individual's assets minus their total liabilities.

*   **Surplus vs. Deficit:**
    *   **Surplus:** Occurs when net worth is a positive figure ($\text{Net Worth} > 0$).
    *   **Deficit:** Occurs when net worth is a negative figure ($\text{Net Worth} < 0$).

### Financial Literacy: Calculating Net Worth

To determine the financial position of a household, we calculate the **Net Worth**. This is done by identifying all assets (what you own) and all liabilities (what you owe).

#### Key Concepts
*   **Assets:** Items of value owned by an individual or household (e.g., property, vehicles, cash).
*   **Liabilities:** Debts or financial obligations owed to others (e.g., bank bonds, car loans).
*   **Net Worth:** The residual value of the household, calculated as:
    $$\text{Net Worth} = \text{Total Assets} - \text{Total Liabilities}$$

---

#### 2. Understanding Investments

**Definition of an Investment**
An investment occurs when a business commits money with the expectation of earning future income. This involves purchasing a financial product (such as an investment plan or policy) or a physical asset (such as a valuable painting) with the goal of receiving a financial return when the item is eventually sold.

**Investments as 'Forced' Saving**
Investments are considered a form of 'forced' saving for the following reasons:
*   **Capital Allocation:** When a business invests, it removes that capital from daily circulation for the duration of the investment.
*   **Restricted Access:** Because the money is 'tied up' in the investment, it is inaccessible for day-to-day operational spending.
*   **Effective Saving:** By preventing the business from spending the money on immediate needs, the investment acts as a mechanism to ensure long-term capital growth.

### Business Finance: Savings, Expenses, and Capital

#### 1. Business Savings
Businesses must manage cash flow and plan for their financial future by saving and investing a portion of their profits. 

**Why businesses save:**
*   **Income Generation:** Savings and investments earn interest, which creates additional income for the business.
*   **Emergency Preparedness:** Savings provide a financial buffer to handle unexpected emergencies.
*   **Separation of Funds:** It is critical to keep personal savings separate from business savings. 

**Key Reason for Separation:**
Keeping funds separate ensures that business owners can access capital quickly for the continuous service delivery and efficient running of the business. If business funds are used for personal expenses, the business may face cash flow shortages that hinder daily operations.

---

#### 2. Business Expenses
**Definition:** Business expenses are the costs incurred by a business to remain operational. These costs vary depending on the nature and size of the business.

**Examples of Business Expenses:**
*   **Capital Expenses:** Costs associated with starting up the business.
*   **Human Resources:** Staff salaries, wages, medical aid, pension fund contributions, unemployment fund contributions, and staff education/training.
*   **Infrastructure:** Renting or buying business premises.
*   **Operational Equipment:** Specialized machinery (e.g., ovens for a bakery, trucks for a removal company, textiles for a clothing business).
*   **Office Requirements:** Computers, furniture, and stationery.
*   **Financial & Legal:** Tax on business income, interest on loans or credit, and insurance premiums.
*   **Development:** Research and development.
*   **Utilities:** Water, electricity, telephone lines, and Internet connections.
*   **Production:** Raw materials used in the manufacturing process.

**Definition:** Shares are portions of a business that are purchased by shareholders. 

**Purpose:**
When individuals or entities buy shares, they provide the necessary capital (money) for the business to start up and operate. In return, shareholders may receive a portion of the profits (dividends) or benefit from the growth in the value of the business.

### Business Income and Expenses

#### 1. Types of Income
Businesses generate income based on their primary activities:
*   **Service Business:** Income earned by providing a service (e.g., doctors, dentists). This is referred to as **current income** or **service rendered**.
*   **Retail Business:** Income earned by selling physical goods. This is referred to as **sales**.

#### 2. Types of Expenses
Expenses are the costs incurred by a business to operate:
*   **Cost of Sales:** The direct cost associated with acquiring or producing the goods sold by the business.
*   **Operating Expenses:** Costs paid to keep the business running, such as salaries or wages paid to employees for services delivered.
*   **Other Expenses:** Costs related to financing, such as interest on bank overdrafts, loans, or bonds.

#### 3. Profitability Concepts
Profit is calculated in two main stages:

*   **Gross Profit:** The profit made after deducting only the **cost of sales** from the total income.
    $$\text{Gross Profit} = \text{Total Income} - \text{Cost of Sales}$$
*   **Net Profit:** The final profit remaining after all expenses (including operating and other expenses) have been deducted from the gross profit.
    $$\text{Net Profit} = \text{Gross Profit} - \text{Total Expenses}$$

#### 4. Savings and Banking
*   **Savings:** Money set aside for future use.
*   **Current Bank Account (Cheque Account):** An account opened by a business to facilitate regular, daily transactions. 
    *   *Note:* These accounts typically do not earn interest.
