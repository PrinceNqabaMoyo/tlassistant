# Topic: The Economy - Needs and Wants

## 1. Core Concepts
In this topic, we explore the fundamental economic problem: how to satisfy **unlimited wants** with **limited resources**.

*   **Needs:** Things that are essential for survival and well-being.
*   **Wants:** Desires for goods and services that are not essential for survival but improve the quality of life.
*   **Resources:** The limited means (natural, human, and capital) available to produce goods and services to satisfy needs and wants.

---

## 2. Categorizing Needs
Needs are classified into different levels to help us understand human requirements:

| Category | Definition | Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary (Basic) Needs** | Essential for physical survival. | Food, water, basic shelter, basic clothing. |
| **Secondary Needs** | Important for well-being, social interaction, and reaching potential, but not strictly for survival. | Love, support from family/friends, education, healthcare. |

---

## 3. The Economic Problem
The central economic problem is that human wants are **unlimited**, while the resources available to satisfy them are **limited** (scarce).

*   **Economic Activity:** The process of producing, distributing, and consuming goods and services to satisfy needs and wants.
*   **Service Delivery:** A practical example of the economic problem in South Africa, where the government must allocate limited resources to meet the basic needs of communities.

# The Economic Problem: Needs, Wants, and Resources

## 1. Core Concepts
*   **Basic Needs:** Essential requirements for survival (e.g., food, shelter).
*   **Secondary Needs:** Desires that improve quality of life once basic needs are met.
*   **Unlimited Needs and Wants:** The human tendency to always desire more, regardless of the level of satisfaction already achieved.
*   **Limited Resources:** The scarcity of inputs available to satisfy all human needs and wants.
*   **The Economic Problem:** The fundamental challenge of how an economy uses its limited resources to satisfy the unlimited needs and wants of its citizens.

## 2. Factors of Production
Factors of production are the inputs used to produce goods and services. They are categorized into four main groups:

| Factor | Definition |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Land** | Natural resources provided by the earth. |
| **Labour** | The human capacity and effort used to work. |
| **Capital** | The money, machinery, and infrastructure used to produce goods and services. |
| **Entrepreneurship** | The initiative to combine the other three factors to create production. |

## 3. The Relationship Between Needs and Production
The economic problem is directly linked to the hierarchy of needs. A country must prioritize the allocation of its limited resources based on the following logic:

1.  **Prioritization:** A country must satisfy basic needs (e.g., food security) before it can allocate resources to secondary needs (e.g., luxury goods).
2.  **Resource Allocation:** If a country has limited production inputs, it must decide what to produce to best serve its citizens.
3.  **Investment:** To move from basic survival to a more advanced economy, a country must invest in projects that increase output (e.g., agricultural development to ensure food security).

## 4. Summary of Needs by Level
*   **Individuals:** Want money for food, clothing, and luxury goods.
*   **Families:** Want money for a more comfortable lifestyle.
*   **Communities:** Want facilities like libraries, sports fields, and services like policing and healthcare.
*   **Countries:** Want stronger economies to support their populations.

## 2. Primary vs. Secondary Needs
To live a healthy, normal life, humans require specific essentials.

*   **Primary Needs:** Essentials required for survival (e.g., clean air, food, water, clothing, shelter, learning, and fun).
*   **Secondary Needs (Wants):** Things that are not essential for survival but are "nice to have."

## 3. Economic Principles
### Unlimited Wants
*   People generally desire more than they actually need.
*   Human wants are never fully satisfied.
*   **Business Impact:** Satisfying these needs and wants keeps people motivated and drives economic activity.

### Limited Resources
*   Because human wants are unlimited, resources are insufficient to satisfy everyone's desires.
*   **Definition:** A "limited resource" refers to the fixed amount of raw materials, labour, money, land, and work opportunities available to produce goods and services.
*   **Provision:**
    *   **Business:** Provides most goods and services to satisfy needs.
    *   **Government:** Supplies essential services such as housing, clean water, sanitation, electricity, and healthcare.

## Activity 4: Revise Types of Needs and Wants

### 4.1 The Difference Between Needs and Wants
*   **Needs:** Essential items or conditions that you cannot survive without.
*   **Wants:** Items or desires that you can survive without.

---

### 4.2 Primary vs. Secondary Needs
**Primary (Basic) Needs:** These are the physical requirements for survival, such as food, water, warmth/shelter, rest, and safety/security. If these needs are not met, a person cannot survive.

**Secondary Needs (Wants):** These are desires that are not essential for survival. While they are important for human development and improving one's quality of life, they are not required to stay alive. 

**The Relationship:** Basic needs must be met first. Once they are satisfied, individuals can focus on secondary needs. The drive to satisfy both needs and wants motivates people to work hard, seek promotions, and study to improve their circumstances.

---

### 4.3 Examples of Needs and Wants

| Basic Needs | Secondary Needs (Wants) |
| :--- | :--- |
| Food | Working hard for a promotion |
| Water | Studying to improve oneself |
| Shelter | Buying luxury goods |
| Rest | |

---

### 4.4 Importance of Primary Needs
Primary needs are more important than secondary needs because they form the foundation for all other human activities. If a person is struggling to meet basic requirements—such as having enough food or living in a safe environment—they lack the stability required to focus on secondary goals like education or career advancement. Therefore, primary needs must be satisfied before secondary needs can be addressed.

### Activity 5: Unlimited Needs and Wants vs. Limited Resources

#### 5.1 Why are needs and wants unlimited while resources are limited?
Individuals, families, communities, and countries possess unlimited needs and wants. As one desire is satisfied, new ones emerge.
*   **Individuals:** Desire more money for food, clothing, and luxury goods.
*   **Families:** Desire more money for a comfortable lifestyle.
*   **Communities:** Desire more money for facilities (libraries, sports fields) and services (policing, healthcare).
*   **Countries:** Desire more money for stronger economies.

Despite these infinite desires, the resources available to satisfy them are **limited**. We do not have enough resources to provide everything that is needed or wanted.

#### 5.2 The Economic Problem
The conflict between unlimited needs/wants and limited resources is known as the **economic problem**.

#### 5.3 Types of Limited Resources (Factors of Production)
In economics, the resources used to produce goods and services are called the **factors of production**. These are limited in supply:

| Resource | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Labour** | The capacity of a country's people to work. |
| **Capital** | The money and assets a country has for producing goods and services. |
| **Natural Resources** | Raw materials such as timber, water, coal, oil, and metals (e.g., gold). |

#### 5.4 The Factors of Production
An economy uses the following four factors of production to produce goods and services:
1.  **Labour**
2.  **Capital**
3.  **Land**
4.  **Entrepreneurship**

#### 5.5 The Link Between Needs and the Economic Problem
Because a country has limited factors of production, it must make careful choices regarding what to produce to best satisfy the needs and wants of its citizens. 

**Example:** A country facing poverty must prioritize producing enough food to feed its population rather than using its limited capital to set up factories that produce luxury goods. The country must prioritize satisfying primary needs before secondary needs.

---

### Economic Priorities and Resource Allocation

In the context of national economic development, a government must prioritize the welfare of its population by focusing on essential requirements before addressing non-essential desires.

#### Key Principles

1.  **Prioritizing Basic Needs:** A nation must ensure that the fundamental needs of its citizens (such as food, water, shelter, and healthcare) are met before allocating resources toward the production or provision of luxury goods.
2.  **Strategic Investment in Agriculture:** To achieve food security, a government should prioritize investment in projects designed to increase agricultural output. This ensures that the country can sustain its population through domestic production.

#### Summary of Economic Logic

| Priority Level | Focus Area | Objective |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary** | Basic Needs (e.g., Food) | Ensure survival and stability of the population. |
| **Secondary** | Luxury Goods | Address non-essential consumer demands once basic needs are met. |
