### 2.1 The Digestive System

Our cells require **protein, carbohydrates, fats, vitamins,** and **minerals** to function. Because the food we eat consists of pieces too large to pass through selectively permeable cell membranes, the digestive system is required to break these down.

#### Purpose of the Digestive System
The digestive system is responsible for breaking down food into small particles that can be **absorbed** into the bloodstream and **transported** to cells throughout the body.

The system consists of the **alimentary canal**, a long, twisting pipe-like structure approximately 9 meters in total length, starting at the mouth and ending at the anus.

#### Main Processes in the Digestive System
There are four main processes that occur along the alimentary canal:

| Process | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Ingestion** | Taking food into the body through the mouth by eating or drinking. |
| **Digestion** | Breaking down large food pieces into small particles that can be absorbed and pass through cell membranes. |
| **Absorption** | Digested particles move into the cells of the digestive tract and then into the bloodstream to be carried to all cells in the body. |
| **Egestion** | Undigested or unwanted particles travel through the digestive tract and are passed out as faeces. |

# Components of the Digestive System

## New Words
*   **Anus:** The opening at the end of the digestive tract through which waste is eliminated.
*   **Chemical digestion:** The process of using enzymes to break down food into smaller molecules.
*   **Egestion:** The process of passing out undigested food (faeces) from the body.
*   **Faeces:** The waste matter remaining after food has been digested.
*   **Fibre:** Indigestible plant material that helps move waste through the digestive system.
*   **Ingestion:** The process of taking food into the body through the mouth.
*   **Jaundice:** A condition causing yellowing of the skin and eyes, often related to liver function.
*   **Metabolic:** Relating to the chemical processes that occur within a living organism to maintain life.
*   **Saliva:** A watery liquid secreted into the mouth by glands, providing lubrication and enzymes.
*   **Small intestine:** The part of the digestive system where most digestion and absorption occur.
*   **Stomach:** The internal organ where food is broken down into a liquid.
*   **Ulcer:** A sore or lesion on the lining of the stomach or intestine.

### 1. The mouth and oesophagus
Digestion begins in the mouth. Food is chewed and mixed with saliva. When you swallow, the food travels down the oesophagus.

### 2. Stomach
The chewed food enters the stomach for further digestion. The stomach contains substances called **enzymes** that help break down food. The stomach also contracts to turn the food into a liquid.

### 3. Small intestine
Most of the digestion process occurs here. This is also the primary site for the absorption of food particles into the body.

### 4. Large intestine (or colon)
By the time food reaches the large intestine, most nutrients have already been absorbed. The large intestine absorbs water and salts from the remaining indigestible fibre.

### 5. Rectum and Anus
The remaining waste substances, known as **faeces**, are stored in the rectum and then passed out through the anus. This process is called **egestion**.

Bacteria that live in the large intestine are beneficial to the human body. They produce Vitamin K and some Vitamin B, which are essential for health.

### TAKE NOTE: Egestion vs. Excretion
It is important to distinguish between these two biological processes:
*   **Egestion:** The process of passing out undigested food particles as faeces.
*   **Excretion:** The process by which the body gets rid of metabolic waste products formed from chemical reactions within the body.

#### 2.2 The Circulatory System
The circulatory system is the network within the human body responsible for transporting blood.

**Purpose of the Circulatory System:**
*   Transporting blood with oxygen ($O_2$) from the lungs to cells.
*   Transporting blood with carbon dioxide ($CO_2$) back to the lungs.
*   Distributing nutrients from the digestive system to the cells.
*   Removing waste products to be excreted.

**Components of the Circulatory System:**
The system consists of the heart and a network of blood vessels.

1.  **Heart:** A strong muscle that pumps blood throughout the body. It contains four chambers:
    *   **Atria (singular: atrium):** The two top chambers.
    *   **Ventricles:** The two bottom chambers.
2.  **Blood vessels:** The pathways that carry blood throughout the body, categorized as:
    *   Arteries
    *   Capillaries
    *   Veins

### 3. Blood
Blood is the medium used to transport substances throughout the body. These substances are carried in two ways:
*   **Dissolved in blood plasma:** Includes nutrients, waste products, and carbon dioxide ($CO_2$).
*   **Within red blood cells:** Includes oxygen ($O_2$).

---

### Main Processes in the Circulatory System
The circulatory system functions as two integrated systems:
1.  **Pulmonary Circulation:** A short system circulating blood between the heart and the lungs.
2.  **Systemic Circulation:** A longer system circulating blood from the heart to the rest of the body and back.

#### The Cycle of Circulation
*   **Gaseous Exchange:** Blood travels from the heart to the lungs. In the lungs, carbon dioxide ($CO_2$) leaves the blood and oxygen ($O_2$) enters it. This process is called **gaseous exchange**.
*   **Oxygenated Blood:** Once the blood contains more oxygen than carbon dioxide, it is referred to as **oxygenated**. This blood returns to the heart.
*   **Deoxygenated Blood:** The heart pumps the oxygenated blood to the body's cells. As it delivers oxygen, it collects carbon dioxide. Blood containing more $CO_2$ than $O_2$ is called **deoxygenated**. This blood returns to the heart to be sent back to the lungs for excretion.

This cycle repeats continuously throughout a person's life.

### Health Issues Involving the Circulatory System

Common diseases of the circulatory system include:

*   **High Blood Pressure:** This occurs when the force with which the blood pushes against the walls of the blood vessels is too high. This can cause damage to the capillaries and several organs.
*   **Heart Attacks:** These occur when a narrowing or blood clot develops in one of the blood vessels that supplies the heart muscle with blood. If the narrowing or blood clot is significant enough, it can stop the blood flow to the heart muscle, preventing the heart from pumping. This can be fatal.
*   **Strokes:** These occur when cells in your brain are deprived of oxygen. This often happens as a result of a blockage in the blood vessels leading to the brain, or when one of these vessels ruptures (breaks or bursts open).

### 2.3 The Respiratory System

The respiratory system is closely linked to the circulatory system. While the circulatory system maintains the circulation of blood in the body, the respiratory system deals with the exchange of gases.

#### Purpose of the Respiratory System
The respiratory system is responsible for:
1.  Supplying the body's cells with oxygen.
2.  Removing carbon dioxide from the body.

*   **Rupture:** To break or burst open.

*   **Exhale:** To breathe air out of the lungs.

*   **Mucus:** A sticky substance that traps particles and germs.
*   **Pharynx:** The throat area.

## Structures of the Respiratory System

### 1. Mouth and Nose
Oxygen-rich air enters the body through the mouth and nose, where it is warmed before traveling further into the system.

### 2. Trachea (Windpipe)
The trachea is a tube that enters the chest, allowing air to flow from the mouth into the bronchi.
*   **Structure:** It is kept open by cartilage rings.
*   **Protection:** It is lined with **mucus** to trap dust and germs, and **cilia** to move these trapped particles out of the body.
*   **Expulsion:** Sneezing and coughing are mechanisms used to expel mucus and foreign particles from the body.

### 3. Bronchi
The trachea splits into two main air tubes called bronchi, which connect to each lung. These tubes branch out into smaller and smaller tubes that eventually connect to the **alveoli**.

### 4. Lungs
The lungs are the primary organs of the respiratory system. They contain tiny air sacs called **alveoli**, which are surrounded by small capillaries where gaseous exchange takes place.

### 5. Diaphragm
The diaphragm is a dome-shaped muscle located below the lungs. It is the primary muscle used for breathing:
*   **Inhalation:** The diaphragm contracts and moves downwards, allowing the lungs to fill with air.
*   **Exhalation:** The diaphragm relaxes and moves upwards, forcing air out of the lungs.

# The Respiratory System



![Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr9_Cells-as-the-basic-units-of-life.pdf-0009-06.png](images/Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr9_Cells-as-the-basic-units-of-life.pdf-0009-06.png)


## Main Processes in the Respiratory System
There are three distinct processes that occur within the respiratory system:

1.  **Breathing**: The process of taking oxygen into the lungs and pushing carbon dioxide out of the body. It occurs in two phases:
    *   **Inhalation**: Drawing air into the lungs.
    *   **Exhalation**: Pushing air out of the lungs.
2.  **Gaseous Exchange**: This occurs via the process of **diffusion** at two specific locations:
    *   **In the alveoli**: Oxygen diffuses from the lungs into the blood, while carbon dioxide diffuses from the blood back into the lungs.
    *   **At the body tissues**: Oxygen diffuses from the blood into the cells, while carbon dioxide diffuses from the cells into the blood.
3.  **Cellular Respiration**: A process occurring within the mitochondria of cells to release chemical energy stored in food.

---

### 💡 Take Note: Breathing vs. Respiration
It is a common error to confuse breathing with respiration:
*   **Breathing** is the mechanical act of moving air into and out of the lungs.
*   **Cellular Respiration** is a chemical process that takes place inside cells to release energy when oxygen is combined with glucose and other nutrients.

---

# 2.4 The Musculoskeletal System

All body movements rely on a system of muscles, tendons, ligaments, bones, and joints working together.

## Purpose of the Musculoskeletal System
*   **Movement:** Muscle tissue is responsible for producing movement. Muscles must be attached to a frame structure to function effectively.
*   **Support:** The skeleton provides the frame for muscles to attach to, enabling movement.
*   **Protection:** The skeleton protects soft, fragile organs such as the heart, lungs, and brain.



![Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr9_Cells-as-the-basic-units-of-life.pdf-0010-00.png](images/Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr9_Cells-as-the-basic-units-of-life.pdf-0010-00.png)


## Components of the Musculoskeletal System

The system consists of the following key components:

### 1. Muscles
Muscles allow for movement through two primary actions:
*   **Contract:** The muscle becomes shorter.
*   **Relax:** The muscle becomes longer.

### 2. Bones
Bones provide structural support and define the shape of the body.
*   **Joints:** The specific location where two or more bones meet (e.g., knee, elbow, finger, and toe joints).

### 3. Cartilage
Cartilage is a tissue that is both stiff and flexible. It is found in several areas:
*   Between bones in joints.
*   Between the ribs and the breastbone.
*   In the ears, nose, and bronchial tubes.
*   As discs between the bones of the spinal column.

---

## New Words Glossary
*   **Brittle:** Hard but liable to break or shatter easily.
*   **Cartilage:** Firm, flexible connective tissue.
*   **Collagen:** A protein that provides structural support to tissues.
*   **Contract:** To shorten or tighten a muscle.
*   **Fracture:** A break in a bone.
*   **Frame structure:** A supporting framework.
*   **Joint:** A point where two bones meet.
*   **Ligament:** A short band of tough, flexible fibrous connective tissue which connects two bones or cartilages.
*   **Mineral salts:** Inorganic substances (like calcium) that provide hardness to bones.
*   **Muscle:** A band or bundle of fibrous tissue that has the ability to contract.
*   **Tendons:** A flexible but inelastic cord of strong fibrous collagen tissue attaching a muscle to a bone.

# The Excretory System

## Purpose of the Excretory System
Cells use oxygen and nutrients to function. During this process, they produce metabolic waste products, including:
*   **Urea:** A substance formed when protein is broken down in the liver.
*   **Carbon dioxide ($CO_2$):** A by-product of cellular respiration.

The excretory system is responsible for removing these harmful waste products from the blood to prevent them from building up to toxic concentrations. Additionally, the system helps maintain the correct balance of water in the body.

---

## Components of the Excretory System

The body uses the **urinary system** to remove metabolic waste from the blood in the form of urine. It consists of four main parts:

### 1. Kidneys
*   **Function:** They filter all the blood in the body to remove urea.
*   **Description:** You have two kidneys, each about the size of a fist and bean-shaped.
*   **Output:** They produce urine, which is a combination of excess water and waste products.

### 2. Ureters
*   **Function:** These are thin tubes that connect each kidney to the bladder.
*   **Purpose:** They carry urine from the kidneys to the bladder.

*(Note: The bladder and urethra are also components of this system, as indicated in the diagram.)*

### Main Processes in the Excretory System

There are four main processes involved in the function of the excretory system:

1.  **Filtration:** All blood in the body passes through the kidneys as part of the circulatory system. The kidneys filter the blood to remove unwanted minerals, urea, and excess water. Metabolic waste products are then excreted in solution as liquid urine.
2.  **Absorption:** Once the blood is filtered, substances that the body still requires are re-absorbed back into the bloodstream so they are not lost in the urine.
3.  **Diffusion:** Substances are transported into and out of the specialised cells of the kidney through the process of diffusion.
4.  **Excretion:** The kidneys funnel liquid urine through the ureters to the bladder for storage. When the bladder is full, muscles force the urine out of the body through the urethra.

## 2.6 The Nervous System

### Purpose of the Nervous System
The nervous system is a complex network responsible for transmitting nerve impulses between different parts of the body. It receives **stimuli** (singular: **stimulus**) from the environment (via the ears, eyes, skin, or tongue) or from inside the body. These stimuli are converted into **impulses** that are sent to the brain and spinal cord.

### Components of the Nervous System
The nervous system is composed of several integrated parts.



![Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr9_Cells-as-the-basic-units-of-life.pdf-0015-00.png](images/Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr9_Cells-as-the-basic-units-of-life.pdf-0015-00.png)


#### 1. Nerves
*   **Definition:** Nerves are long fibres that transmit messages to and from the brain and spinal cord.
*   **Structure:** Each nerve is an enclosed bundle of nerve cells called **neurons**.
*   **Function:** Nerves work together to carry messages throughout the body and form the nerve tissue of the nervous system.

#### 2. Brain
*   **Location:** The brain is located inside the skull.
*   **Function:** It is the central part of the nervous system. It sends messages to the rest of the body. The brain consists of different areas with specific functions that communicate with one another.

### 3. Spinal Cord
The spinal cord is a bundle of nervous tissue and other support cells that runs from the brain through your spine. It is protected by your vertebral column. Together with the brain, the spinal cord forms the **central nervous system**.



![Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr9_Cells-as-the-basic-units-of-life.pdf-0016-00.png](images/Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr9_Cells-as-the-basic-units-of-life.pdf-0016-00.png)


### 4. Sensory Organs
The nervous system is divided into two main parts:
1.  **Central Nervous System:** Comprised of the brain and the spinal cord.
2.  **Peripheral Nervous System:** Connects the central nervous system to the muscles and organs.

> **TAKE NOTE:** "Peripheral" means on the outside. Therefore, the peripheral nervous system is located on the outside of the central nervous system.

Sensory organs are responsible for collecting information from the environment and sending it via sensory nerves to the central nervous system. The sensory organs include:
*   Ears
*   Nose
*   Eyes
*   Skin
*   Tongue

---

### Main Processes in the Nervous System
The nervous system is responsible for key processes in the body, primarily the **sending and receiving of impulses**.

*   **Mechanism:** Nerve cells in the brain send and receive multiple messages from various sources simultaneously. The central nervous system interprets these signals, allowing us to perceive the world around us.
*   **Example (Hearing):** Sound waves enter the ear and are transformed into electrical signals. These signals travel along the auditory nerve to the brain, where they are interpreted as sound.

> **DID YOU KNOW?** The amount of electricity used by the brain to send and receive messages is enough to power the light in your refrigerator!

### The Nervous System: Functions and Health

#### 1. Sensory Functions
The nervous system processes information from our environment through specialized receptors:

*   **Seeing:** Light enters the eye and stimulates specialized cells. These cells transmit signals via the optic nerve to the brain for interpretation.
*   **Feeling (Touch):** Millions of nerve endings (receptors) in the skin, muscles, bones, joints, and internal organs allow us to experience pressure, pain, temperature, and movement.
*   **Tasting:** Taste buds on the tongue and mouth distinguish between flavours (sweet, sour, bitter, salty). This works in conjunction with the sense of smell.
*   **Smelling:** Nerve cells in the nasal lining respond to molecules in the air, sending messages to the brain, which can recognize approximately $10\,000$ different smells.

#### 2. Regulation
The nervous system maintains internal balance (homeostasis). A primary example is **temperature regulation**:
*   The body must be kept at approximately $37^\circ\text{C}$ to function effectively.
*   **If too hot:** The body increases sweating to cool down.
*   **If too cold:** The body shivers to generate heat energy.

> **Did you know?**
> *   Nerve impulses travel to and from the brain at $274\text{ km/h}$.
> *   The brain makes up less than $2\%$ of total body weight, yet it uses $20\%$ of the body's energy.

---

#### 3. Health Issues Involving the Nervous System

**Trauma and Injuries**
*   **Brain and Spinal Cord Damage:** Injuries to these areas can have devastating effects. For example, a broken neck can damage the spinal cord, preventing the brain from sending or receiving messages, which may result in paralysis.

**Stroke**
*   **Definition:** A stroke occurs when blood flow to the brain is interrupted. Brain cells begin to die within minutes due to a lack of oxygen and blood.
*   **Consequence:** A part of the brain's function is lost.

# Revision: The Digestive System

### 1. Definition of Digestion
Digestion is the biological process by which food is broken down into smaller, soluble components that the body can absorb and use for energy, growth, and cell repair. It involves both mechanical breakdown (physical) and chemical breakdown (enzymatic).

### 2. The Four Main Processes of the Digestive System
The digestive system performs four primary functions:
1. **Ingestion:** Taking food and liquids into the body through the mouth.
2. **Digestion:** Breaking down complex food molecules into simpler nutrients.
3. **Absorption:** Moving the broken-down nutrients into the bloodstream to be transported to cells.
4. **Egestion (Elimination):** Removing undigested waste materials from the body.

### 3. Components of the Digestive System and Their Functions

| Component | Primary Function |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Mouth** | Mechanical breakdown (chewing) and chemical digestion of starch via saliva. |
| **Oesophagus** | Transports food from the mouth to the stomach via peristalsis. |
| **Stomach** | Mixes food with gastric juices (acid and enzymes) to break down proteins. |
| **Small Intestine** | Primary site of chemical digestion and absorption of nutrients into the blood. |
| **Liver** | Produces bile to help emulsify fats. |
| **Gallbladder** | Stores and concentrates bile produced by the liver. |
| **Pancreas** | Secretes digestive enzymes and bicarbonate into the small intestine. |
| **Large Intestine** | Absorbs water and electrolytes; forms and stores faeces. |
| **Rectum/Anus** | Stores waste and facilitates the process of egestion. |

### 4. Diarrhoea in Babies
**Why it is dangerous:**
Babies have a small body mass and a high water content. Diarrhoea causes rapid loss of fluids and essential electrolytes (dehydration). Because babies cannot communicate their thirst or compensate for fluid loss as effectively as adults, they can become critically ill or suffer organ failure very quickly.

**Prevention:**
* **Hygiene:** Ensure clean water, proper sanitation, and frequent handwashing.
* **Nutrition:** Exclusive breastfeeding for the first six months and ensuring safe preparation of formula/food.
* **Vaccination:** Keeping up to date with vaccinations (such as the Rotavirus vaccine) that prevent common causes of diarrhoeal disease.

### Chapter 2: Systems in the Human Body - Review Exercises

**5. Distinguish between inhalation and exhalation. [2 marks]**
*   **Inhalation:** The process of taking air into the lungs. The diaphragm contracts and moves downwards, and the rib cage moves upwards and outwards, increasing the volume of the chest cavity.
*   **Exhalation:** The process of releasing air from the lungs. The diaphragm relaxes and moves upwards, and the rib cage moves downwards and inwards, decreasing the volume of the chest cavity.

**6. Is carbon dioxide in your body excreted or egested? Explain why you say so. [3 marks]**
*   Carbon dioxide is **excreted**.
*   **Reasoning:** Excretion is the process of removing metabolic waste products produced by the body's cells (such as $CO_2$ from cellular respiration). Egestion refers to the removal of undigested food material (faeces) from the digestive tract, which was never part of the body's metabolism.

*   **Focus:** Understanding the biological processes required for human reproduction.

## 2.3 The Circulatory System

The circulatory system consists of both blood and lymph. At this level of study, the focus is specifically on the **blood circulatory system**.

### Activity: Charting the Circulatory System
Learners are encouraged to create visual representations of the circulatory process. There is a distinct difference between the two types of diagrams:

*   **Flow Chart:** Illustrates a process from beginning to end, where arrows start at one point and terminate at another.
*   **Circular Diagram:** Illustrates a process that occurs repeatedly, forming a continuous cycle.



![Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr9_Cells-as-the-basic-units-of-life.pdf-0030-01.png](images/Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr9_Cells-as-the-basic-units-of-life.pdf-0030-01.png)

**The Circulatory Cycle:**
1.  **In lungs:** $CO_2$ is removed and $O_2$ is added to the blood.
2.  **Heart to Body:** Oxygenated blood is pumped from the lungs to the heart, then from the heart to the body.
3.  **At the cells:** $O_2$ and nutrients are delivered to cells, while $CO_2$ and waste products are added to the blood.
4.  **Deoxygenated blood:** Blood is pumped from cells to the heart, and then from the heart to the lungs to restart the cycle.

## 2.7 The Nervous System

The nervous system can be studied by examining the impact of various substances on brain function. It is important to note that many drugs can be beneficial when used correctly at the prescribed dosage, but become harmful when abused or used in excessive amounts.

### Effects of Drugs on the Brain

*   **Stimulants:** Drugs like methamphetamines (tik), cocaine, and caffeine speed up the body's reactions. Methamphetamines and cocaine create a sense of reward, which often leads to addiction.
*   **Sedatives and Hypnotics:** Substances like alcohol and marijuana (dagga) have the opposite effect; they slow down brain activity.
*   **Hallucinogens:** Drugs such as LSD, Ecstasy, Woonga, and Nyaope cause the user to perceive things that are not real.
*   **Pain Relievers:** Morphine and heroin are medically used to alleviate severe pain, but when misused, they significantly slow down the user's reactions.

# Revision: Life Sciences

### 1. Definition of Digestion
Digestion is the process of breaking food into small soluble parts that can be transported in the blood and absorbed by the cells.

### 2. The Four Stages of Nutrition
*   **Ingestion:** Taking food into the body.
*   **Digestion:** Breaking down food.
*   **Absorption:** Taking nutrients into the blood.
*   **Egestion:** Removing undigested waste.

### 3. The Digestive Process
*   **Mouth and Oesophagus:** Food is chewed in the mouth and passed through the oesophagus to the stomach.
*   **Stomach:** The bolus enters the stomach where it is mechanically digested through churning and chemically digested by gastric juices.
*   **Small Intestine:** Most chemical digestion and absorption occurs here. Food is broken into tiny pieces and absorbed by the intestinal walls into the blood.
*   **Large Intestine:** Water is absorbed from the remaining material, and indigestible fibre is prepared for removal.
*   **Rectum and Anus:** Undigested substances (faeces) pass from the large intestine to the rectum and are egested through the anus.

### 4. Health Note: Diarrhoea in Infants
Babies with diarrhoea lose large quantities of water and nutrients, leading to rapid dehydration. This can be fatal without medical attention. Prevention involves sterilizing feeding equipment to stop the transfer of bacteria.

### 5. Breathing Definitions
*   **Inhalation:** Breathing in.
*   **Exhalation:** Breathing out.

### 6. Egestion vs. Excretion
*   **Egestion:** The removal of undigested substances and fibre from the body.
*   **Excretion:** The removal of waste products of metabolism (e.g., carbon dioxide produced during cellular respiration).

### 7. Breathing vs. Respiration
*   **Breathing:** The physical process of taking air into and out of the body through the mouth, trachea, bronchi, and lungs.
*   **Respiration:** A cellular process where oxygen is used to release energy from food. Carbon dioxide is a by-product of this process and is returned to the blood to be exhaled.

### 8. Musculoskeletal System
*   **Muscles:** Contract and relax to move bones at the joints, allowing for locomotion.
*   **Tendons and Ligaments:** Connective tissues used to facilitate movement and stability.
*   **Bones:** Provide an attachment point for muscles, as well as protection and structural support for the body.

### 9. Effects of Drug and Alcohol Abuse
Substance abuse can lead to severe, often irreversible, health consequences:
*   **Dependence:** Addiction makes it difficult to withstand cravings, often leading to desperate behaviours to obtain more.
*   **Organ Damage:** Chronic use can lead to organ failure and death.
*   **Neurological Harm:** Reduced brain activity, seizures, impaired vision, motor coordination issues, and blackouts.
*   **Infection:** Intravenous drug use carries a high risk of HIV and other infectious diseases.
*   **Respiratory/Vascular Impact:** Stimulants like nicotine and cocaine damage the respiratory and vascular systems.

### 10. Foetal Alcohol Syndrome
If a pregnant woman consumes alcohol, it can cause permanent and irreversible damage to the baby, including Foetal Alcohol Syndrome, physical abnormalities, and central nervous system defects.
