## 1.1 Cell structure

### What are cells?
All living **organisms**, including plants, animals, bacteria, and fungi, are made up of **cells**. Cells are the smallest parts of all living organisms.

#### Types of Organisms
There are two main types of organisms based on the structure of their cells:
1. **Eukaryotic cells:** Cells that contain a **nucleus**. These make up organisms such as plants and animals.
2. **Prokaryotic cells:** Cells that do not have a nucleus. An example of an organism with prokaryotic cells is bacteria.

#### Definitions
* **Structural and functional units:** Cells are the basic building blocks that perform all life functions.
* **Microscopic:** Cells are too small to be seen with the naked eye; they must be viewed using a **microscope**.

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

### Historical Context
Robert Hooke was the first cytologist to identify cells under his microscope in 1665. He named them "cells" because the microscopic shapes he saw in a slice of cork reminded him of the small rooms (cells) that monks lived in at a monastery.

---

## New Words Glossary
* **Cell:** The basic structural and functional unit of life.
* **Cytoplasm:** The jelly-like substance inside a cell.
* **Eukaryote:** An organism whose cells contain a nucleus.
* **Membrane:** The thin layer that surrounds a cell.
* **Microscope:** An instrument used to see objects that are too small for the naked eye.
* **Microscopic:** So small that it can only be seen with a microscope.
* **Nucleus:** The control center of the cell.
* **Organism:** Any individual living thing.
* **Prokaryote:** A unicellular organism that lacks a nucleus.

## Cell Structure

While there are many different types of cells, there are components common to all cells, and other structures found in most (but not all) cells.

### Common Cell Structures
All cells possess the following:
*   **Cell membrane:** A selectively permeable barrier.
*   **Cytoplasm:** The jelly-like substance within the cell.
*   **Nucleus:** Found in most eukaryotic cells, acting as the control centre.

### Organelles
An **organelle** is a specialised structure within a cell that performs a specific function for the cell. Common examples include:
*   **Vacuoles:** Storage sacs within the cell.
*   **Mitochondria:** The site of cellular respiration where energy is produced.

## 1. Cell Membranes
All cells are enclosed by a cell membrane, a thin layer that separates the cell's internal contents from its external environment.

*   **Function:** The cell membrane controls which substances are allowed to enter or leave the cell.
*   **Property:** The cell membrane is **selectively permeable**.
*   **Note:** If a substance is 'permeable', it means that substances, such as gases and liquids, can pass through it freely.

## 2. Cytoplasm
The cytoplasm consists of all living parts of the cell within the cell membrane, excluding the nucleus. It is composed of two main components:
1.  **The Cytosol:** A watery, jelly-like medium (usually colourless) made of $70\% - 90\%$ water. It is a mixture of substances dissolved in water, including salts, elements (e.g., sodium, potassium), and complex molecules like **proteins**.
2.  **Cell Organelles:** Specialized structures suspended within the cytosol.

### Key Organelles
*   **Mitochondria:** Sites where many chemical reactions take place.
*   **Vacuoles:** Organelles enclosed by a membrane containing mostly water with other molecules in solution. The size and number of vacuoles vary depending on the type and function of the cell.
*   **Chloroplasts:** Mentioned as part of the cell organelles found within the cytoplasm.

### Important Terminology
*   **Medium:** In Natural Sciences, a "medium" refers to the solution in which organelles, cells, or organs are grown or suspended.

## 3. The Nucleus
The nucleus acts as the control centre of the cell and consists of the following structures:
*   **Nucleolus:** A dense structure within the nucleus.
*   **Nuclear Membrane:** The boundary that encloses the nucleus.
*   **DNA Material:** The genetic material contained within the nucleus.

### Cell Biology: The Nucleus and DNA

#### 1. Key Concepts
*   **Eukaryotic Cells:** Cells that possess a nucleus containing genetic material surrounded by a membrane.
*   **Prokaryotic Cells:** Cells where genetic material (DNA) floats freely in the cytoplasm without a membrane-bound nucleus.
*   **Nucleus:** The control centre of the cell that regulates chemical reactions and processes.
*   **DNA (Deoxyribonucleic Acid):** The molecule that carries genetic information and inherited characteristics.
*   **Variation:** The differences in DNA between individuals or species.

---

#### 2. The Nucleus
The nucleus is a vital organelle found in both plant and animal cells. It is structured as follows:

*   **Nuclear Membrane:** A double membrane that encloses the DNA. It contains pores (holes) that allow substances to pass in and out.
*   **Nucleolus:** A dense, darker area located inside the nucleus.
*   **Genetic Material:** The nucleus houses DNA, which contains the instructions for **inherited (hereditary) characteristics**, such as eye colour.



![Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr9_Cells-as-the-basic-units-of-life.pdf-0004-00.png](images/Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr9_Cells-as-the-basic-units-of-life.pdf-0004-00.png)
> **[Diagram Context - Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr9_Cells-as-the-basic-units-of-life.pdf-0004-00.png]**
> This diagram illustrates the ultrastructure of an animal cell, featuring labels for the cell membrane, cytoplasm, mitochondria, vacuole, and the components of the nucleus (nucleolus, nuclear membrane, and DNA material). It serves as a foundational visual aid for Grade 10 Life Sciences, helping learners identify key organelles and understand the compartmentalized organization of eukaryotic cells.



---

#### 3. DNA and Variation
DNA is essential to all life. It encodes traits passed down through generations, including physical characteristics (skin and eye colour), allergies, and susceptibility to certain illnesses.

*   **Uniqueness:** Every organism possesses unique DNA.
*   **Variation:** Differences in DNA between individuals are known as variation.
*   **Impact of Variation:** Even slight differences in DNA can lead to significant changes within a species.
    *   *Examples:* The occurrence of albino animals or the inheritance of specific conditions like sickle cell anemia.

---

#### 4. Supplementary Notes

**TAKE NOTE: Eukaryotic vs. Prokaryotic**
The fundamental difference between these two cell types is the presence of a membrane-bound nucleus. Eukaryotic cells have a nucleus; prokaryotic cells do not.

**DID YOU KNOW?**
Identical twins originate from the same fertilised egg that splits into two. While they share the same DNA, they are not *exactly* the same because environmental factors influence their development. Non-identical twins develop from two different eggs and two different sperm.

# Mitochondria

## Introduction
Just as wood is burned to release stored potential energy to heat water, the food we eat must be broken down to release energy for our bodies to function. **Mitochondria** are the organelles responsible for this process.



![Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr9_Cells-as-the-basic-units-of-life.pdf-0005-00.png](images/Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr9_Cells-as-the-basic-units-of-life.pdf-0005-00.png)

The image provides a high-magnification view of cellular organelles, including the nucleus and membrane-bound vacuoles, to help learners identify and distinguish these structures within the cytoplasm.



## Structure
Mitochondria are organelles enclosed by a **double membrane** (consisting of an outer membrane and an inner membrane).

> **TAKE NOTE:**
> *   **Mitochondrion:** The singular form of the word.
> *   **Mitochondria:** The plural form of the word.

## Function and Cellular Activity
Mitochondria are the sites of **cellular respiration**. During this process, the mitochondrion combines molecules from food with oxygen to release energy.

Cells that are highly active require more energy and therefore contain a higher number of mitochondria compared to less active cells.

*   **Example:** A muscle cell (which requires significant energy for movement) will have more mitochondria than a bone cell.

### NEW WORDS
* **Cell wall:** A rigid outer layer that provides structural support to plant cells.
* **Cellulose:** A complex carbohydrate that makes up the cell wall.
* **Chloroplast:** An organelle found in plant cells that conducts photosynthesis.
* **Turgid:** Swollen and hard due to high fluid content (usually water).
* **Flaccid:** Soft and drooping due to a lack of water.

## Cell wall

All animal and plant cells are enclosed or surrounded by a cell membrane. However, animal cells often have an irregular shape, whereas plant cells have a much more regular, rigid shape.

Plant cells have an additional layer surrounding the cell on the outside of the cell membrane known as the **cell wall**.

### The Cell Wall
The cell wall is a protective framework that provides support and stability for the plant cell.

*   **Composition:** It is formed from various compounds, the primary one being **cellulose**.
*   **Function:** Cellulose helps maintain the shape of the plant cell, allowing the plant to remain rigid and upright, even as it grows to great heights.

> **DID YOU KNOW?**
> Other organisms, such as bacteria or fungi, also have cell walls. However, these are not the same as plant cell walls and are not made of cellulose.

---

### Chloroplasts
Chloroplasts are green organelles found in plant cells that are absent in animal cells.

*   **Function:** They contain a green pigment called **chlorophyll**. Chlorophyll absorbs radiant energy from the sun and converts it into chemical energy through the process of **photosynthesis**.
*   **Significance:** Because animal cells lack chloroplasts, they are unable to photosynthesise.

### Vacuoles
Vacuoles are large organelles in plant cells that can occupy up to $90\%$ of the cell's volume.

*   **Cell Sap:** The liquid contained within the vacuole is called cell sap.
*   **Support Mechanism:** The full vacuoles push out against the cell wall, providing structural support to the plant.
*   **Turgidity:** When the vacuoles are full, the cells are described as **turgid**. This state keeps the plant's stems and leaves upright and firm.
*   **Flaccidity:** The opposite of turgid is **flaccid**. If a plant lacks water (e.g., due to dry soil), the vacuoles lose water, causing the stems and leaves to droop.

### Vacuoles and Cell Turgidity

*   **Turgid Vacuoles:** When plant vacuoles are full of water, they exert pressure against the cell wall, making the plant rigid and upright.
*   **Flaccid Vacuoles:** When water is lost, the vacuoles become flaccid, causing the plant to droop. This process is known as **wilting**.
*   **Animal Cell Vacuoles:** Vacuoles are not always present in animal cells. When they are present, they are typically very small and have a short life-span.

# The Light Microscope: Parts and Safety

## Parts of a Light Microscope



![Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr9_Cells-as-the-basic-units-of-life.pdf-0012-00.png](images/Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr9_Cells-as-the-basic-units-of-life.pdf-0012-00.png)


| Part | Function |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Ocular lens / Eyepiece** | A cylinder containing two or more lenses held at the correct working distance to bring the object into focus. |
| **Revolving nose piece** | Holds the objectives in place so that they can rotate and be changed easily. |
| **Objective** | Magnifies the object. Usually includes three objectives: $4\times$, $10\times$, and $40\times$ magnification. |
| **Stage** | The area where the microscope slide is placed; contains clips to prevent the slide from moving. |
| **Diaphragm and condenser** | Controls the amount of light which passes through the slide. |
| **Light source / mirror** | Provides a source of light so that the object can be viewed. |
| **Frame** | A rigid structure for stability, supported by a U-shaped foot leading to the base. |
| **Coarse adjustment screw** | Used for the initial focus of the object. |
| **Fine adjustment screw** | Used for the final and clear focus of the object. |

---

## Microscope Safety Precautions

When using a microscope, follow these safety guidelines:

1.  **Carrying:** Always use two hands—one hand supports the base and the other holds the frame.
2.  **Placement:** Set the microscope down on a stable, horizontal, and clear counter.
3.  **Cleaning:** Clean lenses only with proper lens paper. Do not touch lenses with your fingers. Ensure the stage and slides are clean.
4.  **Handling Slides:** Do not use broken or cracked slides. Always handle cover slips by the edges.
5.  **Focusing:** Focus smoothly and slowly. Be careful with the objectives to avoid scratching them.
6.  **Storage:** When finished, always turn the lowest magnification objective into place before storing the microscope.

### Microscope Maintenance and Specimen Preparation

#### Microscope Care
*   Ensure the stage and slides are clean before storing the microscope.
*   Always store the microscope in a box or cover it with a dust jacket to prevent dust from settling on the lenses.

#### Preparing Specimens
To view cells under a microscope, you must prepare a **specimen** on a **slide**. A specimen is a small part, slice, or example of an organism. 

*   **Light Transmission:** For light to pass through the specimen, it must be cut into extremely thin slices (less than $0.5\text{ mm}$).
*   **Placement:** The prepared slice is placed on a glass slide.

#### Techniques for Preparing Slides
| Technique | Description | Best Used For |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Wet mount** | Specimen is placed in a liquid medium. | Living organisms and aquatic samples. |
| **Dry mount** | Specimen is placed directly on the slide without liquid. | Hair, feathers, pollen grains, or dust. |
| **Smears** | Material is spread thinly across the slide and allowed to dry. | Blood or slime. |
| **Staining** | Adding chemicals (e.g., iodine, methylene blue, crystal violet) to the specimen. | Improving colour contrast on the slide. |

> **DID YOU KNOW?**
> You can use water, brine (salt water), glycerine, or immersion oil for wet mounts.

# Practical Investigation: Preparing an Onion Cell Slide

## Required Materials
* Dropper
* Tissue paper or filter paper
* Distilled water
* Iodine solution
* Light microscope

## Instructions

### Step 1: Preparation
Prepare your microscope and slides according to the safety methods previously discussed.

### Step 2: Cutting the Onion

### Preparing and Viewing Onion Cells Under a Microscope

#### Procedure: Mounting and Focusing

*   **Step 6:** Gently lower a coverslip at an angle onto the onion cells. Hold the coverslip up with a dissection needle and gently lower the slip. This prevents air bubbles from getting trapped under the cover slip.
*   **Step 7:** Wipe off excess fluid around the edge of the coverslip with tissue paper or filter paper.
*   **Step 8:** Make sure the lowest power objective lens (the shortest lens) is in line with the eyepiece. Switch on the lamp or use the mirror to reflect the light onto your stage. Place the prepared slide onto the stage and secure it with the stage clips.
    *   

![Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr9_Cells-as-the-basic-units-of-life.pdf-0016-00.png](images/Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr9_Cells-as-the-basic-units-of-life.pdf-0016-00.png)

*   **Step 9:** While on the low power, look from the side and lower the objective lens to just above the coverslip. Then look through the eyepiece and use the fine focus to focus your image.
*   **Step 10:** Magnify your cells by swapping the objective lens to a higher powered lens. Only use the fine focus adjustment to focus clearly.
*   **Step 11:** Make careful drawings of your observations in the space provided below. Remember to label what you see. Add a heading including the specimen, the stain used, and the magnification.

---

#### Important Note
> **TAKE NOTE:** If you accidentally trap an air bubble, gently press on the middle of the coverslip to get rid of any trapped air using the dissecting needle, or drop some extra fluid right next to the edge of the coverslip.

#### New Words

#### Concept Notes
We have previously examined the basic differences between plant and animal cells. However, it is important to note that not all plant cells and not all animal cells are the same. 

Cells within an organism have different shapes and sizes because they must fulfill different functions. For example, if you look at a rose, the cells in the roots, stem, leaves, and petals do not look the same. Because these parts perform very specific functions, their cells have adapted to have different sizes and shapes to suit those roles.

# Specialised Cells

## Introduction
Cells are not all identical. Your body contains a vast number of **specialised cells**. This means that they have specific structures that allow them to perform different functions. When cells develop these specific structures to perform a particular task, we say they have **differentiated**.

### Examples of Specialisation
*   **Rose Petal Cells:** Contain pigments in their vacuoles, giving the petals their red colour.
*   **Leaf Cells:** Contain many chloroplasts for photosynthesis and have a long, rectangular shape.

### Stem Cells

**Definition:** Stem cells are unspecialised cells that have the ability to divide and develop into many different types of specialised cells.

*   **Key Characteristics:**
    *   They can divide and multiply while maintaining the ability to differentiate into other cell types.
    *   **Embryonic stem cells:** These form 4–5 days after conception (a ball of 50–150 cells). They are unique because they can become any cell type in the body (e.g., blood, nerve, muscle, or brain cells).
*   **Research and Ethics:** Scientists use stem cells for medical research. While this offers many potential benefits, it is also a subject of significant controversy and ethical debate.

---

### Microscopic and Macroscopic Organisms

Organisms are classified based on the number of cells they contain:

*   **Unicellular:** Organisms made of only a single cell. In these organisms, all life functions are performed within that one cell.
*   **Multicellular:** Organisms made of many cells (e.g., plants and animals). These organisms contain millions of cells with different specialised functions.

#### Microscopic Organisms

*   **Macroscopic organisms:** Organisms that are visible to the naked eye. They consist of many cells, ranging from a few cells working together to trillions of cells forming larger organisms.

## 2. Organisation of Cells in Macroscopic Organisms

In macroscopic organisms, cells are **specialised**, meaning they perform specific functions rather than all life processes. These cells are organised into a hierarchy of complexity:

### The Hierarchy of Biological Organisation

1.  **Cells:** The basic building blocks. Specialised cells perform specific functions.
2.  **Tissues:** A group of specialised cells that work together to perform a specific function.
    *   *Example:* Muscle cells group together to form muscle tissue.
    *   *Example:* Epithelial cells group together to form skin or line the inside of the stomach.
    *   *Example:* Nerve cells group together to form the brain and nerves.
3.  **Organs:** A group of different tissues that work together to perform a specific function.
    *   *Example:* The stomach is an organ made of muscle tissue (for contraction) and epithelial tissue (for producing mucus).
4.  **Systems (Organ Systems):** A group of organs that work closely together to perform body functions.
5.  **Organism:** The complete living entity formed by the interaction of various systems.

# Biological Organization: From Cells to Organisms

## The Hierarchy of Life
Living organisms are structured in a specific hierarchy. Each level of organization builds upon the previous one to create a functional, complex system.

The progression of biological organization is as follows:
$$\text{Cells} \rightarrow \text{Tissues} \rightarrow \text{Organs} \rightarrow \text{Organisms}$$

### Definitions
*   **Cells:** The basic structural and functional units of life (e.g., muscle cells).
*   **Tissues:** Groups of similar cells that work together to perform a specific function (e.g., the tissue lining the stomach).
*   **Organs:** Structures composed of different types of tissues working together to perform complex functions (e.g., the stomach).
*   **Organisms:** A complete living individual formed by various organ systems working together.

## Key Concepts

### 1. The Nature of Cells
*   **Definition:** Cells are the basic structural and functional units of all living organisms.
*   **Visibility:** Cells are microscopic and can only be seen under a microscope.
*   **Diversity:** Cells come in many different shapes and sizes.

### 2. Cell Structure and Components
*   **Basic Components:** Plant and animal cells contain cell membranes, cytoplasm, and a nucleus. They may also contain organelles such as mitochondria and vacuoles.
*   **Cell Membrane:** 
    *   Encloses the cell contents and separates the cell from its environment.
    *   It is **selectively permeable**, meaning it only allows certain substances to pass in and out.
*   **Cytoplasm:** 
    *   Includes the organelles and the cytosol.
    *   The **cytosol** is the jelly-like medium where many chemical reactions occur.
    *   Everything inside the cell membrane, excluding the nucleus, is considered the cytoplasm.
*   **Nucleus:** In eukaryotic cells, it is enclosed by a nuclear membrane and contains the DNA.
*   **DNA:** Contains inherited characteristics and controls cell activities. It is unique to each organism, causing variation within a species.
*   **Mitochondria:** Responsible for cellular respiration, which is the process of releasing energy from food.

### 3. Plant Cell Specifics
*   **Cell Wall:** A rigid structure surrounding the cell membrane that provides support and protection.
*   **Chloroplasts:** Contain the pigment **chlorophyll**, which is used to photosynthesise and produce glucose.
*   **Vacuoles:** Plant cells have large vacuoles used to store water and glucose, providing support to the plant. (Note: Vacuoles in animal cells are temporary or absent and much smaller).

### 4. Specialised Cells and Organisation
*   **Stem Cells:** Cells that have the ability to divide and develop into many different cell types.
*   **Specialised Cells:** Cells that perform specific functions. When they work together to perform a specific function, they form a **tissue**.
*   **Levels of Organisation:**
    *   A group of different tissues makes up an **organ**.
    *   Organs working together in groups form **systems** (or organ systems).
    *   Organ systems make up an **organism** (e.g., a human).

### 5. Microscopic vs. Macroscopic Organisms
*   **Microscopic Organisms:** Can only be seen under a microscope. This includes all single-celled organisms (e.g., bacteria) and some multicellular organisms (e.g., dust mites).
*   **Macroscopic Organisms:** Consist of many cells and can be seen with the naked eye.

## 1. Introduction to Cells
Cells are the basic unit of all living organisms. They are defined by their structure and function, and they exist in many different shapes and sizes.

*   **Microscopic organisms:** Can consist of one cell (unicellular).
*   **Macroscopic organisms:** Consist of many cells (multicellular).
*   **Observation:** Cells are typically seen under a microscope.

## 2. Classification of Organisms
Living organisms are categorized based on their cellular composition:
*   **Plants:** Contain specific structures for photosynthesis and support.
*   **Animals:** Other cell types that work together to form complex structures.

## 3. Stem Cells
Stem cells are a unique type of cell that can divide and develop into other specialized cell types.

## 4. Cell Structure and Organelles
Cells contain common structures known as **organelles**, which are enclosed by the cell contents (jelly medium).

### Key Organelles and Functions
| Organelle | Function |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Cell Wall** | Provides support (found in plants). |
| **Chloroplasts** | Used for photosynthesis. |
| **Large Vacuoles** | Used for storage and support (found in plants). |
| **DNA** | Contains inherited characteristics and accounts for variation. |

## 5. Specialized Functions
Cells are adapted for specialized functions. When cells work together, they make up tissues and organs that perform specific tasks within the organism.

## 6. Biological Processes
Cells facilitate essential life processes:
*   **Photosynthesis:** The process of converting light energy into chemical energy (primarily in plants).
*   **Respiration:** The process of releasing energy through cellular activity.

## 7. Summary of Key Concepts
*   **Jelly Medium:** The cytoplasm that encloses the organelles.
*   **Inherited Characteristics:** Genetic information stored in DNA that determines traits and variation.
*   **Adaptation:** Cells change their structure to better suit their specific function.

# Cell Structure and Function

## 1. Functional Efficiency of Cells
In Life Sciences, it is essential to understand how the **structure** of a biological component is adapted to its **function**. This is known as functional efficiency.

### Examples of Cellular Adaptation
*   **Nerve Cells:**
    *   **Structure:** Elongated (like long, thin wires) and branched.
    *   **Function:** Transmit messages throughout the body from the brain.
    *   **Adaptation:** The elongated, branched shape allows them to send messages over long distances and connect with many other cells.
*   **Red Blood Cells:**
    *   **Structure:** Small and lack a nucleus.
    *   **Function:** Carry oxygen from the lungs to the rest of the body.
    *   **Adaptation:** The small size allows them to pass through narrow capillaries. The absence of a nucleus provides more space for oxygen transport.

---

## 2. Cell Structure and Selective Permeability
The cell membrane acts as a barrier that controls what enters and leaves the cell. This is called **selective permeability**.

*   **Analogy:** Think of a gate and a security wall around a home.
    *   The gate allows entry to specific people while denying access to others.
    *   Waste products are removed from the perimeter.
    *   Resources (food/water) are allowed to enter.
    *   **Note:** Just as thieves can bypass home security, viruses and diseases can sometimes penetrate the cell's defenses to attack the organelles.

---

## 3. Cytoplasm and Protoplasm
It is a common misconception that the cytoplasm is merely a "jelly-like substance."

*   **Cytosol:** The actual jelly-like substance within the cell.
*   **Cytoplasm:** The combination of the cytosol and all cell organelles (excluding the nucleus).
*   **Protoplasm:** The total combination of the cytoplasm (cytosol + organelles) and the nucleus (containing nucleoplasm).

---

## 4. Plant Cell Components
When observing a plant cell under a microscope, several key structures can be identified:

*   **Cell Wall:** The outer, light grey, rigid structure providing support.
*   **Cell Membrane:** The inner, more folded boundary located inside the cell wall.
*   **Nucleus:** A prominent structure containing a dark nucleolus.
*   **Vacuoles:** Storage sacs often present in large numbers in plant cells.
*   **Starch Grains:** Grey-white structures used for energy storage.
*   **Mitochondria:** Grey, oval-shaped structures involved in energy production.

### Studying Micrographs
A micrograph is a photograph taken through a microscope (specifically a transmission electron microscope). It is important to distinguish between schematic diagrams, which provide an idealized view of a cell, and actual micrographs, which show the complex reality of cellular structures.

### Mitochondria
*   **Function:** Mitochondria act as the "powerhouse" of the cell, releasing energy from food. Food is considered fuel for the body, providing stored potential energy.
*   **Distribution:** Cells with high energy demands, such as cardiac (heart) muscle tissue, contain a higher frequency of mitochondria to support constant movement and activity.
*   **Mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA):** 
    *   mtDNA is inherited exclusively from the mother.
    *   It is used by scientists to trace maternal lineage.
    *   **Endosymbiotic Theory:** One scientific theory suggests that mitochondria originated from ancient bacteria that invaded eukaryotic cells and established a symbiotic relationship, eventually losing the ability to live independently.

### Activity: Evaluating Microscopic Images

This activity is designed to help learners understand how to troubleshoot common issues when using a microscope. Even without physical access to a microscope, learners can develop the skills to identify image quality problems and determine the necessary adjustments.

#### Key Concepts
*   **Focus:** The sharpness of the image.

*   **Contrast:** The difference in lighting between different areas of the specimen, which allows specific structures to be distinguished from one another.

#### Teacher's Note on Contrast
Learners often find the concept of "contrast" difficult to grasp. It is important to clarify the distinction between brightness and contrast:
*   **Brightness:** Refers to the overall level of light or darkness in the image.
*   **Contrast:** Refers to the variation in lighting between different parts of the specimen, which makes the internal structures visible.

### Why Use a Wet Mount?

Before preparing a slide, it is important to understand why a liquid medium is used. A wet mount provides the following advantages:

*   **Support:** The liquid supports the specimen, protecting delicate cells from damage.
*   **Refraction:** The liquid fills the space between the slide and the coverslip, allowing light to pass through clearly.
*   **Preservation:** The liquid prevents the specimen from drying out or being displaced from the slide.
*   **Visualization:** Using a stain (instead of water) provides contrast, making cell structures and organelles (such as cell membranes and nuclei) stand out prominently.

---

### Activity: Making a Wet Mount (Onion and Cheek Cells)

#### Safety and Risk Assessment
*   **Hygiene:** Always use clean, sterile sticks. Do not re-use or swap sticks between learners.
*   **Allergies:** Some individuals are allergic to iodine or shellfish. Do not use iodine solution if allergies are present.
*   **Chemical Safety:** Stains like methylene blue and crystal violet can be irritants. Avoid direct contact with the skin.
*   **Physical Safety:** When collecting cheek cells, scrape the inside of the cheek gently with the broad side of a toothpick or ice cream stick to avoid injury.

#### Procedure Summary
1.  **Collection:** Gently scrape the inside of the cheek to collect cells.
2.  **Mounting:** Place the cells on a slide and add a drop of water or stain.
3.  **Observation:** Place a glass coverslip over the specimen.
4.  **Staining:** If using water, cells may be difficult to see. Use methylene blue or iodine solution to provide color contrast. Note that cheek cells are significantly smaller than onion cells and appear as tiny, irregular "flakes."

### Microscopic vs. Macroscopic Organisms

It is essential to distinguish between the size of an organism and the number of cells it contains:

| Term | Definition |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Microscopic** | Organisms too small to be seen with the naked eye. |
| **Macroscopic** | Organisms that can be seen with the naked eye. |

### Revision: Cell Biology

#### 1. Definition of a Cell
A cell is the smallest unit that can live independently. It serves as the fundamental functional and structural unit of all living organisms.

#### 2. Membrane Permeability
*   **Selectively permeable:** A property of the cell membrane that allows only specific substances to pass into and out of the cell.

#### 3. Prokaryotic vs. Eukaryotic Cells
*   **Prokaryotic cells:** Lack a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles. DNA is typically loose within the cell.
*   **Eukaryotic cells:** Contain a defined nucleus and membrane-bound organelles. DNA is enclosed within the nucleus.

#### 4. The Nucleus
*   **Function:** Acts as the control center for all cellular processes.
*   **Genetic Role:** Contains DNA, which carries the inherited characteristics of the organism.

#### 5. Energy Production
*   **Mitochondrion:** The organelle responsible for cellular respiration and energy production.

#### 6. Comparison: Plant vs. Animal Cells

| Feature | Plant Cells | Animal Cells |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cell Membrane** | Present | Present |
| **Cytoplasm** | Present | Present |
| **Nucleus** | Present | Present |
| **Cell Wall** | Present (provides shape/rigidity) | Absent |
| **Vacuoles** | Large, permanent | Small, temporary (or absent) |
| **Chloroplasts** | Present | Absent |
| **Shape** | Regular/Fixed | Irregular |

#### 7. Guidelines for Biological Drawings
To ensure scientific accuracy and clarity, follow these standards when drawing biological specimens:

*   **Medium:** Use a pencil for the drawing; use a pen for labels.
*   **Proportions:** The size of the drawing must be proportional to the actual specimen.
*   **Title:** Provide a clear, descriptive title.
*   **Labeling:**
    *   Lines must end exactly at or just inside the feature being identified.
    *   Do **not** use arrows on label lines.
    *   Use a ruler for all label lines.
    *   Label lines must never cross each other.
    *   Label lines should be horizontal and aligned neatly to one side of the drawing.
*   **Text:** All text (magnification, title, labels) must be written in neat print, not cursive.

### 8. Specialised Cells and Their Functions
Specialised cells are cells that have developed specific structures to perform particular tasks within an organism.

| Cell/Tissue Type | Primary Function |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Smooth muscle tissue** | Contracts to enable movement. |
| **Nerve cell** | Receives signals and helps the body respond to stimuli. |
| **Red blood cells** | Carry oxygen throughout the body in mammals. |

### 9. Levels of Biological Organisation
Macroscopic organisms (organisms visible to the naked eye) are complex systems organised into a hierarchy. The structure follows this progression:

1.  **Specialised Cells:** Groups of cells that function together in a specific way.
2.  **Tissues:** Formed when groups of specialised cells work together.
3.  **Organs:** Formed from different tissues working together.
4.  **Organ Systems:** Made of individual organs that work together in a very particular way to sustain the organism.
