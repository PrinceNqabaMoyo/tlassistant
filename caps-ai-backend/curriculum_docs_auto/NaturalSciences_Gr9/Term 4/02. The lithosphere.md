The Earth is a system consisting of many parts. In the previous chapter, we looked at how these parts or spheres interact. In this section, we are going to look more closely at one of these spheres, namely the lithosphere.

## 2.1 What is the lithosphere?

On Earth, water, air, stone, and life interact. Let's think about the stone part of this statement. Where on Earth do we find stone? What are the different forms of stone that are found on Earth? Why is it important to know about this part of the Earth? Let's investigate these questions.

### Forms of Stone
| Form | Examples |
| :--- | :--- |
| Fine particles | Sand |
| Small fragments | Pebbles |
| Solid material | Stones |
| Large masses | Large boulders |

---

### New Words
| Term | Definition |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Geosphere** | The solid, rocky part of the Earth. |
| **Lithosphere** | The rigid outer layer of the Earth, consisting of the crust and upper mantle. |
| **Continental crust** | The relatively thick part of the Earth's crust that forms the large landmasses. |
| **Oceanic crust** | The relatively thin part of the Earth's crust which underlies the ocean basins. |
| **Crust** | The outermost layer of the Earth. |
| **Mantle** | The layer of the Earth between the crust and the core. |
| **Core** | The central part of the Earth below the mantle. |
| **Composition** | The nature of something's ingredients or constituents. |

# Activity: Investigating Stones

## Materials Needed
*   Magnifying glasses
*   Hammers
*   Paper towels
*   Samples collected (sand, pebbles, small stones/rocks, larger rocks)

## Instructions
1.  **Collection:** Collect sand, pebbles, a small stone/rock, and a larger rock. Bring these to school.
2.  **Selection:** Choose samples that look interesting and different from one another.
3.  **Variety:** Ensure you have at least four different items from different locations.
4.  **Observation:** Study the samples. If you have a magnifying glass, use it to observe the fine details of each sample.
5.  **Testing:** Wrap some samples in a paper towel and attempt to crush them with a hammer (perform this step outside under teacher supervision).

## Key Concepts
*   **Lithosphere:** The rigid outer part of the earth, consisting of the crust and upper mantle.
*   **Etymology:** The word 'lithos' comes from Ancient Greek, meaning 'stone'.
*   **Composition:** When we look at the composition of an object, we are examining what materials it is made of.

### The Lithosphere

The lithosphere is the outer layer of the Earth. It encompasses all mountains, rocks, stones, topsoil, and sand found on the planet, including those located under the sea and beneath the Earth's surface.

---

### Inside the Earth

The Earth is composed of concentric layers. 

> **TAKE NOTE:** Concentric objects are objects that share the same point as their centre.

The Earth is divided into three primary layers:
1. **The Crust:** The outermost solid layer.
2. **The Mantle:** The layer beneath the crust.
3. **The Core:** The innermost layer of the Earth.

# The Layers of the Earth

## Structure of the Earth
The Earth is composed of several distinct layers, each with unique physical properties:

*   **The Core:** Divided into two parts:
    *   **Inner Core:** Solid.
    *   **Outer Core:** Liquid.
*   **The Mantle:** Divided into two parts:
    *   **Lower Mantle**
    *   **Upper Mantle**
*   **The Crust:** The outermost layer, divided into:
    *   **Oceanic Crust:** Found under the oceans.
    *   **Continental Crust:** Forms the continents.

### Key Definitions
*   **Lithosphere:** The brittle upper part of the mantle and the crust combined.
*   **Geosphere:** The collective term for the lithosphere, mantle, and core. This is one of the four major parts of the Earth, alongside the **hydrosphere**, **atmosphere**, and **biosphere**.

### 2.2 The Rock Cycle

#### Understanding Cycles
A **cycle** is a combination of processes that occur in a specific sequence and repeat continuously. Cycles do not have a definitive end point. A common example is the **water cycle**, which describes the continuous movement of water through the biosphere (forming clouds, rain, and rivers). Similarly, the **carbon cycle** describes the movement of carbon through carbon dioxide, fossil fuels, and carbohydrates.

The **rock cycle** is a process within the **lithosphere** that describes how rocks change from one form into another over time, eventually returning to their original form.

#### Categories of Rocks
Rocks on Earth are classified into three broad categories based on where and how they are formed:

1.  **Sedimentary rocks**
2.  **Metamorphic rocks**
3.  **Igneous rocks**

***

#### New Words Glossary
*   **Cycle:** A repeating series of events.
*   **Weathering:** The breaking down of rocks.
*   **Compaction:** The process of pressing sediments together.
*   **Erosion:** The movement of weathered rock material.
*   **Deposition:** The settling of sediments in a new location.
*   **Melting:** Turning from solid to liquid due to heat.
*   **Cooling:** The process of losing heat.
*   **Solidify:** Turning from liquid to solid.
*   **Sedimentary rock:** Rock formed from accumulated layers of sediment.
*   **Metamorphic rock:** Rock changed by heat and pressure.
*   **Igneous rock:** Rock formed from cooling magma or lava.
*   **Sediment:** Small particles of rock or organic matter.
*   **Sedimentation:** The process of settling or being deposited as a sediment.
*   **Cementation:** The binding of particles together to form rock.

***

#### Take Note
*   Not all rock on Earth is recycled.
*   Thousands of tons of rock fall to Earth from space every year.

# The Rock Cycle

The rock cycle is a natural, continuous process in which rocks form, are broken down, and re-form again over long periods of time.

## Key Concepts and Definitions

*   **Weathering and Erosion:** Wind, water, heat, and cold break rocks on the Earth's surface into smaller pieces, eventually forming sand.
*   **Deposition:** Wind and water transport sand and small stones, depositing them into lakes and oceans as **sediments**.
*   **Sedimentary Rock:** Over time, layers of sediment settle at the bottom of bodies of water. The pressure from overlying layers causes the sediment to compact and solidify into sedimentary rock.
*   **Metamorphic Rock:** When sedimentary rocks are buried deep beneath the Earth's surface (often where tectonic plates meet), they are subjected to intense heat and pressure. These conditions cause chemical changes, transforming them into metamorphic rock.
*   **Magma:** If metamorphic rock is pushed even deeper into the Earth, the temperature becomes high enough to melt the rock, turning it into magma.
*   **Igneous Rock:** Magma moves toward the surface through volcanic pipes. As it cools, it solidifies to form igneous rock. If magma reaches the surface as lava, it cools quickly to form igneous rock on the surface.

---

## Important Notes

*   **Take Note:** Heat causes the expansion of rocks, while cold causes contraction.
*   **Did You Know?** A **geologist** is a scientist who studies the Earth, the rocks from which it is made, and the processes and history that have shaped it.

### The Rock Cycle

The Earth's crust is continually recycled through a process known as the **rock cycle**. Rocks of all types can move deep into the Earth, melt, and mix with magma. 

*   **Metamorphic rock:** Formed deep under the Earth's surface. It becomes exposed only when the layers of rock above it are removed by erosion.
*   **Igneous and Sedimentary rock:** These can be pushed deeper into the Earth, where they are subjected to increased pressure and temperatures, transforming them into metamorphic rock.

---

### Take Note: Magma vs. Lava
While both refer to molten rock, the terminology depends on the location:
*   **Magma:** Molten rock located beneath the Earth's surface.
*   **Lava:** Molten rock that has erupted out of a volcano onto the Earth's surface.

#### Core Concepts

**1. Weathering and Erosion**
*   **Weathering:** The physical and chemical breakdown of rocks, minerals, and soils at or near the Earth's surface by agents such as water, ice, wind, and biological organisms.
*   **Erosion:** The process by which the products of weathering (sediment) are transported from one location to another by natural forces like flowing water, wind, or glaciers.

**2. Compaction**
Compaction is a geological process where layers of sediment are squeezed together by the weight of overlying materials. As the layers accumulate, the pressure reduces the pore space between sediment grains, turning loose sediment into solid rock.

**3. Sedimentary Rock Formation**
Sedimentary rocks are formed through the processes of **deposition** (the settling of sediments), **compaction**, and **cementation**. Over long periods, these layers harden into rock.

**4. Magma and the Rock Cycle**
*   **Definition:** Magma is molten (liquid) rock located beneath the Earth's surface. When it reaches the surface, it is referred to as lava.
*   **Role in the Rock Cycle:** Magma is the primary source material for **igneous rocks**. When magma cools and solidifies (crystallizes), it forms igneous rock. If rocks are pushed deep into the Earth's crust, they may melt due to high **temperature** and **pressure**, returning to a magma state and restarting the cycle.

#### Sedimentary Rock
Sedimentary rocks are formed when layers of sediment solidify over time. 

**Key Definitions:**
*   **Sediments:** Layers of particles derived from pre-existing rock or once-living organisms (e.g., shells).
*   **Weathering:** The process where rocks on the Earth's surface break down due to expansion and contraction caused by temperature changes, wind, and water.
*   **Erosion:** The process by which larger rocks are broken down into smaller and smaller particles.

**Factors Contributing to Erosion:**
1.  **Temperature:** Changes in temperature cause rocks to expand and contract, leading to cracks. Plants may grow in these cracks, causing the rock to break apart further.
2.  **Water (Rain):** Rain wears down rocks, causing smaller pieces to break off.
3.  **Animals:** Physical movement and activity by animals can break rocks into smaller particles.

# The Formation of Sedimentary Rock

### 1. Erosion and Transportation
Wind and water act as agents of change on the Earth's surface. They transport loose, smaller particles, debris, and large stones from one location to another. 

*   **Definition:** **Erosion** is the process by which wind and water transport loose particles and debris, eventually depositing them in flood plains and in the sea.



![Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr9_Digestive-system.pdf-0010-00.png](images/Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr9_Digestive-system.pdf-0010-00.png)

*(Image: Soil erosion due to water)*

### 2. Deposition and Compaction
Once the transported material reaches bodies of water (oceans, rivers, lakes, and swamps), it settles at the bottom. 

*   **Accumulation:** The material, along with remains from living organisms, settles in layers.
*   **Compaction:** As these layers build up over time, the weight of the upper layers causes the lower layers to be compressed.
*   **Solidification:** Over long periods, these bottom layers solidify to form sedimentary rock.



![Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr9_Electric-cells-as-energy-systems.pdf-0010-01.png](images/Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr9_Electric-cells-as-energy-systems.pdf-0010-01.png)

*(Image: The formation of sedimentary rock showing sediment washing into the sea, organic material dying, and layers forming and turning into rock)*

### 3. Key Facts and Observations
*   **Distribution:** Although sedimentary rock is found in most places on Earth, it makes up only $8\%$ of the Earth's crust.
*   **Visibility:** We can observe different layers of sedimentary rock in mountains and rock formations in our daily environment.
*   **Case Study:** The Grand Canyon is a prime example where layers of sediment, solidified over millions of years, are clearly visible.

# Sedimentary Rocks

Sedimentary rocks are formed through the accumulation and compression of sediments over time. A prominent example of these layers can be seen in the sandstone formations of Table Mountain in Cape Town.

## Types of Sedimentary Rocks
There are several varieties of sedimentary rock, each with unique compositions and origins:

*   **Sandstone:** Composed of sand-sized mineral particles or rock fragments.
*   **Limestone:** Primarily made from the mineral calcium carbonate ($CaCO_3$). It is often formed from the remains of marine animal skeletons. It is widely used as building stone and in the manufacture of lime and cement.
*   **Dolomite:** A sedimentary rock composed of calcium magnesium carbonate ($CaMg(CO_3)_2$).
*   **Coal:** Formed from the solidified remains of ancient plants that accumulated at the bottom of swamps.
*   **Shale:** A fine-grained rock formed from the deposition and compaction of mud and silt, resulting in very thin, distinct layers.
*   **Conglomerate:** A rock composed of rounded fragments such as small stones, shells, and other sediment.

## Key Processes and Characteristics

### Cementation
**Cementation** is the geological process whereby sand, shells, pebbles, and other sediments are bound together by minerals to form solid sedimentary rock.

### Erosion and Formation
*   **Softness:** Sedimentary rocks are generally softer than other rock types.
*   **Erosion:** They are susceptible to erosion caused by wind, water, and ice (glaciers).
*   **Fossilization:** Sedimentary rocks are the primary location for finding fossils. When plants or animals die, they are often covered by layers of sand or sediment. Over time, this sediment turns into rock, effectively trapping and preserving the remains of the organism as a fossil.

### Sedimentary Rocks: Formation and Characteristics

Sedimentary rocks are formed through the accumulation and compression of sediment layers over long periods. These layers are often visible in rock formations and can contain fossils.

#### Examples of Sedimentary Rock Formations
*   **Dolomite Mountains:** Large-scale rock formations showing distinct layering.
*   **Fossils in Sedimentary Rock:** Evidence of ancient life preserved within the sediment layers.
*   **Limestone and Shale:** Different types of sedimentary rock often found in sequence, such as creamy-brown limestone resting on top of dark grey shale.
*   **Conglomerate:** A type of sedimentary rock characterized by visible pebbles embedded within the rock matrix.

### Metamorphic Rock

**Definition:**
Metamorphic rock makes up a large part of the Earth's crust. These rocks are formed when existing sedimentary or igneous rocks are exposed to intense heat and pressure.

**Key Characteristics:**
*   **Location:** They do not form on the Earth's surface. They form deep underground where temperatures and pressures are significantly higher.
*   **Process:** When rocks are subjected to high pressure and heat, the rock crystals are "squashed" together. This causes physical and chemical changes in the crystal structure, transforming them into metamorphic rock.
*   **Cycle:** Metamorphic rock may move even deeper into the Earth, where the extreme heat causes them to melt into **magma**. This magma can later cool to form **igneous rock**.

**Examples of Metamorphic Rocks:**
*   Slate
*   Marble
*   Soapstone
*   Quartzite

### Igneous Rock
Igneous rock is formed when magma cools down.

#### New Words
*   **Extrusive rock:** Igneous rock formed on the Earth's surface.
*   **Intrusive rock:** Igneous rock formed under the Earth's surface.

#### Take Note
"Molten" refers to liquids that are extremely hot, and whose usual form is solid (e.g., rock). However, something "melted" does not need to be hot, nor a complete liquid (e.g., butter).

#### Factors in Igneous Rock Formation
1.  **Where it is formed:**
    *   **Intrusive:** Formed under the surface.
    *   **Extrusive:** Formed on the surface.
2.  **How quickly it cools:**
    *   **Quick cooling:** Results in small crystals (fine-grained texture).
    *   **Slow cooling:** Results in larger crystals (coarse-grained texture, often visible to the naked eye).
3.  **How much gas is trapped:**
    *   Magma contains molten rock and gas under high pressure.
    *   When magma reaches the surface, gas is released.
    *   If magma cools very quickly, gas is trapped, resulting in cavities and openings in the rock.

### Volcanoes
A volcano is an opening or rupture in the surface of the Earth's crust (or another planet) which allows hot lava and volcanic ash to escape in an eruption from the magma chamber below.

# Chapter 2: The Lithosphere - Igneous Rocks

## Overview
Igneous rocks are formed through the cooling and solidification of molten rock. Common examples include basalt, granite, and pumice.

---

## 1. Basalt
Basalt is the most common type of igneous rock and constitutes a significant portion of the Earth's crust, particularly the oceanic crust.

*   **Characteristics:** It is a dark-coloured rock.
*   **Uses:** Frequently used as a building material, specifically for stone walls.
*   **Extraterrestrial Presence:** Basalt is found on the Moon and Mars.
*   **Notable Formation:** Olympus Mons, the largest known volcano in our solar system (located on Mars), was formed from basaltic lava flows.

## 2. Granite
Granite is an igneous rock characterized by its large grains.

*   **Formation:** It is formed from magma that cools and crystallises slowly beneath the Earth's surface.
*   **Uses:** Due to its durability and aesthetic appeal, it is commonly used for:
    *   Tabletops
    *   Floor tiles
    *   Paving stones

### Pumice Rock

Pumice is a specific type of **extrusive igneous rock**.

*   **Formation:** It is formed from lava emitted during volcanic explosions.
*   **Cooling Process:** The lava cools down very quickly.
*   **Structure:** Because of the rapid cooling, gas becomes trapped inside the rock. This creates a highly **porous** structure (full of holes).
*   **Unique Property:** Due to its porosity, pumice is the only rock capable of floating on water.
*   **Uses:** 
    *   Manufacturing lightweight concrete.
    *   Used as an abrasive in industrial applications and household products (e.g., as an exfoliator).

## Mathematical Context
When analyzing the composition of the crust, we often calculate the relative abundance of elements. If you are comparing the mass of a specific element ($m_e$) to the total mass of the crust ($m_t$), the percentage abundance ($A$) is calculated as:

$$A = \left( \frac{m_e}{m_t} \right) \times 100$$

**Example Calculation:**
If a sample of crustal rock has a total mass of $500\text{ kg}$ and contains $138.5\text{ kg}$ of Silicon, the percentage of Silicon is:

$$A = \left( \frac{138.5}{500} \right) \times 100 = 27.7\%$$

# Summary: The Lithosphere and the Rock Cycle

## Key Concepts

*   **Layers of the Earth:** The Earth is composed of four concentric layers:
    *   Inner core
    *   Outer core
    *   Mantle
    *   Crust
*   **The Lithosphere:** This consists of the solid, outermost part of the mantle and the crust, including the sediments covering it.
*   **The Rock Cycle:** A natural, continuous process where rocks form, are broken down, and re-form over long periods of time.
*   **Types of Rocks:** There are three primary categories of rocks:
    1.  Igneous rock
    2.  Sedimentary rock
    3.  Metamorphic rock
*   **Formation of Sedimentary Rock:**
    *   Rocks on the surface undergo weathering.
    *   Small particles, along with plant and animal material, are deposited as sediments at the bottom of lakes, oceans, and rivers.
    *   Over time, layers of sediment accumulate.
    *   Increased pressure causes compaction, resulting in the formation of hard layers of sedimentary rock.
    *   **Fossils:** These are frequently found in sedimentary rock, as organisms become incorporated into the sediment layers upon death.
*   **Formation of Igneous Rock:**
    *   **Intrusive:** Formed when hot magma deep below the surface cools slowly.
    *   **Extrusive:** Formed when magma pushes through the crust (e.g., a volcano) and cools rapidly.
*   **Formation of Metamorphic Rock:** Hot magma can heat surrounding rock, causing changes that transform other rock types into metamorphic rock.
*   **Minerals:** These are formed by different combinations of elements and compounds found within the Earth's crust.

The rock cycle is a natural, continuous process that occurs over a long period. It involves the transformation of rocks through various geological activities.

### Magma and Volcanic Activity
*   **Magma** pushes up through the Earth's crust.
*   When it erupts through a **volcano**, it cools to form rocks.
*   **Cooling rates:**
    *   If it cools **slowly**, it forms specific rock types.
    *   If it cools **rapidly**, it forms other rock types.

### Weathering and Erosion
*   Surface rocks undergo **weathering**.
*   This process breaks rocks down into small particles.
*   These particles are transported by:
    *   Water
    *   Wind
*   Particles are eventually laid down in the sea.

### Formation of Sedimentary Layers
*   Over time, particles are compacted by pressure.
*   This creates lower layers of rock.

### Rock Types and Transformations
There are 3 main types of rocks. These rocks can be reformed through heat and pressure.
*   **Heat:** Magma heats surrounding rock, causing chemical structure changes.
*   **Transformations:**
    *   Shale can become other rock types.
    *   Limestone can become other rock types.

### Lesson: Igneous Rocks and Mineral Formation

#### 1. Formation of Igneous Rocks
Igneous rocks are formed from the cooling and solidification of molten rock (magma or lava). The rate of cooling determines the texture and type of the rock:

*   **Intrusive Igneous Rocks (e.g., Granite):** These form when magma cools slowly beneath the Earth's surface. Because the cooling process is slow, large mineral crystals have time to grow, resulting in a coarse-grained texture.
*   **Extrusive Igneous Rocks (e.g., Pumice):** These form when lava cools rapidly on the Earth's surface. Rapid cooling prevents the formation of large crystals. Pumice, specifically, forms from frothy lava that cools so quickly that gas bubbles are trapped inside, creating a highly porous, lightweight rock.

#### 2. Iron and Hematite
Iron is a metallic element found in the Earth's core. When it reacts with oxygen, it forms iron oxides.

**a) Chemical Formula for Iron (III) Oxide**
The formula for iron (III) oxide is:
$$Fe_{2}O_{3}$$

**b) How Iron Ends Up in Sedimentary Rock**
Iron reaches sedimentary environments through the following process:
1.  **Weathering:** Iron-bearing minerals in igneous rocks are broken down by chemical and physical weathering.
2.  **Transportation:** The weathered iron particles are transported by water (rivers and streams) in the form of ions or small particles.
3.  **Deposition:** In specific environments (such as ancient marine basins), the iron precipitates out of the water or settles as sediment, eventually being compressed and cemented over millions of years to form sedimentary iron formations.

**c) Importance of Hematite**
Hematite is an economically important mineral because it is the primary **ore of iron**. It is the main source used in the industrial production of steel and iron products.

## Mathematical Context in Earth Science
While the study of the lithosphere is primarily geological, we often use mathematical models to represent the Earth's structure. For example, if we represent the Earth as a sphere, we use the formula for the volume of a sphere ($V$):

$$V = \frac{4}{3} \pi r^{3}$$

Where:
*   $V$ = Volume
*   $\pi \approx 3.14159$
*   $r$ = Radius of the Earth layer being measured

When calculating the density ($\rho$) of rock samples found in the lithosphere, we use the relationship between mass ($m$) and volume ($V$):

$$\rho = \frac{m}{V}$$

*   **Mass ($m$):** Measured in grams ($g$) or kilograms ($kg$).
*   **Volume ($V$):** Measured in cubic centimeters ($cm^{3}$) or cubic meters ($m^{3}$).

# The Structure of Planet Earth

Planet Earth is composed of four primary interconnected systems. These systems interact to support life and maintain the planet's physical environment.



![Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr9_Mining-of-mineral-resources.pdf-0031-00.png](images/Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr9_Mining-of-mineral-resources.pdf-0031-00.png)


## 1. The Four Spheres of Earth

*   **Geosphere:** The solid, rocky part of the Earth.
*   **Hydrosphere:** Represents all the water on Earth.
*   **Atmosphere:** Represents all the air on Earth.
*   **Biosphere:** Represents all life on Earth.

---

## 2. Composition of the Geosphere

The geosphere is divided into three main structural layers:

### A. Crust
The outermost layer of the Earth. It is divided into two types:
*   **Continental Crust:** Forms the landmasses.
*   **Oceanic Crust:** Forms the ocean floors.
*   **Lithosphere:** The brittle, rigid outer part of the Earth, formed by the crust and the uppermost part of the mantle.

### B. Mantle
The layer located beneath the crust. It is divided into:
*   **Upper Mantle**
*   **Lower Mantle**

### C. Core
The central part of the Earth, divided into two distinct regions:
*   **Inner Core:** The solid, innermost center.
*   **Outer Core:** The liquid layer surrounding the inner core.

# 21.2 The Rock Cycle

## Understanding the Rock Cycle
The rock cycle is a continuous geological process that describes how rocks change from one type to another over millions of years.



![Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr9_Mining-of-mineral-resources.pdf-0032-00.png](images/Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr9_Mining-of-mineral-resources.pdf-0032-00.png)


### Key Processes and Definitions
Based on the rock cycle diagram, the following concepts are essential for understanding how rocks transform:

1.  **Magma:** Molten (melted) rock found beneath the Earth's surface. When it flows onto the surface, it is called **lava**.
2.  **Cooling:** The process by which magma or lava loses heat and solidifies to form **Igneous rock**.
3.  **Weathering and Erosion:** The action of wind, water, and ice that breaks down existing rocks into smaller pieces called **sediments**.
4.  **Compaction and Cementation:** The process where layers of sediment are squeezed together and hardened by minerals to form **Sedimentary rock**.
5.  **Heat and Pressure:** When rocks are buried deep underground, intense heat and pressure change their physical or chemical structure, turning them into **Metamorphic rock**.
6.  **Melting:** When rocks are subjected to extreme heat, they melt back into magma, restarting the cycle.

# The Rock Cycle

The rock cycle is the natural, continuous process in which rocks form, are broken down, and re-form over long periods of time.

## The Three Types of Rocks
1. **Igneous rocks**
2. **Sedimentary rocks**
3. **Metamorphic rocks**

---

## The Stages of the Rock Cycle

1. **Magma Movement:** Molten rock from the mantle (magma) pushes up through the Earth's crust.
2. **Cooling:** Pools of magma cool down slowly in the crust to form igneous rocks (e.g., granite).
3. **Volcanic Activity:** Some magma escapes to the surface as lava.
4. **Igneous Formation:** Lava cools to form igneous rocks. The rate of cooling determines the properties of the rock.
5. **Weathering:** Rocks on the surface are weathered by heat (expansion), cold (contraction), wind, and water, breaking them into smaller particles.
6. **Erosion and Transport:** Wind and water transport these particles to floodplains and the sea.
7. **Deposition:** Particles are laid down as sediments.
8. **Compaction:** Sediments are covered by more layers. The pressure of these layers turns the lower layers into sedimentary rock (e.g., sandstone).
9. **Metamorphism:** Magma heats surrounding rock, changing its chemical structure to form metamorphic rock (e.g., slate from shale, or marble from limestone).
10. **Melting:** Some rock is pushed below the crust, where it melts and becomes magma again.

### Rock Formation Processes

#### 1. Sedimentary Rock Formation (Model Analogy)
The process of sedimentary rock formation can be modeled using layers of material (such as books) to represent the accumulation of sediment over time:
*   **Compression:** As new layers are added, the bottom layers experience increased pressure.
*   **Cementation:** Over time, the pressure causes the layers to become "squashed" and compacted.
*   **Result:** The original distinct layers become difficult to distinguish as they transform into solid sedimentary rock.

#### 2. Metamorphic Rock
Metamorphic rocks are formed through the transformation of existing rock types under intense heat and pressure.
*   **Practical Activity:** Learners should examine and handle physical samples of slate, marble, sandstone, and granite to identify their unique textures and characteristics.

#### 3. Igneous Rock: The Formation of Pumice
Igneous rocks are formed from the cooling and solidification of molten rock (magma or lava). Pumice is a specific type of igneous rock formed during explosive volcanic activity.

**The Process:**
1.  **Gas Pressure:** Magma beneath the Earth's surface contains trapped gases under high pressure.
2.  **Eruption:** When the magma breaches the Earth's crust, the pressure is released rapidly.
3.  **Explosion:** The sudden expansion of gas forces the molten rock out of the volcano with great force.
4.  **Cooling:** The ejected material cools very rapidly in the air, forming rocks that range in size from small pebbles to large boulders.

**Classroom Demonstration: The Fizzy Drink Analogy**
This experiment illustrates how gas pressure drives a volcanic eruption.

*   **Materials:** A bottle of carbonated (fizzy) drink.
*   **Procedure:** 
    *   Shake the bottle to increase internal pressure.
    *   Open the cap to allow the gas to escape suddenly.
*   **Observation:** The liquid is forced out of the bottle rapidly, mimicking the eruption of lava and gas from a volcano.
*   **Analogy:** The liquid represents the magma, and the dissolved gas represents the volcanic gases. The rapid release of pressure causes the "eruption."

*   Magma is molten rock found beneath the Earth's surface.
*   Common sedimentary materials include sand, stone, rocks, pebbles, and clay.

---

## 2. Rock Formation
Rocks are formed through various geological processes, resulting in a wide variety of combinations of rock minerals.

---

## 3. The Rock Cycle: Sedimentary to Metamorphic
*   **a)** Sedimentary rock layers are formed at the bottom of the ocean.
*   **b)** The primary processes involved are **sedimentation** and **cementation**.
*   **c)** As rocks are buried deeper, they experience higher temperatures and increased pressure. This causes the rocks to become more compact and triggers changes in their chemical compounds, eventually forming **metamorphic rock**.

---

## 4. Fossilisation
When plants or animals die, their remains are often covered by layers of sand over time. As the sand becomes compacted, it turns into sedimentary rock, trapping the fossilised remains of the organism inside.

---

## 5. Igneous Rocks
*   **Intrusive Igneous Rock (e.g., Granite):** Forms from magma cooling slowly beneath the Earth's surface, allowing large crystals to grow.
*   **Extrusive Igneous Rock (e.g., Pumice):** Forms when magma is pushed out to the surface (lava) and cools very quickly, often trapping bubbles of gas.

---

## 6. Iron and Chemical Processes
*   **a) Chemical Formula:** The formula for iron(III) oxide is $Fe_{2}O_{3}$.
*   **b) Formation:** Sedimentary hematite crystals formed as evaporating oceans left deposits of iron in sedimentary layers. This iron combined with oxygen molecules produced by photosynthesis.
*   **c) Applications:** Iron is a vital industrial material used to manufacture steel, stainless steel, and vehicles.
