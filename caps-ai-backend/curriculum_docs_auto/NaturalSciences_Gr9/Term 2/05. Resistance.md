## New Words
* **Resistance:** The opposition to the flow of electric current.
* **Resistor:** A component that limits or regulates the flow of electrical current in a circuit.
* **Electric current:** The rate of flow of electric charge.
* **Electric charge:** A physical property of matter that causes it to experience a force when placed in an electromagnetic field.
* **Delocalised:** Electrons that are not bound to a single atom or covalent bond.
* **Conductor:** A material that allows electricity to flow through it easily.

---

## 3.1 What is Resistance?

### The Analogy of Movement
Think about learners moving from an open school field into a narrow school corridor.
* **On the field:** There is a great deal of space, allowing learners to move easily and freely. This represents low resistance.
* **In the corridor:** The space is restricted, causing learners to bump into each other. This represents higher resistance.
* **At the doorway:** This is the most restricted area, allowing only a few learners through at a time. This represents the highest resistance.

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

### Electrical Resistance
The movement of learners in the analogy is similar to the movement of electrons in an electrical conductor:
1. **Electric Current:** Defined as the rate of charge flow in a closed, electric circuit.
2. **Electrons:** In an atom, electrons are arranged in the outer space around the central nucleus. In conductors, these electrons are often **delocalised**, meaning they are free to move.
3. **Resistance:** Just as the corridor restricts the movement of learners, components in a circuit restrict the flow of electrons. This opposition to the flow of current is called **electrical resistance**.

### Metallic Bonding and Electric Current

#### 1. Delocalised Electrons in Metals
In metals, electrons are not associated with a specific atom. Instead, they are able to move freely throughout the metal structure. These are known as **delocalised electrons**.

*   **Model of a metal:** We can think of a metal as a lattice of positive metal ions held in fixed positions, surrounded by a "sea" of delocalised electrons.

#### 2. Electric Current
When a metal wire is connected to a source of energy in a complete circuit, the delocalised electrons are forced to move in the same general direction toward the positive terminal of the battery.

*   **Definition:** **Electric current** is the movement of electrons per unit of time through a conductor.

> **TAKE NOTE:**
> **Electric charge** is the physical property of matter that causes it to experience a force when close to other electrically charged matter. There are two types of electric charges: positive and negative. Electrons have a negative charge, and protons have a positive charge.

#### 3. Resistance
**Resistance** is the property of an electrical circuit that opposes the passage of electrons.

*   **Unit of measurement:** The ohm.
*   **Symbol:** $\Omega$

#### 4. Conductors and Resistors
*   **Electrical Conductor:** A material that allows electric current to flow through it easily.
*   **Resistor:** An electrical component specifically designed to add electrical resistance to a circuit.

#### How Resistance Works
On a microscopic level, resistance occurs because electrons moving through a conductor collide or interact with the particles of the metal conductor. 

*   **Energy Transfer:** During these collisions, electrons transfer kinetic energy to the particles of the conductor.
*   **Heating Effect:** This energy transfer causes the resistor to heat up. 
*   **Real-world Example:** When you charge a cellphone, the charger becomes warm because the internal circuits contain resistors that generate heat as they regulate the flow of electricity.

---

#### 3.2 Uses of Resistors

Resistors are primarily used to control the amount of current flowing through a circuit.

#### Energy Transfers
Resistors are also used to facilitate useful energy transfers. Every system involves an **input energy** and an **output energy**.

*   **Input Energy:** The energy entering the system.
*   **Output Energy:** The energy provided by the system.
*   **Wasted Energy:** Some energy is inevitably lost to the surroundings (often as heat).

**Examples of Useful Energy Transfers:**
*   **Light Bulb:** Resistors are used to transfer electrical energy into light.
*   **Kettle Element:** Resistors are used to transfer electrical energy into heat.

---

#### New Vocabulary
*   **LED:** Light Emitting Diode
*   **Motor:** A device that converts electrical energy into mechanical energy.
*   **Variable resistance:** A resistor whose resistance value can be adjusted.
*   **Rheostat:** A type of variable resistor used to control current.
*   **Sankey diagram:** A visual representation of energy flow where the width of the arrows is proportional to the flow quantity.
*   **Input energy:** The energy supplied to a system.
*   **Output energy:** The energy delivered by a system.

# Activity: Useful Resistance

## Concept: The Role of Resistors
Resistors are components that resist the movement of electrons. While this may seem counter-intuitive, this resistance is often used to perform useful work by converting electrical energy into other forms, such as thermal energy (heat) or light.



![Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr9_Acids-bases-and-the-pH-value.pdf-0003-00.png](images/Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr9_Acids-bases-and-the-pH-value.pdf-0003-00.png)


### Case Study 1: The Electric Kettle
Inside an electric kettle, there is a large metal coil known as the **heating element**. 

*   **Process:** When the kettle is switched on, electrons flow through the resistor (the coil).
*   **Energy Transformation:** As electrons move through the resistor, they release energy in overcoming the resistance. This electrical energy is transferred to the water as thermal energy, causing the water to boil.

#### Questions
1. What is the input energy in this system?
2. What is the useful output energy?

### Case Study 2: The Incandescent Light Bulb
Inside a standard incandescent light bulb, there is a small coiled wire called the **filament**.

*   **Material:** The filament is made of tungsten, a metal chosen for its high resistance.
*   **Process:** When current flows through the high-resistance tungsten filament, it heats up significantly, causing it to glow brightly and emit light.

## Did You Know?
The unit of electrical resistance, the **ohm ($\Omega$)**, is named after the German physicist **Georg Simon Ohm**. 

Ohm discovered the fundamental relationship known as **Ohm's Law**, which states that the potential difference ($V$) across a conductor is directly proportional to the electric current ($I$) flowing through it, provided the temperature remains constant.

The mathematical relationship is expressed as:
$$V = I \times R$$

Where:
*   $V$ = Potential difference (measured in Volts, $V$)
*   $I$ = Current (measured in Amperes, $A$)
*   $R$ = Resistance (measured in Ohms, $\Omega$)

# Rheostats

## Definition
A **rheostat** is a type of resistor that provides **variable resistance**. Unlike a fixed resistor, a rheostat allows you to change the amount of resistance in an electric circuit.

## Applications
Rheostats are used in circuits where it is necessary to adjust the current. Common examples include:
*   Adjusting volume in sound equipment.
*   Dimmer switches for lights.
*   Controlling the speed of electric motors.

#### Practical Investigation: Graphite as a Resistor

**Instructions:**
Observe the circuit diagram provided. The circuit consists of a power source (battery), a measuring instrument (ammeter), a light bulb, and a graphite rod connected via crocodile clips.

**Questions:**

1. **Does current flow through the circuit? How do you know?**
   *   *Guidance:* Look for indicators of a closed circuit and the presence of a light bulb. If the bulb glows or the meter shows a reading, current is flowing.

2. **The crocodile clips are connected on either end of the graphite rod. Predict what you think would happen if you moved the crocodile clips closer towards the centre of the piece of graphite.**
   *   *Guidance:* Consider the relationship between the length of a conductor and its resistance. Recall that resistance ($R$) is proportional to the length ($l$) of the material: $R \propto l$.

3. **Move the crocodile clips closer towards the centre of the graphite rod. What do you observe?**
   *   *Guidance:* Observe the brightness of the bulb or the reading on the ammeter. As the length of the graphite in the circuit decreases, the resistance decreases, which should lead to an increase in current ($I$). According to Ohm's Law, $I = \frac{V}{R}$.

4. **How do you think the length of the graphite connected to the circuit has affected the current strength?**
   *   *Guidance:* Explain the inverse relationship between resistance and current. A shorter length of graphite provides less resistance, allowing more current to flow through the circuit, resulting in a brighter bulb or a higher ammeter reading.

#### Understanding Sankey Diagrams
A **Sankey diagram** is a visual tool used to represent energy transfer within a system. It illustrates the flow of energy from input to output.

*   **Input Energy:** The total energy entering the system.
*   **Useful Output Energy:** The portion of energy that performs the intended task (represented by the arrow pointing to the right).
*   **Wasted Output Energy:** The portion of energy lost, usually as heat (represented by the arrow pointing downwards).

#### Key Principles of Sankey Diagrams
1.  **Conservation of Energy:** The width of the input energy arrow must equal the sum of the widths of the useful output energy arrow and the wasted output energy arrow.
    *   Formula: $\text{Width}_{\text{input}} = \text{Width}_{\text{useful}} + \text{Width}_{\text{wasted}}$
2.  **Scale:** Sankey diagrams are drawn to scale. The width of the arrows provides a visual representation of the quantity of energy being transferred.

### Energy Efficiency and Sankey Diagrams

#### Concept: Sankey Diagrams
A Sankey diagram is a visual representation of energy flow. The width of the arrows is proportional to the quantity of energy. It illustrates how energy is converted from an input form into useful output and wasted energy (usually heat).

**Did You Know?**
Sankey diagrams are named after the Irish Captain Matthew Sankey, who first used this type of diagram in 1898 in a publication on the energy efficiency of a steam engine.

---

#### Question 1: LED Energy Analysis


![Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr9_Acids-bases-and-the-pH-value.pdf-0009-00.png](images/Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr9_Acids-bases-and-the-pH-value.pdf-0009-00.png)
> **[Diagram Context - Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr9_Acids-bases-and-the-pH-value.pdf-0009-00.png]**
> 1. A row of colorful test tubes, each containing a different colored liquid.



**a) Describe the energy transfer in an LED:**
An LED converts $100\text{ J}$ of electrical energy into $75\text{ J}$ of useful light energy and $25\text{ J}$ of wasted heat energy.

**b) Is the LED efficient or inefficient? Explain:**
The LED is relatively efficient. Efficiency is calculated as:
$$\text{Efficiency} = \frac{\text{Useful Energy Output}}{\text{Total Energy Input}} \times 100\%$$
$$\text{Efficiency} = \frac{75\text{ J}}{100\text{ J}} \times 100\% = 75\%$$
Since $75\%$ of the input energy is converted into the desired output (light), it is considered efficient.

# Investigation: Testing Electrical Conductors

## Variables
In a scientific investigation, it is essential to identify the variables to ensure a fair test:

1. **Constant Variables:** Factors that must be kept the same throughout the experiment to ensure the results are valid (e.g., the number of cells used, the length and thickness of the conductors being tested, and the light bulb used).
2. **Independent Variable:** The factor that you change intentionally (the type of material being tested as a conductor).
3. **Dependent Variable:** The factor that you measure or observe (the current reading on the ammeter).

---

## Materials and Apparatus
To conduct this investigation, you will need:
* Three $1,5\text{ V}$ cells
* Insulated conducting wires with crocodile clips
* Various conductors of different materials to test
* Ammeter
* Light bulb

---

## Method

1. Set up a circuit with the three cells, ammeter, and light bulb connected in series.
2. Test each of the conductors by adding each to the circuit individually. Use the crocodile clips to connect each conductor to the circuit.

#### Analysis and Evaluation

After completing your investigation into electrical resistance, answer the following questions based on your collected data:

**1. Which material offered the most resistance in the electric circuit? How do you know this?**
*   *Guidance:* Look for the material that resulted in the lowest current reading in your circuit. According to Ohm's Law ($V = IR$ or $R = \frac{V}{I}$), if the voltage ($V$) remains constant, a smaller current ($I$) indicates a higher resistance ($R$).

**2. Which material offered the least resistance in the electric circuit? How do you know this?**
*   *Guidance:* Look for the material that resulted in the highest current reading in your circuit. A higher current indicates that the material allows charge to flow more easily, signifying lower resistance.

**3. Are there any potential problems with the way in which this investigation was set up, or are there any ways in which you could have improved the design?**
*   *Guidance:* Consider factors such as:
    *   **Control variables:** Were the lengths and thicknesses of the materials identical?
    *   **Measurement errors:** Did the contact points of the wires affect the readings?
    *   **Equipment precision:** Could a more sensitive ammeter have provided more accurate data?
    *   **Temperature:** Did the materials heat up during the experiment, potentially changing their resistance?

#### Core Concepts
In this investigation, we explore how the physical properties of a conductor (specifically its thickness) affect the flow of current in an electrical circuit. 

*   **Independent Variable:** The variable that is changed or controlled in a scientific experiment to test the effects on the dependent variable. In this experiment, this is the **thickness of the wire**.
*   **Dependent Variable:** The variable being tested and measured in a scientific experiment. In this experiment, this is the **current (measured in Amperes, $A$)** shown on the ammeter.

---

3.  **Testing:** Insert each conductor into the circuit one at a time using the crocodile clips.

### Factors Affecting Electrical Resistance

#### 1. Length of the Resistor
The physical length of a conductor (resistor) directly influences the amount of resistance it offers to an electric circuit:
*   **Longer resistors:** Offer higher resistance.
*   **Shorter resistors:** Offer lower resistance.

**Case Study: The Incandescent Light Bulb**
Incandescent light bulbs use a tungsten filament. To fit a very long wire into a small space, the wire is coiled tightly. 



![Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr9_Chemical-reactions.pdf-0020-00.png](images/Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr9_Chemical-reactions.pdf-0020-00.png)


*   **Concept:** Electrons must travel through the entire length of this long, high-resistance wire.
*   **Discussion Point:** Why is a long, coiled wire more beneficial for a light bulb than a short, straight wire?

> **TAKE NOTE:** We do not investigate the effect of temperature on resistance in practical school investigations because it is difficult to control the temperature of a wire accurately during an experiment.

---

#### 2. Temperature of the Conductor
Temperature is a key factor that affects the resistance of a conductor.

*   **Relationship:** As the temperature of a resistor increases, its resistance increases.
*   **Scientific Explanation:** 
    *   When a conductor is hot, its atoms possess more kinetic energy and vibrate much faster.
    *   These rapid vibrations make it more difficult for electrons (the electric current) to move through the conductor.
*   **Cold Resistors:** Offer less resistance to the circuit because the atoms are vibrating less, providing a clearer path for electrons.

# Summary: Resistance

## Key Concepts

*   **Definition:** Resistance is the opposition to the flow of electric current in a circuit.
*   **Resistor:** An electrical component specifically designed to add resistance to an electrical circuit.
*   **Utility:** Resistance is often useful. For example, the filaments in light bulbs and the heating elements in toasters are designed with high resistance to generate heat or light.

## Factors Affecting Resistance

There are four primary factors that influence the amount of resistance in a conductor:

| Factor | Relationship to Resistance |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Type of Material** | Different materials have different inherent atomic structures, resulting in varying levels of resistance. |
| **Length** | Resistance is directly proportional to length. Longer resistors offer more resistance than shorter resistors. |
| **Thickness** | Resistance is inversely proportional to cross-sectional area. Thicker resistors offer less resistance than thinner resistors. |
| **Temperature** | For most conductors, resistance increases as temperature increases. Therefore, hot resistors offer more resistance than cold resistors. |

## 2. Understanding Resistance
Resistance is the opposition to the flow of electric charges through an electric circuit. All conductors offer some degree of resistance.

### Factors Affecting Resistance
The resistance offered by a conductor is influenced by the following factors:
*   **Type of material:** Different materials have different atomic structures that affect electron flow.
*   **Thickness of conductor:** Thinner wires offer more resistance than thicker wires.
*   **Length of conductor:** Longer wires offer more resistance than shorter wires.
*   **Temperature of conductor:** Generally, an increase in temperature leads to an increase in resistance.

## 3. Resistors in a Circuit
A **resistor** is a conducting material used in an electric circuit to provide specific resistance.

### Purpose of Resistors
*   **Current Control:** Resistors are used to regulate the amount of electric current flowing through a circuit.
*   **Energy Transfer:** Resistors facilitate the transfer of electrical energy into other useful forms, most commonly **heating**.

## 5. Mathematical Context
While the concept map focuses on qualitative relationships, resistance ($R$) in a circuit is quantitatively defined by Ohm's Law:

$$R = \frac{V}{I}$$

Where:
*   $R$ = Resistance measured in Ohms ($\Omega$)
*   $V$ = Potential difference (Voltage) measured in Volts ($V$)
*   $I$ = Current measured in Amperes ($A$)

When current flows through a resistor, electrical energy is converted into heat. The power ($P$) dissipated as heat can be calculated as:

$$P = I^{2}R$$

#### Key Concept: The Relationship Between Resistance and Current
When resistance in a circuit increases, the current decreases. Adding more resistance increases the opposition to the flow of charge, making it more difficult for charge to move through the circuit.

*   **Definition:** Current is the rate of flow of charge.
*   **Proportionality:** Current is **inversely proportional** to resistance.
    *   As resistance increases, current decreases.

### Data Presentation and Analysis in Electric Circuits

#### 1. Data Presentation Requirements
When presenting experimental data for electric circuits, ensure the following standards are met:

*   **Tables:**
    *   Provide a clear, descriptive heading for the table.
    *   Provide a heading for each column.
    *   Include the appropriate units of measurement within the column headings.
*   **Graphs:**
    *   Provide a descriptive heading for the graph (e.g., "Graph showing the current in an electric circuit with different types of conducting materials being tested").
    *   **X-axis:** Label with the type of material being tested.
    *   **Y-axis:** Label with the ammeter reading (current).
    *   **Graph Type:** Use a bar graph. Ensure the bars are **not touching**, as the data being represented is not continuous.

#### 2. Analysis and Evaluation
When analyzing the relationship between current and resistance in your experiment, apply the following principles:

1.  **Identifying High Resistance:** The material that produces the lowest ammeter reading (lowest current) possesses the highest resistance.
2.  **Identifying Low Resistance:** The material that produces the highest ammeter reading (highest current) possesses the lowest resistance.
3.  **Experimental Limitations:** 
    *   It is difficult to control the temperature of conductors during testing.
    *   To minimize errors caused by temperature fluctuations, record the ammeter reading immediately upon adding the test material to the circuit.

### Investigation: How does the length of a conductor affect the resistance of the conductor?

#### 1. Overview
This investigation explores the relationship between the length of a wire and its electrical resistance. This can be performed using physical apparatus or a PhET simulation.

*   **Simulation Note:** The PhET simulation defaults to a $50\text{ cm}$ length of $26\text{ SWG}$ constantan wire with a resistance of $1,585\text{ }\Omega$. Sliders allow for the adjustment of length, material, and cross-sectional area.

#### 2. Hypothesis
A suggested hypothesis for this investigation is: 
> "The longer the length of the conductor, the higher the resistance."

#### 3. Materials and Apparatus
*   **Wire:** Must be uninsulated (e.g., Nichrome or constantan, between $28$ and $32\text{ SWG}$). Note: Copper wire often has a varnish coating that must be removed.
*   **Power Source:** A $3\text{ V}$ supply (e.g., a combination of cells or a low-voltage power supply).
*   **Indicator:** An ammeter to measure current. If an ammeter is unavailable, a light bulb may be used as a qualitative indicator of current strength.

#### 4. Variables
*   **Controlled Variables:** The material of the wire and the voltage of the power source.
*   **Independent Variable:** The length of the wire.
*   **Dependent Variable:** The resistance (measured via changes in current).

#### 5. Data Analysis and Results
When plotting the results, use the following structure:
*   **x-axis:** Independent variable (Length of wire).
*   **y-axis:** Dependent variable (Ammeter reading/Current).

**Expected Trend:** As the length of the resistance wire increases, the ammeter reading (current) decreases.

**Extension Calculation:**
Learners can calculate resistance ($R$) using Ohm's Law and plot resistance versus length directly:
$$R = \frac{V}{I}$$
Where:
*   $R$ = Resistance (measured in Ohms, $\Omega$)
*   $V$ = Voltage (measured in Volts, $V$)
*   $I$ = Current (measured in Amperes, $A$)

#### 6. Conclusions
1.  There is a direct relationship between the length of the resistor and the current strength.
2.  Increasing the length of the conductor decreases the current strength.
3.  Because the current decreases as length increases, it confirms that the resistance of the conductor has increased.

### Revision: Electrical Resistance and Heating Elements

**1. Examples of heating appliances:**
*   Kettle
*   Stove top plate
*   Light bulb
*   Heaters

**2. Heating elements and energy transfer:**
*   **a)** The element has high resistance, which opposes electric current, causing the wire to heat up and glow.
*   **b)** Electrical energy is transferred to the heating element. Due to high resistance, significant energy is converted to heat, causing the element to glow and warm up (e.g., toasting bread).
*   **c)** Light emitted by the element is considered "wasted energy" because it does not contribute to the primary function of heating the food.

**3. Factors affecting resistance:**
*   Length of material
*   Type of material
*   Temperature of the resistor
*   Cross-sectional area of the resistor

**4. Length and Resistance:**
Wire B has higher resistance than Wire A because it is longer. As the length increases, electrons have a greater distance to travel, encountering more resistance.

**5. Cross-sectional Area and Resistance:**
Wire A has lower resistance than Wire B because it has a thicker diameter. A larger cross-sectional area provides more space for electrons to move, reducing resistance.

**6. Efficiency in Heating Appliances:**
*   **a)** Coiling a wire increases the surface area for heating and allows a longer resistor to fit into a smaller space, increasing stove efficiency.
*   **b)** The input energy is **Electrical energy**.
*   **c)** The output energy consists of **Light and heat**.
*   **d)** The primary purpose of a heating element is to heat food. While some energy is lost as light, the majority is converted into useful heat energy.
