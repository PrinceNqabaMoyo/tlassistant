## Introduction to Metal Reactions
In previous studies, we explored how to write and balance chemical equations. Three primary examples of metal-oxygen reactions include:

1. $\text{magnesium} + \text{oxygen} \rightarrow \text{magnesium oxide}$
2. $\text{iron} + \text{oxygen} \rightarrow \text{iron oxide}$
3. $\text{copper} + \text{oxygen} \rightarrow \text{copper oxide}$

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

### Take Note
> The metals will react similarly with the other elements in the same group as oxygen (group 16).

---

## Understanding Metal Oxides
The elements that react with oxygen in the examples above are all **metals**. 

* **Location:** Metals are found in the specific region occupied by metals on the Periodic Table.
* **Commonality:** The products of these reactions (magnesium oxide, iron oxide, copper oxide) are all classified as **metal oxides**.

### Definition: Metal Oxides
Metal oxides are compounds in which a metal is chemically combined with oxygen in a fixed ratio.

## Practical Observation
To understand these reactions, we observe them in a laboratory setting. 
* **Process:** The teacher will demonstrate the reactions while learners record observations.
* **Scientific Language:** After observing, we will represent these reactions using formal chemical reaction equations.

### ACTIVITY: Three different levels of interpretation in science

Scientists interpret chemical reactions on three distinct levels:
1.  **The macroscopic level:** Observations made using the senses (seeing, hearing, smelling, touching, tasting).
2.  **The submicroscopic level:** Visualizing or imagining the behavior of particles (atoms, molecules, ions) that cannot be seen.
3.  **The symbolic level:** Using chemical formulae and reaction equations to represent substances and changes.

#### Levels of Interpretation Table

| When we do the following: | We are operating on this level: |
| :--- | :--- |
| Observe actual reactions (see, hear, smell, touch, taste). Describe what we see in words. | Macroscopic |
| Imagine the behaviour of particles during reactions. Draw pictures of particles in substances. | Submicroscopic |
| Write chemical formulae. Write reaction equations. | Symbolic |

### 3.1 The reaction of iron with oxygen

The purpose of scientific demonstrations is to allow for **macroscopic observations** of chemical changes. These observations are then linked to the submicroscopic (particle pictures) and symbolic (equations) levels.

**New Words:**
*   **Combustion:** A chemical process in which a substance reacts rapidly with oxygen and gives off heat.

**Context:**
When steel wool (wire wool made of very fine steel threads) burns in oxygen, it is an example of iron reacting with oxygen. Since steel is an alloy made mostly of iron, this experiment allows us to observe the reaction of iron with oxygen in real-time.

### Combustion and Metal Alloys

#### Key Concept: Metal Alloys
**Take Note:** A metal alloy is a solid mixture of two or more different metal elements. Common examples include steel and brass.

---

#### Combustion Reactions
When a substance burns in air, the reaction is called a **combustion reaction**. When a substance combusts in air, it is chemically reacting with oxygen.

*   **Example 1:** Steel wool spinning creates orange sparks as the iron within the steel reacts with oxygen.
*   **Example 2:** Iron shavings produce sparks when burned in the blue flame of a Bunsen burner.

### Chemical Reactions: Magnesium and Oxygen

#### Key Concepts
*   **Group Trends:** Elements in the same group of the Periodic Table (e.g., Group 2) exhibit similar chemical properties and reactivity patterns.
*   **Reactivity:** Magnesium, like calcium, reacts with oxygen to form a metal oxide.
*   **Representations of Matter:** Chemical reactions can be understood through three levels of representation:
    *   **Macroscopic:** What we observe with the naked eye (e.g., bright light, white powder).
    *   **Submicroscopic:** The behavior of atoms and molecules (e.g., particles rearranging).
    *   **Symbolic:** The use of chemical formulas and equations to describe the reaction.

---

#### Reaction Analysis
The reaction between magnesium and oxygen is represented by the following process:



![Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr9_Acids-bases-and-the-pH-value.pdf-0005-00.png](images/Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr9_Acids-bases-and-the-pH-value.pdf-0005-00.png)


**Chemical Equation:**
The reaction can be expressed symbolically as:
$$2Mg(s) + O_2(g) \rightarrow 2MgO(s)$$

*   **Reactants:** Magnesium ($Mg$) and Oxygen ($O_2$).
*   **Product:** Magnesium oxide ($MgO$).

#### Did You Know?
*   **Historical Context:** Early camera flashes did not use electronic bulbs. Instead, they used "flash powder" containing magnesium grains.
*   **Mechanism:** When ignited, the magnesium burned extremely brightly for a very short duration, providing the necessary light for photography in dark conditions.

### 3.3 The General Reaction of Metals with Oxygen

#### New Words
*   **Word equation:** A way of representing a chemical reaction using the names of the substances involved.
*   **Picture equation:** A representation of a chemical reaction using diagrams of particles.
*   **Chemical equation:** A symbolic representation of a chemical reaction using chemical formulas.
*   **Reactants:** The starting substances in a chemical reaction.
*   **Product:** The substance(s) formed as a result of a chemical reaction.
*   **Metal oxide:** The compound formed when a metal reacts with oxygen.

---

#### The Word Equation
Word equations are used to describe reactions at a macroscopic level. The general word equation for a metal reacting with oxygen is:

$$\text{metal} + \text{oxygen} \rightarrow \text{metal oxide}$$

---

#### The Picture Equation
When we represent a chemical reaction using particle diagrams, we are operating at the submicroscopic level.

#### The Chemical Equation
We can translate the picture equation into a symbolic representation known as a chemical equation. For the reaction between magnesium and oxygen, the equation is:

$$2\text{Mg} + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{MgO}$$

**Key Concepts for Review:**
*   **Coefficients:** The numbers in front of the chemical formulas (e.g., the $2$ in $2\text{Mg}$).
*   **Subscripts:** The numbers inside a chemical formula (e.g., the $2$ in $\text{O}_2$).

### Reactions of Metals with Oxygen

Metals in the same group of the Periodic Table react with oxygen in similar ways. For example, the reaction between calcium and oxygen is represented by the following chemical equation:

$$2\text{Ca} + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{CaO}$$

#### General Formulas for Metal Oxides
A metal oxide generally follows the formula $\text{MO}$ or $\text{M}_2\text{O}$, where $\text{M}$ represents a metal atom and $\text{O}$ represents oxygen. For Group 2 metals, the general equation is:

$$2\text{M} + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{MO}$$

#### Rules for Determining Metal Oxide Formulas

| Group | General Formula |
| :--- | :--- |
| Group 1 | $\text{M}_2\text{O}$ |
| Group 2 | $\text{MO}$ |

> **Note:** While other types of metal oxides such as $\text{M}_2\text{O}_3$ and $\text{MO}_2$ exist, this discussion is limited to the two types listed above.

> **Did You Know?**
> * Group 1 metals are known as **Alkali Metals**.
> * Group 2 metals are known as **Alkaline Earth Metals**.

### Concept: Rust and Iron Oxide

**Definition:** Rust is a flaky, crusty, reddish-brown substance that forms on iron when it reacts with oxygen in the air. This process is a specific example of **corrosion**.

*   **Chemical Composition:** Rust is a mixture of different oxides of iron. A primary component is iron(III) oxide, represented by the chemical formula: $Fe_{2}O_{3}$.
*   **The Reaction:** When iron is exposed to oxygen, it reacts to form iron oxide. This reaction occurs slowly under normal conditions but happens much more quickly under wet conditions.

#### Factors Affecting Corrosion
The rate of rusting is influenced by environmental conditions:

| Condition | Effect on Rusting |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Wet conditions** | Increases the rate of reaction. |
| **Presence of salt** | Significantly increases the rate of reaction (e.g., near the ocean). |
| **Presence of acids** | Increases the rate of reaction (e.g., in laboratories or factories). |

**Corrosive Climate:** This term refers to an area where the air contains moisture mixed with substances like salt or acid, which accelerates the corrosion of metals.

# Matter and Materials: The Problem with Rust

## Corrosion and Material Choice
In environments with high moisture or salt content (corrosive climates), such as near the ocean, the choice of material is critical for durability.

*   **Iron and Steel:** These metals are prone to rusting. In coastal areas, they corrode rapidly.
*   **Wood:** Does not rust. It is often used for window frames and doors in coastal homes as a durable alternative to iron.
*   **Aluminium:** Does not rust. It is a popular choice for outdoor structures in corrosive environments.

## The Nature of Rust
*   **Definition:** Rust is a natural chemical process.
*   **Chemical Identity:** Rust is chemically known as **iron oxide**.
*   **Properties:** Iron and iron oxide are two completely different materials with distinct physical and chemical properties. While rust can sometimes be used for aesthetic purposes (such as in garden sculptures to add texture), it is generally destructive to the structural integrity of iron objects.

#### 3.5 Ways to Prevent Rust

**The Process of Rusting:**
Rust forms on the surface of iron or steel objects when the surface comes into contact with oxygen. 
1. Oxygen molecules collide with the iron atoms on the surface of the object.
2. They react to form **iron oxide** (rust).

**Key Concept:** To prevent rust, we must create a barrier that prevents oxygen molecules from reaching the iron atoms.

### Paint as a Barrier to Rust

To prevent iron atoms from reacting with oxygen molecules, a physical barrier can be placed between them. Painting an iron surface acts as this barrier, preventing oxygen molecules from colliding with the iron.



![Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr9_Acids-bases-and-the-pH-value.pdf-0012-00.png](images/Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr9_Acids-bases-and-the-pH-value.pdf-0012-00.png)


**Note:** Paint is not a permanent solution. If the paint surface is scratched or begins to peel, the underlying metal becomes exposed, allowing rust to form.

### Other Metals as Barriers to Rust

Rust is a **porous material**. This means that air and water can penetrate through a layer of rust to reach the iron underneath. Consequently, iron will continue to corrode even if it is already covered in rust, as oxygen molecules can still reach the metal surface.

#### Alternative Protection Methods
To stop or slow down rust, iron can be covered with a different metal that does not corrode easily, such as chromium. For example, bathroom taps are often "chromed" to protect the iron surface from contact with air.



![Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr9_Chemical-reactions.pdf-0012-00.png](images/Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr9_Chemical-reactions.pdf-0012-00.png)


#### Galvanising
Zinc is another metal used to protect iron. Zinc reacts with oxygen to form zinc oxide:

$$2 \text{Zn} + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow 2 \text{ZnO}$$

**Key Concept: Galvanising**
*   **Zinc Oxide (ZnO):** Unlike rust, zinc oxide is not porous. It forms a dense, protective layer that cannot be penetrated by oxygen or water.
*   **The Process:** Iron is coated with a thin layer of zinc. The zinc reacts quickly with oxygen to form this protective zinc oxide layer, which prevents further oxidation of the zinc and protects the iron underneath from contact with oxygen.

> **TAKE NOTE:** Zinc is in a different group on the Periodic Table compared to iron. This indicates that it does not react in the same way as iron does with oxygen.

### Galvanisation and Metal Protection

#### Concept: Galvanised Steel
Galvanisation is a process used to protect steel from corrosion (rusting) by coating it with a layer of zinc. 

*   **Protective Mechanism:** The zinc coating acts as a physical barrier between the underlying steel and the environment (oxygen and moisture).
*   **Damage to Coating:** If the zinc coating is scratched, the underlying steel is exposed to the air. Because zinc is more reactive than iron, it acts as a "sacrificial anode," meaning the zinc will corrode preferentially to protect the iron underneath.



![Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr9_Acids-bases-and-the-pH-value.pdf-0013-00.png](images/Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr9_Acids-bases-and-the-pH-value.pdf-0013-00.png)


#### Applications of Galvanised Steel
Galvanised iron is widely used in construction and industry due to its durability and resistance to rust. Common examples include:
*   Roofing panels and wall cladding.
*   Garden equipment (e.g., watering cans).
*   Fasteners (e.g., nuts, bolts, screws, and nails).
*   Industrial flooring.

### Enrichment: Oxidation in Everyday Life

#### Why do apples turn brown?
When an apple is cut, the iron-containing compounds within the apple flesh are exposed to oxygen in the air. This triggers an oxidation reaction.

*   **The Role of Enzymes:** This reaction is accelerated by specific enzymes present in the apple.
*   **Prevention:** To prevent browning, one can apply an acid, such as lemon juice. The acid destroys the enzymes responsible for the reaction, thereby preventing the oxidation process.

Metals react with oxygen to form metal oxides. This process is often referred to as **combustion** or **burning**.

## 2. Key Concepts

### Oxidation
When metals react with oxygen, they undergo a chemical change. The general word equation for this reaction is:
$$\text{Metal} + \text{Oxygen} \rightarrow \text{Metal Oxide}$$

### Examples of Metal-Oxygen Reactions
*   **Magnesium:** Magnesium reacts with oxygen to form **magnesium oxide**.
    $$\text{2Mg} + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow \text{2MgO}$$
*   **Iron:** Iron reacts with oxygen (and moisture) to form **iron oxide** (commonly known as rust).

## 3. Rusting
Rusting is a specific type of oxidation that occurs when iron is exposed to both oxygen and moisture.

### Prevention of Rusting
Rusting can be prevented by applying a protective coating to the metal surface to block contact with oxygen and water. Common methods include:
*   **Painting:** Creating a physical barrier between the metal and the environment.
*   **Coating:** Using other materials to cover the metal surface.

## 4. Summary Table: Metal Reactions

| Metal | Reactant | Product |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Magnesium | Oxygen | Magnesium oxide |
| Iron | Oxygen + Moisture | Iron oxide (Rust) |

## 5. Study Notes
*   **Combustion:** The process of burning a substance in oxygen.
*   **Corrosion:** The gradual destruction of materials (usually metals) by chemical reaction with their environment. Rusting is a form of corrosion.
*   **Prevention:** To stop rusting, you must prevent the iron from coming into contact with oxygen and water.

### The Chemical Equation
Chemical equations use symbols and formulas to represent reactions. 
*   **Subscripts:** The small numbers within a chemical formula (e.g., the $2$ in $\text{O}_2$).
*   **Coefficients:** The large numbers placed in front of a chemical formula to balance the equation (e.g., the $2$ in $2\text{CaO}$).

### Understanding Rust

*   **Definition:** Rust is a form of **iron oxide**.
*   **Characteristics:** It is typically a browny, red colour.
*   **Corrosion:** The term 'corrosive' is used to describe substances that cause damage to materials, such as strong acids and bases.

---

### ACTIVITY: Why is rust a problem?

1.  **Properties of Iron:** Iron is a metal; it is hard, strong, and flexible.
2.  **Common Uses:** Iron is used to make tools, locks, hinges, screws, nails, and garage doors.
3.  **The Problem with Rust:** When iron rusts, it forms a new compound. This new compound has different properties than pure iron and is significantly weaker, which can lead to the failure of objects made from iron.

---

### 8.5 Ways to prevent rust

To prevent rust from forming, you must create a barrier between the iron and oxygen so they cannot make contact.

*   **Paint as a Barrier:** Painting iron objects provides a protective layer that prevents oxygen and moisture from reaching the metal.
*   **Other Metals as Barriers:** Other metals can be used to coat iron to protect it.
    *   **Zinc:** Found in Group 12 of the Periodic Table, zinc is often used to coat iron.
    *   **Note on Damage:** If a protective coating (like paint or metal) is scratched, the steel underneath is exposed to the air and will begin to rust over time.
*   **Environmental Factors:** Protection is especially important for items like bathroom taps, as they are constantly exposed to a moist, humid environment, which accelerates the rusting process.
