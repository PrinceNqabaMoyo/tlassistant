## 7.1 What is electrical power?

Electrical power is the rate of electrical energy supply. It is the amount of energy supplied per unit of time, or more simply, how fast electrical energy is supplied.

### Measuring Power
Power is measured in **watts (W)**. We calculate power using the following formula:

$$P = \frac{E}{t}$$

Where:
* $P$ = power
* $E$ = energy (joules)
* $t$ = time (seconds)

**Definitions:**
* 1 watt is equivalent to 1 joule of energy transferred per second ($1\text{ W} = 1\text{ J/s}$).
* 1 kilowatt ($\text{kW}$) = $1000\text{ watts}$.

### Power Ratings
Different appliances use different amounts of power depending on their function. Manufacturers provide a stamp or sticker on each appliance indicating its power rating.

| Appliance | Power Rating |
| :--- | :--- |
| Electric pan | 1400 W |
| Fan | 120 W |

## New Words
* **Electrical power:** The rate at which electrical energy is transferred.
* **Watt:** The SI unit of power.
* **Rate:** A measure of how much something changes over a specific period of time.

### Energy and Change: Power Ratings and Conversions

#### Key Concepts
*   **Rate:** A rate is a ratio where one quantity is compared to time (e.g., $\text{km/h}$ compares distance in kilometers to time in hours).
*   **Power Calculation:** Power is calculated using the following formula:
    $$\text{power} = \frac{\text{energy}}{\text{time}}$$
*   **Conversion Note:** If you are given the amount of energy transferred in kilojoules ($\text{kJ}$), you must convert it to joules ($\text{J}$) to calculate power in watts ($\text{W}$).

### Exercises: Calculating Power

**a) If 180 kJ of energy is transferred to your bedroom lamp in half an hour, what is the power rating of your lamp?**

*   **Given:**
    *   Energy ($E$) = $180\text{ kJ} = 180\,000\text{ J}$
    *   Time ($t$) = $0.5\text{ hours} = 30\text{ minutes} = 1\,800\text{ seconds}$
*   **Formula:**
    $$P = \frac{E}{t}$$
*   **Calculation:**
    $$P = \frac{180\,000\text{ J}}{1\,800\text{ s}} = 100\text{ W}$$
*   **Answer:** The power rating of the lamp is $100\text{ W}$.

**b) 100 000 kJ of energy passes through a power station every minute. What is the power rating of the power station?**

*   **Given:**
    *   Energy ($E$) = $100\,000\text{ kJ} = 100\,000\,000\text{ J}$
    *   Time ($t$) = $1\text{ minute} = 60\text{ seconds}$
*   **Formula:**
    $$P = \frac{E}{t}$$
*   **Calculation:**
    $$P = \frac{100\,000\,000\text{ J}}{60\text{ s}} \approx 1\,666\,666.67\text{ W} \approx 1.67\text{ MW}$$
*   **Answer:** The power rating of the power station is approximately $1.67\text{ MW}$ (Megawatts).

### 7.2 The Cost of Energy Consumption

Eskom charges for electrical energy based on household consumption. The more electrical energy used to run household appliances, the higher the cost.

#### Key Concepts
*   **Eskom:** The primary electricity supplier in South Africa.
*   **Power Consumption:** The rate at which electrical energy is used.
*   **Tariff:** The rate at which electricity is charged.
*   **Tiered Tariff:** A pricing structure where the cost per unit increases as consumption increases.
*   **Electrical Appliance:** A device that uses electricity to perform a task.
*   **VAT:** Value Added Tax applied to the cost of electricity.
*   **Kilowatt-hour (kWh):** The standard unit used to measure electrical energy consumption.

#### Calculating Energy
To determine the energy used by an appliance, we use the relationship between power and time.

**The Power Equation:**
$$P = \frac{E}{t}$$

Where:
*   $P$ = Power (measured in Watts, W)
*   $E$ = Energy (measured in Joules, J)
*   $t$ = Time (measured in seconds, s)

**Rearranged for Energy:**
$$E = P \times t$$

### Energy Consumption and Measurement

#### Core Concepts
*   **Energy Formula:** Energy is calculated by multiplying power by time.
    $$\text{Energy} = \text{Power} \times \text{Time}$$
    *   In this formula, energy is measured in **Joules (J)**, power in **Watts (W)**, and time in **seconds (s)**.
*   **Example Calculation:**
    For a $1000\text{ W}$ microwave used for $60\text{ s}$:
    $$\text{Energy} = 1000\text{ W} \times 60\text{ s} = 60\,000\text{ J}$$

#### The Kilowatt-hour (kWh)
Because measuring energy in Joules for an entire household results in extremely large, impractical numbers, we use the **kilowatt-hour (kWh)**.
*   **Definition:** $1\text{ kWh}$ is the energy used if a $1000\text{ W}$ appliance is used for $1\text{ hour}$.
*   **Conversion:** $1\text{ kWh} = 3\,600\,000\text{ J}$.
*   **Calculation:** To find energy consumption in kWh, multiply the power rating (in kW) by the time used (in hours).

### Energy Consumption Calculations

#### Key Concept: Calculating Energy Consumption
To calculate the energy consumption in kilowatt-hours ($kWh$), use the following formula:

$$E = P \times t$$

Where:
*   $E$ = Energy consumption in kilowatt-hours ($kWh$)
*   $P$ = Power rating in kilowatts ($kW$)
*   $t$ = Time in hours ($h$)

> **TAKE NOTE:** A kilowatt-hour ($kWh$) requires that the unit of time is in hours. You must convert any time given in minutes into hours by dividing by $60$ before performing your calculations.

*   **Step 1: Convert Power to $kW$**
    $$P = \frac{3600\ W}{1000} = 3,6\ kW$$
*   **Step 2: Calculate Energy**
    $$E = 3,6\ kW \times 1\ h = 3,6\ kWh$$

*   **Step 1: Convert Power to $kW$**
    $$P = \frac{2200\ W}{1000} = 2,2\ kW$$
*   **Step 2: Convert Time to hours**
    $$t = \frac{6\ minutes}{60} = 0,1\ h$$
*   **Step 3: Calculate Energy**
    $$E = 2,2\ kW \times 0,1\ h = 0,22\ kWh$$

### Energy Consumption Calculations

**Problem:** A $120\text{ W}$ light bulb is left on for $2\text{ hours}$. A $60\text{ W}$ light bulb is left on for $3.5\text{ hours}$. Which light bulb has a higher energy consumption?

**Formula:**
Energy is calculated using the formula:
$$E = P \times t$$
Where:
*   $E$ = Energy in watt-hours ($\text{Wh}$)
*   $P$ = Power in watts ($\text{W}$)
*   $t$ = Time in hours ($\text{h}$)

**Calculations:**

1.  **For the $120\text{ W}$ bulb:**
    $$E = 120\text{ W} \times 2\text{ h} = 240\text{ Wh}$$

2.  **For the $60\text{ W}$ bulb:**
    $$E = 60\text{ W} \times 3.5\text{ h} = 210\text{ Wh}$$

**Conclusion:**
The $120\text{ W}$ light bulb has a higher energy consumption ($240\text{ Wh} > 210\text{ Wh}$).

### Understanding Electricity Tariffs

Electricity is charged based on the number of kilowatt-hours ($\text{kWh}$) used. South Africa uses a **tiered tariff system**, where the cost per unit increases as your monthly consumption increases.

| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Block 1** [$\le 50\text{ kWh}$] | $67.07$ | $2.28$ | $69.35$ |
| **Block 2** [$51 - 350\text{ kWh}$] | $83.32$ | $2.28$ | $85.60$ |
| **Block 3** [$351 - 600\text{ kWh}$] | $124.74$ | $2.28$ | $127.02$ |
| **Block 4** [$> 600\text{ kWh}$] | $137.03$ | $2.28$ | $139.31$ |

**How to use this table:**
To calculate your electricity costs, identify which block your total monthly usage falls into. 
*   *Example:* If your home uses $252\text{ kWh}$ in a month, you fall into **Block 2**. You would then use the rate of $85.60\text{ c/kWh}$ to calculate your total cost.

### Calculating Electricity Costs

#### 1. Tiered Tariff Calculation
Electricity is often charged using a tiered system. This encourages consumers to save electricity, as the cost per unit increases as consumption increases.

**Example: Calculating a monthly bill for 252 kWh**
*   **Block 1 (First 50 kWh):** $50 \times 69,35 = 3\,467,5$ cents
*   **Block 2 (Remaining units):** $(252 - 50) = 202$ units
*   **Cost for Block 2:** $202 \times 85,60 = 17\,291,2$ cents
*   **Total cost in cents:** $3\,467,5 + 17\,291,2 = 20\,758,7$ cents
*   **Conversion to Rands:** $\frac{20\,758,7}{100} = R207,59$

> **TAKE NOTE:** The tiered tariff system is used to encourage people to save electricity and use it wisely as the less you use, the less you pay per unit of electricity.

---

#### 2. Calculating the Cost of Specific Appliances
To calculate the cost of running a specific appliance, use the following formula:

$$\text{Cost} = \text{Power rating (kW)} \times \text{Time (hours)} \times \text{Unit price (c/kWh)}$$

> **TAKE NOTE:** Power ratings on appliance labels are often in Watts (W). You must convert these to kilowatts (kW) before calculating the cost by dividing by $1\,000$.

**Worked Example: Small Oven**
*   **Problem:** Calculate the cost of using a $1\,500\text{ W}$ oven for $1$ hour at a price of $71,65\text{ c/kWh}$.

| Step | Action |
| :--- | :--- |
| **1. Formula** | $\text{Cost} = \text{power rating} \times \text{time} \times \text{price}$ |
| **2. Given Values** | $\text{Power} = 1\,500\text{ W} = 1,5\text{ kW}$; $\text{Time} = 1\text{ hour}$; $\text{Price} = 71,65\text{ c/kWh}$ |
| **3. Substitution** | $\text{Cost} = 1,5\text{ kW} \times 1\text{ hour} \times 71,65\text{ c/kWh} = 107,475\text{ cents}$ |
| **4. Solution** | $\text{Cost} = R1,07$ |

### Energy Consumption and Cost

#### Understanding Fridge Energy Usage
Fridges are energy-intensive appliances. They use a **thermostat** to monitor internal temperature. When the temperature rises, the thermostat activates the **compressor** and **condenser** to cool the air. Insulation helps maintain the temperature, but frequent opening of the door increases energy demand.

#### Worked Example: Calculating Daily Cost
To calculate the cost of running an appliance, use the following formula:
$$\text{Cost} = \text{Power rating (kW)} \times \text{Time (hours)} \times \text{Price (c/kWh)}$$

**Scenario:** A fridge with a power rating of $2200\text{ W}$ running for $24$ hours at a price of $71,65\text{ c/kWh}$.

1. **Convert Power to kW:**
   $$2200\text{ W} \div 1000 = 2,2\text{ kW}$$
2. **Calculate Cost:**
   $$\text{Cost} = 2,2\text{ kW} \times 24\text{ hours} \times 71,65\text{ c/kWh}$$
   $$\text{Cost} = 3783,12\text{ cents}$$
   $$\text{Cost} = \text{R37,83 per day}$$

#### Questions

**1. Calculating Monthly Electricity Cost**
*Problem:* Imagine your family has used $320\text{ kWh}$ of electricity this month. Calculate the total cost of the $320\text{ kWh}$.

*Note:* To solve this, you must multiply the total units consumed by the specific tariff rate (cost per $\text{kWh}$) applicable to your household.
$$\text{Total Cost} = \text{Units used (kWh)} \times \text{Tariff (R/kWh)}$$

---

**2. Comparing Appliance Efficiency**
*Problem:* A potato takes approximately $1\text{ hour}$ to cook in a conventional oven, but only $12\text{ minutes}$ in a microwave. Calculate the cost of cooking the potato in each appliance and determine which option is cheaper.

*Instructional Steps:*
1. **Identify Power Ratings:** You need the power rating ($P$) in kilowatts ($\text{kW}$) for both the oven and the microwave.
2. **Calculate Energy Consumption ($E$):** Use the formula $E = P \times t$, where $t$ is the time in hours.
   *   For the oven: $t = 1\text{ hour}$
   *   For the microwave: $t = \frac{12}{60}\text{ hours} = 0.2\text{ hours}$
3. **Calculate Cost:** Multiply the energy consumed by the tariff rate.
   $$\text{Cost} = E \times \text{Tariff}$$
4. **Comparison:** Compare the two final values to identify the cheaper method.

### Mathematical Reference for Energy Calculations

To solve these problems, use the following formulas related to electrical energy consumption:

**1. Power Consumption (in Kilowatts):**
$$P(kW) = \frac{P(W)}{1000}$$

**2. Energy Used (in Kilowatt-hours):**
$$E(kWh) = P(kW) \times t(h)$$
*Where $t$ is the time in hours.*

**3. Cost Calculation:**
$$\text{Cost} = E(kWh) \times \text{Tariff Rate (R/kWh)}$$

**4. Time Calculation (for Question 4):**
$$t(h) = \frac{\text{Total Budget (R)}}{\text{Power (kW)} \times \text{Tariff Rate (R/kWh)}}$$

### Calculating the Cost of Electrical Energy

To calculate the cost of running an appliance, use the following formula:

$$\text{Cost} = \text{Power (kW)} \times \text{Time (h)} \times \text{Electricity Rate (R/kWh)}$$

#### Key Concepts: Power Ratings and Efficiency

*   **Power Ratings:** Different appliances have different power ratings, meaning they consume electricity at different rates. Appliances with higher power ratings are more expensive to run.
*   **Energy Loss:** Some appliances are inefficient. For example, an incandescent light bulb is less efficient than a fluorescent light bulb because it loses most of its energy as heat rather than producing light.
*   **Consumption:** Understanding the power rating and duration of use allows families to monitor and manage their total electricity consumption.

### Concept: The Joule Electric Car
The Joule was a significant project in South African automotive history, representing an early attempt to design and develop an electric vehicle locally. 

**Key Learning Point:**
Electric vehicles (EVs) operate by converting electrical energy stored in batteries into kinetic energy to power the motor. This is a practical application of the principle of **Energy and Change**, where energy is transferred from a chemical source (the battery) to mechanical work.

#### Exercise: Calculating Electricity Costs
**4. Calculate the cost to run a light bulb**
To calculate the cost of running a light bulb for a year, use the following formula:
$$\text{Cost} = \text{Power (kW)} \times \text{Time (hours)} \times \text{Rate (cents/kWh)}$$

*   **Time:** 5 hours/day $\times$ 365 days = $1\,825$ hours per year.
*   **Rate:** $71,65$ cents/kWh.

#### Environmental Impact and Careers
Using light bulbs that consume less electricity has a positive "knock-on" effect:
1.  **Reduced Demand:** Lower electricity usage decreases the overall demand on the national grid.
2.  **Fossil Fuel Conservation:** Since South Africa relies heavily on coal-powered power stations, lower demand means fewer fossil fuels are burnt.
3.  **Greenhouse Gas Reduction:** Burning fewer fossil fuels leads to a direct reduction in the emission of greenhouse gases, which helps mitigate climate change.

**Career Exploration:**
There are many important and rewarding careers in the electrical energy sector. Learners are encouraged to research roles such as electrical engineers, energy auditors, and renewable energy technicians.

## Key Concepts

*   **Electrical Power:** Defined as the rate of energy supply. It is measured in watts ($W$).
*   **Power Definition:** $1\text{ watt}$ of power is equal to $1\text{ joule}$ per second.
*   **Appliance Variation:** Different electrical appliances consume different amounts of power.
*   **Electrical Energy Measurement:** Energy is sold in units called kilowatt-hours ($kWh$), which serves as a measure of total energy consumption.
*   **Energy Calculation:** $1\text{ kWh}$ is defined as the energy used by a $1000\text{ W}$ appliance operating for $1\text{ hour}$.
*   **Tariff Structure:** Eskom utilizes tiered tariffs to manage demand and discourage excessive electricity consumption.
*   **Cost Calculation:** The cost of using a single appliance can be calculated using the following relationship:
    $$\text{Cost} = \text{Power rating (kW)} \times \text{Time (hours)} \times \text{Unit cost of electricity}$$

# Cost of Electrical Energy



![Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr9_Birth-life-and-the-death-of-a-star.pdf-0019-10.png](images/Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr9_Birth-life-and-the-death-of-a-star.pdf-0019-10.png)


## Core Concept
The central topic is the **Cost of Electrical Energy**. In the South African curriculum, this involves calculating how much electrical appliances cost to run based on their power consumption and the time they are used.

## Key Formulas

To calculate the cost of electricity, we use the following relationship:

### 1. Power Consumption
Power ($P$) is measured in kilowatts ($kW$). If power is given in watts ($W$), convert it by dividing by $1000$:
$$P(kW) = \frac{P(W)}{1000}$$

### 2. Energy Consumption
Energy ($E$) is measured in kilowatt-hours ($kWh$):
$$E = P(kW) \times t(h)$$
Where:
*   $E$ = Energy in $kWh$
*   $P$ = Power in $kW$
*   $t$ = Time in hours ($h$)

### 3. Total Cost
The total cost is calculated by multiplying the energy consumed by the electricity tariff (price per $kWh$):
$$\text{Total Cost} = E(kWh) \times \text{Tariff (cents/kWh or R/kWh)}$$

---

## Worked Example

**Scenario:** A heater with a power rating of $2000\,W$ is used for $3$ hours. The electricity tariff is $R2.50$ per $kWh$. Calculate the cost.

**Step 1: Convert Power to $kW$**
$$P = \frac{2000\,W}{1000} = 2\,kW$$

**Step 2: Calculate Energy ($kWh$)**
$$E = 2\,kW \times 3\,h = 6\,kWh$$

**Step 3: Calculate Cost**
$$\text{Cost} = 6\,kWh \times R2.50/kWh = R15.00$$

### Electrical Energy Consumption Exercises

#### Key Concepts
*   **Power ($P$):** Measured in Watts ($W$) or Kilowatts ($kW$). To convert Watts to Kilowatts, divide by $1000$:
    $$P(kW) = \frac{P(W)}{1000}$$
*   **Energy ($E$):** Measured in Kilowatt-hours ($kWh$), which are the "units" on an electricity bill.
    $$E = P(kW) \times \text{time}(h)$$
*   **Cost Calculation:** Total cost is calculated by multiplying the total units consumed by the rate per unit.

#### Exercise 8: Kettle Consumption
**Problem:** An electric kettle is rated at $1000\ W$. If it is used for $1\ \text{hour}$ every day, find the number of units of electrical energy it consumes for the month of August.



![Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr9_Cost-of-electrical-energy.pdf-0022-00.png](images/Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr9_Cost-of-electrical-energy.pdf-0022-00.png)


**Solution Steps:**
1. Convert power to $kW$:
   $$P = \frac{1000\ W}{1000} = 1\ kW$$
2. Determine total time for August ($31\ \text{days}$):
   $$\text{Total time} = 31\ \text{days} \times 1\ \text{hour/day} = 31\ \text{hours}$$
3. Calculate total energy ($kWh$):
   $$E = 1\ kW \times 31\ \text{h} = 31\ kWh$$

    $$P(kW) = \frac{P(W)}{1000}$$
*   **Energy ($E$):** Measured in Kilowatt-hours ($kWh$).

    $$\text{Cost} = \text{Energy}(kWh) \times \text{Unit Cost}$$

#### Exercise 10: Electric Blanket
**Problem:** A $120\ W$ electric blanket is left on for $8$ hours.

**a) How many kilowatt hours of electrical energy is used by the blanket?**
1. Convert power to $kW$:
   $$P = \frac{120\ W}{1000} = 0,12\ kW$$
2. Calculate energy:
   $$E = 0,12\ kW \times 8\ h = 0,96\ kWh$$

**b) If the unit cost of electricity is $71,65$ cents, what is the cost, in rands, of using the electric blanket for $8$ hours?**

   $$\text{Cost} = 0,96\ kWh \times 71,65\ \text{cents/kWh} = 68,784\ \text{cents}$$

### Energy Consumption and Cost Calculations

#### Key Concepts
*   **Energy ($E$):** Measured in kilowatt-hours ($kWh$).
*   **Power ($P$):** Measured in watts ($W$) or kilowatts ($kW$). To convert watts to kilowatts, divide by $1000$: $P_{(kW)} = \frac{P_{(W)}}{1000}$.

*   **Formula for Energy:** $E = P_{(kW)} \times t_{(h)}$

## ACTIVITY: Calculating the cost of energy consumption

The cost is calculated by multiplying the energy consumed (in kWh) by the unit price of electricity.

### Worked Examples
1. **Calculate cost:**
   $$\text{Cost} = 320\text{ kWh} \times 71,65\text{ cents} = 22928\text{ cents} = \text{R}229,28$$

2. **Comparing appliances ($12\text{ minutes} = 0,2\text{ hours}$):**
   * Microwave ($1360\text{ W} = 1,36\text{ kW}$): $\text{Cost} = 1,36 \times 0,2 \times 71,65 = 19,49\text{ cents} = \text{R}0,19$
   * Conventional oven ($6000\text{ W} = 6\text{ kW}$): $\text{Cost} = 6 \times 1 \times 71,65 = 429,9\text{ cents} = \text{R}4,30$
   * **Conclusion:** The microwave is cheaper to run.

3. **Comparing light bulbs ($1\text{ hour}$ usage):**
   * Incandescent ($100\text{ W} = 0,1\text{ kW}$): $\text{Cost} = 0,1 \times 1 \times 71,65 = 7,165\text{ cents} = \text{R}0,07$
   * Fluorescent ($40\text{ W} = 0,04\text{ kW}$): $\text{Cost} = 0,04 \times 1 \times 71,65 = 2,866\text{ cents} = \text{R}0,03$
   * **Conclusion:** The fluorescent light bulb is cheaper.

4. **Calculating usage time from cost:**
   * $\text{R}15 = 1500\text{ cents}$
   * $105\text{ W} = 0,105\text{ kW}$
   * $\text{Number of kWh} = \frac{1500}{71,65} = 20,94\text{ kWh}$
   * $\text{Number of hours} = \frac{20,94}{0,105} = 199,43\text{ hours}$

5. **Cost calculation:**
   * $1600\text{ W} = 1,6\text{ kW}$
   * $30\text{ minutes} = 0,5\text{ hours}$
   * $\text{Cost} = 1,6 \times 0,5 \times 71,65 = 57,32\text{ cents} = \text{R}0,57$

### Energy Consumption Calculations

#### Worked Example: Calculating Electricity Cost
To calculate the cost of electricity for an appliance, follow these steps:

**Step 1: Write down the formula**
$$\text{cost} = \text{power rating} \times \text{time} \times \text{price}$$

**Step 2: List all the given values**
*   **Power rating:** Convert Watts (W) to kilowatts (kW) by dividing by $1000$.
    *   Example: $120\text{ W} = \frac{120}{1000}\text{ kW} = 0,12\text{ kW}$
*   **Time:** The duration the appliance is used (in hours).
*   **Price:** The cost per kilowatt-hour (c/kWh).

**Step 3: Substitute the values into the formula**
$$\text{cost} = 0,12\text{ kW} \times 2\text{ hours} \times 71,65\text{ c/kWh}$$
$$\text{cost} = 17,196\text{ cents}$$

**Step 4: Write the final solution with units**
$$\text{Cost of electricity} = 17\text{ cents (R0,17)}$$

*   Time: $\frac{48,85 \text{ kWh}}{0,36 \text{ kW}} = 135,69 \text{ hours}$

**13. Comparison: LED vs CFL**
