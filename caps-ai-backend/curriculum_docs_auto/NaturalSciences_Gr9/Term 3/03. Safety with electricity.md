## 5.1 Safety Practices

### Electrical Circuits in Houses
Houses are wired using **parallel circuits**. 

* **Advantage:** If one branch of a parallel circuit breaks (e.g., one light bulb burns out), the rest of the circuit continues to function. This allows you to operate different lights and plugs independently.
* **Resistance:** Unlike series circuits, adding resistors (appliances) in a parallel circuit does not increase the total resistance of the circuit, meaning the current does not decrease as more appliances are added.

### The Danger of Overloading
While parallel circuits are efficient, they are susceptible to **overloading**.
* **Definition:** Overloading occurs when too many branches are added to a circuit.
* **Consequence:** Excessive current flows through the wires, generating too much heat. This is a significant fire hazard that can cause extensive damage to a building.

## New Words
* **Armature:** A rotating coil or core of a dynamo or motor.
* **Reset:** To set a device back to its original state.
* **Excess:** An amount of something that is more than necessary or permitted.
* **Fuse:** A safety device consisting of a strip of wire that melts and breaks an electric circuit if the current exceeds a safe level.
* **Circuit breaker:** An automatic device for stopping the flow of current in an electric circuit as a safety measure.
* **Earthing:** Connecting an electrical device to the ground to prevent electric shock.
* **Earth leakage:** A safety device that detects small currents leaking to earth and cuts off the power.
* **Short circuit:** An electrical circuit that allows a current to travel along a path with very low or no electrical impedance, often resulting in excessive current flow.

# Earthing

## Concept: What is Earthing?
Earthing is a critical safety feature used in electrical appliances to prevent electric shocks. It involves connecting the metal casing of an appliance to the ground (earth) via a dedicated wire.

## The Three-Wire System
Electrical appliances, such as a washing machine, typically use a three-wire system to function and remain safe:

| Wire Type | Colour Code | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Live Wire** | Brown | Provides the potential difference needed for the motor to turn. |
| **Neutral Wire** | Blue | Completes the circuit for the motor. |
| **Earth Wire** | Green and Yellow striped | Provides a safety path to the ground in case of a fault. |

*Note: In some instances, the earth wire may be solid green or solid yellow.*

## How Earthing Works
1. **Normal Operation:** The earth wire usually carries no current. The live and neutral wires provide the necessary potential difference to operate the appliance motor.
2. **Fault Condition:** If an internal fault occurs (e.g., the live wire becomes exposed and touches the metal casing), the casing becomes "live."
3. **Safety Mechanism:** Because the earth wire is connected to the metal casing and has a **very low resistance**, it provides an easy path for the current to flow directly into the ground.
4. **Short Circuit:** This connection creates a **short circuit**. The surge of current typically causes the appliance to stop working (or trips a circuit breaker), preventing the electricity from passing through a person who might touch the casing.

## Importance of Earthing
Without an earth wire, the metal casing of an appliance would become part of the electrical circuit during a fault. If a person were to touch the casing, their body would provide a path to the ground, resulting in a dangerous or fatal electric shock. Earthing ensures that the current is diverted away from the user.

### Short Circuits and Electrical Safety

#### Understanding Short Circuits
A short circuit is an unintended electrical pathway that occurs by mistake. 
*   **Mechanism:** An extra electrical pathway is created with very low resistance.
*   **Consequence:** Because resistance is low, the current increases significantly.
*   **Dangers:** The increased current can cause appliances to overheat, potentially leading to fires.
*   **Safety Measures:** Various safety devices are used in electrical systems to interrupt the flow of current if a short circuit occurs.

#### Experimental Procedure

1. **Initial Observation:** Close the switch and observe the state of the light bulb and the steel wool.
2. **Measurement:** Record the reading on the ammeter. The ammeter measures the current ($I$) flowing through the circuit in Amperes ($A$).
3. **Adjustment:** Open the switch to break the circuit.
4. **Variable Resistance:** Adjust the variable resistor to its lowest resistance setting.
5. **Final Observation:** Close the switch again and observe the changes in the circuit.

---

#### Key Concepts

*   **Ammeter:** An instrument used to measure the electric current in a circuit. It is connected in series.
*   **Variable Resistor (Rheostat):** A component used to vary the resistance in a circuit, which in turn changes the amount of current flowing through the circuit according to Ohm's Law:
    $$V = I \cdot R$$
    Where:
    *   $V$ = Voltage (measured in Volts, $V$)
    *   $I$ = Current (measured in Amperes, $A$)
    *   $R$ = Resistance (measured in Ohms, $\Omega$)
*   **Heating Effect:** When current flows through a conductor (like steel wool), electrical energy is converted into heat energy. If the current is high enough, the conductor may glow or melt.

### Circuit Analysis and Safety Components

#### Review Questions
**2. Why is the light bulb included in the circuit?**
The light bulb acts as a load in the circuit. It provides visual evidence that current is flowing and allows for the observation of changes in brightness, which corresponds to changes in the amount of current passing through the circuit.

**3. When you decreased the resistance, what happened to the current? In other words, what happened to the reading on the ammeter?**
According to Ohm's Law, $I = \frac{V}{R}$. If the voltage ($V$) remains constant and the resistance ($R$) is decreased, the current ($I$) must increase. Therefore, the reading on the ammeter would increase.

**4. What do you think happens to the electric current when the steel wool has burnt? Explain your answer.**
When the steel wool burns, the circuit is broken (an open circuit). As a result, the electric current drops to zero because there is no longer a continuous conducting path for the electrons to flow through.

---

#### Understanding Fuses
A fuse is a safety device designed to protect electrical circuits from damage caused by excessive current.

*   **Function:** A fuse contains a thin wire that melts when the current flowing through it exceeds a specific, predetermined limit.
*   **Mechanism:** When the wire melts, it creates a gap in the circuit, effectively disconnecting the power source from the appliance. This stops the flow of electricity, preventing overheating, fire, or damage to components.
*   **Rating:** Fuses are stamped with a maximum current rating (measured in Amperes). For example, a $5\text{ A}$ fuse is designed to melt if the current exceeds $5\text{ amperes}$.

| Feature | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Purpose** | Circuit protection against overloads and short circuits. |
| **Operation** | The internal wire melts when current exceeds the rated limit. |
| **Result of Activation** | The circuit becomes "open," stopping the flow of current. |
| **Common Application** | Motor vehicles (battery systems) and household appliances. |

**Example:**
If a device is protected by a $5\text{ A}$ fuse, the relationship between the current ($I$) and the fuse limit is:
*   If $I \le 5\text{ A}$: The fuse remains intact; the circuit functions normally.
*   If $I > 5\text{ A}$: The fuse wire melts; the circuit is broken to prevent damage.

### Circuit Breakers
Circuit breakers are essential safety devices used in modern homes and buildings to prevent fires and hazards caused by electrical wiring faults or equipment failures.

#### How they work:
*   **Function:** A circuit breaker acts similarly to a switch. If an electrical current surge occurs, the breaker automatically "trips" or breaks the circuit to stop the flow of electricity.
*   **Advantage over fuses:** Unlike a fuse, which melts and must be discarded after a single use, a circuit breaker can be reset. 
*   **Location:** You can typically find these switches on the main circuit or distribution board in your home or school.

# Circuit Breakers: Safety with Electricity

## 1. Concept: Electromagnets
An electromagnet is a type of magnet that forms when an electric current flows around a metal bar. 
*   **Key Principle:** The strength of the magnet is directly proportional to the amount of electric current flowing through it. 
*   **Relationship:** As current increases, the magnetic force increases.

## 2. Structure of a Circuit Breaker
A basic circuit breaker is a safety device consisting of a switch connected to an electromagnet. Its primary function is to automatically shut off electricity when current levels become unsafe.



![Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr9_Birth-life-and-the-death-of-a-star.pdf-0007-00.png](images/Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr9_Birth-life-and-the-death-of-a-star.pdf-0007-00.png)


### Components:
*   **Reset Button:** Used to manually reconnect the contacts after a fault is cleared.
*   **Iron Catch:** A lever held in place to keep the circuit closed.
*   **Electromagnet:** A coil that becomes magnetic when current flows through it.
*   **Moving/Stationary Contacts:** The points where the electrical circuit is connected or broken.
*   **Spring:** Provides the mechanical force to pull the contacts apart when the catch is released.

## 3. How a Circuit Breaker Functions

### Normal Operation
1.  The switch is in the "on" position.
2.  Current flows from the input, through the moving contact, across to the stationary contact.
3.  The current then flows through the electromagnet and out to the rest of the circuit.
4.  The iron catch holds the moving contact in place, keeping the circuit complete.

### Operation During a Fault (Overload)
1.  If the current increases to unsafe levels, the electromagnet becomes significantly stronger.
2.  The magnetic force becomes strong enough to pull the iron catch lever.
3.  The iron catch releases the moving contact.
4.  The spring pulls the contacts apart, breaking the circuit and shutting off the electricity.

Once the electrical fault has been identified and fixed, the circuit is safe to reconnect. The **reset button** is pushed to force the contacts back together, re-engaging the iron catch and restoring the flow of electricity.

# Earth Leakage and Electrical Safety

## 1. Principles of Electric Charge
Electric charge naturally moves from areas of high potential energy to areas of lower potential energy. A common natural example of this is lightning, where excess electrons from clouds move to the ground, transferring a large amount of energy in the process.

## 2. The Earth Leakage Circuit Breaker
In households and businesses, electrical safety is managed through the distribution board. The **earth leakage circuit breaker** is a critical safety device installed within this board.

*   **Function:** It acts as a safety switch that can automatically cut off the electricity supply to the house.
*   **Detection:** It monitors the flow of current. If it detects that current is moving through the earth wire (indicating a short circuit or a fault, such as in a faulty appliance like a washing machine), it immediately shuts down the power to prevent accidents.

## 3. Lightning Protection
Lightning poses a significant risk to electrical circuits. In regions prone to frequent lightning strikes, a **lightning spike** (or lightning rod) is used as a protective measure.

*   **Structure:** It consists of a metal pole installed on the roof of a building.
*   **Operation:** The pole is connected to the house with one end buried deep in the ground, providing a safe path for the electrical discharge to travel to the earth rather than through the building's wiring.

### The Function of the Three Wires
For a complete electrical circuit, wires must both enter and leave the house.

*   **Live wire:** The wire that carries current into the home.
*   **Neutral wire:** The wire that carries current away from the home. It is earthed as it leaves the house.
*   **Earth wire:** This wire has very low resistance and is connected through the plug socket to the earth cable of the house, which leads into the ground. 

**Safety Function:**
If an electrical appliance becomes charged due to an electrical fault, the current can discharge through the earth wire and the earth cable into the ground. This safety mechanism prevents people from receiving an electric shock from a faulty appliance.

# Electrical Connections and Wiring

## Connection to the National Grid
Houses and other buildings are connected to the national electricity grid through two primary wires:
*   **Live wire:** Enters the house to provide electrical power.
*   **Neutral wire:** Leaves the house to complete the circuit.

### Important Note: The Earth Connection
The neutral wire is earthed as it leaves the house. This is a safety measure designed to protect the national grid from lightning strikes. If lightning strikes the overhead transmission lines, the current is directed into the ground rather than traveling into the house and burning up the electrical network.

### Electrical Wiring: 3-Pin Plug Connections

#### Review Questions
1. Which pin is the green-yellow cable connected to?
2. Which pin is the blue neutral wire attached to?
3. Which pin is the brown live wire always attached to?

---

#### Instructions for Wiring a 3-Pin Plug

Follow these steps carefully to wire a plug:

1. **Prepare the cord:** Cut about $2\text{ cm}$ of the white outer insulation off the electrical cord to expose the three internal wires. Be careful not to slice into the wires themselves.
2. **Expose the copper:** Cut away about $0.5\text{ cm}$ of insulation from each of the three smaller wires to expose the copper strands.
3. **Twist:** Gently twist the copper wires with your fingers to ensure the strands are tight.
4. **Open:** Open the plug cover.
5. **Loosen:** Unscrew the small screws on the three metal pins.
6. **Connect:** Insert the copper wires into the corresponding metal pins:
    * **Green and yellow wire:** Connect to the top pin (labelled 'E' for earth).
    * **Blue wire:** Connect to the pin on the right (labelled 'N' for neutral).
    * **Brown wire:** Connect to the pin on the left (labelled 'L' for live).
7. **Secure:** Tighten each of the small screws to trap the wires firmly in place.
8. **Close:** Replace the plug cover.

# Activity: Wiring a House

## Objective
Design a functional electrical circuit for a doll's house that includes independent lighting for multiple rooms, a main control switch, and safety protection.

## Requirements
The doll's house consists of the following rooms:
*   2 Bedrooms
*   1 Bathroom
*   1 Lounge
*   1 Kitchen

## Design Specifications
Your circuit design must adhere to the following technical requirements:
1.  **Independent Control:** Each light bulb must be able to be switched on and off individually without affecting the other lights in the house. This implies a **parallel circuit** configuration.
2.  **Main Control:** A single mains switch must be included, located in the kitchen.
3.  **Safety:** A fuse must be integrated into the circuit to prevent electrical overload.
4.  **Documentation:** The final design must include clear labels for every room.

## Instructions
1.  **Floor Plan:** Draw the layout of the doll's house showing the 2 bedrooms, bathroom, lounge, and kitchen.
2.  **Circuit Diagram:** Draw the wiring system onto your floor plan. Ensure you use standard circuit symbols for:
    *   Cells/Batteries (Power source)
    *   Switches
    *   Light bulbs
    *   Fuses
3.  **Drafting:** Practice your design on rough paper before finalizing it in your workbook.

# 5.2 Illegal connections

### NEW WORDS
*   **Illegal:** Forbidden by law.
*   **Provision:** The action of providing or supplying something for use.

An illegal electricity connection is made when a person attaches their home's electrical circuit to the national grid without a meter. This is done without the consent or knowledge of Eskom. Eskom cannot monitor the electricity consumption, and consequently, the electricity is being stolen as these consumers do not pay for it.

Some individuals generate income by supplying illegal connections, while others resort to them due to a lack of legal access to the grid. Additionally, some individuals with access to legal electricity choose illegal methods to avoid payment. These connections are classified as energy theft and pose significant safety risks, including fire hazards and the danger of electrocution.

#### Doornbach informal settlement celebrates electricity provision
*WestCapeNews, July 2012*

There was much celebration in Doornbach, an informal settlement just outside of Cape Town, when the City switched on about 200 new electrical connections over the period of a few months in 2012. ### Case Study: Electricity Provision in Doornbach

#### Background
A fifty-two-year-old resident of Doornbach shared her experience regarding the transition from illegal electricity connections to formal municipal service. Having lived in the settlement for 18 years, she previously received no municipal services because the residents had settled on privately owned land. According to National legislation, the City was unable to provide services to private property.

In May 2011, the City of Cape Town purchased the land, allowing them to begin the process of providing legal electricity to the households. This development allowed residents to use household appliances, such as electric irons and refrigerators.

#### Challenges and Social Dynamics
*   **Illegal Infrastructure:** Residents previously relied on makeshift poles and illegal wiring.
*   **Community Action:** As a symbolic gesture, residents removed the illegal wires. Some youth climbed the dangerous makeshift poles to collect wire to sell to scrap yards.
*   **Illegal Trade:** Despite the new legal connections, some residents in neighbouring formal housing continued to profit by selling and supplying electricity illegally to Doornbach.
*   **Safety and Security:** The City installed street lighting in Doornbach, which is expected to help reduce the local crime rate.

### SUMMARY: Key Concepts

*   **Safety Devices:** Electricity can be dangerous; therefore, safety devices such as fuses and circuit breakers are required to reduce risk.
*   **Fuses:** A safety device containing a wire with very low resistance. It is designed to melt when it experiences a current that is too high. This breaks the circuit, protecting the appliance and preventing potential fire hazards.
*   **Circuit Breakers:** Similar to a fuse, but functions as a switch that breaks the circuit in response to an electrical fault or overload. Unlike a fuse, it can be reset.
*   **Earthing:** Many electrical appliances with metal casings have an earth wire attached to prevent electric shocks in the event of a short circuit.
*   **Three-pin Plugs:** These consist of three specific wires:
    *   **Brown wire:** Live wire.
    *   **Blue wire:** Neutral wire.
    *   **Green and yellow striped wire:** Earth wire.
*   **Earth Wire Function:** The earth wire has very low resistance and is connected through the plug socket to the house's earth leakage system and into the ground.
*   **Plug Safety:** A plug must be connected correctly to ensure it is safe to use.
*   **Illegal Connections:** Illegal electricity connections are dangerous and constitute a crime (theft of electricity).

## 1. Illegal Connections
*   **Definition:** Illegal connections are unauthorized links to the electrical grid.
*   **Consequence:** They are considered **energy theft** and are **very dangerous**.

## 2. Safety Practices and Devices
To ensure safety when using electricity, we employ specific practices and devices to manage the load on the mains circuit.

### Overload on Mains Circuit
*   **Cause:** Occurs due to **many parallel connections** in a circuit.
*   **Prevention:** Use of safety devices such as:
    *   **Circuit breakers**
    *   **Fuses**

## 3. The 3-Pin Plug and Earthing
The 3-pin plug is a standard safety feature for many appliances.

### Components and Functionality
*   **Earth Leakage System:** A critical safety component connected to the appliance.
*   **Resistance:** The earth connection provides a path of **almost zero resistance**.
*   **Safety Mechanism:**
    *   If a fault occurs, the casing of the appliance becomes **charged**.
    *   The earth connection **allows** the electrical discharge to flow into the **ground**.
    *   This prevents electric shock by ensuring the current follows the path of least resistance away from the user.

*   *Guidance:* A short circuit occurs when a low-resistance path is created, causing an excessive amount of current to flow. This can lead to overheating, melting of insulation, and potential electrical fires.

**8. What is the colour of the live wire in an electrical cable? [1 mark]**
*   *Answer:* Brown.

**9. Write down one safety precaution that should be followed when wiring a 3-pin plug. [1 mark]**
*   *Guidance:* Ensure the wires are securely fastened to the correct terminals, ensure no loose strands of wire are touching other terminals, or ensure the cable grip is tightened to hold the outer insulation firmly.

**10. What is the purpose of the green and yellow wire in an electrical cable? [2 marks]**
*   *Answer:* This is the **earth wire**. Its purpose is to provide a low-resistance path for current to flow to the ground in the event of a fault, preventing the metal casing of an appliance from becoming live and causing an electric shock.

### Colour Coding for Wires
When wiring a 3-pin plug, it is essential to use the correct colour-coded wires for safety:

| Wire | Colour |
| :--- | :--- |
| Neutral wire | Blue |
| Earth wire | Green-and-yellow striped |
| Live wire | Brown |

### Safety and Connection Guidelines
*   **Stripping:** Wires must be stripped carefully. If the copper core is cut or damaged, it creates an unsafe connection.
*   **Earth Wire:** Always connected to the uppermost pin.
*   **Neutral Wire:** When viewed from the underneath of the plug, this is attached to the pin on the right.
*   **Live Wire:** When viewed from the underneath of the plug, this is attached to the pin on the left.
*   **General Safety:** Always ensure the appliance is disconnected from the power source and work on a dry surface.

# Revision: Electrical Safety and Circuits

## 1. Fuses and Circuit Protection
*   **Definition of a Fuse:** A fuse is a low-resistance wire designed to melt if the current exceeds a specific limit. When it melts, it breaks the circuit, stopping the flow of electricity and preventing fires or other safety hazards.
*   **Fuse Ratings:** A fuse must be rated higher than the normal operating current of the device. For example, if a device draws $8\text{ A}$, a $3\text{ A}$ fuse would burn out immediately, preventing the device from working. A fuse with a rating higher than $8\text{ A}$ is required.
*   **Fuses vs. Circuit Breakers:**
    *   **Fuse:** Destroyed when it "blows" and must be replaced.
    *   **Circuit Breaker:** Can be reset after tripping; it does not need to be replaced.
*   **Safety Warning:** If a fuse blows, the underlying electrical problem must be fixed before replacing it. If the fault remains, the new fuse will also blow, and there is a risk of electric shock.

## 2. Short Circuits
*   **Definition:** A short circuit is an alternative pathway in a circuit with extremely low resistance.
*   **Consequences:** Because resistance is very low, the current increases significantly, causing an overload.
*   **Dangers:** The high current creates a risk of electrocution. If a person touches the short circuit or becomes part of the path, they can be severely injured or killed.

## 3. Electrical Wiring and Plugs
*   **Live Wire:** The brown wire.
*   **Neutral Wire:** The blue wire. It is attached to the pin on the right when viewing the plug from the bottom.

#### Circuit Components
*   **Cell:** The power source providing electromotive force (EMF).
*   **Light Bulbs:** Components that convert electrical energy into light and heat.
*   **Resistor:** A component that opposes the flow of electric current.

#### Circuit Configuration
In this specific circuit:
1.  The top bulb is connected in **parallel** to the branch containing the resistor and the second bulb.
2.  The resistor and the second bulb are connected in **series** with each other.

#### Key Electrical Principles
When analyzing such circuits, we apply Ohm's Law and the rules for series and parallel circuits:

*   **Ohm's Law:** The relationship between voltage ($V$), current ($I$), and resistance ($R$) is given by:
    $$V = I \times R$$

*   **Series Circuits:**
    *   The current ($I$) is the same through all components: $I_{total} = I_1 = I_2$
    *   The total resistance ($R_{total}$) is the sum of individual resistances: $R_{total} = R_1 + R_2 + ... + R_n$

*   **Parallel Circuits:**
    *   The voltage ($V$) across each parallel branch is the same: $V_{total} = V_1 = V_2$
    *   The total resistance ($R_{total}$) is calculated using the reciprocal formula:
        $$\frac{1}{R_{total}} = \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2} + ... + \frac{1}{R_n}$$
