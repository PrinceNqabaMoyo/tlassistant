## Introduction
**Visible light** is the form of light that we can perceive with our own eyes. While there are other forms of light (such as ultraviolet light) that are invisible to the naked eye, this chapter focuses on the visible light spectrum, how we see colours, and how light behaves.

### New Words
*   **Luminous:** Objects that emit their own light.
*   **Radiation:** The emission or transmission of energy in the form of waves or particles.
*   **Rectilinear:** Moving or arranged in a straight line.
*   **Propagation:** The way in which light travels or spreads.

---

## 4.1 Radiation of Light
Light originates from **luminous objects**. These are objects that produce and emit their own light.

*   **Natural Light:** The Sun is the primary source of natural light on Earth.
*   **Artificial Light:** Man-made objects, such as light bulbs, are also luminous because they emit light.

# Chapter 4: Visible Light

## 1. The Nature of Light
Light is a form of energy that is transferred through space by **radiation**. This energy travels in the form of **electromagnetic waves** that move in straight lines.

### Key Facts about Light Speed and Travel
*   **Speed of Light:** Light travels through space at a speed of $300\,000\text{ km/s}$.
*   **Travel Time:** It takes approximately $8$ minutes for light to travel from the Sun to the Earth.
*   **Distance Comparison:** If you could travel at the speed of light, you could circle the Earth's equator $7,5$ times in just $1$ second.

## 2. Luminous vs. Non-luminous Objects
*   **Luminous Objects:** Objects that emit their own light (e.g., the Sun).
*   **Non-luminous Objects:** Objects that do not emit their own light. They are only visible because they reflect light from a luminous source.
    *   *Example:* The Moon is a non-luminous object; it does not produce its own light but reflects light from the Sun.

#### Scientific Explanation: Why is the image upside down?
When you look through the pinhole camera, you will observe an **inverted (upside down) image**. 

This occurs because light travels in a straight line. Light reflecting off the bottom of an object travels in a straight line through the pinhole and reaches the top of the screen inside the camera. Conversely, light from the top of the object travels in a straight line through the pinhole and reaches the bottom of the screen.

#### Key Concept: Rectilinear Propagation
The fact that light forms a clear, inverted image through a pinhole proves that light travels in straight lines. This phenomenon is known as the **rectilinear propagation of light**.

#### Ray Diagrams
A ray diagram is a scientific drawing used to represent the path of light. 

*   **Representation:** Light rays are represented by straight lines.
*   **Direction:** Arrowheads are placed on the lines to indicate the direction in which the light is travelling.

# The Visible Light Spectrum and Wave Properties

## 1. Electromagnetic Waves
Light travels in the form of **electromagnetic waves**. Humans can only perceive light within a specific range of wavelengths, known as the **visible light spectrum**.

## 2. Defining Wavelength
A **wavelength** is the physical distance between two corresponding points on two consecutive waves. 
*   **Measurement:** It is typically measured from one **peak** (crest) to the next peak, or from one **trough** to the next trough.

### 4.3 Opaque and Transparent Substances

When light strikes a surface, one of three things happens:
1. **Reflected:** The light bounces off the surface.
2. **Absorbed:** The light is taken in by the material.
3. **Transmitted:** The light passes through the material.

#### Key Definitions
*   **Transparent:** A material that allows most light to be transmitted through it (e.g., glass).
*   **Opaque:** A material that is not transparent; it does not allow light to pass through.
*   **Translucent:** A material that allows some light to pass through but scatters it.
*   **Transmit:** To allow light to pass through a medium.

# 4.4 Absorption of Light

## Understanding Colour
The colours we perceive in objects around us are determined by how those objects interact with light. When light strikes a surface, two main processes occur:
1. **Absorption:** The surface takes in some of the light energy.
2. **Reflection:** The surface bounces some of the light back.

The light that is reflected from an object is the light that reaches our eyes, allowing us to see the object and its specific colour.

## How We See Colour
White light (such as sunlight) is a mixture of all the colours of the visible spectrum. When white light hits an object, the object absorbs most of the colours and reflects only the colour that we perceive.

### Example: The Ladybird
*   **The Scenario:** A ladybird has a red shell and sits on a green leaf.
*   **The Process:** When white light hits the red shell of the ladybird, the shell absorbs all the other colours in the white light spectrum.
*   **The Result:** Only the red light is reflected back to our eyes. Because red light reaches our eyes, we see the ladybird as red.

# Lesson: Understanding Light and Colour

## Core Concept: How We See Colour
The colour of an object is determined by the light it reflects. White light is composed of all the colours of the visible spectrum. When white light hits an object, the object absorbs some colours and reflects others. Our eyes perceive the colour that is reflected back to them.

### The Role of Filters
A coloured filter (like a piece of red plastic) works by absorbing all colours of the spectrum except for the colour of the filter itself. When white light passes through a red filter, only red light is transmitted.

---

## Activity: Why do objects look red under red light?

### Materials Required

## Scientific Explanation

### 1. Reflection of Light
When white light hits a white object, the object reflects all wavelengths of visible light equally. Because all colours are reflected back to our eyes, our brain interprets the object as **white**.

### 2. The Effect of the Filter
When you place a red filter in front of the light source:
*   The filter absorbs all colours except for red.
*   Only red light reaches the white paper.
*   Since the paper is white, it is capable of reflecting all colours, including red.
*   Therefore, the paper reflects the red light back to your eyes, making the paper appear **red**.

### Mathematical Representation of Light
If we represent white light ($W$) as the sum of all visible colours ($C_1, C_2, ... C_n$):
$$W = \sum_{i=1}^{n} C_i$$

When a red filter ($F_{red}$) is applied, the transmitted light ($L_t$) is:
$$L_t = W \times F_{red} = \text{Red Light}$$

When this light hits a white surface ($S_{white}$), the reflected light ($L_r$) is:
$$L_r = L_t \times S_{white} = \text{Red Light}$$

# 4.5 Reflection of Light

When light hits a surface, it is often reflected off that surface. A still, flat surface (like a calm lake) can act as a mirror, creating a mirror image of objects.

## New Words
*   **Reflect:** To bounce off a surface.
*   **Incident ray:** The ray of light that hits a surface.
*   **Reflected ray:** The ray of light that bounces off a surface.
*   **Normal line:** An imaginary line drawn perpendicular ($90^\circ$) to the surface at the point of incidence.
*   **Angle of incidence:** The angle between the incident ray and the normal line.
*   **Angle of reflection:** The angle between the reflected ray and the normal line.
*   **Perpendicular:** At an angle of $90^\circ$ to a surface.

## Understanding Reflection
When light strikes a reflective surface, it changes direction. To study this scientifically, we use a diagrammatic approach:

1.  **Incident Ray:** The incoming light beam.
2.  **Reflected Ray:** The outgoing light beam.
3.  **Normal:** An imaginary line used as a reference point for measurements. It is always drawn **perpendicular** to the surface.

### Key Angles
The two primary angles measured in reflection are:
*   **Angle of incidence ($i$):** Measured between the incident ray and the normal.
*   **Angle of reflection ($r$):** Measured between the reflected ray and the normal.

### The Human Eye: Structure and Function

#### Pupil Regulation
The size of the pupil changes based on light intensity to regulate the amount of light entering the eye:
*   **Bright light:** The pupil contracts (gets smaller) to limit the amount of light entering.
*   **Low light:** The pupil dilates (gets bigger) to allow more light to enter.

### How We See Colours

#### The Physics of Colour Reflection
When white light (which contains all colours of the visible spectrum) strikes an object, the object interacts with that light. 

*   **Reflection:** If an object appears a specific colour, it is because that object absorbs all other colours of light and **reflects** only the colour that we see.
*   **Example:** When white light hits a red ladybird, the surface absorbs all colours except red. The red light is reflected into our eyes. This reflected light hits the retina, where it is converted into electrical impulses and sent to the brain, allowing us to perceive the colour red.

### Did You Know? (Eye Anatomy)

*   **The Blind Spot:** Each eye has a small "blind spot" at the back of the retina where the optic nerve attaches. We do not normally notice this hole in our vision because our eyes work together to fill in the missing information for each other.
*   **Retinal Cells:** The cells in your eye come in different shapes:
    *   **Rod-shaped cells:** Allow you to see shapes.

### Understanding Colour: Absorption and Reflection

#### Key Concept: How we see colour
We perceive the colour of an object based on which wavelengths of light are **reflected** into our eyes and which are **absorbed** by the object's surface.

*   **White light:** Contains all the colours of the visible spectrum.
*   **Reflection:** When an object appears a specific colour, it is because that object reflects that specific colour of light while absorbing all other colours.
*   **Absorption:** When an object appears black, it absorbs all colours of light and reflects none.

# Investigation: Light Passing Through a Glass Block

## Objective
To investigate the behavior of a light ray as it travels from air into a glass block and back out into the air.

---

## Key Concepts

### 1. Refraction
Refraction is the change in direction of a light ray when it passes from one transparent medium into another (e.g., from air into glass).

### 2. Measuring Angles
To analyze the path of light, we use a **normal line**, which is an imaginary line drawn at a $90^{\circ}$ angle to the surface where the two media meet.

*   **Angle of Incidence ($i$):** The angle measured between the incident light ray and the normal line.
*   **Angle of Refraction ($r$):** The angle measured between the refracted light ray and the normal line.



![Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr8_Energy-transfer-in-electrical-systems.pdf-0028-00.png](images/Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr8_Energy-transfer-in-electrical-systems.pdf-0028-00.png)


---

## Analysis of Light Behavior

When light passes through a transparent substance, it changes direction. By comparing the angle of incidence ($i$) and the angle of refraction ($r$), we can determine how the light bends:

*   **Bending towards the normal:** If the angle of refraction ($r$) is smaller than the angle of incidence ($i$), the light ray has bent towards the normal line.
*   **Bending away from the normal:** If the angle of refraction ($r$) is larger than the angle of incidence ($i$), the light ray has bent away from the normal line.

### Experiment: Refraction of Light through a Glass Block

This experiment demonstrates how light changes direction when passing from one medium (air) into another (glass) and back again.

#### Procedure

1.  **Initial Entry:** Place the glass block on a sheet of paper. Trace the incident ray and mark the point where it enters the glass block.

### Refraction of Light

#### Key Concepts
*   **Refraction:** The change in direction of a light wave when it passes from one medium to another.
*   **Medium:** The material through which light travels (e.g., air, water, glass).
*   **Density:** The measure of how compact the particles in a substance are. Light travels at different speeds depending on the density of the medium.

#### Principles of Light Propagation
1.  **Right Angles:** When light enters a different medium at an angle of $90^{\circ}$ (perpendicular to the surface), it does not change direction.
2.  **Change in Speed:** Light behaves as a wave. It travels faster in less dense media (like air) and slower in more dense media (like water or glass).
3.  **Change in Direction:** When light enters a medium at an angle other than $90^{\circ}$, it changes speed and consequently changes direction (refracts).

---

#### Rules of Refraction

**1. From Less Dense to More Dense Medium**
*   When light moves from a less dense medium (e.g., air) into a more dense medium (e.g., glass), the light slows down.
*   The light ray bends **towards** the normal line.

**2. From More Dense to Less Dense Medium**
*   When light moves from a more dense medium to a less dense medium, the light speeds up.
*   The light ray bends **away** from the normal line.

*   **Apparent Depth:** When looking down into a pool of water, objects (like your legs) appear shorter or at a different depth than they actually are due to the refraction of light as it exits the water into the air.

### Types of Lenses

Lenses are optical devices that refract light. They are categorized into two main types based on how they affect light rays.

| :--- | :--- | :--- |

| **Focal Point** | Located on the other side of the lens. | Traced back to a point in front of the lens. |

### Applications: The Magnifying Glass

A magnifying glass is a practical application of a lens. When used to view an object, it enlarges the image.

#### How a Magnifying Glass Works
*   **Positioning:** When an object (such as a ladybird) is placed **closer** to the lens than the focal point, the lens creates a **virtual image**.
*   **Result:** The virtual image appears **larger** than the actual object.
*   **Process:** Light rays reflected from the object are refracted by the magnifying glass before entering the observer's eye.

#### Critical Thinking Exercise
*   **Question:** Is a magnifying glass an example of a diverging or a converging lens?
*   **Hint:** Consider the behavior of the lens in enlarging an image when the object is placed within the focal length.

---

### Magnifying Glasses and Human Vision

#### How Magnifying Glasses Work
A magnifying glass uses a convex lens to change how we perceive objects based on their distance from the lens.

*   **Distant Objects:** When you view a distant object through a magnifying glass, the object appears smaller and upside down. This occurs when the object is placed **beyond the focal point** of the lens.
*   **Close-up Objects:** When the object is placed between the lens and the focal point, the magnifying glass creates a **virtual image** that is larger than the actual object.
*   **Curvature:** The magnification power of a convex lens is directly related to its curvature; the more curved the lens, the greater its ability to magnify objects.

# Revision Notes: Visible Light

## Core Concepts of Light

### 1. Nature and Propagation
*   **Rectilinear Propagation:** Light travels in straight lines.
*   **Composition:** White light is composed of all the colours of the visible spectrum.
*   **Dispersion:** White light can be separated into its constituent colours when passed through a prism or a raindrop (creating a rainbow).
*   **Interaction with Matter:**
    *   **Opaque objects:** Light cannot pass through them.
    *   **Transparent objects:** Light can pass through them.
    *   **Absorption:** Some materials absorb specific wavelengths of light.
*   **Colour Perception:** A material appears to be a specific colour because it reflects that part of the colour spectrum while absorbing the other wavelengths.

### 2. Reflection
Reflection occurs when light bounces off a surface. The fundamental law of reflection is:
$$\theta_i = \theta_r$$
Where:
*   $\theta_i$ is the angle of incidence.
*   $\theta_r$ is the angle of reflection.

| Surface Type | Reflection Characteristics |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Smooth Surface** | Parallel rays are reflected at the same angle (Specular reflection). |
| **Rough Surface** | Light is scattered in many directions (Diffuse reflection), resulting in an unclear image. |

### 3. Refraction
Refraction is the bending of light as it passes from one medium to another at an angle, caused by changes in the speed of light.

*   **Slowing down:** If light enters a denser medium and slows down, it bends **towards** the normal line.
*   **Speeding up:** If light enters a less dense medium and speeds up, it bends **away** from the normal line.

### 4. Lenses and Prisms
*   **Converging Lenses:** Refract and focus light rays to a point.
*   **Diverging Lenses:** Spread light rays apart.
*   **Triangular Prisms:** Refract and disperse white light into the visible spectrum.
*   **Applications:** These optical components are essential in the construction of glasses, microscopes, telescopes, and magnifying glasses.

### 5. Human Vision
The human eye functions as a biological optical system:
1.  Light enters the eye.
2.  Specialised cells in the **retina** convert light energy into electrical nerve impulses.
3.  These impulses are transmitted via the **optic nerve** to the brain.
4.  The brain interprets these impulses as visual images.

# Study Guide: Properties of Visible Light

## 1. The Nature of Visible Light
Visible light is a form of energy that is emitted from sources such as the **Sun** and **light bulbs**. 

*   **Spectrum:** Visible light consists of a spectrum of colours.
*   **Composition:** This spectrum is made up of different **wavelengths** and **frequencies**.
*   **Range of Colours:** The range of colours is determined by these specific wavelengths and frequencies.

## 2. Interaction with Objects
When light encounters an object, it behaves in different ways depending on the nature of the material:

*   **Transparent/Translucent Objects:** Light can pass through these materials.
*   **Opaque Objects:** Light cannot pass through these materials, which results in the casting of **shadows**.
*   **Colour Perception:**
    *   **White objects:** Reflect all colours of the visible spectrum.
    *   **Black objects:** Absorb all colours of the visible spectrum.

## 3. Reflection
Reflection occurs when light bounces off a surface. The nature of the reflection depends on the surface texture:

*   **Smooth surfaces:** Cause regular reflection where the light is reflected in one specific direction.
*   **Rough surfaces:** Cause diffuse reflection where the light is scattered in many directions.
*   **Law of Reflection:** The angle of reflection is equal to the angle of incidence, both measured from the **normal** (a line perpendicular to the surface).

## 4. Refraction
Refraction is the change in the direction of light when it passes from one medium into another.

*   **Change in Direction:**
    *   When light enters a denser medium, it changes direction towards the normal.
    *   When light leaves a medium, it changes direction away from the normal.
*   **Applications:**
    *   **Lenses:** Refract and focus light.
    *   **Prisms:** Refract and disperse light into its component colours.
    *   **Rainbows:** Formed when light is refracted due to water droplets in the atmosphere.

## 5. Human Vision
The human eye detects light and processes it into visual information:

1.  Light enters the eye and stimulates **receptor cells**.
2.  These cells send an **impulse** to the brain.
3.  The brain interprets these impulses, allowing us to "see" the world around us.

### Refraction of Light: Core Concepts

Refraction is the bending of light as it passes from one transparent medium into another of a different optical density. This occurs because light travels at different speeds in different materials.

#### Key Principles
1.  **Optical Density:** When light enters a **more dense** medium (e.g., air to glass), it slows down and bends **towards the normal**.
2.  **Optical Density:** When light enters a **less dense** medium (e.g., glass to air), it speeds up and bends **away from the normal**.
3.  **Normal:** An imaginary line drawn perpendicular ($90^\circ$) to the surface at the point where the light ray strikes.
4.  **Incident Ray:** The incoming ray of light.
5.  **Emergent Ray:** The ray that exits the medium.
6.  **Angle of Incidence ($i$):** The angle between the incident ray and the normal.
7.  **Angle of Refraction ($r$):** The angle between the refracted ray and the normal.

---

| **Result** | The emergent ray is parallel to the incident ray but laterally displaced (shifted). |

### Activity: Understanding Refraction in Daily Life

**Question:** Why does the tree trunk appear "skew" or shifted when viewed through a glass block?

**Explanation:**
The light rays reflecting from the tree trunk pass through the glass block. Because the glass is more optically dense than air, the light rays refract (bend) as they enter the glass and refract again as they exit back into the air. This change in the path of the light rays causes the image of the tree trunk to appear shifted or "skewed" relative to the part of the trunk seen directly through the air.

### Summary Table: Refraction Rules

| Scenario | Speed Change | Direction of Bending |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Air to Glass | Slows down | Towards the normal |
| Glass to Air | Speeds up | Away from the normal |

**Mathematical Note:** The relationship between the angles and the refractive index ($n$) of the materials is governed by Snell's Law:
$$n_1 \sin(i) = n_2 \sin(r)$$
Where:
*   $n_1, n_2$ are the refractive indices of the two media.
*   $i$ is the angle of incidence.
*   $r$ is the angle of refraction.

### 10.1 Radiation of Light
The study of light begins with the concept of **rectilinear propagation of light** (light travelling in straight lines).

**Classroom Demonstration: Camera Obscura**
* **Method:** Cover all windows with black paper and tape gaps to block all external light. Leave one small area of a window uncovered.
* **Setup:** Hang a white sheet in the centre of the room opposite the exposed window.
* **Observation:** The view from outside is projected onto the sheet, appearing upside down.

## 10.2 Spectrum of Visible Light

### Activity: Splitting White Light (LB page 195)

This activity demonstrates that white light is a mixture of the seven colours of the visible spectrum.

**Instructions:**
1. Darken the room as much as possible to ensure clear results.
2. If a ray box is unavailable, create a simple one by cutting a small slit into a piece of cardboard. Hold this in front of a light bulb to produce a coherent beam of light.
3. Use a prism to refract the light. Rotate the prism until the colours become visible.

**Observations:**
*   White light enters the prism, refracts, and emerges as a spectrum of seven colours.
*   The order of colours is always: Red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, violet.
*   **Conclusion:** White light is a combination of these seven colours.
*   Note the relative bending of red light versus violet light as they pass through the prism.

### Activity: Colour Spinning Wheels (LB page 196)

This activity demonstrates that the seven colours of the spectrum can combine to form white light.

**Construction Instructions:**
1. Find the centre of a circle and mark it with a dot.
2. Draw a straight line from the centre to the edge of the circle.
3. Use a protractor to measure and mark segments. Since a full circle is $360^\circ$, each of the 7 segments should ideally be:
   $$\frac{360^\circ}{7} \approx 51.4^\circ$$
4. For practical purposes, you may draw each segment at approximately $52^\circ$. (Note: If drawing only 6 segments, the angle would be $60^\circ$).
5. Colour each segment with one of the colours of the spectrum.

**Observations:**
*   When the wheel is spun rapidly, the colours appear to "mix."
*   The goal is to observe a white colour; however, depending on the quality of the pens or pencils used, you may observe a light grey.

# 10.3 Opaque and Transparent Substances

## Activity: Shadow Play
This activity demonstrates how different materials interact with light to form shadows.

### Key Observations
*   **Opaque Objects:** These objects do not allow light to pass through them. Consequently, they cast a dark shadow on the side furthest from the light source.
*   **Shadow Size:** The size of a shadow is determined by the size of the object and its distance from the light source.
    *   Larger objects cast larger shadows.
    *   As an object moves closer to the light source, the shadow becomes larger.
    *   As an object moves further from the light source, the shadow becomes smaller.
*   **Transparent Objects:** These materials allow light to pass through them. Therefore, they do not form a distinct shadow. If a faint outline appears, it is usually due to the thickness of the edges or imperfections in the material.
*   **Translucent (Semi-transparent) Objects:** These materials allow some light to pass through but not all of it. They cast a lighter, less defined shadow compared to opaque objects.

### Scientific Principles
*   **Rectilinear Propagation:** Light travels in straight lines and cannot bend around objects. When an opaque object blocks these straight paths, a shadow is formed on the surface behind it.
*   **Light Interaction:**
    *   **Opaque:** Blocks light completely.
    *   **Transparent:** Allows light to pass through.
    *   **Translucent:** Allows partial light transmission.

# 10.4 Absorption of Light

## Concepts
The absorption and reflection of light are linked to the energy of photons.

*   **Reflection:** When light hits a surface, some of it is reflected. The colour we perceive is determined by the frequency of the reflected light, which corresponds to the energy of the photons.
*   **Absorption:** Surfaces absorb light energy. 
    *   **Matt Black Surfaces:** Absorb most of the light and heat energy falling on them.
    *   **White and Silver Surfaces:** Reflect most of the light and heat energy.
*   **White Objects:** These objects do not absorb the colours of the visible spectrum; instead, they reflect all colours together, which our eyes perceive as white.

### ACTIVITY: Why do objects look red under red light? (LB page 201)

**Instructions:** Use a dimly lit classroom so that the main source of light is the torch or light bulb.

1. **Observation of White:** Normal light contains all 7 colours of the visible spectrum mixed together. All colours are reflected from a white page and enter our eyes, which is why we perceive the page as white.
2. **Observation of Red:** When viewed through red plastic, the page appears red. This is because the red plastic only allows red light to pass through it. The red light is reflected off the paper, and since it is the only colour that reaches the eye, the paper appears to be red.

### INVESTIGATION: Is there a relationship between the angles of incidence and reflections? (LB page 203)

**Objective:** To determine the relationship between the angle of incidence and the angle of reflection.

**Key Principle:** The angle of reflection is always equal to the angle of incidence.

**Methodology Notes:**
*   **Materials:** A mirror, a ray box (or laser pointer), and paper.
*   **Alternative Materials:** You may use a piece of crumpled aluminium foil instead of a mirror, or a sheet of corrugated cardboard instead of paper. If using cardboard, you can stick pins into the board along the light ray to trace the lines.
*   **Scientific Inquiry:** When conducting this investigation, formulate a clear question, such as: *"How is the angle of reflection related to the angle of incidence of the incident ray?"*
*   **Accuracy:** To improve the accuracy of your measurements, use a protractor printed directly on the page.

### 10.6 How do we see light?

#### Activity: Seeing Colours
We perceive the colour of an object based on which wavelengths of light are reflected into our eyes and which are absorbed by the object.

**Example: A Sunflower**
*   **Yellow Petals:** When white light (which contains all colours of the spectrum) strikes the yellow petals, the petals absorb all colours except yellow. The yellow light is reflected into our eyes, so we perceive the petals as yellow.
*   **Black Centre:** When white light strikes the black centre, the centre absorbs all the colours of the spectrum. Because no light is reflected into our eyes, our brain interprets this absence of light as the colour black.

### Colour Perception
When white light from the Sun strikes an object, the surface of that object absorbs most colours of the light spectrum. Only specific colours are reflected back to our eyes. 

*   **Example:** When looking at a blue car, the surface absorbs all colours except blue. The blue light is reflected into our eyes, which our brain interprets as the colour blue.

### 10.7 Refraction of Light

Refraction is the change in direction of a light ray as it passes from one medium to another (e.g., from air into glass).

#### Investigation: What happens to light when it passes through a glass block?

**Materials needed:**
*   A light source (a laser pointer or a light bulb with a cardboard screen featuring a thin slit).
*   A glass block with parallel sides.

**Procedure and Observations:**
1.  **Setup:** Create a thin ray of light using your chosen light source.
2.  **Observation:** Observe the path of the light ray as it travels from air into the glass block, and subsequently from the glass block back into the air.
3.  **Key Principle:** Light changes direction when passing between media of different densities because the angle of incidence is not equal to the angle of refraction.

**Key Definitions:**
*   **Medium 1 (Air):** A less dense medium.
*   **Medium 2 (Glass):** A more dense medium.
*   **Refraction behavior:** When light enters a more dense medium (glass) from a less dense medium (air), the refracted ray bends **towards** the normal line.

### Refraction of Light: Key Concepts

#### 1. Observations on Light Direction
*   **Refraction:** When light passes from one medium to another (e.g., air to glass), it changes direction.
*   **Bending Away:** When light moves from a more dense medium to a less dense medium, the refracted ray bends **away** from the normal line.
*   **Bending Towards:** When light moves from a less dense medium to a more dense medium, the light bends **towards** the normal line.

#### 2. Geometric Relationships in Refraction
When observing light passing through a glass block:
*   **Angle Equality:** Angle 1 is equal to angle 4, and angle 2 is equal to angle 3.
*   **Mathematical Basis:** The equality of angles 2 and 3 is explained by the geometry of parallel lines and alternate angles. Since the normal lines are parallel, the alternate angles between them are equal.
*   **Definitions:**
    *   Angles 1 and 4: Angles of incidence.
    *   Angles 2 and 3: Angles of refraction.
*   **Conclusion:** The angle of incidence is not equal to the angle of refraction. This confirms that light changes direction when entering and exiting the glass block.

### ACTIVITY: Magic Coin Trick (LB page 214)

**Objective:** To demonstrate how the refraction of light affects visual perception.

**Procedure Notes:**
*   Secure the coin to the bottom of the bowl to prevent movement when water is added.
*   Pour water gently into the bowl to ensure the coin remains in its original position.

**Observations and Explanations:**
1.  **Visibility:** The coin becomes visible only after water is added to the bowl.
2.  **Apparent Position:** The coin appears to be higher in the bowl than it actually is.
3.  **The Science of Perception:**
    *   Without water, there is no direct line of sight to the coin.
    *   When water is added, light rays from the coin are refracted as they leave the water.
    *   Because the human brain interprets light as traveling in straight lines, it projects the light rays backward, making the coin appear higher than its true position.

### ACTIVITY: Research Careers in Optics

This section provides an overview of two primary career paths in the field of optics: Optometry and Ophthalmology.

---

#### 1. Optometry
Optometrists are healthcare professionals who measure the efficiency of a patient's eyes. They examine eyes for vision problems, diseases, and other abnormal conditions. They test for proper depth and colour perception, as well as the ability to focus and coordinate the eyes. They specialize in visual defects and are qualified to prescribe spectacles or contact lenses to rectify or alleviate conditions such as far-sightedness, short-sightedness, astigmatism (image distortion), and presbyopia (age-related far-sightedness).

**Educational Requirements:**
*   **School Subjects:** National Senior Certificate (NSC) meeting degree or diploma requirements.
*   **Compulsory Subjects:** Mathematics, Physical Sciences.
*   **Recommended Subjects:** Life Sciences.
*   **Note:** Each institution sets its own minimum entry requirements.

**Training Paths:**
*   **Degree:** BOptometry (4 years full-time study) at UJ, UFS, or UL. Requires a one-year internship post-degree before registration as a professional optometrist.
*   **Diploma:** N.Dip: Optical Dispensing and B.Tech at CPUT (3 years). A fourth year culminates in B.Tech Optometry. Students have patient contact during the third and fourth years and must complete a one-year internship.
*   **Professional Registration:** Must register with the Interim National Medical and Dental Council (INMDC) of SA to practice.

---

#### 2. Ophthalmology
Ophthalmologists are medical doctors who diagnose and treat diseases of the eye, including glaucoma, cataracts, and eye injuries. They also treat vision problems like near-sightedness. Their practice combines medicine and surgery, ranging from lens prescriptions to delicate surgical procedures.

**Educational Requirements:**
*   **School Subjects:** National Senior Certificate (NSC) meeting degree requirements.
*   **Compulsory Subjects:** Mathematics, Physical Sciences.
*   **Recommended Subjects:** Life Sciences.
*   **Note:** Competition for medical studies is high; applicants with excellent grades are given preference.

**Training Path (MBChB Degree):**
*   **Institutions:** UP, UCT, UFS, Wits, US, UL, UKZN.
*   **Theoretical Training:** 6 years.
*   **Student Internship:** 1 year.
*   **Practical Work:** 1 year (House doctor year).
*   **Specialisation:** Post-graduate study for 3 – 5 years to qualify as an ophthalmologist.

### Shadows and Light
*   **Definition:** An opaque object casts a shadow because it prevents light from passing through it.
*   **Mechanism:** Light is either reflected or absorbed by the object. A shadow forms on the side opposite to the light source because light cannot reach that area.

### Reflection and Colour
*   **Reflection off uneven surfaces:** When light hits an uneven surface (like water ripples), the light rays are scattered rather than reflected off a smooth surface.
*   **The Nature of Colour:**
    *   **White:** A combination of all colours in the visible spectrum. White objects reflect all colours equally.
    *   **Black:** An absence of colour. Black objects absorb all colours and reflect none.
*   **Colour Absorption and Reflection Table:**

| Colour of Object | Absorption | Reflection |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Black** | All colours | None |
| **Yellow** | All colours except yellow | Yellow |
| **Green** | All colours except green | Green |
| **Blue** | All colours except blue | Blue |
| **Red** | All colours except red | Red |
| **White** | None | All colours |

### Refraction of Light
*   **Definition:** Refraction is the bending of light as it passes from one transparent medium to another.
*   **Rules of Refraction:**
    1.  **Less dense to more dense:** Light refracts and bends **towards** the normal line.
    2.  **More dense to less dense:** Light refracts and bends **away** from the normal line.
*   **Observations in a dense medium (e.g., a glass block):**
    *   When light enters a denser medium, it slows down, causing the ray to bend towards the normal.
    *   When light exits into a less dense medium (like air), it speeds up, causing the ray to bend away from the normal.
    *   Refraction causes images to appear distorted (e.g., a tree trunk appearing "skew" through a glass block).

### Vision and Lenses
*   **Far-sightedness (Hyperopia):**
    *   Occurs when light focuses on a point behind the retina, resulting in a blurred image.
    *   **Correction:** Can be corrected using a **convex lens**.
    *   **Function:** The convex lens bends the light rays before they enter the eye to ensure they focus correctly on the retina.

### The Human Eye: Focusing Mechanism

The provided text describes the final stage of the visual process within the human eye.

#### Key Concept: Image Formation
For clear vision to occur, light rays entering the eye must be refracted (bent) by the lens so that they converge precisely on the **retina**.

*   **The Lens:** Acts as a converging lens that adjusts its shape to focus light from objects at varying distances.
*   **The Retina:** The light-sensitive inner lining at the back of the eye where the image is formed.

#### Summary of the Process
1.  Light enters the eye through the pupil.
2.  The light passes through the crystalline lens.
3.  The lens focuses the light rays.
4.  **Result:** The rays are focused clearly on the retina, allowing the brain to interpret the image.
