# **GLOSSARY** 

|**abiotic:**|non-living; devoid of life|
|---|---|
|**adapt:**|to change, become adjusted to new conditions|
|**antibiotic:**|a compound, often produced by microorganisms,<br>which kills or slows down the growth of bacteria|
|**bacteria:**|microscopic organisms, lacking a nucleus; they can<br>inhabit many different environments (air, water,<br>soil, the bodies of other organisms etc.)|
|**biosphere:**|the regions of the surface and atmosphere of the<br>Earth where different organisms live|
|**biotic:**|relating to living organisms|
|**camouflage:**|an adaptation in which an animal can hide by<br>blending in with its surroundings|
|**carnivore:**|an animal that feeds on other animals|
|**chemical potential**<br>**energy:**|stored energy in the form of chemical compounds|
|**chlorophyll:**|a green pigment found in green plants (and certain<br>bacteria) that absorbs radiant energy from the Sun<br>to provide energy for photosynthesis|
|**chloroplast:**|a part inside the plant cell of green plants that<br>contains chlorophyll, where photosynthesis occurs|
|**community:**|**.**<br>all the animals, plants or microorganisms that live<br>together and interact in a certain area at a specific<br>time|
|**consumer:**|an organism that cannot produce its own food and<br>therefore has to eat other organisms; also called a<br>heterotroph; e.g. all animals, fungi|
|**contaminate:**|unwanted or waste material enters a place where it<br>does not belong e.g. sewage entering a river,<br>bacteria entering a wound|
|**decomposers:**|organisms that decompose (break down) organic<br>material, including the remains of dead plant and<br>animal material; usually bacteria or fungi|
|**disease:**|an abnormal condition (or sickness) of an organism<br>that interrupts the normal functioning; often<br>includes pain, weakness and other symptoms|
|**ecologist:**|a scientist who studies the interactions of<br>organisms with each other and with their<br>environment|
|**ecology:**|the branch of biology that deals with the<br>interactions of organisms with one another and<br>with the physical and chemical environment|
|**ecosystem:**|a biological community of interacting organisms<br>and their physical environment|
|**endangered:**|organisms that are seriously at risk of extinction|
|**energy pyramid:**|a triangular picture of a food chain with producers<br>at the bottom and consumers higher up|

Life and living 

|**extinct:**|an organism that no longer exists; the death of an<br>entire species|
|---|---|
|**fermentation:**|the chemical breakdown of a substance (by<br>microorganisms such as bacteria or yeast) in the<br>absence of oxygen, producing simpler compounds<br>and energy|
|**fever:**|dangerously high body temperature|
|**fixed (fix, fixation):**|the process in nitrogen or carbon in their elemental<br>forms are assimilated into biological molecules, eg<br>nitrogen fixation by bacteria, carbon fixation during<br>photosynthesis|
|**food chain:**|a series of organisms linked together to show<br>which one eats what; arrows show the flow of<br>energy through it|
|**food web:**|many food chains interlinked in an area form a food<br>web, so organisms have many different food<br>sources|
|**fungi:**|a kingdom of organisms which includes moulds,<br>yeasts and mushrooms, that do not contain<br>chlorophyll, produce spores to reproduce and feed<br>on other matter|
|**glucose:**|a type of sugar, produced by plants during<br>photosynthesis|
|**habitat:**|a particular type of environment in which an<br>organism lives|
|**herbivore:**|**.**<br>an animal that eats only plants|
|**hibernating:**|an instinctive behaviour in which some animals<br>spend time where conditions are not ideal (e.g.<br>winter; periods of food scarcity) in an inactive<br>(dormant) state|
|**immune system:**|the system that defends the body against<br>infections, disease and foreign substances|
|**infect:**|a microorganism enters the body and multiplies,<br>causing illness and damage to the organs|
|**insectivore:**|animals that feed on insects and other smaller<br>invertebrates such as worms|
|**insoluble:**|substances that do not dissolve in a liquid|
|**interact:**|to have an effect on somebody/something else or<br>on one another by being or working closely<br>together|
|**instinct:**|a pattern of behaviour that requires no thinking<br>and is biologically driven|
|**legumes:**|group of plants, including beans, lentils, peas and<br>peanuts, that have edible seeds inside fruit that<br>forms a pod|
|**limewater:**|a solution of calcium hydroxide in water which<br>turns cloudy white in the presence of carbon<br>dioxide|
|**limit:**|a restriction on the size or amount of something<br>available or possible|
|**migrate:**|to move from one region or habitat to another<br>according to the seasons|

|**migration:**|a seasonal movement of animals move from one<br>place to another and back again|
|---|---|
|**mimicry:**|an adaptation in which one animal imitates (copies)<br>another in appearance or behaviour|
|**nitrogen:**|an important element that forms part of proteins in<br>all living organisms|
|**nocturnal:**|active at night|
|**omnivore:**|an animal that eats both plant and animal material|
|**pathogen:**|a microorganism that causes a disease|
|**pigment:**|is a molecule that absorb certain wavelengths of<br>light and reflect others to produce colours|
|**photosynthesis:**|the process by which green plants and some<br>bacteria use radiant energy from the Sun to turn<br>carbon dioxide and water into glucose and oxygen|
|**population:**|a group of organisms from the same species that<br>interbreed and live in the same place at the same<br>time|
|**population ecology:**|the study of what contributes to the rise and fall of<br>numbers of a species|
|**predator:**|an animal that naturally preys on other animals for<br>food|
|**primary consumer:**|an organism that eats plant material|
|**producer:**|an organisms that is able to make its own food; for<br>example, all green plants|
|**protist:**|**.**<br>member of a diverse group of microorganisms that<br>are not viruses, bacteria or fungi; can be animal-like<br>e.g. protozoa, plant-like e.g. algae or fungi-like e.g.<br>slime moulds, water moulds|
|**radiant energy:**|energy contained in light rays or other forms of<br>radiation|
|**respiration:**|the process by which energy is released from the<br>glucose in food in a series of chemical reactions|
|**secondary consumer:**|an organisms that eats herbivores and primary<br>consumers|
|**soluble:**|substances that are able to dissolve in a liquid|
|**species:**|a group of organisms classified by common<br>attributes that can breed and produce fertile<br>offspring|
|**starch:**|a substance which consists of many glucose<br>molecules joined together; plants store glucose<br>produced by photosynthesis in this complex form|
|**terrestrial**<br>**environment:**|an environment on dry land|
|**tertiary consumer:**|an organism that eats secondary consumers; a<br>carnivore at the highest level in a food chain that<br>feeds on other carnivores|
|**transmitted:**|to cause something to be passed from one<br>individual to another; eg. disease-causing<br>microorganisms passed from one person to another|
|**trophic level:**|a feeding level in a food web, chain or pyramid; all<br>organisms at the same trophic level get their<br>energy in the same way|

Life and living
