# Chapter 1. Photosynthesis and Respiration

## Introduction

The form of energy that the Sun produces is called **radiant energy**. Although the Sun provides us with both light and warmth, plants only use the light energy from the Sun to photosynthesise.

*The Sun provides us with energy in the form of light and heat.*

Most organisms cannot directly use the energy from the sun to perform the seven life processes. For example, a reptile can lie in the Sun to warm up from the heat energy, but this does not provide the necessary energy for that animal to move, reproduce or excrete waste.

Except for a few sea slugs, plants are the only organisms on Earth that can absorb the Sun's radiant energy and convert it into food for themselves and for other living organisms.

---

## Radiant Energy to Chemical Potential Energy

What is potential energy? Do you remember that we spoke about energy for movement (kinetic energy) and energy that is stored (potential energy) in Energy and Change in Gr. 6 and 7? What are some things that have kinetic energy and some that have potential energy? Remember to take down some notes in the margins of your workbook as you discuss things in class.

All living organisms can use energy in the form of **chemical potential energy** for the life processes. This is the energy that is stored in the food that organisms eat. Plants are able to capture the radiant energy from the Sun and transfer it to chemical potential (stored) energy for other organisms to use. They do this through the process of **photosynthesis**. All organisms release the stored potential energy from the food that they eat to support their life processes. This process is called **respiration**.

Photosynthesis takes place in small structures called **chloroplasts**, which are inside the cells of the leaves and stems of green plants. Inside the chloroplasts are green **pigments** called **chlorophyll**. This is what gives plants their green colour. Photosynthesis is the process in which chlorophyll molecules absorb the radiant energy from the sun and transfers it into chemical potential energy. The only function of chlorophyll is to trap the sunlight energy; chlorophyll is not produced or used up during photosynthesis.

## Glucose storage and use

The glucose that a plant produces when it photosynthesises is the food for the plant. The plant can use this glucose directly, and release the energy during its own respiration or it can store the glucose or convert it into other chemical compounds.

Glucose is **soluble** in water. As we learnt in Matter and Materials in Gr. 6, this means that glucose can dissolve in water. This is useful to the plant as it means it can transport the glucose in water to where it is needed elsewhere in the plant. However, in order to store large amounts of glucose, plants need to convert it into compounds which are **insoluble** in water. Therefore the plant converts glucose into **starch**, which is insoluble in water. Why do you think the plant might need to store some glucose?

## Plant Support and Chemical Compounds

In addition to starch, plants also convert glucose into **cellulose**. 

* **Cellulose:** Used to support and strengthen plants.
* **Animal Support:** Animals do not have cellulose for support; instead, they have other structures to provide support and protect the body.

Glucose is also converted into other chemical compounds that enable processes in the plant such as reproduction and growth.

## Testing for Photosynthesis (Starch Test)

We have learned about how plants produce glucose and store it as starch, but how do we know for sure? As young scientists, we need to question whether this explanation of photosynthesis is accurate. Is there an investigation we can do to test for the presence of these compounds? Let's find out!

To see if a plant photosynthesises and produces starch, we can study the properties of starch and glucose:

1. **Taste:** Glucose tastes sweet, but starch does not taste sweet at all.
2. **Solubility:** Glucose will dissolve in water, while starch will not dissolve in water.
3. **Iodine Test:** Iodine changes from brownish-orange to dark blue-black when it comes into contact with starch.

# INVESTIGATION: Which leaves photosynthesise?

There are two parts to this investigation. First, we want to find out which leaves are able to photosynthesise. We will place some pot plants in the light for a day, and some other pot plants in a dark cupboard for a day, and then perform the investigation on the leaves of plants from both groups.

In the second part of the investigation, we will use what we have learnt to investigate which parts of variegated leaves photosynthesise.

## Part 1: Leaves in light and dark

### AIM:
1. What do you wish to establish by conducting this investigation?

### HYPOTHESIS:
1. What do you think or predict will happen when you conduct this investigation?

### MATERIALS AND APPARATUS:
* gloves
* a range of pot plants that can be easily moved around
* 100 ml beaker or glass jar in a saucepan with water
* bunsen burner, spirit lamp or a stove
* tweezers
* ethyl alcohol (or methylated spirits)
* glass petri dishes, white saucer or white tile
* stopwatch or timer
* glass pipette or dropper
* iodine solution

### METHOD:
1. Work in groups of three or four.
2. Place half of the plants in the dark for at least 24-48 hours and the others in a well-lit area of the class that is exposed to lots of natural sunlight.
3. After 24 hours, pour 50 ml of the ethyl alcohol into the beaker and place it in the saucepan with water. Heat the saucepan over the bunsen burner or the stove. The water in the saucepan will distribute the heat evenly to warm the ethyl alcohol evenly.
4. Remove one healthy looking leaf from the pot plants that were in the well-lit area exposed to direct sunlight.
5. Using the tweezers, dip a leaf into the boiling water for 1-2 minutes. This helps to remove the waxy cuticle that covers the leaf and breaks down the cell walls.

# 1.2 Respiration

## Core Concepts

* **Respiration:** The process by which energy is released from glucose in a series of chemical reactions within living organisms.
* **Chemical Potential Energy:** Energy stored within the chemical bonds of fuels (such as wood, coal, oil) and food (such as glucose).
* **By-product:** A secondary product derived from a manufacturing process or chemical reaction, sometimes referred to as a waste product.

---

## Energy from Food

* All organisms need to break down food to release stored chemical potential energy for vital life processes, movement, and work.
* Although plants make their own food through photosynthesis, respiration takes place in **all** organisms, including plants.
* When glucose from food is combined with oxygen in a series of chemical reactions, the bonds are broken down and energy is released.

---

## Products of Respiration

Respiration can be represented by the following word equation:

$$\text{glucose} + \text{oxygen} \rightarrow \text{carbon dioxide} + \text{water} + \text{energy}$$

### Key Components:
1. **Inputs (Reactants):** Glucose and Oxygen.
2. **Outputs (Products):** Carbon dioxide, water, and energy.
3. **By-products:** Carbon dioxide and water are produced as by-products (waste products) during the release of energy.

---

## Review & Activity Questions

1. **Define Respiration:**
   * *Answer:* The process by which energy is released from glucose in a series of chemical reactions in all living organisms.

2. **List the two primary ingredients required for respiration to take place:**
   * *Answer:* Glucose and oxygen.

3. **What are the by-products produced during respiration?**
   * *Answer:* Carbon dioxide and water.

# SUMMARY:

## Key Concepts

* The need for energy drives the interactions and interdependence in an ecosystem.
* The Sun provides energy to the Earth in the form of radiant (light) energy and heat energy.
* Photosynthesis is the process whereby green plants use carbon dioxide from the air, water from the soil and radiant energy from the Sun in a series of chemical reactions to produce glucose (food) and oxygen.
* Plants are able to photosynthesise because they contain chlorophyll, a green pigment that can capture light energy from the Sun.
* Plants change the glucose that they produce into starch that can be stored more easily.
* Plants also produce cellulose fibres that give plants strength and support and are important to our digestive systems as roughage.
* The food that a plant produces is used by animals when they eat the plant and by other animals that eat them.
* This food contains chemical potential energy that needs to be released from the food.
* Respiration is the process in all living organisms by which energy is released from glucose in a series of chemical reactions.
* Respiration uses oxygen while carbon dioxide and water are given off as by-products.

# 1 Photosynthesis and Respiration

## Chapter Overview
* **Duration:** 2 weeks

This chapter provides learners with an introduction to, and revision of, the concepts in photosynthesis and respiration in preparation for a study of the ecosystem. Learners have already looked at photosynthesis and respiration in previous grades. They know that respiration is one of the seven life processes of living things. They have also been introduced to photosynthesis in the context of green plants and food chains in Gr. 5 and 6. The emphasis in this chapter is on the use of energy and on how radiant (light) energy is transferred to chemical potential energy and later released during respiration. This concept is developed within the scope of the CAPS prescriptions and will be used as a scaffold to explain the transfer of energy in the ecosystem in the subsequent chapter.

# Photosynthesis Investigation: Starch Test

## Investigation Overview
* **Carbon Dioxide Test:** Begins with carbon dioxide testing using limewater, followed by demonstrating the starch test.
* **Learner Skills:** Learners must draw their own tables to record observations. This serves as an initial high school investigation to gauge student proficiency and recording abilities.

---

## Scientific Principles & Key Concepts

### Role of Reagents and Heat
* **Hot Water:** Removes the waxy cuticle of the leaf.
* **Ethyl Alcohol:** Dissolves the chlorophyll, releasing the green pigment so that it does not mask the color change expected with the iodine solution. The leaves should appear white after this step.
* **Iodine Solution:** Acts as an indicator for starch.
  * **Positive Result:** Turns **blue-black** (or dark blue-black) in the presence of starch, indicating that photosynthesis occurred and glucose was produced and stored as starch.
  * **Negative Result:** Remains **brownish-orange**, indicating that starch is absent and photosynthesis did not take place.

---

## Experimental Guidelines for Learners

1. **Comparative Controls:** This investigation allows comparison between plants allowed to photosynthesize and those denied the conditions to do so. Plants with access to light/CO₂ produce starch, while those without produce little to no starch.
2. **Plant Selection:** 
   * **Geranium leaves** and **variegated mint-leaves** (common in KwaZulu-Natal) are excellent options because they are thin and easy to penetrate.
   * **Ivy leaves** are less suitable due to a very thick cuticle that prevents iodine penetration.
3. **Independent Report Writing:** Unlike guided practicals, learners must compile their own reports individually or in groups.

---

## Structure of the Experimental Report

### Aim
To find out which parts of variegated leaves photosynthesize and store starch.

### Hypothesis
The green parts of variegated leaves will turn blue-black in iodine solution, indicating that they photosynthesize and store starch, whereas the white parts will not turn blue-black (will stay brown).

### Materials and Apparatus
* Must be recorded in a bulleted list.
* Must include specific measurements where applicable.

### Method
* Numbered steps written in full sentences.
* Must accurately reflect the measurements and procedures used.

### Results
* Learners must draw and format their own data tables with appropriate column and row headings, plus a descriptive table title.
* Learners should make drawings of their leaves at the start of the experiment, indicating the different colored regions (since boiling in alcohol removes the color, initial drawings help map out where pigments were located).

### Discussion
* Explain the results and reference the scientific reasoning: the white parts of the leaves lack chlorophyll and therefore cannot photosynthesize, proving that **chlorophyll is crucial for photosynthesis**.
* Include a self-assessment or reflection on what could be improved in the experimental procedure.

### Conclusion
A short statement directly answering the aim or investigative question.

# INVESTIGATION: Why do bananas become sweeter as they ripen? (LB page 8)

*Note: It is not crucial to do this investigation if you do not have time. This is an **optional extension** of the starch test.*

### Investigation Guidance and Expected Responses

1. **Aim / Objective (Learner-dependent answer)**
   * Example of a possible answer: To investigate the presence of starch in ripe and unripe bananas; to investigate why bananas become sweeter as they ripen; etc.
   * If the unripe banana does not taste as sweet compared to the very sweet-tasting ripe banana, then perhaps it contains more starch and less glucose. The starch test might show that the unripe banana contains more starch.

2. **Observations (Learner-dependent answer)**
   * Learners should be able to describe the taste and texture of each banana: the ripe one being soft and sweet, and the unripe one being firm and not too sweet. 
   * Learners might deduce that the unripe banana contains more starch and less glucose as it is not as sweet as the ripe banana. However, this is not a very accurate test on its own, and different learners might experience tastes differently, making subjective taste results unreliable.
   * Learners should be able to quite easily observe differences based on the speed with which iodine changes colour in the unripe banana compared to the ripe one.

3. **Data Presentation (Learner-dependent answer)**
   * Allow learners the freedom to tabulate their results in any way they choose, as long as it is easy to interpret and understand. 
   * Use this as a teaching opportunity to help those who struggle with tabulation, and when everyone is done, compare the different methods used in the class.
   * Use this comparison to help learners understand that the **reliability** of an experiment rests on the fact that although different people perform the same test, they should all reach very similar results.

4. **Conclusions**
   * **Conclusion:** Unripe bananas contain more starch than ripe bananas. This question was specifically included to introduce learners to the concept of **validity**, and teachers are encouraged to allow learners to debate this issue in the class.
   * **Scientific Fact:** As bananas ripen, the starch is converted into glucose.

## 1.2 Respiration

The term **respiration** can refer to two distinct processes:

* **Physiology:** Refers to the transport of oxygen from the outside air to the cells, and the transport of carbon dioxide out of the tissues and into the air. This is often confused with **breathing**, which is the physical movement of air in and out of the breathing organs (such as lungs or gills) and does not take place in all organisms, whereas respiration does.
* **Biochemical Level (Cellular Respiration):** Refers to cellular respiration. This is the metabolic process in all organisms where oxygen is combined with glucose to release water, carbon dioxide, and energy in the form of **ATP** (adenosine triphosphate). Cellular respiration takes place in individual cells within the organism, whereas physiological respiration involves the bulk transport of gases and other compounds between the organism and the outside air.

# Concepts & Structure

## Respiration

* **Definition & Timing:** Respiration is a continuous process that occurs constantly, both during the day and during the night. It must not be confused with photosynthesis, which requires sunlight and only takes place during daylight hours.
* **Key Terms & Ingredients:** The primary ingredients involved are glucose (food) and oxygen. In future chemical reactions, these ingredients will be formally defined as **reactants**.
* **Energy Release & Storage:** 
  * Respiration releases energy, which is stored in the chemical bonds of **ATP** (Adenosine Triphosphate) molecules as chemical potential energy.
  * ATP is not energy itself, but rather stores energy. When ATP molecules are broken down, they release this energy to drive other biological processes.
* **Energy in Equations:** 
  * In photosynthesis, sunlight energy is placed above the reaction arrow.
  * In respiration, energy is placed as a **product** because it is contained within the molecules produced (ATP).
* **By-products:** The resulting by-products of respiration are carbon dioxide and water.

## ACTIVITY: Does our breath contain carbon dioxide? (LB page 11)

### Preparation of Limewater

To perform this activity, limewater must be prepared beforehand using the following steps:

1. Place a few tablespoons of calcium hydroxide, $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$, into a clear $500\text{ ml}$ reagent bottle and fill it with water. Shake or stir to create a cloudy suspension.
2. Leave the suspension to settle for a few days. The clear liquid above the solid $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ sludge is a saturated solution of $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$, commonly known as **clear limewater**.
3. Carefully decant as much of the clear liquid as needed without disturbing the solid $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ sludge at the bottom.
4. To make more limewater, add more water, shake, and let it settle again. If the sludge dissolves completely, add more solid $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$.

### Apparatus & Safety Notes

* For health and hygiene reasons, ensure there is **one straw or rubber tube per learner**.
* **Safety Warning:** Always remove any needles from syringes before handing them out in class. Alternative options include using a bicycle pump or a balloon blower if syringes are unavailable.

# Revision Notes: Photosynthesis, Respiration, and Plant Growth

## 1. Seed Germination and Early Plant Growth
* **a)** Depending on the growing conditions, observers may notice a root and first leaves forming during early germination.
* **b)** Young bean plants might form small initial leaves, but without proper conditions, they will slowly start to die.
* **c)** Plants will die if leaves lack sufficient radiant energy (sunlight) to allow them to photosynthesise and produce glucose for growth and development.
* **d)** To ensure survival, plants must be placed in a location where they receive adequate radiant energy (sunlight) to photosynthesise.

## 2. Requirements for Photosynthesis
The essential raw materials required for photosynthesis are:
* Carbon dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$)
* Sunlight (Radiant energy)
* Water ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$)

## 3. Storage of Food and Starch Testing Procedures
* **a)** Plants convert produced glucose into starch because starch is easier to store. Consequently, leaves will indicate a high starch content.
* **b)** **Starch Test Procedure:**
  1. Remove chlorophyll by dipping the leaf into boiling water for $1\text{--}2$ minutes to soften and remove the waxy top layer.
  2. Place the leaf in alcohol and heat it over water to extract the chlorophyll.
  3. Remove the leaf and dip it back into hot water to soften it (as ethanol makes the leaf brittle).
  4. Add iodine solution onto the leaf. 
  5. **Observation:** Iodine changes color from orange-brown to dark blue-black in the presence of starch, indicating that starch is present.

## 4. Comparison of Photosynthesis and Respiration
* **Photosynthesis:** Occurs only during the day (requires sunlight energy).
* **Respiration:** Occurs all day and all night. All living organisms must undergo respiration to release energy from chemical potential energy to perform the seven life processes.

## 5. Experimental Analysis: Gas Production and Limewater Tests
* **a)** **Testing for Carbon Dioxide:** Collecting gas formed from a solution and bubbling it through limewater. If the limewater changes from clear to cloudy white, it indicates the presence of carbon dioxide.
* **b)** **Experimental Control:** Ensuring that no gas escapes before bubbling through the limewater guarantees that only the experimental gas affects the limewater, rather than atmospheric carbon dioxide.
* **c)** **Identifying Results:** Test tube B contains clear limewater that turns milky. This occurs because yeast and sugar in test tube A (Setup 2) ferment, releasing carbon dioxide that turns the limewater milky.
* **d)** **Role of Reactants:** Yeast requires sugar to react and form carbon dioxide. When sugar is unavailable, the reaction cannot occur, and carbon dioxide is not produced.

## Revision Notes: Photosynthesis and Cellular Respiration

### 6. Relationship Between Photosynthesis and Cellular Respiration

Photosynthesis and cellular respiration are complementary biological processes that cycle matter and energy in living systems.

* **Photosynthesis** captures light energy to convert carbon dioxide and water into glucose and oxygen.
* **Cellular Respiration** breaks down glucose in the presence of oxygen to release usable energy, producing carbon dioxide and water as byproducts.

---

### 7. Comparison Table: Photosynthesis vs. Respiration

| Characteristic | Photosynthesis | Respiration |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Starting ingredients/ requirements (reactants)** | Carbon dioxide, sunlight energy and water | Glucose and oxygen |
| **End products/what the process produces** | Glucose and oxygen | Energy, carbon dioxide and water |
| **Organisms in which this process takes place** | Green plants | All living organisms |
| **When this process takes place** | During the daytime/when there is sunlight | All the time as organisms continuously respire |
