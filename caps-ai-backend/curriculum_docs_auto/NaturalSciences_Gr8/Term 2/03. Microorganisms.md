# CHAPTER 1: BIODIVERSITY AND CLASSIFICATION OF MICRO-ORGANISMS

## Introduction
**Biodiversity** refers to the wide variety of plants, animals, and micro-organisms on Earth. Organisms that are too small to be seen with the naked eye are referred to as **micro-organisms**. 

Micro-organisms can be **unicellular** (consisting of one cell) or **multicellular** (consisting of many cells). While some micro-organisms are harmful and cause diseases, others are beneficial to the environment and humans (e.g., yeasts used to make bread).

### Key Terminology

| Term | Definition |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Unicellular** | An organism consisting of only one cell |
| **Multicellular** | An organism made up of many cells |
| **Biodiversity** | The variety of organisms found in an area or on Earth |

---

## The Classification of Organisms
Scientists classify organisms into specific groups to make them easier to study. There are five major groups known as **Kingdoms**:

1. **Kingdom Monera** (e.g., bacteria)
2. **Kingdom Protista**
3. **Kingdom Fungi**
4. **Kingdom Plantae**
5. **Kingdom Animalia**

# Viruses

Viruses are classified in a separate group rather than a kingdom because they exhibit both living and non-living characteristics.

## Key Terminology

| Term | Definition |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Capsid** | A protein coat surrounding the nucleic material of a virus. |
| **Acellular** | Non-cellular. |
| **Obligate parasite** | A parasitic organism that cannot complete its life-cycle without exploiting a suitable host (if an obligate parasite cannot obtain a host, it will fail to reproduce). |
| **Host** | An organism that harbours a parasite. |
| **Pathogenic** | An organism that causes disease. |
| **Bacteriophage** | A type of virus that infects bacteria; the word "phage" means to eat. |
| **Nucleoid** | An irregularly shaped region within the cell of a prokaryote that contains all or most of the genetic material. |

---

## Structure and Characteristics of Viruses

*   **Size:** Viruses are microscopic, ranging from $20 - 300\text{ nm}$, and can only be studied using an electron microscope.
*   **Composition:** Viruses consist of a core of either DNA or RNA enclosed by a protein coat called a **capsid**.

### Viruses: Characteristics
*   **Structure:** Occur in a variety of shapes and are **acellular** (not made of cells).
*   **Metabolism:** Cannot respire, feed, or excrete waste.
*   **Cellular Components:** Do not have a cytoplasm or membrane-bound organelles (e.g., mitochondria or nuclei).
*   **Genetic Material:** Contain either DNA or RNA, surrounded by a protective protein coat called a **capsid**. (Note: All other living organisms contain both DNA and RNA).
*   **Nutrition:** Do not have chlorophyll and cannot perform photosynthesis.
*   **Reproduction:** They are **obligate internal parasites**, meaning they cannot multiply without infecting a living host.
*   **Host Range:** Can infect bacteria, protists, plants, and animals. Viruses that specifically infect bacteria are called **bacteriophages**.
*   **Pathogenicity:** Viruses are **pathogenic** (disease-causing). In humans, they cause diseases such as HIV/AIDS, poliomyelitis, chickenpox, herpes, and influenza.
*   **Survival:** If a virus cannot find a host, it can become dormant.

### Bacteria
Bacteria belong to the Kingdom Monera and are found everywhere on Earth. While some are pathogenic (e.g., causing tuberculosis), most are useful.

#### Key Terminology

| Term | Definition |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Prokaryotic** | An organism where the nuclear material is **not enclosed** in a membrane. |
| **Eukaryotic** | Any single or multicellular group of organisms that have a **membrane-bound nucleus** containing genetic material. |
| **Flagellum** | A **whip-like**, protruding filament that helps cells or microorganisms move; plural is flagella. |
| **Autotrophic** | Organisms which can synthesize their own food, e.g., green plants, algae, and some bacteria. |
| **Heterotrophic** | Any organism that sources food from its environment because it cannot make its own food, e.g., animals, fungi, most bacteria. |
| **Saprophytic** | Plant or fungal microorganisms that feed on dead or decaying tissues of other organisms. |

### Key Concepts in Microbiology: Bacteria

#### Definitions
| Term | Definition |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Binary fission** | Asexual reproduction of a single cell which divides; the cell regenerates as two or more separate cells having the same chromosomal identities as the parent cell. |
| **Endospore** | A tough, protective, non-reproductive bacterial structure that contains DNA and cytoplasm; it lies dormant to survive unfavourable environmental conditions until it can germinate. |
| **Plasmid** | A small, circular, double-stranded DNA molecule that is distinct from a cell's chromosomal DNA. |

# Structural Characteristics of Bacteria

All bacteria share specific structural characteristics that define their biological nature.



![Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr8_Atoms.pdf-0004-00.png](images/Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr8_Atoms.pdf-0004-00.png)


### Key Structural Components

*   **Cell Wall:** A protective outer layer composed of polysaccharides.
*   **Slime Capsule:** An additional outer layer found in some bacteria that protects the organism from drying out (desiccation).
*   **Cell Membrane:** A membrane that encloses the cytoplasm.
*   **Cytoplasm:** The jelly-like substance inside the cell membrane.
*   **Organelles:** Bacteria do **not** contain any membrane-bound organelles.
*   **Nucleoid:** The region containing the DNA. The DNA exists as an irregular loop. Because there is no membrane surrounding this nuclear material, bacteria are classified as **prokaryotic**.
*   **Plasmid:** Small, circular, double-stranded DNA molecules found floating within the cytoplasm, separate from the main nucleoid DNA.
*   **Flagellum (plural: flagella):** A whip-like appendage used for locomotion. By rotating, the flagella propel the bacterium forward through liquid environments.

### Summary Table of Bacterial Structures

| Structure | Function |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Cell Wall** | Provides structural support and protection (made of polysaccharides). |
| **Slime Capsule** | Prevents the bacterium from drying out. |
| **Cell Membrane** | Regulates the movement of substances in and out of the cell. |
| **Nucleoid** | Contains the genetic material (DNA) in an irregular loop. |
| **Plasmid** | Small, circular DNA providing extra genetic information. |
| **Flagellum** | Enables movement (propulsion) in liquid. |
| **Ribosomes** | Sites for protein synthesis (present in the cytoplasm). |

# Nutrition and Reproduction of Bacteria; Introduction to Protista

## 1. Nutrition of Bacteria

Bacteria are classified based on how they obtain their nutrients:

### Autotrophic Bacteria
These bacteria can manufacture their own food.
*   **Photosynthetic bacteria:** Use sunlight energy to produce food.
*   **Chemosynthetic bacteria:** Obtain energy from chemical processes.

### Heterotrophic Bacteria
These bacteria cannot manufacture their own food and must obtain it from external sources.
*   **Parasitic bacteria:** Obtain food from other living organisms.
*   **Saprotrophic bacteria:** Act as decomposers by obtaining food from dead organic plant and animal matter.
*   **Mutualistic bacteria:** Form a symbiotic relationship with another organism where both parties benefit.

---

## 2. Reproduction of Bacteria

Bacteria reproduce rapidly under favourable conditions through a simple form of cell division.

*   **Binary Fission:** The process by which a single bacterial cell divides into two identical daughter cells.

### Characteristics of the Protista

Protists are defined by the following biological characteristics:

*   **Cellular Structure:** They are simple unicellular or multicellular eukaryotic organisms.
*   **Tissue Differentiation:** They possess no tissue differentiation.
*   **Habitat:** They are found mainly in water.
*   **Nutrition:** They can be autotrophic (producing their own food) or heterotrophic (consuming other organisms).
*   **Size:** They are usually microscopic, though some can reach several meters in length (e.g., seaweeds).
*   **Locomotion:** Some are sessile or free-floating, while others move using:
    *   **Flagella:** Whip-like tails (e.g., *Euglena*).
    *   **Pseudopodia:** False feet (e.g., *Amoeba*).
*   **Reproduction:** They can reproduce both sexually and asexually.

*   **Reproduction:** Fungi reproduce both sexually and asexually.

    *   **Multicellular fungi:** Reproduce asexually via **spores**.

# Micro-organisms and Ecological Relationships

## 1. Micro-organisms as Producers in Food Chains
Certain micro-organisms, such as autotrophic bacteria, phytoplankton, and algae, are capable of manufacturing their own food through the process of **photosynthesis**.

*   **Function:** They produce carbohydrates which serve as a food source for consumers.
*   **Ecological Role:** They form the first link in a food chain.
*   **By-product:** Oxygen, a waste product of photosynthesis, is released and made available to other organisms for respiration.

## 2. The Role of Micro-organisms as Decomposers
Decomposers are essential for recycling nutrients within an ecosystem.

*   **Primary Decomposers:** Bacteria and fungi.
*   **Process:** They break down dead plant and animal remains, returning vital nutrients to the soil.
*   **Definition:** Organisms that break down dead organic matter to obtain nutrients are known as **saprophytes**.

## 3. The Role of Bacteria in the Nitrogen Cycle
Bacteria are critical in maintaining the nitrogen balance in the environment.

*   **Nitrogen Fixation:** Free-living bacteria convert atmospheric nitrogen into ammonia and nitrates.
*   **Plant Dependency:** Higher plants cannot absorb atmospheric nitrogen directly; they rely on bacteria to convert it into usable nitrates.
*   **Specialized Relationships:** Some plants form symbiotic relationships with **nitrogen-fixing bacteria**.
*   **Denitrification:** When plants and animals die, **de-nitrifying bacteria** convert nitrogen compounds back into atmospheric nitrogen through a process called **denitrification**.

## 4. Symbiotic Relationships
**Symbiosis** is defined as the living together of two or more species of organisms. These relationships may benefit one or both members, or be beneficial to one while harmful to the other.

### Key Terminology

| Term | Definition |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Mutualism** | A symbiotic relationship where both organisms benefit. |
| **Commensalism** | A symbiotic relationship where one organism benefits without harming or affecting the other organism. |

### Types of Symbiosis

Symbiosis refers to close, long-term biological interactions between two different biological organisms. There are three primary types:

*   **Mutualism:** Both organisms benefit from the relationship (e.g., lichens).
*   **Commensalism:** One species benefits while the other neither benefits nor is harmed.
*   **Parasitism:** One species benefits while the other is harmed.

### Focus: Lichens

Lichens are a prime example of a mutualistic relationship. 

**The Biological Challenge:**
Algae require a moist environment to survive and cannot typically survive on dry land. 

**The Mutualistic Solution:**
*   **The Fungus:** Provides the alga with protection from the harsh external environment.
*   **The Alga:** Being photosynthetic, the alga produces food. Since fungi cannot produce their own food, they obtain nutrients from the alga.

Because both organisms provide a service that the other requires for survival, this is classified as a mutualistic relationship.

# Symbiotic Relationships in Nature

## 1. The Relationship Between Nitrogen-Fixing Bacteria and Plants

Plants require nitrogen to manufacture proteins, but they cannot absorb nitrogen directly from the atmosphere. They must obtain it in the form of **nitrates**.

*   **The Process:** Certain soil bacteria convert free atmospheric nitrogen into nitrates that plants can absorb.
*   **The Relationship:** These bacteria live in special nodules found on the roots of leguminous plants (e.g., beans and peas).
*   **Mutual Benefit:**
    *   **The Bacteria:** Receive a habitat (a place to live), carbohydrates, and water from the plant.
    *   **The Plant:** Receives essential nitrates produced by the bacteria.

## 2. The Relationship Between *E. coli* and the Human Intestine

Not all bacteria found in the human body are harmful. Some, such as *Escherichia coli* (*E. coli*), exist in a mutualistic relationship within the human gut.

*   **The Process:** *E. coli* lives on the undigested remains of food in the human intestine. In return, the bacteria produce **Vitamin K**.
*   **The Benefit:** Vitamin K is essential for the human body as it plays a critical role in blood clotting.
*   **Mutual Benefit:** Both the human host and the bacteria benefit from this interaction.

### Rabies: Symptoms and Management

#### Symptoms of Rabies
Initial symptoms include:
* Nausea
* Fatigue or tiredness

These are followed by an **agitated phase** characterized by:
* Convulsive seizures
* Excessive salivation
* **Hydrophobia:** Fear of water
* Difficulty in swallowing and breathing

**Note:** Once symptoms appear, there is no known cure. Death typically occurs within 10 days due to heart failure or respiratory failure.

#### Management and Prevention
Rabies can be managed through the following strategies:
* Vaccination of dogs and livestock in endemic areas.
* Immunization of people in high-risk occupations (e.g., veterinarians).
* Immunization of travellers visiting areas where the disease is prevalent.
* Training of health workers and veterinarians.
* Culling (destroying) of animals confirmed to be infected.

#### Treatment and Safety
* Because the disease is difficult to treat, prevention is critical.
* Avoid contact with animals infected with rabies.
* Do not touch wild animals that suddenly appear tame.
* If contact occurs with an animal behaving suspiciously, seek immediate medical attention.

# HIV/AIDS: Transmission, Effects, and Impact

## 1. Transmission of the HI Virus
The HI virus is primarily spread through the transfer of infected body fluids (such as semen and blood) from an infected person to another.

**Methods of Transmission:**
*   Sexual intercourse.
*   Blood transfusions using untested blood.
*   Sharing contaminated syringes (e.g., among drug users).
*   From an infected mother to the foetus during pregnancy or birth.

**Important Note:** The virus is **not** transferred through the air, saliva, or by shaking hands with an infected person.

---

## 2. Effects of HIV/AIDS on the Individual
The progression of the virus affects the body in distinct phases:

*   **Initial Phase:** Often characterized by a lack of symptoms, which can last for several years.
*   **Early Symptomatic Phase:** Flu-like symptoms may appear, including:
    *   Headaches and fever.
    *   Tiredness (fatigue).
    *   Swelling of lymph glands in the armpits, throat, or groin.
*   **Immune System Weakening:** As the immune system declines, the body becomes susceptible to:
    *   Repeated cold-sore infections.
    *   Prolonged fevers and night sweats.
    *   Chronic diarrhoea.
    *   Extreme weight loss.
*   **Opportunistic Infections:** A weakened immune system allows secondary infections to take hold, such as:
    *   Respiratory infections and pneumonia.
    *   Tuberculosis.
    *   Epilepsy and dementia.
    *   Skin and lymph cancers.
*   **Final Phase (AIDS):** The advanced stage of HIV infection. Death often occurs during this phase due to the body's inability to fight off secondary infections.

---

## 3. Impact on Families
HIV/AIDS creates significant social and economic challenges for families:
*   **Loss of Income:** If the primary breadwinner becomes ill, the family may fall into poverty.
*   **Mother-to-Child Transmission:** The virus can be passed to an unborn child during pregnancy.
*   **Orphanhood:** If both parents are infected and pass away, their children become orphans.
*   **Family Fragmentation:** Siblings may be separated from one another following the death of their parents.

---

## 4. Impact on the Economy
The disease has broad implications for a country's economic stability:
*   **Labour Force Reduction:** The disease is most prevalent among young, working-age individuals, which significantly reduces the available labour force, particularly in sectors like mining.
*   **Economic Burden:** The national costs associated with medical treatment, care, and support systems are extremely high.

### Definition and Causes
Cholera is an infectious disease caused by the bacterium *Vibrio cholerae*. It is typically found in environments characterized by:
*   Overcrowding.
*   Unsafe drinking water.
*   A lack of proper sanitation.

The primary symptoms of cholera include:
*   Watery diarrhoea (runny tummy), which leads to severe dehydration.
*   Vomiting.

### Management and Prevention
To manage and prevent the spread of cholera, the following measures should be implemented:

| **Patient Care** | Provide infected individuals with fluids containing added electrolytes. |
| **Medical Intervention** | Severely infected patients must be placed on a drip. |
| **Hygiene/Sterilization** | Dispose of bed linen and clothes of infected people. |
| **Disinfection** | Wash items touched by patients in hot water and sterilize them using chlorine bleach. |
| **Education** | Educate people living in affected areas about the importance of hygiene and encourage them to boil water before drinking. |

*   **Entry:** Spores are either inhaled by the animal or enter the body through open wounds.
*   **Infection:** Once inside the body, the bacteria enter the bloodstream and multiply rapidly.
*   **Toxin Release:** The bacteria release potent toxins that cause tissue breakdown, internal bleeding, and eventually death.

#### Human Infection
Humans can become infected through exposure to infected animals or products derived from them.

#### Symptoms in Humans
*   Severe breathing problems and shock.
*   Inflammation of the gastro-intestinal tract.
*   A painless skin ulcer characterized by a black necrotic (dead tissue) area in the center.

### Management of Anthrax

Anthrax is a serious disease that requires strict management protocols to prevent spread:

*   **Vaccination:** Routine vaccination of stock animals.
*   **Outbreak Response:**
    *   Isolate infected animals immediately.
    *   Treat infected animals with antibiotics.
    *   Vaccinate all other animals that have been in contact with the infected ones.
*   **Disposal of Carcasses:** Dead animal bodies must be burned. This is critical to destroy spores, which can survive in the environment for up to 90 years.
*   **Human Safety:**
    *   Humans in contact with anthrax must wash thoroughly with antimicrobial soap.
    *   Clothing worn during contact must be burned.
    *   Human remains of those who died from anthrax should be cremated to prevent further spread.

### Malaria: Consequences and Management

#### Consequences of Untreated Malaria
If left untreated, malaria can progress to severe stages, potentially leading to a coma and death.

#### Economic Impact
Malaria significantly impacts the economy in the following ways:
* **Loss of Income:** If a breadwinner is unable to work or passes away due to the disease, it often results in household poverty.
* **Cost of Treatment:** Malaria treatment is expensive, making it unaffordable for many people living in underdeveloped countries.

#### Management and Prevention
The most effective way to manage malaria is to prevent mosquito bites in endemic areas. Recommended strategies include:
* Staying indoors between sunset and sunrise.
* Installing gauze on doors and windows to prevent mosquito entry.
* Sleeping under mosquito nets.
* Applying insect repellents to exposed skin.
* Wearing long-sleeved clothing and long pants when outdoors at night.
* Eliminating standing water (e.g., in drains, ponds, gutters, and old tyres) where mosquitoes breed.

#### Medical and Government Intervention
* **Prophylaxis and Treatment:** Anti-malarial drugs can be taken as a preventative measure before entering a malaria-prone area, and are also used to treat those already infected.
* **Public Health:** Governments are responsible for providing healthcare facilities (such as clinics) and controlling mosquito populations through the use of insecticides like DDT.

#### Overview
Thrush is a fungal infection caused by the *Candida* fungus. While it can manifest as oral thrush (as seen in the image above), it also commonly occurs as vaginal thrush.

#### Vaginal Thrush
Vaginal thrush is common in pregnant women and women using oral contraceptives. 
*   **Contributing factors:** Wearing tight-fitting pants and underwear.
*   **Symptoms:** Severe itching, a burning sensation during urination, and a greyish-white vaginal discharge.

#### Factors Contributing to the Development of Thrush
The *Candida* fungus thrives in specific environments. Factors that increase the risk of infection include:
*   **Weakened immune system:** Caused by HIV/AIDS or chemotherapy.
*   **Diabetes:** The fungus thrives on high blood sugar levels.

*   **Hygiene:** Babies left in wet nappies for prolonged periods.
*   **Lifestyle:** Poor health resulting from stress, lack of sleep, and a diet rich in sugars.

## 2. Athlete’s Foot
Athlete’s foot is a fungal infection that primarily affects the skin between the toes and on the arches of the feet.

*   **Cause:** It is caused by the fungus *Tinea pedis*.
*   **Mechanism:** The fungus feeds on the keratin (a type of protein) found in the skin. This results in flaky and cracked skin. These cracks can create an entry point for bacteria, leading to secondary infections.
*   **Environment:** The fungus thrives in warm, moist environments.
*   **Transmission:** It is commonly contracted by walking barefoot in public areas, such as:
    *   Public showers.
    *   Public swimming pool areas.
    *   Changing rooms.

### Athlete's Foot: Treatment and Management

Athlete's foot is a fungal infection of the skin. To treat and manage the condition effectively, follow these guidelines:

*   **Keep the area dry:** Fungi thrive in moist environments.
*   **Footwear choices:** Wear open sandals during hot weather.

*   **Hygiene practices:** Wash feet thoroughly and ensure they are dried well, especially between the toes.
*   **Medication:** Apply antifungal ointments or powders to the infected areas as directed.
*   **Prevention:** Wear slip-slops in public showers to avoid contracting or spreading the infection.

### The Response of Plants Against an Infecting Micro-organism

Plants utilize two primary lines of defense to protect themselves from invading pathogens:

1.  **First Line of Defence:** Includes physical barriers such as the waxy cuticle, bark, and closely packed epidermal cells. If a plant is injured, it produces sticky gums and resins to seal the wound and prevent infection.
2.  **Second Line of Defence:** Activated when a plant becomes infected by a pathogen. The plant triggers a **natural immune response** by releasing chemical compounds, such as **salicylic acid**. These compounds are transported via the phloem to unaffected cells, which then produce their own chemical defenses to protect themselves.

### The Response of Animals Against an Infecting Micro-organism

Animals possess two types of immunity:
*   **Natural immunity:** Present at birth.
*   **Acquired immunity:** Develops after exposure to pathogens.

#### The First Line of Defence in Animals
The body employs several mechanisms to prevent pathogens from entering the system:
*   **Skin:** A multi-layered physical barrier.
*   **Tears:** Contain antiseptic properties.
*   **Mucus:** Lines air passages to trap pathogens.
*   **Saliva:** Contains enzymes (lysozyme).
*   **Ear wax:** Located in the ear canal.
*   **Stomach:** Contains hydrochloric acid and enzymes to destroy pathogens.

# The Second Line of Defence

If pathogens successfully bypass the body's initial barriers, the second line of defence is activated through two distinct responses:

### 1. Primary Response
This response aims to destroy the pathogen and prevent its spread. It is characterized by:
*   **Inflammation:** Swelling and redness in the affected local area.
*   **Fever:** An increase in body temperature to inhibit pathogen growth.

### 2. Secondary Response
This response activates the immune system to perform two critical functions:
*   Destruction of invading pathogens.
*   Retention of "memory" regarding destroyed pathogens to reduce or prevent future re-infection.

The immune system relies on two primary groups of white blood cells: **lymphocytes** and **phagocytes**.

---

## Lymphocytes

Lymphocytes are located in the tonsils, lymph glands, spleen, and blood. There are two main types:
1.  **B-lymphocytes**
2.  **T-lymphocytes**

### B-lymphocytes and Natural Immunity

*   **Antigens:** Special proteins found on the surface of pathogens.
*   **Antibodies:** Special proteins produced by B-lymphocytes that recognize and bind to specific antigens.
*   **Mechanism:** When B-lymphocytes encounter an antigen, they produce antibodies to destroy the germ. If the same germ enters the body again, the immune system responds rapidly. This process is known as **natural immunity**.

### Immune System Mechanisms

#### Functions of Antibodies
Antibodies protect the body by destroying germs through the following methods:
*   **Lysis:** Causing bacterial cells to burst.
*   **Opsonisation:** Labelling germs so that phagocytes can easily identify and ingest them.
*   **Agglutination:** Making germs clump together for easier recognition.
*   **Neutralisation:** Neutralising bacterial toxins.

#### T-lymphocytes
T-lymphocytes are primarily located in the lymph glands and are categorised into two types:
1.  **CD4 cells (Helper T-cells):** Responsible for initiating the immune response.
2.  **Killer T-cells:** Responsible for destroying body cells that have been infected by viruses or parasites.

---

#### Phagocytes
Phagocytes are specialised cells, such as macrophages, that identify and eliminate bacteria.

**The Process of Phagocytosis:**
1.  The phagocyte produces **pseudopodia** (false feet) that flow around the bacteria.
2.  The bacteria are engulfed into a vacuole.
3.  Vacuoles containing digestive enzymes, known as **lysosomes**, fuse with the vacuole containing the bacteria.
4.  The enzymes destroy the bacteria.

---

#### Vaccinations
A **vaccine** is a suspension containing dead, weakened, or fragmented micro-organisms (or their toxins). Its purpose is to stimulate the lymphocytes to produce antibodies.

*   **Vaccination/Immunisation:** The process of administering a vaccine (via injection or orally) to prevent disease.
*   **Artificially Acquired Active Immunity:** The resulting state where antibodies remain in the blood, providing long-lasting protection against specific diseases.
*   **Common Examples:** Children are typically vaccinated against measles, mumps, and rubella.

# Biotechnology

Biotechnology is the application of micro-organisms to produce substances that are beneficial to humans. Common applications include:
*   **Medicines:** Antibiotics and insulin.
*   **Food products:** Maas (fermented milk), bread, wine, and cheese.

## The Making of Antibiotics

Natural antibiotics are produced by specific fungi, such as *Penicillium* (a type of mould).

**The Production Process:**
1.  **Collection:** The mould is collected from the skins of fruits.
2.  **Cultivation:** The mould is placed in a vat containing sugar and amino acids at a temperature of approximately $25^\circ\text{C}$.
3.  **Growth:** Under these conditions, the mould grows and multiplies rapidly.
4.  **Extraction:** After approximately five days, the penicillin produced by the mould is harvested and purified for medical use.

## The Making of Insulin

Insulin is a hormone produced by the pancreas that regulates blood glucose levels. When the pancreas fails to function correctly, a person develops **diabetes mellitus**. Patients with this condition must manage their sugar intake and require daily insulin injections.

### Genetic Engineering Process
Bacteria are used as biological factories to produce human insulin. The process involves modifying the bacterial DNA:

1.  **Plasmid Removal:** A plasmid (a small, circular DNA molecule) is removed from a bacterium.
2.  **Cutting:** Enzymes are used to "cut" the plasmid open.
3.  **Insertion:** A specific DNA fragment (containing the instructions for making insulin) is inserted into the cut plasmid.
4.  **Recombinant DNA:** The resulting structure is known as recombinant DNA. This modified bacterium can now produce human insulin.

### Production of Insulin using Genetic Engineering

The process of using bacteria to produce human insulin involves the following steps:

1.  A plasmid is removed from a bacterium.
2.  The plasmid is cut open using an enzyme.
3.  A piece of DNA containing the gene for making insulin is extracted from a chromosome taken from a human pancreas cell.
4.  The human DNA is joined to the bacterial plasmid to form **recombinant DNA**.
5.  The recombinant DNA is inserted back into a bacterium.
6.  The genetically engineered bacteria are grown in large vats containing nutrients.
7.  The DNA in the bacteria instructs the bacteria to produce insulin.
8.  The insulin is then extracted and purified.

**Note:** The most commonly used bacterium for this process is *E. coli*.

*   **A:** Capsule
*   **B:** Cell wall
*   **C:** Cell membrane
*   **D:** Cytoplasm

#### 1.4.2 Function of part E (DNA/Nucleoid)
*   **E** represents the bacterial DNA (nucleoid). Its function is to control the cellular activities and contain the genetic information of the bacterium.

#### 1.4.3 Manufacturing of Insulin using part F (Plasmid)
*   **F** represents a plasmid.
*   The plasmid is extracted from the bacterial cell.
*   The human gene responsible for insulin production is isolated and inserted into the plasmid using restriction enzymes.
*   The recombinant plasmid is re-inserted into the bacterium.

    *   Not completing the full course of prescribed antibiotics, which allows surviving bacteria to develop resistance.

### Activity 3: Nitrogen Use

#### Key Concepts
1. **Nitrogen Fixation:** Plants cannot utilize nitrogen directly from the atmosphere. It must be converted into the form of **nitrates** for plant absorption.
2. **Conversion Processes:**
   * **Lightning:** Converts atmospheric nitrogen and oxygen into nitrates.
   * **Bacteria:** Free-living soil bacteria and root nodule bacteria are capable of fixing nitrogen into nitrates.
3. **Mutualism:** A lichen represents a mutualistic relationship between a fungus and an algal species.
4. **The Nitrogen Cycle:** After plants and animals die, decomposition bacteria break down organic matter, returning nitrogen to the atmosphere.
5. **Mycorrhizal Fungi:**
   * These are filamentous fungi that penetrate plant roots.
   * **Benefit:** By penetrating the roots, the fungus increases the surface area for absorption. This allows the plant to absorb more nutrients and water, resulting in a faster growth rate compared to plants without this fungal association.
