## 2.1 The Milky Way Galaxy

### Introduction
At the darkest places on Earth, far away from city lights, you can see thousands of stars at night using nothing but your eyes. In fact, there are many more stars in the sky which are too faint for us to see.

### Definitions
* **Galaxy:** A massive collection of stars, gas, and dust all held together by gravity.
* **Galaxy Disk:** The flattened, rotating part of a spiral galaxy that contains most of the stars and interstellar matter.
* **Galaxy Bulge:** The tightly packed group of stars found in the center of most spiral galaxies.
* **Spiral Arm:** The curved regions of stars and gas that extend from the center of a spiral galaxy.

### The Milky Way
* All of the individual stars that you can see are members of our **Milky Way Galaxy**.
* The Milky Way contains approximately $200 \times 10^{9}$ (200 billion) stars.
* Our Sun is just one of these stars.
* From Earth, the Milky Way appears as a bright, hazy band of light across the sky, interspersed with dark, dusty patches.

### Historical Naming
* **Greek origin:** Called *Galaxies Kuklos*, meaning "Milky Circle," because they thought it looked like milk spilled across the sky.
* **Roman origin:** Changed the name to *Via Lactea*, meaning "Milky Road" or "Milky Way."

## The Milky Way Galaxy

The Milky Way is the galaxy that contains our Solar System. Because we are located inside it, we cannot take a photograph of the entire galaxy from the outside. The images we have are reconstructions based on scientific observations and data.

### Structure and Composition
*   **Spiral Arms:** The galaxy features spiral arms, which appear bluish due to the presence of hot, young stars, as well as gas and dust.
*   **Dust Lanes:** Dark wisps visible in the galaxy are regions filled with dense dust.
*   **The Centre:** The central region appears orangey in colour. This is because the stars located at the centre are older and cooler than the young, blue stars found in the spiral arms.

### Major Spiral Arms
Scientists have identified five major spiral arms in the Milky Way:
1.  Norma Arm
2.  Scutum-Crux Arm
3.  Sagittarius Arm
4.  Perseus Arm
5.  Cygnus Arm

### Location of the Sun
Our Sun is situated in a smaller spiral arm known as the **Orion (or Local) Arm**. This arm is positioned between the Sagittarius Arm and the Perseus Arm, placing our Sun approximately halfway out from the centre of the galaxy.

---

## Key Facts and Figures

*   **Orbital Motion:** All stars in the galaxy revolve around the galactic centre.
*   **Orbital Speed:** The Sun travels around the centre of the Milky Way at a speed of $250 \text{ km/s}$.
*   **Orbital Period:** It takes the Sun approximately $225 \text{ million years}$ to complete one full orbit around the centre of the galaxy.
*   **Size:** The Milky Way is massive, measuring approximately $950\,000\,000\,000\,000\,000 \text{ km}$ across.

---

## Did You Know?
Even though our Solar System orbits the centre of the Milky Way at thousands of kilometres per hour, the scale of the galaxy is so vast that it takes over $200 \text{ million years}$ to complete a single orbit.

# The Milky Way Galaxy

## Structure of the Milky Way
The Milky Way Galaxy is a spiral galaxy with a distinct shape that can be compared to a giant fried egg. Its structure consists of two main parts:

*   **The Bulge:** The central, thicker lump of the galaxy.
*   **The Disk:** The flatter, thinner region that extends outward from the bulge.

The galaxy is approximately 100 times wider than it is thick.

### The Milky Way Galaxy

#### Key Concepts
*   **The Sagittarius Arm:** The thin band of light visible in the night sky is composed of stars located in the Sagittarius arm of the Milky Way.
*   **Globular Clusters:** These are vast, spherical collections of hundreds of thousands of ancient stars, held together by gravity.
    *   The Milky Way contains approximately 160 globular clusters.
    *   The stars within these clusters are among the oldest in the galaxy, some dating back nearly to the age of the Universe itself (approx. 12.5 billion years old).
    *   In comparison, our Sun is approximately 4.5 billion years old.

### Activity: Draw the Milky Way

#### Materials Needed
*   Black paper
*   White crayon, pencil, or paint
*   Glue (optional)
*   Glitter or sand (optional)
*   Newspaper (to protect your workspace)
*   White or silver pencil/pen (for labelling)
*   Sticker (optional)

#### Instructions
1.  **Sketch:** Draw or paint the Milky Way on the black paper. Use the provided reference image as a guide. Ensure you include the five major spiral arms, the smaller arms (including the Orion Arm), and the central bulge.
2.  **Add Texture:** If using glitter or sand, apply glue along the spiral arms and the central bulge.
3.  **Represent Stars:** Scatter the glitter or sand over the glued areas. Each grain represents a star within our galaxy.
4.  **Clean Up:** Tilt the paper over the newspaper to remove any excess glitter or sand.
5.  **Label:** Use a white or silver pen to label each of the major spiral arms of the Milky Way.
6.  **Locate the Sun:** Place a sticker or mark a point on the Orion Arm, approximately halfway out from the central bulge, to represent the position of the Sun.

### Mapping the Milky Way

#### Concept: Understanding Galactic Structure
Astronomers determine the shape of the Milky Way from within by:
1. Observing the sky in all directions.
2. Counting the number of stars visible.
3. Measuring the distance to each star.
4. Constructing a three-dimensional map of the galaxy.

**Challenge:** Astronomers must account for interstellar dust, which dims the optical light emitted by stars, making it difficult to see through the galaxy.

#### The Alpha Centauri System

### 2.3 Light years, light hours and light minutes

#### The Need for New Units
Distances within our solar system and beyond are vast. For example:
*   **The Moon:** Average distance of $384\ 400\text{ km}$.
*   **Venus:** Closest distance of approximately $42\text{ million km}$.
*   **The Sun:** Approximately $150\text{ million km}$ away.
*   **Pluto:** Closest distance of $4.3\text{ billion km}$.

Using kilometres to describe these distances results in numbers that are impractical to work with. Just as we use metres instead of centimetres to measure the distance to school (e.g., $7.5\text{ km}$ instead of $7\ 500\ 000\text{ cm}$), astronomers use units based on the speed of light to make calculations smaller and easier to manage.

#### New Vocabulary
*   **Light minute:** The distance light travels in one minute.
*   **Light hour:** The distance light travels in one hour.
*   **Light year:** The distance light travels in one year.

## The Speed of Light

Light travels at an extremely high velocity, significantly faster than any of the objects listed above.

*   **Speed in km/h:** $\approx 1\,080\,000\,000\text{ km/h}$ (1 080 million km/h)
*   **Speed in m/s:** $299\,792\,458\text{ m/s}$

### Conversion Note
To convert speed from $\text{km/h}$ to $\text{m/s}$, use the following conversion factor:
$$1\text{ km/h} = \frac{1\,000\text{ m}}{3\,600\text{ s}} = \frac{1}{3.6}\text{ m/s}$$

Therefore, to convert $\text{km/h}$ to $\text{m/s}$, divide by $3.6$:
$$\text{Speed in m/s} = \frac{\text{Speed in km/h}}{3.6}$$

# Measuring Distances in Space

## Key Concepts
In astronomy, we use units based on the speed of light to measure vast distances. Although these units contain the words "hour," "minute," or "year," they are **units of distance**, not time.

### 1. Light Hours and Light Minutes
*   **Definition:** A **light hour** is the distance that light travels in one hour.
*   **Definition:** A **light minute** is the distance that light travels in one minute.
*   **Application:** These units are primarily used to measure distances within our solar system.

### 2. Light Years
*   **Definition:** A **light year** is the distance that light travels in one year.
*   **Scale:** One light year is approximately $10 \times 10^{12}$ kilometres (10 trillion kilometres).
*   **Application:** These units are used to measure the immense distances between stars and galaxies.

---

## The Relationship Between Distance and Time
Because these units are based on the speed of light, they provide a direct link between distance and the time it takes for light to travel from a source to an observer.

*   If an object is $1$ light year away, it takes light $1$ year to reach your eyes.
*   If an object is $1$ light hour away, it takes light $1$ hour to reach your eyes.
*   If an object is $1$ light minute away, it takes light $1$ minute to reach your eyes.

---

## Why We Don't Notice the Delay
Light travels at an incredible speed. In everyday life, we perceive light from local sources (like a lamp) as being instantaneous.

*   **Example:** If you are standing $1$ metre away from a lamp, it takes approximately $3 \times 10^{-9}$ seconds (three billionths of a second) for the light to reach your eyes. Because this duration is so small, the human brain cannot perceive the delay.

# 2.4 What is beyond the Milky Way Galaxy?

## Key Concepts and Definitions

*   **Galaxy:** A massive system consisting of stars, gas, and dust held together by gravity.
*   **Galaxy Group:** A small collection of galaxies bound together by gravity.
*   **Galaxy Cluster:** A large structure consisting of hundreds to thousands of galaxies.
*   **Filament:** A massive, thread-like structure that forms the boundaries of large voids in the universe.
*   **Void:** A vast, empty space between filaments that contains very few or no galaxies.
*   **Universe:** The totality of all space, time, matter, and energy.

---

## The Milky Way Galaxy
Our galaxy, the Milky Way, is an average-sized spiral galaxy.
*   **Size:** Approximately $100\,000$ light years across.
*   **Composition:** Contains around $200$ billion stars, along with vast amounts of gas and dust.
*   **Context:** It is one of approximately $100$ to $200$ billion galaxies in the Universe.

---

## Andromeda: Our Neighbour
*   **Distance:** Andromeda is $2.5$ million light years away from the Milky Way.
*   **Implication:** Because light takes $2.5$ million years to travel from Andromeda to Earth, the light we see today was emitted before humans existed on Earth.

# Types of Galaxies

There are five main types of galaxies. While you do not need to memorize these names for assessment purposes, they are provided for your interest.

### The Five Main Types
*   **Spiral:** Galaxies with a rotating disk of stars and interstellar medium, often with a central bulge.
*   **Barred spiral:** Similar to a spiral galaxy, but with a central "bar" structure composed of stars.
*   **Elliptical:** Galaxies that have an approximately ellipsoidal shape and a smooth, nearly featureless brightness profile.
*   **Lenticular:** A lens-shaped galaxy that is an intermediate type between an elliptical and a spiral galaxy.
*   **Irregular:** A galaxy that does not have a distinct regular shape, unlike a spiral or an elliptical galaxy.

## The Structure of the Universe
The universe is organized in a hierarchical structure, ranging from our home planet to the vast observable universe.



![Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr8_Beyond-the-solar-system.pdf-0017-00.png](images/Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr8_Beyond-the-solar-system.pdf-0017-00.png)

### Key Concepts and Definitions

*   **Galaxy Groups:** Most galaxies are not isolated; they are found gathered together in "neighbourhoods" known as galaxy groups.
*   **The Local Group:** This is the specific galaxy group that contains our own galaxy, the Milky Way.
*   **Galaxy Clusters:** These are larger structures than groups, spanning tens of millions of light years. They can contain hundreds or even thousands of individual galaxies.
*   **Superclusters:** These are massive structures formed when multiple galaxy clusters come together. 
    *   *Note:* Our own Local Group is part of the **Virgo supercluster**.
*   **Gravity:** This is the fundamental force that holds galaxies together within groups, clusters, and superclusters.

---

## The Observable Universe
The observable universe represents the portion of the universe that we can see from Earth. Astronomers use powerful tools like the Hubble Space Telescope to peer into deep space.

The **Hubble Extreme Deep Field** is currently the most distant picture of the universe ever taken. By focusing the Hubble Telescope on a tiny, seemingly empty patch of sky, astronomers discovered approximately **5500 galaxies** of various shapes, sizes, and colours.

# The Observable Universe

## Structure of the Universe
On the largest scales, the Universe resembles a giant bath sponge. Its structure is defined by two main features:
*   **Filaments:** Thin walls where galaxy clusters are arranged.
*   **Voids:** Huge gaps between filaments that contain very few galaxies.



![Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr8_Beyond-the-solar-system.pdf-0018-00.png](images/Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr8_Beyond-the-solar-system.pdf-0018-00.png)

It conceptually demonstrates the vast scale of space by showing how smaller celestial bodies are nested within increasingly larger structures, helping learners visualize their position within the universe.



## The Observable Universe
The *observable Universe* is the portion of the Universe from which light has had enough time to reach Earth since the beginning of the Universe.

### Key Concepts
*   **Age of the Universe:** Astronomers estimate the Universe is approximately $13.7$ billion years old.
*   **Light Travel Time:** Because the Universe is $13.7$ billion years old, we can theoretically see objects up to $13.7$ billion light years away in any direction.
*   **The Expansion Factor:** While one might assume the diameter of the observable Universe is $2 \times 13.7 = 27.4$ billion light years, this is incorrect. 
*   **Actual Size:** The observable Universe is estimated to be $93$ billion light years in diameter.

### Why is it larger than expected?
The size is much larger than the age of the Universe suggests because the Universe is **expanding**. 
1. Galaxies are moving further away from Earth as space itself expands.
2. We are able to see galaxies that are now very far away because, at the time they emitted the light we see today, they were much closer to Earth.
3. The size of the *whole* Universe remains unknown, as it includes regions too far away for their light to have reached us yet.

# Summary: Beyond the Solar System

## Key Concepts

*   **Galaxy:** A collection of millions or billions of stars, together with gas and dust, held together by gravity.
*   **Galaxy Diversity:** Galaxies exist in a wide variety of shapes and sizes.
*   **The Milky Way:** Our home galaxy is a spiral galaxy containing approximately $200$ billion stars. Our Sun is one of these stars.
*   **Nearest Star:** After the Sun, the nearest star to Earth is Alpha Centauri, which is the brighter of the two "pointer stars" in the Southern Cross constellation.
*   **Measuring Cosmic Distances:** Because distances in space are immense, astronomers use units based on the speed of light:
    *   **Light minute:** The distance light travels in one minute.
    *   **Light hour:** The distance light travels in one hour.
    *   **Light year:** The distance light travels in one year.
*   **The Universe:** There are many galaxies beyond the Milky Way. Astronomers estimate the diameter of the observable Universe to be $93$ billion light years.

## 1. Measuring Cosmic Distances
In space, distances are so vast that standard units like kilometers are impractical. We use **light years** to measure these distances.

*   **Definition:** A light year is the distance that light travels in one year.
*   **Conversion:** $1 \text{ light year} \approx 10 \text{ trillion km}$ ($10 \times 10^{12} \text{ km}$).
*   **Example:** Our nearest star, Alpha Centauri, is located at a vast distance from Earth.

## 2. The Milky Way Galaxy
Our Solar System is located within the **Milky Way Galaxy**.

*   **Structure:** It is a **spiral-shaped** galaxy.
*   **Composition:** It contains billions of stars of various shapes and sizes.
*   **Appearance:** From Earth, it is seen as a hazy path across the night sky.
*   **Scale:** The observable universe is estimated to be $93 \text{ billion light years}$ across.

## 3. Stars and Our Sun
*   **The Sun:** Our Sun is just one star among billions in the Milky Way.
*   **Location:** The Sun is situated on the edge of the galaxy.

## Chapter Overview
**Duration:** 3 weeks

This chapter introduces astronomy beyond our solar system, focusing on galaxies and the structure of the Universe.

### Core Concepts
*   **Stars:** The Sun is our closest star. If it were further away, it would appear as a point of light in the night sky, similar to other stars.
*   **Galaxies:** Stars are arranged in galaxies, held together by the force of gravity. Our own galaxy is the **Milky Way Galaxy**.
*   **The Universe:** Contains billions of galaxies in various shapes and sizes.
*   **Scale and Measurement:** Distances between stars and galaxies are so vast that standard units like kilometres are insufficient.
*   **Large-scale Structure:** On the largest scale, matter in the Universe is arranged like a bath sponge, consisting of thin filamentary structures with large voids in between.

### 12.1 The Milky Way Galaxy

The Milky Way is our home galaxy, containing approximately 200 billion stars. 

#### Key Features of the Milky Way
* **Central Bulge:** The dense, spherical center of the galaxy.
* **Flat Disk:** The main body of the galaxy where most stars reside.
* **Spiral Arms:** The curved structures extending from the center.

#### The Sun's Location
The Sun is not located at the center of the galaxy. It is positioned on the edge of the galaxy, approximately halfway out from the center, situated on a minor arm known as the **Orion Arm**.

#### Understanding the Milky Way in the Night Sky
* Every individual star visible to the naked eye at night is part of our Milky Way.
* Because the Milky Way is flat, looking at the "band" of light across the sky is equivalent to looking along the plane of the disk toward the center.
* The high density of stars in this direction makes them indistinguishable to the naked eye, appearing as a continuous white band of light.

#### Milky Way Spiral Arms Reference Table

| Common name | Alternative name |
| :--- | :--- |
| Norma Arm | 3 kiloparsec Arm |
| Scutum-Crux Arm | Centaurus Arm |
| Sagittarius Arm | Sagittarius-Carina Arm |
| Orion Arm | Local Arm |
| Perseus Arm | – |
| Cygnus Arm | Outer Arm |

#### Mathematical Note
The diameter of the Milky Way can be expressed using scientific notation as:
$$9.5 \times 10^{17} \text{ km}$$

# 12.3 Light Years, Light Hours, and Light Minutes

## Core Concepts
In astronomy, distances between stars (interstellar) and planets (interplanetary) are so vast that standard units like metres ($m$) or kilometres ($km$) are impractical. To measure these, we use units based on the distance light travels in a specific amount of time.

**Important Misconception:** Although these units contain words like "year," "hour," or "minute," they are **units of distance**, not time.

### The Fundamental Formula
To calculate these distances, we use the relationship between speed, distance, and time:

$$\text{Speed} = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Time}}$$

Rearranging this to solve for distance:

$$\text{Distance} = \text{Speed} \times \text{Time}$$

*   **Speed of light:** Approximately $300\,000$ kilometres per second ($km/s$).
*   **Light minute:** The distance light travels in one minute. Because a minute is shorter than an hour, a light minute is a shorter distance than a light hour.

### 12.4 What is beyond the Milky Way Galaxy?
Our Universe contains billions of galaxies of various shapes and sizes. Galaxies are not randomly scattered; they are arranged into groups and clusters, which are further organized into large-scale structures known as **voids** and **filaments**.

#### Types of Galaxies
While learners are not required to memorize the names of all shapes, they must understand that galaxies have different structures.

| Galaxy Type | Characteristics |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Spiral** | Features a central bulge and a flat disk with spiral arms. |
| **Barred Spiral** | Spiral arms start at the ends of a bright straight bar that crosses the centre. |
| **Elliptical** | Smooth, shaped like giant rugby balls; no spiral arms; contain mostly old stars with little gas and dust. |
| **Lenticular** | A transition shape between spiral and elliptical; disk-shaped but lacks defined arms. |
| **Irregular** | Do not fit into spiral or elliptical categories; often the result of two or more galaxies colliding. |

#### Key Concepts for Classification

*   **Irregular Galaxy:** A galaxy that does not have a distinct regular shape (neither spiral nor elliptical).

#### Distance Measurement
In astronomy, distances are often measured in **light years**. A light year is the distance light travels in one year, approximately $9.46 \times 10^{12}$ km. 

*   **Galaxy M 89:** $60,000,000$ light years away.
*   **Galaxy NGC 4622:** $111,000,000$ light years away.
*   **Large Magellanic Cloud:** $163,000$ light years away.

### Extension Content: Dark Matter and Gravitational Lensing

#### Dark Matter
*   **Definition:** A mysterious, unseen type of matter that has not yet been identified.
*   **Characteristics:** It cannot be seen directly, but its presence is inferred from its gravitational effects on gas and galaxies within a cluster.
*   **Mass:** Astronomers estimate that dark matter could be approximately $5$ times more massive than all the visible galaxies and hot gas in a cluster combined.
*   **Distribution:** It is not limited to galaxy clusters but is spread throughout space.

#### Gravitational Lensing
*   **Observation:** In images of galaxy clusters (such as Abell 2218), thin, distorted arcs can be seen. These are images of distant galaxies located behind the cluster.
*   **Mechanism:** The cluster of galaxies acts as a **gravitational lens**. Because the cluster is so massive, it bends and distorts the light coming from more distant galaxies.
*   **Cause of Distortion:** While the distant galaxies are typically elliptical or spiral in shape, they appear distorted due to the bending of light by the matter (primarily dark matter) within the foreground cluster.

### Galaxies and the Structure of the Universe

#### 1. Definition of a Galaxy
A galaxy is a massive collection of stars, dust, and gas held together by gravity. A typical galaxy contains hundreds of billions of stars (approximately $200 \times 10^{9}$ stars).

#### 2. Types of Galaxies
Galaxies are classified into four main types:
*   Elliptical galaxies
*   Spiral galaxies
*   Barred spiral galaxies
*   Irregular galaxies

#### 3. The Milky Way Galaxy
*   **Classification:** The Milky Way is a **barred spiral galaxy**.
*   **Location of the Sun:** The Sun is located in the Orion spiral arm, halfway out from the centre of the galaxy.
*   **Appearance from Earth:** The Milky Way appears as a band across the night sky. When we look at this band, we are looking along the plane of the disk towards the centre, where there is a high density of stars.

#### 4. Galactic Structures
*   **Galaxy Cluster:** A collection of 50 or more galaxies held together by gravity.
*   **Superclusters:** Clusters of galaxies often group together to form larger structures known as superclusters.
*   **The Local Group:** The specific cluster of galaxies to which the Milky Way belongs.

#### 5. The Observable Universe
*   **Size:** The observable universe is approximately $93 \times 10^{9}$ light years in diameter.
*   **Large-Scale Structure:** The Universe is composed of thin walls called **filaments**, which contain galaxies, gas, and dust.
*   **Voids:** The empty spaces located between the filaments are called voids.
