### Physical Sciences Grade 11: Newton's Laws and Application of Newton's Laws

#### 1. Annual Teaching Plan (ATP) & Timelines
| Topic | Time Allocation | Pacing Guideline |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Newton's Laws and Application of Newton's Laws | 23 Hours (Total) | 5 Hours (Friction focus) |

#### 2. Content & Cognitive Scope
**Core Concepts:**
*   **Forces:** Normal force ($N$), weight, applied force (push, pull), tension (strings or cables).
*   **Friction:** Static friction ($f_s$) and kinetic/dynamic friction ($f_k$).

**Skills to be Mastered:**
*   Defining normal force as the force exerted by a surface on an object in contact with it.
*   Identifying the direction of normal force (perpendicular to the surface, regardless of inclination).
*   Defining frictional force ($f$) as the force opposing motion, acting parallel to the surface.
*   Distinguishing between static and kinetic friction.
*   Calculating maximum static friction and kinetic friction on horizontal and inclined planes.

**Mathematical Formulas:**
*   Maximum static friction: $f_s^{\text{max}} = \mu_s N$
*   Static friction condition: $f_s < \mu_s N$
*   Kinetic friction: $f_k = \mu_k N$

**Depth of Treatment & Cognitive Levels:**
*   **Knowledge:** Understanding definitions of forces and the nature of surface irregularities.
*   **Routine Procedures:** Calculating frictional forces using provided coefficients ($\mu_s, \mu_k$) and normal force ($N$).
*   **Complex Procedures:** Investigating the relationship between normal force and frictional force through experimental design.
*   **Indigenous Knowledge Systems:** Understanding the historical context of friction usage.

#### 3. Practical Activities & Resource Requirements
| Practical Activity | Recommended Investigation | Resource Material |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Friction Investigation** | 1. Investigate the relationship between normal force and maximum static friction. | Spring balance, blocks of varying sizes with hooks, various surface textures (rough, smooth), various angles of inclination. |
| | 2. Investigate the effect of different surfaces on maximum static friction (keeping object constant) and/or relationship between normal force and dynamic friction. | |

#### 4. Guidelines for Teachers
*   **Static Friction:** Emphasize that static friction has a range of values from zero up to a maximum value ($\mu_s N$).
*   **Dynamic Friction:** Note that dynamic friction on an object is constant for a given surface and equals $\mu_k N$.
*   **Conceptual Basis:** Explain friction in terms of the inter-locking of surface irregularities which impedes motion.
*   **Contextualization:** Incorporate Indigenous Knowledge Systems by discussing how early humans utilized friction for fire-making.

### Physical Sciences Grade 11: Curriculum Specifications

#### 1. Annual Teaching Plan (ATP) & Timelines
| Time | Topic |
| :--- | :--- |
| 3 Hours | Force diagrams, free-body diagrams |

#### 2. Content & Cognitive Scope
The following core concepts and skills are required for mastery within this unit:

*   **Force Diagrams:** Understanding that a force diagram is a representation of an object of interest with all forces acting on it drawn as arrows.
*   **Free-Body Diagrams (FBD):** 
    *   Representing the object of interest as a single dot.
    *   Drawing all forces acting on the object as arrows pointing away from the dot.
*   **Vector Resolution:** 
    *   Resolving two-dimensional forces (e.g., weight of an object on an inclined plane) into components parallel ($x$) and perpendicular ($y$) to the surface.
*   **Net Force Calculation:**
    *   The resultant force in the $x$-direction is the vector sum of all components in the $x$-direction: $F_{net,x} = \sum F_x$
    *   The resultant force in the $y$-direction is the vector sum of all components in the $y$-direction: $F_{net,y} = \sum F_y$

#### 3. Cognitive Levels & Depth of Treatment
*   **Knowledge:** Identification of forces and correct representation in diagrams.
*   **Routine Procedures:** Drawing standard free-body diagrams for objects on horizontal and inclined surfaces.
*   **Complex Procedures:** Resolving weight vectors into $x$ and $y$ components using trigonometric functions:
    *   $F_x = F \sin(\theta)$ or $F \cos(\theta)$ (depending on angle orientation)
    *   $F_y = F \cos(\theta)$ or $F \sin(\theta)$
*   **Problem Solving:** Calculating the net force in two dimensions by summing components.

#### 4. Assessment Matrix
*Note: This specific unit (3 hours) is typically assessed as part of the Mechanics section in formal term tests or mid-year examinations.*

| Assessment Type | Focus Area | Weighting |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Informal | Drawing FBDs for various scenarios | Formative |
| Formal | Application of vector resolution in Mechanics | Part of Paper 1 |

***

**Mathematical Notation Summary:**
*   Net force in $x$-direction: $F_{net,x} = \sum F_x$
*   Net force in $y$-direction: $F_{net,y} = \sum F_y$
*   Component resolution: $F_{\parallel} = mg \sin(\theta)$ and $F_{\perp} = mg \cos(\theta)$ (for an inclined plane).

### Physical Sciences Grade 11: Newton’s Laws of Motion

#### 1. Annual Teaching Plan (ATP) & Pacing
| Time Allocation | Topic |
| :--- | :--- |
| **11 Hours** | Newton’s First, Second, and Third Laws |

#### 2. Content & Cognitive Scope
*   **Core Concepts:**
    *   **Newton’s First Law:** Objects remain at rest or in uniform motion unless acted upon by an unbalanced (net/resultant) force.
    *   **Newton’s Second Law:** The relationship between net force, mass, and acceleration defined by: $$\vec{F}_{net} = m\vec{a}$$
    *   **Equilibrium vs. Non-equilibrium:** Analysis of objects at rest, moving with constant velocity, or accelerating.
    *   **Force Diagrams:** Drawing free body diagrams for various motion states.
*   **Skills to be Mastered:**
    *   Application of Newton’s Second Law in $x$ and $y$ directions.
    *   Superposition of forces.
    *   Conceptual application (e.g., importance of seatbelts).
    *   Experimental verification of Newton’s Second Law.
*   **Cognitive Levels:**
    *   **Knowledge:** Stating laws and definitions.
    *   **Routine Procedures:** Drawing free body diagrams; calculating $F_{net}$ for simple systems.
    *   **Complex Procedures:** Applying Newton’s Second Law to multi-object systems; resolving forces into components.
    *   **Problem Solving:** Analyzing motion on an inclined plane; verifying physical laws through experimental data.

#### 3. Assessment Matrix
| Assessment Type | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Formal Assessment** | Prescribed experiment: Investigate the relationship between force and acceleration (Verification of Newton’s second law). |
| **Informal Assessment** | Classroom discussion on the importance of seatbelts; practice exercises on free body diagrams. |

#### 4. Instructional Guidelines & Formulas
*   **Mathematical Formulas:**
    *   Newton's Second Law: $$\vec{F}_{net} = m\vec{a}$$
*   **Teacher Guidelines:**
    *   **Equilibrium:** For objects at rest or moving with constant velocity, all forces in the direction of motion and perpendicular to the plane of motion must sum to zero.
    *   **Superposition:** The principle of superposition must be applied when multiple forces act on an object.
    *   **System Analysis:** When an object accelerates, apply $$\vec{F}_{net} = m\vec{a}$$ separately in $x$ and $y$ directions. For systems with more than one object, draw individual free body diagrams for each object.
*   **Required Materials:**
    *   Trolleys, different masses, incline plane, rubber bands, meter rule, ticker timer apparatus, and graph paper.

When object $A$ exerts a force on object $B$, object $B$ simultaneously exerts an oppositely directed force of equal magnitude on object $A$.

**Key Instructional Requirements:**
*   **Action-Reaction Pairs:** Learners must be able to identify and list the properties of action-reaction pairs.
*   **Problem Solving:** Learners must demonstrate proficiency in solving problems involving:
    *   Horizontal motion: $F_{net} = ma$
    *   Inclined planes: Resolving forces into components parallel ($F_{\parallel} = mg \sin \theta$) and perpendicular ($F_{\perp} = mg \cos \theta$) to the surface.
    *   Vertical motion: Accounting for normal force ($N$) in accelerating systems (e.g., lifts).
    *   Connected systems: Applying Newton's Second Law to individual bodies and the system as a whole.

### Physical Sciences Grade 11: Chemical Bonding (Curriculum Specification)

#### 1. Content, Concepts & Skills
The curriculum focuses on the application of Lewis electron pair presentation to deduce bond formation.

*   **Core Concepts:**
    *   **Covalent Bonding:** Sharing of valence electrons between atoms with unpaired electrons.
    *   **Octet Rule:** The tendency of atoms to achieve a stable electron configuration (noting its limitations as a general guideline rather than an absolute rule).
    *   **Lone Pairs:** Paired valence electrons not involved in bonding.
    *   **Multiple Bonds:** Formation of double or triple bonds to satisfy valence requirements.
    *   **Co-ordinate (Dative) Covalent Bonding:** Sharing of a lone pair from one atom with another atom (e.g., $NH_4^+$, $H_3O^+$).
    *   **Lewis Diagrams:** Representation of valence electrons and bonding in molecules.

*   **Skills to be Mastered:**
    *   Deducing bond formation based on valence electron configurations.
    *   Drawing Lewis diagrams for simple molecules (e.g., $F_2$, $H_2O$, $NH_3$, $HF$, $OF_2$, $HOCl$).
    *   Drawing Lewis diagrams for molecules with multiple bonds (e.g., $N_2$, $O_2$, $HCN$).
    *   Identifying and representing co-ordinate covalent bonds.

#### 2. Instructional Guidelines for Teachers
*   **Pedagogical Approach:** Start with familiar molecules like $H_2O$. Use the two H-atoms bonding to one O-atom to introduce the octet rule.
*   **Conceptual Nuance:** 
    *   Clarify that the "octet rule" is a useful model, not an absolute law. 
    *   Highlight exceptions (e.g., $BF_3$) to demonstrate that it is a general guideline.
    *   Distinguish between "shared electrons" and "lone pairs."
*   **Co-ordinate Covalent Bonds:** Limit instruction to the definition and specific examples ($NH_4^+$, $H_3O^+$). Do not over-complicate the mechanism.

#### 3. Summary Table: Chemical Bonding Specifications

| Topic Component | Content & Skills | Teacher Guidelines |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Bond Formation** | Deduce bonds using valence electrons; identify lone pairs vs. shared pairs. | Use $H_2O$ as the introductory model for the octet rule. |
| **Octet Rule** | Application of the octet rule to simple molecules. | Treat as a useful model/guideline; acknowledge exceptions like $BF_3$. |
| **Co-ordinate Bonds** | Define and identify dative covalent bonds in $NH_4^+$ and $H_3O^+$. | Definition only; do not perform in-depth derivation. |
| **Lewis Diagrams** | Draw diagrams for simple molecules ($F_2, H_2O, NH_3, HF, OF_2, HOCl$). | Ensure correct placement of valence electrons and lone pairs. |
| **Multiple Bonds** | Draw diagrams for molecules with double/triple bonds ($N_2, O_2, HCN$). | Emphasize the sharing of multiple electron pairs. |

#### 4. Cognitive Scope
*   **Knowledge:** Recall of definitions (octet rule, co-ordinate bond, lone pair).
*   **Routine Procedures:** Drawing Lewis diagrams for simple molecules with single bonds.
*   **Complex Procedures:** Applying the octet rule to molecules with multiple bonds and identifying exceptions.
*   **Problem Solving:** Deducing the structure of complex ions (e.g., $NH_4^+$) and molecules with multiple bonds using electron configurations.

### Physical Sciences Grade 11: Molecular Shape (VSEPR Theory)

#### 1. Annual Teaching Plan (ATP) & Timelines
| Topic | Time Allocation | Pacing Guideline |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Molecular shape as predicted using the Valence Shell Electron Pair Repulsion (VSEPR) theory | 2 Hours | Single session or split across two lessons |

#### 2. Content & Cognitive Scope
**Core Concepts:**
*   **VSEPR Definition:** A model for predicting molecular shapes where structural electron pairs are arranged around a central atom to maximize angles between them.
*   **Ideal Molecular Shapes (No lone pairs):**
    *   Linear: $AX_2$ (e.g., $CO_2$, $BeCl_2$)
    *   Trigonal planar: $AX_3$ (e.g., $BF_3$)
    *   Tetrahedral: $AX_4$ (e.g., $CH_4$)
    *   Trigonal bipyramidal: $AX_5$ (e.g., $PCl_5$)
    *   Octahedral: $AX_6$ (e.g., $SF_6$)
*   **Non-Ideal Shapes (With lone pairs):**
    *   Molecules where lone pairs on the central atom distort the ideal shape (e.g., $H_2O$).
*   **Application:** Deducing shapes for molecules with multiple bonds, more than four bonds, and specific compounds ($CH_4, NH_3, H_2O, BeF_2, BF_3, PCl_5, SF_6, CO_2, SO_2, C_2H_2$).

**Skills to be Mastered:**
*   Drawing Lewis diagrams to identify bond pairs and lone pairs.
*   Predicting molecular geometry based on the $AX_n$ notation.
*   Applying the principle of electron pair repulsion to explain shape distortion.

**Cognitive Levels:**
*   **Knowledge:** Defining VSEPR theory.
*   **Routine Procedures:** Identifying shapes for molecules without lone pairs.
*   **Complex Procedures:** Deducing shapes for molecules with lone pairs or multiple bonds.
*   **Problem Solving:** Predicting the geometry of complex molecules using Lewis structures.

#### 3. Practical Activities & Methodology
| Activity | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Model Building** | Use Atomic Model kits or Jelly Tots and toothpicks to construct the five ideal molecular shapes. |
| **Lone Pair Simulation** | Remove "legs" (toothpicks) from the central atom to demonstrate how lone pairs alter the ideal geometry. |

**Guidelines for Teachers:**
*   **Note on Lone Pairs:** If a central atom has a lone pair, the "ideal shape" is modified. The lone pair occupies space, effectively removing a "leg" from the ideal geometry.
*   **Scope Limitation:** Hybridization is **NOT** required for Grade 11.
*   **Assessment Focus:** Learners must be able to deduce shapes using Lewis diagrams as the starting point.

### 2. Content & Cognitive Scope

#### Core Concepts
*   **Electronegativity:** Definition and application in determining bond polarity.
*   **Bond Types:**
    *   Non-polar covalent bonds (e.g., $H-H$).
    *   Polar covalent bonds (e.g., $H-Cl$).
*   **Bond Polarity Indicators:**
    *   Use of partial charges ($\delta+$, $\delta-$).
    *   Electronegativity difference ($\Delta EN$) thresholds:
        *   $\Delta EN > 2.1$: Ionic bond (electron transfer).
        *   $\Delta EN > 1$: Polar covalent bond.
        *   $\Delta EN < 1$: Weakly polar covalent bond.
        *   $\Delta EN = 0$: Non-polar covalent bond.
*   **Molecular Polarity:** Understanding that polar bonds do not always result in polar molecules.

#### Skills to be Mastered
*   Calculating $\Delta EN$ using a table of electronegativities.
*   Representing bond polarity using partial charge notation.
*   Constructing molecular models to visualize shapes and relate them to polarity.

#### Cognitive Levels & Depth of Treatment
*   **Knowledge:** Defining electronegativity and identifying bond types based on $\Delta EN$ values.
*   **Routine Procedures:** Calculating $\Delta EN$ and assigning partial charges.
*   **Complex Procedures:** Building molecular models to analyze the relationship between atomic composition, bond polarity, and molecular shape.
*   **Problem Solving:** Predicting bond character and molecular polarity based on electronegativity data.

---

### 3. Practical Activities & Guidelines

| Activity | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Activity 1** | Build molecular models with identical atoms to observe non-polar characteristics. |
| **Activity 2** | Build molecular models with different atoms to observe bond polarity and molecular polarity. |

**Teacher Guidelines:**
*   Link concepts to intermolecular forces.
*   **Note:** $\Delta EN$ values are guidelines for learners, not absolute scientific laws for determining bond character.
*   Emphasize that all bonds possess a degree of both covalent and ionic character.

---

### 4. Mathematical Formulas & Notation
*   **Electronegativity Difference:** $\Delta EN$
*   **Partial Charges:** $\delta+$ (partial positive), $\delta-$ (partial negative)
*   **Bond Thresholds:**
    *   Ionic: $\Delta EN > 2.1$
    *   Polar Covalent: $\Delta EN > 1$
    *   Weakly Polar: $\Delta EN < 1$
    *   Non-polar: $\Delta EN = 0$

### Physical Sciences Grade 11: Curriculum Specifications

#### 1. Annual Teaching Plan (ATP) & Timelines
| Time | Topic | Content, Concepts & Skills |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 hour | **Bond energy and length** | Definition of bond energy and bond length; relationship between bond energy, bond length, and bond strength; influence of bond type (single, double, triple) on length and size. |
| 10 hours | **Intermolecular forces** | Definition and identification of forces between molecules; rationale for state of matter (liquid/solid vs gas) based on attractive forces. |

#### 2. Content & Cognitive Scope

**Core Concepts:**
*   **Bond Energy:** The energy required to break a chemical bond.
*   **Bond Length:** The distance between the nuclei of two bonded atoms.
*   **Bond Strength:** Correlated to bond energy and the number of bonds (single, double, triple).
*   **Intermolecular Forces:** Forces of attraction between molecules that determine physical states.

**Cognitive Requirements & Depth of Treatment:**
*   **Conceptual Understanding:** Learners must explain the inverse relationship between bond length and bond strength.
*   **Analytical Skills:** Learners must distinguish between intramolecular bonds (chemical bonds) and intermolecular forces.
*   **Application:** Learners must apply the concept of intermolecular forces to explain why substances exist as liquids or solids rather than gases.

#### 3. Guidelines for Teachers (Pedagogical Notes)

*   **Bonding Diagrams:** Utilize potential energy diagrams to illustrate the relationship between bond energy and bond length.
*   **Conceptual Clarification:** Explicitly warn students that Lewis structures represent electron density and potential energy, not physical "sticks" or physical bonds.
*   **Sequencing:** The topic of Intermolecular Forces should be taught immediately following Electronegativity and Polarity to provide a logical progression of understanding.

#### 4. Mathematical & Scientific Relationships
The following relationships are central to the mastery of these topics:

*   **Bond Strength vs. Bond Length:** As the number of bonds increases (e.g., single to triple), bond length decreases and bond energy increases.
*   **Intermolecular Forces:** These are the forces that prevent molecules from moving apart, effectively maintaining the condensed phases (liquid and solid). 

*Note: While specific formulas for bond energy calculations are not provided in this snippet, the qualitative relationship is defined as:*
$$\text{Bond Strength} \propto \frac{1}{\text{Bond Length}}$$

### 2. Content & Cognitive Scope

#### Core Concepts & Skills
*   **Intermolecular Forces:**
    *   Ion-dipole forces
    *   Ion-induced dipole forces
    *   Dipole-dipole forces
    *   Dipole-induced dipole forces
    *   Induced dipole forces (London dispersion forces)
    *   Hydrogen bonds (a special case of dipole-dipole forces)
*   **Physical Properties:**
    *   Physical state and density explained via intermolecular forces.
    *   Particle kinetic energy and temperature relationships.
    *   Surface tension, solubility, boiling points, and capillarity.
*   **Structural Representation:**
    *   Differentiating between intermolecular and interatomic forces.
    *   Microscopic representation of states of matter: $H_2O(s)$, $H_2O(l)$, and $H_2O(g)$.

#### Depth of Treatment & Guidelines
*   **Hydrogen Bonding:** $10-40 \text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$. Occurs when hydrogen is bonded to a highly electronegative atom (e.g., Oxygen).
*   **Dipole-Dipole Interaction:** $5-25 \text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$. Weaker than hydrogen bonding.
*   **Ionic Compounds:** Lattice energy $\approx 4000 \text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$ (an order of magnitude greater than intermolecular forces).
*   **Viscosity:** Must be taught as a factor influenced by intermolecular forces.

    *   Electric field due to a point charge: $E = \frac{kQ}{r^2}$
*   **Vector Nature:** Recognition that $\vec{E}$ and $\vec{F}$ are vectors.
*   **Superposition:** Calculating the electric field at a point due to a number of point charges.

#### Cognitive Depth & Guidelines for Teachers
*   **Conceptual Understanding:** Discuss electric field lines as a representation of the field. Emphasize that field lines point away from positive charges and towards negative charges.
*   **Field Line Properties:** Field lines are drawn closer together where the field is stronger. The number of lines passing through a surface is proportional to the charge enclosed.
*   **Calculation Methodology:**
    *   When calculating the electric field due to multiple charges, do not substitute the sign of the charge into the equation $E = \frac{kQ}{r^2}$.
    *   Instead, choose a positive direction, then add or subtract the contribution of each charge based on whether the resulting field points in the positive or negative direction.
*   **Problem Solving Constraints:** Restrict 2D problems to three charges in a right-angled formation to determine the net force acting on the charge positioned at the right angle.

### 3. MATHEMATICAL FORMULAS

The following formulas are required for mastery of the Electric Field topic:

*   **Electric Field Magnitude (General):**
    $$E = \frac{F}{q}$$

*   **Force on a Charge:**
    $$F = qE$$

*   **Electric Field (Point Charge):**
    $$E = \frac{kQ}{r^2}$$

*   **Vector Notation:**
    $$\vec{E} \text{ and } \vec{F} \text{ are vectors.}$$
