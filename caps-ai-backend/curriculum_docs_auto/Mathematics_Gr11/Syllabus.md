#### 3. Mathematical Specifications & Examples

**Exponents and Surds:**
*   Definition: $x^{\frac{p}{q}} = \sqrt[q]{x^p}$ where $x > 0; q > 0$.
*   Example 1: Determine $9^{\frac{3}{2}}$.
*   Example 2: Simplify $(3 + \sqrt{2})(3 - \sqrt{2})$.
*   Example 3: Solve $\sqrt{x} - 2 = 3$.

**Equations and Inequalities:**
*   Quadratic formula application and factorisation.
*   Nature of roots examples:
    *   Show $x^2 - 2x - 7 = 0$ has irrational roots.
    *   Show $x^2 + x + 1 = 0$ has no real roots.
*   Inequalities: Solve $x^2 \leq 4$.
*   Simultaneous equations: Solve $x = (x + 1)(2x - 3) \leq 3$.
*   Problem Solving: Work-rate problems (e.g., two machines completing a job).

**Number Patterns:**
*   Focus on sequences where the second difference is constant.
*   Problem Solving: Application to tournament structures (e.g., World Cup Soccer Finals logic for $n$ teams).

### Grade 11 Mathematics: Term 2 Curriculum Specifications

#### 1. Annual Teaching Plan (ATP) & Timelines
| Term | Topic | Duration |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 2 | Functions | 4 Weeks |

#### 2. Content & Cognitive Scope
The curriculum focuses on the investigation of parameters and the transformation of functions. Students are expected to move from point-by-point plotting to an intuitive understanding of gradients and the effects of parameter changes on graphical representations.

**Core Concepts & Functions:**
*   **Rational Functions:** $f(x) = \frac{a}{x+p} + q$
*   **Exponential Functions:** $f(x) = ab^{x+p} + q$ (where $b > 0, b \neq 1$)
*   **Quadratic Functions:** $f(x) = a(x+p)^2 + q$
*   **Trigonometric Functions:**
    *   Basic: $y = \sin \theta, y = \cos \theta, y = \tan \theta$ (Domain: $\theta \in [-360^\circ; 360^\circ]$)
    *   Parameter $k$: $y = \sin(kx), y = \cos(kx), y = \tan(kx)$
    *   Parameter $p$: $y = \sin(x+p), y = \cos(x+p), y = \tan(x+p)$
    *   Combined: $y = a \sin k(x+p), y = a \cos k(x+p), y = a \tan k(x+p)$

**Skills to be Mastered:**
*   **Parameter Analysis:** Investigating the effects of $a, p, q,$ and $k$ on function graphs.
*   **Graphical Representation:** Sketching graphs based on defined equations.
*   **Deduction:** Making deductions from existing graphs and determining equations from sufficient data.
*   **Numerical Investigation:** Calculating average gradients between two points on a curve.
*   **Application:** Solving real-life problems using prescribed functions.

**Pacing & Depth Guidelines:**
*   **Constraint:** When sketching or analyzing, vary at most two parameters at a time.
*   **Example Task:** Sketching $y = -\frac{1}{2}\sin(x + 30^\circ)$ and $f(x) = \cos(2x - 120^\circ)$ on the same set of axes for the interval $-360^\circ \leq x \leq 360^\circ$.

#### 3. Assessment Matrix
*   **Formal Assessment:** Assessment tasks must include the determination of equations from graphs and the application of functions to real-life scenarios.
*   **Cognitive Levels:** Assessment must balance:
    *   **Knowledge:** Identifying basic shapes and standard forms.
    *   **Routine Procedures:** Plotting points and applying standard transformations.
    *   **Complex Procedures:** Analyzing the combined effect of multiple parameters (e.g., $a, k, p$).
    *   **Problem Solving:** Interpreting real-life applications and deducing equations from graphical data.

#### 2. Content & Cognitive Scope: Euclidean Geometry
**Core Concepts & Theorems to be Mastered:**
*   The line drawn from the centre of a circle perpendicular to a chord bisects the chord.
*   The perpendicular bisector of a chord passes through the centre of the circle.
*   The angle subtended by an arc at the centre of a circle is double the size of the angle subtended by the same arc at the circle (on the same side of the chord as the centre).
*   Angles subtended by a chord of a circle, on the same side of the chord, are equal.
*   The opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral are supplementary.
*   Two tangents drawn to a circle from the same point outside the circle are equal in length.
*   The angle between the tangent to a circle and the chord drawn from the point of contact is equal to the angle in the alternate segment.

**Depth of Treatment & Cognitive Requirements:**
*   **Axioms:** Accept results established in earlier grades as axioms.
*   **Proofs:** Proofs of theorems can be asked in examinations; however, proofs of their converses cannot be asked.
*   **Application:** Use the above theorems and their converses to solve riders (geometry problems).

#### 3. Mathematical Examples & Formulas
**Example Problem Specification:**
Given a circle with centre $O$ and two chords $AB$ and $CD$.
*   $OM \perp AB$ and $ON \perp CD$.
*   $AB = 50\text{mm}$, $OM = 40\text{mm}$, and $ON = 20\text{mm}$.
*   Determine the radius of the circle and the length of $CD$.

**Geometric Relationships:**
*   The central angle is related to the inscribed angle: $\hat{O}_1 = 2x$.
*   Sum of angles in the provided diagram: $\hat{K}_1 + \hat{M}$.

#### 4. Assessment Notes
*   **Formal Assessment:** The curriculum requires the application of these theorems in formal examination settings.
*   **Cognitive Levels:** Assessment tasks must range from basic recall of theorems (Knowledge) to the application of theorems and their converses to solve complex riders (Problem Solving).

#### 2. Content & Cognitive Scope
The curriculum requires mastery of the following geometric principles:
*   **Tangent-Chord Theorem:** The angle between a tangent and a chord is equal to the angle subtended by the chord in the alternate segment.

**Cognitive Levels:**

*   **Problem Solving:** Integrating multiple theorems to prove geometric statements (e.g., proving a line is a tangent to a circle).

#### 5. Mathematical Formulas & Notation
The following geometric relationships are central to the curriculum content provided:

*   **Perpendicularity:** 
    $$OP \perp MT \implies \angle OPT = 90^\circ$$
*   **Parallelism:** 
    $$AP \parallel BC \implies \text{Alternate angles are equal (e.g., } \angle PAB = \angle ABC)$$
*   **Isosceles Triangle:** 
    $$AB = AC \implies \angle ABC = \angle ACB$$
*   **Tangent-Chord Theorem:** 
    The angle between tangent $PAL$ and chord $AB$ is equal to the angle subtended in the alternate segment:
    $$\angle PAB = \angle ACB$$
*   **Converse of Tangent-Chord Theorem:** 
    If $\angle PAB = \angle ACB$, then $PAL$ is a tangent to the circle at $A$.

#### 2. CONTENT & COGNITIVE SCOPE
The curriculum focuses on the mastery of probability theory, specifically the application of addition and product rules, and the representation of data using Venn diagrams and contingency tables.

**Core Concepts & Formulas:**
*   **Addition Rule (Mutually Exclusive):** $P(A \text{ or } B) = P(A) + P(B)$
*   **Complementary Rule:** $P(\text{not } A) = 1 - P(A)$
*   **General Addition Rule:** $P(A \text{ or } B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A \text{ and } B)$
*   **Product Rule (Independent Events):** $P(A \text{ and } B) = P(A) \times P(B)$

**Skills to be Mastered:**
*   Distinguishing between dependent and independent events.
*   Identifying mutually exclusive vs. non-mutually exclusive events.
*   Deriving and applying probability formulae for three events ($A, B, C$) within a sample space ($S$).
*   Constructing and interpreting Venn diagrams for complex probability scenarios.
*   Using tree diagrams to calculate probabilities of consecutive or simultaneous events.

**Cognitive Levels:**
*   **(K) Knowledge:** Identifying definitions and basic rules.
*   **(R) Routine Procedures:** Applying standard formulas to simple probability problems.
*   **(C) Complex Procedures:** Solving multi-step problems involving three events, Venn diagram construction, and logical deduction from provided data sets.

#### 3. ASSESSMENT & CLARIFICATION EXAMPLES
The following examples represent the required depth of treatment for assessment purposes:

| Example Scenario | Cognitive Level |
| :--- | :--- |
| Given $P(A) = 0,3$ and $P(A \text{ or } B) = 0,615$, determine if events are mutually exclusive or independent. | (R) / (C) |
| Probability of independent events in repeated trials (e.g., rolling a die). | (R) |
| Probability involving multiple subjects (e.g., Mathematics and History) using set theory. | (C) |
| Multi-variable study (e.g., three different drugs A, B, and C) requiring Venn diagram representation and subset calculation. | (C) |

**Assessment Notes:**
*   Learners must be able to record complex data sets (e.g., 80 patients, 3 drugs) into a Venn diagram.
*   Assessment tasks should require learners to calculate probabilities for "at least one," "none," and specific intersections of events.

#### 4. Mathematical Formulas & Notation

*   **Variance ($\sigma^2$):** The average of the squared differences from the Mean ($\bar{x}$):
    $$\sigma^2 = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n} (x_i - \bar{x})^2}{n}$$
*   **Standard Deviation ($\sigma$):** The square root of the variance:
    $$\sigma = \sqrt{\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n} (x_i - \bar{x})^2}{n}}$$
