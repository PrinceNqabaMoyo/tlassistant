# 7 Euclidean geometry

Geometry (from the Greek “geo” = earth and “metria” = measure) arose as the field of knowledge dealing with spatial relationships. Analytical geometry deals with space and shape using algebra and a coordinate system. Euclidean geometry deals with space and shape using a system of logical deductions.

Euclidean geometry was first used in surveying and is still used extensively for surveying today. Euclidean geometry is also used in architecture to design new buildings. Other uses of Euclidean geometry are in art and to determine the best packing arrangement for various types of objects.

In Euclidean geometry we use two fundamental types of measurement: angles and distances.

## 7.1 Introduction

### Angles

An angle is formed when two straight lines meet at a point, also known as a vertex. Angles are labelled with a caret on a letter, for example, $\hat{B}$. Angles can also be labelled according to the line segments that make up the angle, for example $C\hat{B}A$ or $A\hat{B}C$. The $\angle$ symbol is a short method of writing angle in geometry and is often used in phrases such as "sum of $\angle$s in $\Delta$". Angles are measured in degrees which is denoted by $^\circ$, a small circle raised above the text, similar to an exponent.

**Explanation:**

### 7.2 Triangles (EMA5R)

#### Classification of Triangles (EMA5S)

A **triangle** is a closed three-sided polygon. Triangles are classified into two main categories:

1. **Classification According to Sides:**
   * **Equilateral Triangle:** All three sides are equal in length, and all three internal angles are equal ($60^\circ$ each).
   * **Isosceles Triangle:** Two sides are equal in length, and the angles opposite those equal sides are also equal.
   * **Scalene Triangle:** All three sides have different lengths, and all three internal angles have different measures.

2. **Classification According to Angles:**
   * **Acute-angled Triangle:** All three internal angles are acute (less than $90^\circ$).
   * **Obtuse-angled Triangle:** One of the internal angles is obtuse (greater than $90^\circ$).
   * **Right-angled Triangle:** One of the internal angles is a right angle ($90^\circ$).

**Notation:** 
We use the symbol $\triangle ABC$ to refer to a triangle with vertices labelled $A$, $B$, and $C$.

# Congruency (EMA5T)

Two triangles are **congruent** if one fits exactly over the other. This means that the triangles have equal corresponding angles and sides. To determine whether two triangles are congruent, it is not necessary to check every side and every angle. We indicate congruency using the symbol $\equiv$.

## Rules for Congruency

The following table describes the requirements and rules for triangle congruency:

| Rule | Description | Diagram |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **RHS** or **$90^\circ\text{HS}$** ($90^\circ$, hypotenuse, side) | If the hypotenuse and one side of a right-angled triangle are equal to the hypotenuse and the corresponding side of another right-angled triangle, then the two triangles are congruent. | <!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram --> |
| **SSS** (side, side, side) | If three sides of a triangle are equal in length to the corresponding sides of another triangle, then the two triangles are congruent. | <!-- DIAG_PLACEHOLDER: diagram --> |
| **SAS** or **$S\angle S$** (side, angle, side) | If two sides and the **included** angle of a triangle are equal to the corresponding two sides and **included** angle of another triangle, then the two triangles are congruent. | <!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram --> |
| **AAS** or **$\angle\angle S$** (angle, angle, side) | If one side and two angles of a triangle are equal to the corresponding one side and two angles of another triangle, then the two triangles are congruent. | <!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram --> |

---

## Labelling and Notation

The order of letters when labelling congruent triangles is very important:

$$\Delta ABC \equiv \Delta DEF$$

This notation indicates the following properties of the two triangles:
- $\hat{A} = \hat{D}$
- $\hat{B} = \hat{E}$
- $\hat{C} = \hat{F}$
- $AB = DE$
- $AC = DF$
- $BC = EF$

---

**NOTE:**
You might see $\cong$ used to show that two triangles are congruent. This is the internationally recognised symbol for congruency.

# Similarity

Two triangles are similar if one triangle is a scaled version of the other. This means that their corresponding angles are equal in measure and the ratio of their corresponding sides are in proportion. The two triangles have the same shape, but different scales. Congruent triangles are similar triangles, but not all similar triangles are congruent. We use $\sim$ or $\parallel\parallel\parallel$ to indicate that two triangles are similar.

The following table describes the requirements for similarity:

| Rule | Description | Diagram |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **AAA**<br>(angle, angle, angle) | If all three pairs of corresponding angles of two triangles are equal, then the triangles are similar. | $\hat{A} = \hat{D}, \hat{B} = \hat{E}, \hat{C} = \hat{F}$<br>$\therefore \triangle ABC \parallel\parallel\parallel \triangle DEF$ |
| **SSS**<br>(side, side, side) | If all three pairs of corresponding sides of two triangles are in proportion, then the triangles are similar. | $\frac{MN}{RS} = \frac{ML}{RT} = \frac{NL}{ST}$<br>$\therefore \triangle MNL \parallel\parallel\parallel \triangle RST$ |

The order of letters for similar triangles is very important. Always label similar triangles in corresponding order. For example:
* $\triangle MNL \parallel\parallel\parallel \triangle RST$ is correct; but
* $\triangle MNL \parallel\parallel\parallel \triangle RTS$ is incorrect.

> **NOTE:**
> You might see $\sim$ used to show that two triangles are similar. This is the internationally recognised symbol for similarity.

# The theorem of Pythagoras

If $\triangle ABC$ is right-angled with $\hat{B} = 90^\circ$, then $b^2 = a^2 + c^2$.

**Converse:** If $b^2 = a^2 + c^2$, then $\triangle ABC$ is right-angled with $\hat{B} = 90^\circ$.

### Parallelogram

EMA5Y

> **DEFINITION: Parallelogram**
> 
> A parallelogram is a quadrilateral with both pairs of opposite sides parallel.

#### Worked example 3: Properties of a parallelogram

**QUESTION**

$ABCD$ is a parallelogram with $AB \parallel DC$ and $AD \parallel BC$. Show that:
1. $AB = DC$ and $AD = BC$
2. $\hat{A} = \hat{C}$ and $\hat{B} = \hat{D}$

```
       D ____________ C
        /           /
       /   3     4 /
      /           /
     / 1       2 /
    A ____________ B
```

**SOLUTION**

**Step 1: Connect AC to form $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle CDA$**  
Redraw the diagram and draw line $AC$.

**Step 2: Use properties of parallel lines to indicate all equal angles on the diagram**  
On your diagram mark all the equal angles.

**Step 3: Prove $\triangle ABC \equiv \triangle CDA$**  
In $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle CDA$:
$$\hat{A}_2 = \hat{C}_3 \quad (\text{alt } \angle\text{s}; AB \parallel DC)$$
$$\hat{C}_4 = \hat{A}_1 \quad (\text{alt } \angle\text{s}; BC \parallel AD)$$
$$AC \quad (\text{common side})$$
$$\therefore \triangle ABC \equiv \triangle CDA \quad (\text{AAS})$$
$$\therefore AB = CD \quad \text{and} \quad BC = DA$$
$$\therefore \text{Opposite sides of a parallelogram have equal length.}$$

---

We have already shown $\hat{A}_2 = \hat{C}_3$ and $\hat{A}_1 = \hat{C}_4$. Therefore,
$$\hat{A} = \hat{A}_1 + \hat{A}_2 = \hat{C}_3 + \hat{C}_4 = \hat{C}$$

Furthermore,
$$\hat{B} = \hat{D} \ (\triangle ABC \equiv \triangle CDA)$$

Therefore, opposite angles of a parallelogram are equal.

---

### Summary of the Properties of a Parallelogram
* Both pairs of opposite sides are parallel.
* Both pairs of opposite sides are equal in length.
* Both pairs of opposite angles are equal.
* Both diagonals bisect each other.

## Worked Example 4: Proving a Quadrilateral is a Parallelogram

### QUESTION
Prove that if both pairs of opposite angles in a quadrilateral are equal, the quadrilateral is a parallelogram.

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

### SOLUTION

#### Step 1: Find the relationship between $\hat{x}$ and $\hat{y}$
In $WXYZ$:
$$\begin{align*}
\hat{W} = \hat{Y} &= \hat{y} && \text{(given)} \\
\hat{Z} = \hat{X} &= \hat{x} && \text{(given)} \\
\hat{W} + \hat{X} + \hat{Y} + \hat{Z} &= 360^\circ && \text{(sum of } \angle\text{s in a quad)} \\
\therefore 2\hat{x} + 2\hat{y} &= 360^\circ && \\
\therefore \hat{x} + \hat{y} &= 180^\circ && \\
\hat{W} + \hat{Z} &= \hat{x} + \hat{y} && \\
&= 180^\circ &&
\end{align*}$$

But these are co-interior angles between lines $WX$ and $ZY$. Therefore $WX \parallel ZY$.

#### Step 2: Find parallel lines
Similarly $\hat{W} + \hat{X} = 180^\circ$. These are co-interior angles between lines $XY$ and $WZ$. Therefore $XY \parallel WZ$.

Both pairs of opposite sides of the quadrilateral are parallel, therefore $WXYZ$ is a parallelogram.

# Rectangle

## Definition: Rectangle

A rectangle is a parallelogram that has all four angles equal to $90^\circ$.

A rectangle has all the properties of a parallelogram:
* Both pairs of opposite sides are parallel.
* Both pairs of opposite sides are equal in length.
* Both pairs of opposite angles are equal.
* Both diagonals bisect each other.

It also has the following special property:

---

## Worked Example 5: Special property of a rectangle

### Question

$PQRS$ is a rectangle. Prove that the diagonals are of equal length.

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

### Solution

**Step 1:** Connect $P$ to $R$ and $Q$ to $S$ to form $\triangle PSR$ and $\triangle QRS$  
**Step 2:** Use the definition of a rectangle to fill in on the diagram all equal angles and sides  
**Step 3:** Prove $\triangle PSR \equiv \triangle QRS$  

In $\triangle PSR$ and $\triangle QRS$:

$$\begin{align*}
PS &= QR && \text{(opp sides of rectangle)} \\
SR &= SR && \text{(common side)} \\
P\hat{S}R &= Q\hat{R}S = 90^\circ && \text{($\angle$s of rectangle)} \\
\therefore \triangle PSR &\equiv \triangle QRS && \text{(RHS)} \\
\text{Therefore } PR &= QS & &
\end{align*}$$

The diagonals of a rectangle are of equal length.

---

## Summary of the Properties of a Rectangle

* Both pairs of opposite sides are parallel.
* Both pairs of opposite sides are equal in length.
* Both pairs of opposite angles are equal.
* Both diagonals bisect each other.
* Diagonals are equal in length.
* All interior angles are equal to $90^\circ$.

## Rhombus (EMA62)

### DEFINITION: Rhombus
A rhombus is a parallelogram with all four sides of equal length.

A rhombus has all the properties of a parallelogram:
* Both pairs of opposite sides are parallel.
* Both pairs of opposite sides are equal in length.
* Both pairs of opposite angles are equal.
* Both diagonals bisect each other.

It also has two special properties:

---

### Worked example 6: Special properties of a rhombus

#### QUESTION
$XYZT$ is a rhombus. Prove that:
1. the diagonals bisect each other perpendicularly;
2. the diagonals bisect the interior angles.

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

#### SOLUTION
* **Step 1:** Use the definition of a rhombus to fill in on the diagram all equal angles and sides.
* **Step 2:** Prove $\triangle XTO \equiv \triangle ZTO$

---

$XT = ZT$ (sides of rhombus)

$TO = TO$ (common side)

$XO = OZ$ (diags of rhombus)

$\therefore \triangle XTO \equiv \triangle ZTO$ (SSS)

$\therefore \hat{O}_1 = \hat{O}_4$

But $\hat{O}_1 + \hat{O}_4 = 180^\circ$ ($\angle\text{s on a str line}$)

$\therefore \hat{O}_1 = \hat{O}_4 = 90^\circ$

We can further conclude that $\hat{O}_1 = \hat{O}_2 = \hat{O}_3 = \hat{O}_4 = 90^\circ$.

Therefore the diagonals bisect each other perpendicularly.

### Step 3: Use properties of congruent triangles to prove diagonals bisect interior angles

$\hat{X}_2 = \hat{Z}_1$ ($\triangle XTO \equiv \triangle ZTO$)

and $\hat{X}_2 = \hat{Z}_2$ (alt $\angle\text{s}; XT \parallel YZ$)

$\therefore \hat{Z}_1 = \hat{Z}_2$

Therefore diagonal $XZ$ bisects $\hat{Z}$. Similarly, we can show that $XZ$ also bisects $\hat{X}$; and that diagonal $TY$ bisects $\hat{T}$ and $\hat{Y}$.

We conclude that the diagonals of a rhombus bisect the interior angles.

To prove a parallelogram is a rhombus, we need to show any one of the following:
* All sides are equal in length.
* Diagonals intersect at right angles.
* Diagonals bisect interior angles.

### Summary of the properties of a rhombus:
* Both pairs of opposite sides are parallel.
* Both pairs of opposite sides are equal in length.
* Both pairs of opposite angles are equal.
* Both diagonals bisect each other.
* All sides are equal in length.
* The diagonals bisect each other at $90^\circ$.
* The diagonals bisect both pairs of opposite angles.

## Kite (EMA65)

> **DEFINITION:** *Kite*  
> A kite is a quadrilateral with two pairs of adjacent sides equal.

> **Worked example 7: Properties of a kite**  
> 
> **QUESTION**  
> $ABCD$ is a kite with $AD = AB$ and $CD = CB$. Prove that:
> 
> 1. $\hat{ADC} = \hat{ABC}$
> 2. Diagonal $AC$ bisects $\hat{A}$ and $\hat{C}$

---

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

## SOLUTION

### Step 1: Prove $\triangle ADC \equiv \triangle ABC$

In $\triangle ADC$ and $\triangle ABC$:

$$
\begin{align*}
AD &= AB \quad (\text{given}) \\
CD &= CB \quad (\text{given}) \\
AC &= AC \quad (\text{common side}) \\
\therefore \triangle ADC &\equiv \triangle ABC \quad (\text{SSS}) \\
\therefore \hat{A}\hat{D}C &= \hat{A}\hat{B}C
\end{align*}
$$

Therefore, one pair of opposite angles are equal in kite $ABCD$.

### Step 2: Use properties of congruent triangles to prove $AC$ bisects $\hat{A}$ and $\hat{C}$

$$
\begin{align*}
\hat{A}_1 &= \hat{A}_2 \quad (\triangle ADC \equiv \triangle ABC) \\
\text{and } \hat{C}_1 &= \hat{C}_2 \quad (\triangle ADC \equiv \triangle ABC)
\end{align*}
$$

Therefore, diagonal $AC$ bisects $\hat{A}$ and $\hat{C}$.

We conclude that the diagonal between the equal sides of a kite bisects the two interior angles and is an axis of symmetry.

---

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

### Summary of the properties of a kite:

* Diagonal between equal sides bisects the other diagonal.
* One pair of opposite angles are equal (the angles between unequal sides).
* Diagonal between equal sides bisects the interior angles and is an axis of symmetry.
* Diagonals intersect at $90^\circ$.

### Worked example 8: Mid-point theorem

**QUESTION**

Prove that the line joining the mid-points of two sides of a triangle is parallel to the third side and equal to half the length of the third side.

---

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

**SOLUTION**

**Step 1:** Extend $DE$ to $F$ so that $DE = EF$ and join $FC$

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

**Step 2: Prove $BCFD$ is a parallelogram**

In $\triangle EAD$ and $\triangle ECF$:

$$\begin{aligned}
\hat{E}_1 &= \hat{E}_2 && (\text{vert opp } \angle s =) \\
AE &= CE && (\text{given}) \\
DE &= EF && (\text{by construction}) \\
\therefore \triangle EAD &\equiv \triangle ECF && (\text{SAS}) \\
\therefore A\hat{D}E &= C\hat{F}E
\end{aligned}$$

But these are alternate interior angles, therefore $BD \parallel FC$

$$\begin{aligned}
BD &= DA && (\text{given}) \\
DA &= FC && (\triangle EAD \equiv \triangle ECF) \\
\therefore BD &= FC && \\
\therefore BCFD &\text{ is a parallelogram} && (\text{one pair opp. sides} = \text{ and } \parallel)
\end{aligned}$$

Therefore $DE \parallel BC$.

We conclude that the line joining the two mid-points of two sides of a triangle is parallel to the third side.

**Step 3: Use properties of parallelogram $BCFD$ to prove that $DE = \frac{1}{2}BC$**

$$\begin{aligned}
DF &= BC && (\text{opp sides} \parallel m) \\
\text{and } DF &= 2(DE) && (\text{by construction}) \\
\therefore 2DE &= BC && \\
\therefore DE &= \frac{1}{2}BC &&
\end{aligned}$$

We conclude that the line joining the mid-point of two sides of a triangle is equal to half the length of the third side.

### Definitions and Properties of Quadrilaterals

* **Quadrilateral:** A closed shape consisting of four straight line segments.
* **Parallelogram:** A quadrilateral with both pairs of opposite sides parallel.
  * Both pairs of opposite sides are equal in length.
  * Both pairs of opposite angles are equal ($90^\circ$).
  * Both diagonals bisect each other.
* **Rectangle:** A parallelogram that has all four angles equal to $90^\circ$.
  * Both pairs of opposite sides are parallel.
  * Both pairs of opposite sides are equal in length.
  * The diagonals bisect each other.
  * The diagonals are equal in length.
  * All interior angles are equal to $90^\circ$.
* **Rhombus:** A parallelogram that has all four sides equal in length.
  * Both pairs of opposite sides are parallel.
  * All sides are equal in length.
  * Both pairs of opposite angles are equal.
  * The diagonals bisect each other at $90^\circ$.
  * The diagonals of a rhombus bisect both pairs of opposite angles.
* **Square:** A rhombus that has all four interior angles equal to $90^\circ$.
  * Both pairs of opposite sides are parallel.
  * The diagonals bisect each other at $90^\circ$.
  * All interior angles are equal to $90^\circ$.
  * The diagonals are equal in length.
  * The diagonals bisect both pairs of interior opposite angles (i.e. all are $45^\circ$).
* **Trapezium:** A quadrilateral with one pair of opposite sides parallel.
* **Kite:** A quadrilateral with two pairs of adjacent sides equal.
  * One pair of opposite angles are equal (the angles are between unequal sides).
  * The diagonal between equal sides bisects the other diagonal.
  * The diagonal between equal sides bisects the interior angles.
  * The diagonals intersect at $90^\circ$.

### Theorems

* **Mid-point theorem:** The line joining the mid-points of two sides of a triangle is parallel to the third side and equal to half the length of the third side.

## Revision Notes: Geometry and Proofs

### Core Concepts & Geometric Properties

To solve geometric proof problems involving triangles, lines, and parallelograms, recall the following fundamental theorems:

1. **Midpoint Theorem:** 
   The line segment joining the midpoints of two sides of a triangle is parallel to the third side and is equal to half the length of that third side.

2. **Properties of a Parallelogram:**
   A quadrilateral is a parallelogram if:
   * Both pairs of opposite sides are parallel.
   * Both pairs of opposite sides are equal in length.
   * One pair of opposite sides is both parallel and equal in length.

### Step-by-Step Proof Guide

#### Part (a): Prove that $FECG$ is a parallelogram.
1. Identify the given parallel lines from the diagram markings: $FE \parallel GC$ (since line $FED$ is parallel to $BC$, and $G$ lies on $BC$).
2. Identify the other set of parallel lines: $FG \parallel EC$ (as indicated by the arrows on lines $FC$ and $GE$).
3. Since both pairs of opposite sides are parallel ($FE \parallel GC$ and $FG \parallel EC$), quadrilateral $FECG$ satisfies the definition of a parallelogram.

#### Part (b): Prove that $FE = ED$.
1. From part (a), $FECG$ is a parallelogram, which means opposite sides are equal in length:
   $$FE = CG$$
2. Using properties of similar triangles or triangle intercepts (such as the intercept theorem where a line parallel to one side of a triangle divides the other two sides proportionally):
   Since $FE \parallel BC$, triangle $AFE$ is similar to triangle $ABC$, and line segments maintain proportional ratios.
3. By applying standard geometric theorems relating to parallel lines intersecting proportional segments:
   $$ED = CG$$
4. Therefore, substituting $CG$ with $FE$, we establish:
   $$FE = ED$$
