# Chapter 6: Inventories

## 6.1 Inventory Systems

A business can choose between two primary inventory systems to record and control their inventory.

> **Hint:** Inventories (trading stock) are goods that are bought in order to be resold at a profit. Stock is always recorded in the books at cost price.

### Comparison of Inventory Systems

| SYSTEM | IMPORTANT POINTS |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Perpetual** | a) Entries are made in a Balance Sheet account called **trading stock** (an asset account) every time goods are bought and sold.<br>b) Cost of sales needs to be calculated and recorded every time goods are sold.<br>c) When stock is purchased the trading stock account is debited with the cost price (the asset is increasing).<br>d) When stock is sold the trading stock account is credited with the cost price (the asset is decreasing).<br>e) Any additional costs when purchasing stock are debited to the trading stock account (e.g. carriage on purchases).<br>f) This system is better for the internal control of inventory. |
| **Periodic** | a) Businesses would decide to use this system when it is not always feasible to calculate cost of sales every time goods are sold.<br>b) Stock purchases are recorded in a nominal account called **purchases** (an expense account).<br>c) Additional expenses when purchasing stock (e.g. carriage on purchases) are recorded separately in a nominal account called **carriage on purchases** (an expenses account).<br>d) Cost of sales is therefore only calculated periodically by using the formula: |

### Periodic System Formula

To calculate the cost of sales under the periodic system, use the following formula:

$$\text{Opening stock} + \text{purchases} + \text{carriage on purchases} + \text{import duties} + \text{customs duties} - \text{closing stock} = \text{cost of sales}$$

# 6.2 Inventory Valuation Methods

Businesses must choose a specific method to value their inventory. Once a method is chosen, it cannot be changed unless there is a valid business reason to ensure the inventory is better valued.

## Stock Valuation Methods

| METHOD | IMPORTANT POINTS |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Specific Identification method** | a) Simplest form of stock valuation; every item is assigned a specific cost price.<br>b) Relevant for large commodities (e.g., vehicles, machinery).<br>c) Requires the cost price to be identified for every item sold or during stocktaking.<br>d) Manually intensive method.<br>e) Every item is recorded at the specific price originally bought.<br>f) Disadvantage: Prices can be manipulated to misrepresent profit margins. |
| **FIFO** | a) Stands for **First In First Out**.<br>b) Preferred for goods with a limited shelf life (e.g., milk, cellphones).<br>c) Oldest stock is sold first; remaining stock consists of the most recently purchased items.<br>d) Stock on hand is valued at the most recent prices. |
| **Weighted average** | a) Value of stock on hand is not calculated on the most recent prices; all purchase prices are averaged.<br>b) Calculated by: $\frac{\text{Total cost of stock purchased}}{\text{Total number of units purchased}}$<br>c) Stock on hand is not valued at the most recent prices. |

*   **Weighted Average:** 
    *   **Step 1:** Calculate the average cost by dividing the total cost of all available stock by the total number of units purchased.
    *   **Step 2:** Multiply the closing stock units by the calculated average cost per unit.

### 3. Gross Profit Calculation: FIFO vs. Weighted-Average Method

The gross profit is calculated using the fundamental formula:
$$\text{Gross Profit} = \text{Sales} - \text{Cost of Sales}$$

| Method | Calculation | Result |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |

**Key Notes:**
* The **Sales** figure remains constant across both methods.
* The **Cost of Sales** figure differs based on the valuation method used (FIFO or Weighted Average).
* Consequently, the **Gross Profit** will differ between the two methods because the cost of sales is calculated using different inventory valuation principles.

### Chapter 6: Inventories - Trading Account Calculations

#### Key Formulas to Memorise

**1. Calculation of Cost of Sales**
$$\text{Opening Stock} + \text{Net Purchases} + \text{Carriage on Purchases} + \text{Custom Duties} - \text{Closing Stock} = \text{Cost of Sales}$$

**2. Calculation of Gross Profit**
$$\text{Opening Stock} + \text{Net Purchases} + \text{Carriage on Purchases} + \text{Custom Duties} - \text{Closing Stock} - \text{Net Sales} = \text{Gross Profit}$$
*Alternatively:*
$$\text{Net Sales} - \text{Cost of Sales} = \text{Gross Profit}$$

---

#### Important Concepts
*   **'Net'**: Always remember to subtract returns (e.g., Creditors allowances from purchases, or Debtors allowances from sales).
*   **Stock Validation**: This process focuses on the accurate calculation of the closing stock value.
*   **Inventory Control**: By comparing the number of units that *should* be in stock (Total available units - Units sold) with the actual physical count, you can detect if any units were stolen or lost.

# Inventory Valuation and Financial Ratios

This section covers the calculation of closing stock using different methods, determining cost of sales, and analyzing inventory performance through financial ratios.

## 1. Inventory Valuation Methods

### FIFO (First-In, First-Out)
The FIFO method assumes that the oldest stock is sold first, meaning the closing stock consists of the most recently purchased items.

| Calculation Step | Details |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Value of Closing Stock** | 25 units (most recent) |
| $20 \times R20$ | $R400$ |
| $5 \times R15$ | $R75$ |
| $25 \times R2$ | $R50$ (Carriage on purchases per unit) |
| $25 \times R0,50$ | $R13$ (Custom duties per unit) |
| **Total Closing Stock** | **R538** |

### Weighted Average Method
This method calculates an average cost per unit based on the total cost of goods available for sale divided by the total number of units.

*   **Average Price:** $\frac{R1655}{100 \text{ units}} = R16,55 \text{ per unit}$
*   **Closing Stock Value:** $25 \text{ units} \times R16,55 = R413,75$ (Rounded to $R414$)

---

## 2. Financial Calculations

### Cost of Sales (FIFO)
To calculate the Cost of Sales, use the following formula:
$$\text{Opening Stock} + \text{Purchases (net)} + \text{Carriage on purchases} + \text{Custom duties} - \text{Closing Stock} = \text{Cost of Sales}$$
*   **Calculation:** $100 + 1330 + 180 + 45 - 538 = R1117$

### Gross Profit (FIFO)
$$\text{Sales (net)} - \text{Cost of Sales} = \text{Gross Profit}$$
*   **Calculation:** $3000 - R1117 = R1883$

---

## 3. Inventory Performance Ratios

### Mark-up Percentage
Formula: $\frac{\text{Gross Profit}}{\text{Cost of Sales}} \times 100$
*   **Calculation:** $\frac{1883}{1117} \times 100 = 168,58\%$
*   *Note:* If the actual mark-up is lower than the target (e.g., 200%), reasons may include seasonal sales or cash discounts offered to customers.

### Rate of Stock Turnover
Formula: $\frac{\text{Cost of Sales}}{\text{Average Stock}} = \text{times}$
*   **Average Stock:** $\frac{\text{Opening Stock} + \text{Closing Stock}}{2} = \frac{100 + 538}{2} = 319$
*   **Calculation:** $\frac{1117}{319} = 3,5 \text{ times}$
*   *Interpretation:* The stock is replaced 3,5 times per year.

### Period of Stock on Hand
Formula: $\frac{\text{Average Stock}}{\text{Cost of Sales}} \times 365 = \text{days}$
*   **Calculation:** $\frac{319}{1117} \times 365 = 104,24 \text{ days}$
*   *Interpretation:* Stock remains on the shelf for approximately 104 days before being sold.

# Chapter 6: Inventories - FIFO Method

## 1. Key Concepts
*   **FIFO (First-In, First-Out):** An inventory valuation method where the oldest stock items are assumed to be sold first. The remaining stock on hand is valued at the most recent purchase prices.

## 2. Calculations and Worked Examples

### 2.1 Selling Price per Unit
To calculate the selling price per unit when the price is constant:
$$\text{Selling Price} = \frac{\text{Total Sales}}{\text{Total Units Sold}}$$
$$\text{Selling Price} = \frac{R430\,500}{2\,100} = R205$$

### 2.2 Inventory Reconciliation (Missing Stock)
To determine missing stock (theft/loss), use the following reconciliation:
$$\text{Opening Stock} + \text{Purchases} - \text{Closing Stock} = \text{Units that should have been sold}$$
$$\text{Units that should have been sold} - \text{Actual Units Sold} = \text{Units Stolen}$$

**Example Calculation:**
*   Opening stock: $750$
*   Purchases: $+ 2\,480$
*   Less Closing stock: $- 1\,100$
*   **Total available to sell:** $2\,130$
*   Less Actual sales: $- 2\,100$
*   **Missing/Stolen units:** $30$

**Accounting Entry for Theft:**
*   **Debit:** Trading stock deficit / Loss due to theft ($30 \text{ units} \times R110 = R3\,300$)
*   **Credit:** Trading stock ($R3\,300$)
