# Term 3 Topic 1: Cost Accounting/Manufacturing

## Costs of Production

In a manufacturing environment, costs are categorized to determine the total cost of production. These are broadly divided into **Direct/Prime Costs** and **Factory Overheads**.

### Manufacturing Costs Breakdown

| Category | Cost Item | Description |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Direct/Prime costs** | Raw materials / Direct materials (issued or used) | Any materials used in the final product; processed or unprocessed materials; clearly visible in the final product. |
| | Direct labour | Workers directly involved in the production process ("touching" or "working" on the product). Includes wages/salaries plus employer contributions (pension, UIF, medical aid). |
| **Factory overheads** | Indirect materials / Consumables (issued or used) | Materials used in the production process but relatively insignificant. |
| | Indirect labour | Salaries/wages of factory manager, supervisor, security guards, and workers handling raw materials. Includes employer contributions (pension, UIF, medical aid). |
| | Depreciation | Only for factory equipment, machinery, or vehicles used in the factory. |
| | Maintenance and repairs | Of factory equipment, machinery, or vehicles used in the factory. |
| | Insurance | On factory plant, equipment, or vehicles used in the factory. |
| | Rent | Of factory plant or equipment. |
| | Water and electricity | Used in the factory. |
| | Other | Any cost directly related to factory equipment and machinery used in the factory. |

## 1. Manufacturing Costs (Production Costs)

Manufacturing costs are categorized based on their relationship to the level of production.

| Category | Definition | Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Variable costs** | Costs directly related to the level of production. These costs increase as more products are produced. | Raw materials / Direct materials (issued or used), Direct labour, Selling and distribution costs |
| **Fixed costs** | Costs that must be paid irrespective of the levels of production. These must be paid even if there is no production. | Factory overheads, Administration costs |

---

## 2. Non-Manufacturing Costs

These costs are associated with the business operations outside of the direct manufacturing process.

| Category | Definition | Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Administrative costs** | All costs related to the office. | Rent and insurance of office buildings/equipment, Depreciation on office equipment, Water and electricity (office), Salaries and wages (office staff, receptionists, cleaners), Pension fund/medical aid contributions, Maintenance of office buildings, Telephone costs. |
| **Selling and distribution costs (Marketing)** | All costs related to the shop or agents handling sales. | Commission paid to agents, Depreciation on shop equipment, Depreciation on vehicles (agents), Rent and insurance of shop/equipment, Telephone bills (agents), Water and electricity (shop), Bad debts. |

## 3.1 Key Concepts Definitions

| Concept | Definition |
| :--- | :--- |
| **3.1.1 Variable costs** | Costs that change in direct proportion to the level of production or activity (e.g., raw materials). |
| **3.1.2 Fixed costs** | Costs that remain constant regardless of the level of production or activity (e.g., rent, insurance). |
| **3.1.3 Break-even point** | The level of production or sales where total revenue equals total costs, resulting in neither profit nor loss. |
| **3.1.4 Prime costs** | The sum of direct material costs and direct labour costs. |
| **3.1.5 Factory overheads** | All indirect costs incurred in the factory during the manufacturing process (e.g., factory electricity, supervisor salaries). |

---

## 3.2 Calculation of Fixed Costs
Fixed costs are calculated by summing all expenses that do not change with the number of units produced or guests attending.
$$\text{Total Fixed Costs} = \sum \text{Fixed Expenses}$$

---

## 3.3 Calculation of Variable Costs per Person
Variable costs per person are calculated by dividing the total variable costs by the number of units or people.
$$\text{Variable Cost per unit} = \frac{\text{Total Variable Costs}}{\text{Number of units}}$$

---

## 3.4 Calculation of Break-even Point
The break-even point in units is calculated using the following formula:
$$\text{Break-even point} = \frac{\text{Total Fixed Costs}}{\text{Selling Price per unit} - \text{Variable Cost per unit}}$$

*Note: The denominator $(\text{Selling Price} - \text{Variable Cost})$ is also known as the **Contribution Margin**.*

### 1.7 Break-even Point Calculation

The break-even point is the level of production where total revenue equals total costs (no profit, no loss).

**Step 1: Calculate Total Fixed Costs**
$$\text{Fixed costs} = \text{Factory overheads} + \text{Admin costs}$$
$$\text{Fixed costs} = 726\,610 + 521\,380 = 1\,247\,990$$

**Step 2: Calculate Total Variable Costs**
$$\text{Variable costs} = \text{Raw materials} + \text{Direct labour} + \text{Selling and distribution costs}$$
$$\text{Variable costs} = 1\,652\,100 + 1\,237\,500 + 1\,085\,000 = 3\,974\,600$$

**Step 3: Calculate Variable Cost per Unit**
$$\text{Variable cost per unit} = \frac{\text{Total variable costs}}{\text{Units produced}}$$
$$\frac{3\,974\,600}{90\,000} = 44,16$$

**Step 4: Calculate Break-even Point**
$$\text{Break-even point} = \frac{\text{Total Fixed Costs}}{\text{Variable cost per unit}}$$
$$\frac{1\,247\,990}{44,16} = 28\,261 \text{ units}$$

### 1.8 Analysis of Production
The business produced $90\,000$ units. Since the actual production ($90\,000$) is significantly higher than the break-even point ($28\,261$), the business is operating profitably.

#### 3.3 Calculation of Variable Costs per Person
Variable costs are costs that change in direct proportion to the number of guests.

#### 3.4 Number of Guests to Break Even
The break-even point is where total income equals total expenses. The formula used is:
$$\text{Break-even units} = \frac{\text{Fixed Costs}}{\text{Selling Price per unit} - \text{Variable Cost per unit}}$$

$$\text{Number of guests} = \frac{44\,000}{500 - 450} = 880 \text{ guests}$$

#### 3.5 Proof of Break-even Point
*   **Income:** $880 \times R500 = R440\,000$
*   **Total Costs:** $\text{Fixed costs} + (\text{Number of guests} \times \text{Variable cost per person})$
*   **Total Costs:** $44\,000 + (880 \times R450) = 44\,000 + 396\,000 = R440\,000$
*   **Conclusion:** Since Income ($R440\,000$) = Expenses ($R440\,000$), the business breaks even.

#### 3.6 Analysis of Profit/Loss
If the business hosts only 600 guests:
*   **Income:** $600 \times R500 = R300\,000$
*   **Total Costs:** $44\,000 + (600 \times R450) = 44\,000 + 270\,000 = R314\,000$
