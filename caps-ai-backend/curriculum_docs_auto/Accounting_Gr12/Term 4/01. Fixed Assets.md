# Chapter 8: Fixed Assets

## 8.1 Introduction to Fixed Assets

Fixed assets are tangible items purchased by a business with the intention of using them in the daily operations of the business to generate profit, rather than for resale.

### Key Principles and Procedures

*   **Historical Cost (GAAP Principle):** Fixed assets are recorded in the books at their original purchase price, known as the **Cost Price**.
*   **Asset Register:** A separate, detailed record is maintained for every fixed asset. This register tracks the full history of the asset and must be kept up to date at all times.
*   **Depreciation Methods:** Fixed assets lose value over time. This is accounted for using one of two primary methods:
    *   **Cost Price Method:** Also known as the **Straight-line method**.
    *   **Carrying Value Method:** Also known as the **Diminishing balance method** or **Book value method**.
*   **Internal Control:** Businesses must regularly monitor their assets and maintain accurate asset registers to ensure internal control.
*   **Disposal of Assets:** When an asset is sold:
    *   The asset register must be updated.
    *   Additional depreciation must be calculated up to the date of sale.
    *   The asset account is closed off as it no longer belongs to the business.
*   **Year-end Adjustments:** At the end of each financial year, all relevant fixed assets must be depreciated. The total depreciation for the year (including depreciation on assets sold during that year) is disclosed in the **Income Statement**.

## Key Concepts
*   **Carrying Value (Book Value):** The value of an asset after accounting for accumulated depreciation.
    $$\text{Cost price} - \text{Accumulated depreciation} = \text{Carrying value}$$
*   **Depreciation Limit:** Fixed assets can only be depreciated until they reach a scrap value of $R1$. Therefore, the carrying value of a fixed asset cannot be less than $R1$.

# Chapter 8: Fixed Assets - Depreciation at Carrying Value

## 1. Key Concepts
*   **Depreciation:** The allocation of the cost of a fixed asset over its useful life. It is a legal method of reducing net profit to lower tax liability.
*   **Carrying Value (Book Value):** The value of an asset after accumulated depreciation has been subtracted from the original cost.
*   **Diminishing Balance Method:** A method where depreciation is calculated as a percentage of the current carrying value of the asset.
*   **Residual/Scrap Value:** The estimated value of an asset at the end of its useful life. In this curriculum context, depreciation continues until the asset reaches a carrying value of $R1$.

## 2. Formula
The relationship between cost, accumulated depreciation, and carrying value is defined as:
$$\text{Carrying Value} = \text{Cost Price} - \text{Accumulated Depreciation}$$

*   **Item:** Office computer
*   **Cost Price:** $R20\,000$
*   **Depreciation Rate:** $10\%$ p.a. on carrying value (diminishing balance)

## 4. Important Notes
*   **Recording:** Once calculated, the annual depreciation amount must be recorded in the **General Journal** as an expense for the financial year.
*   **Limit of Depreciation:** Depreciation is calculated annually until the asset reaches a carrying value of $R1$. Once the asset reaches this value, no further depreciation can be charged.

### Chapter 8: Fixed Assets - Scrap Value and Asset Disposal

#### 1. The Scrap Value Principle
When an asset is depreciated, it cannot be depreciated below a carrying value of R1. 
*   **Formula:** $\text{Cost Price} - \text{Accumulated Depreciation} = \text{Carrying Value}$
*   **Example:** $\text{R100 000} - \text{R99 999} = \text{R1}$

**Ledger Accounts for R1 Carrying Value:**

#### 3. Practical Application: Asset Not Yet at R1
In practice, the scrap value principle is not applied if the fixed asset has not yet reached its R1 carrying value.

**Example:**
*   Vehicle sold for R40 000 cash.
*   Cost price of vehicle: R100 000
*   Accumulated depreciation: R90 000
*   Carrying value: $\text{R100 000} - \text{R90 000} = \text{R10 000}$
*   Depreciation is calculated at $20\%$ on Cost price.

**Calculations:**
*   Additional depreciation: $\text{R100 000} \times 20\% = \text{R20 000}$
*   The carrying value is R10 000; therefore, depreciation can only be R10 000 (as an asset cannot be depreciated below its cost price).

**Asset Disposal Account:**

| DR | ASSET DISPOSAL | CR |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Vehicle | 100 000 | Accum. depreciation | 100 000 |
| Profit on sale of asset | 40 000 | Bank | 40 000 |
| | **140 000** | | **140 000** |

*Note: Because the principle of R1 is not applied, the profit is R40 000.*

## Steps to Follow When Disposing of a Fixed Asset

1. **Transfer Cost:** Find the cost price of the fixed asset sold and transfer it to the Asset Disposal account.
2. **Additional Depreciation:** Calculate any additional depreciation on the fixed asset sold up to the date of sale.
3. **Transfer Depreciation:** Move/Transfer the total accumulated depreciation on the fixed asset sold to the Asset Disposal account.
4. **Record Sale:** Record the selling price of the fixed asset sold in the Asset Disposal account.
5. **Calculate Profit/Loss:** Determine the profit or loss on the sale of the fixed asset.
6. **Year-end Depreciation:** At the end of the year, record the depreciation for all remaining fixed assets and any new assets acquired during the financial year.

# Chapter 8: Fixed Assets - Depreciation Methods

## 1. Straight Line Method (Cost Price Method)
This method calculates depreciation as a fixed percentage of the original cost price of the asset. The annual depreciation amount remains constant throughout the useful life of the asset.

**Key Concept:** The asset cannot be depreciated below its scrap value (residual value).

### Example 1: Schie Traders (Asset Register No. 1)
*   **Cost Price:** R80 000
*   **Depreciation Rate:** 20% p.a. on cost

| Details | Annual Depreciation Calculations | Accumulated Depreciation | Book Value (Carrying Value) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| End of first year | $80\,000 \times 20\% = 16\,000$ | $16\,000$ | $64\,000$ |
| End of second year | $80\,000 \times 20\% = 16\,000$ | $32\,000$ | $48\,000$ |
| End of third year | $80\,000 \times 20\% = 16\,000$ | $48\,000$ | $32\,000$ |
| End of fourth year | $80\,000 \times 20\% = 16\,000$ | $64\,000$ | $16\,000$ |
| End of fifth year | $15\,999$ | $79\,999$ | $R1$ |

*Note: In the fifth year, the depreciation is limited to R15 999 to ensure the asset retains a scrap value of R1.*

---

## 2. Diminishing Balance Method (Book Value Method)
This method calculates depreciation as a fixed percentage of the current carrying value (book value) of the asset. As the book value decreases each year, the annual depreciation amount also decreases.

### Example 2: Schie Traders (Asset Register No. 2)
*   **Cost Price:** R20 000
*   **Depreciation Rate:** 10% p.a. on carrying value

| Details | Annual Depreciation Calculations | Accumulated Depreciation | Book Value (Carrying Value) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Cost price | - | - | $20\,000$ |
| End of first year | $20\,000 \times 10\% \times \frac{6}{12} = 1\,000$ | $1\,000$ | $19\,000$ |
| End of second year | $19\,000 \times 10\% = 1\,900$ | $2\,900$ | $17\,100$ |
| End of third year | $17\,100 \times 10\% = 1\,710$ | $4\,610$ | $15\,390$ |
| End of fourth year | $15\,390 \times 10\% = 1\,539$ | $6\,149$ | $13\,851$ |
| End of fifth year | $13\,851 \times 10\% = 1\,385,10$ | $7\,534,10$ | $12\,465,90$ |

## 4. Steps to Dispose of a Fixed Asset
1.  **Transfer the cost price** to Asset disposal.
2.  **Calculate additional depreciation** up to the date of sale.
3.  **Transfer total accumulated depreciation** to Asset disposal.
4.  **Record the selling price** in Asset disposal.
5.  **Calculate the profit or loss** on the sale of the asset.
6.  **Calculate the depreciation** of all remaining and new fixed assets.
