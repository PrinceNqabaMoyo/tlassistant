# Chapter 5: Reconciliations

## Definition
Reconciliation is a form of internal control where two sets of information are compared. When differences are identified between these sets, they must be corrected or explained.

## Types of Reconciliations

| Bank Reconciliation | Debtors’ Reconciliation | Creditors’ Reconciliation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| The balance in the bank account in the business’ general ledger should be the same as the balance on the bank statement received from the bank. When these are not the same, they need to be reconciled. | The balance of the debtors’ control account should be the same as the total of the debtors’ list. When these are not the same, they need to be reconciled. | The balance of the creditors’ control account should be the same as the total of the creditors’ list. The statements received from the creditors must match each creditor's balance in the business’ books. When these are not the same, they need to be reconciled. |

# 5.1 Bank Reconciliation

Bank reconciliation is the process of matching the balances in an entity's accounting records (CRJ/CPJ) with the information provided on the monthly Bank Statement. Differences occur due to timing or transactions processed by the bank that have not yet been recorded in the business books.

---

## Common Differences and Recording Procedures

### 1. Bank Charges and Interest Charged
These are expenses incurred by the business. They must be recorded in the **Cash Payments Journal (CPJ)**.

| Transaction | Journal | Details | Amount |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Bank Charges ($10+20+30$) | CPJ | ABSA Bank | 60 |
| Interest on Overdraft | CPJ | ABSA Bank | 40 |

### 2. Interest Earned/Received
This is income for the business. It must be recorded in the **Cash Receipts Journal (CRJ)**.

| Transaction | Journal | Details | Amount |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Interest on current account | CRJ | ABSA Bank | 50 |

### 3. Direct Deposits
When a third party pays directly into the business bank account, it must be recorded in the **CRJ**.

| Transaction | Journal | Details | Amount |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Rent Income | CRJ | E Baloyi | 800 |

### 4. Stop Orders / Debit Orders
These are automatic payments made by the bank on behalf of the business. They must be recorded in the **CPJ**.

| Transaction | Journal | Details | Amount |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Insurance | CPJ | Santam | 367 |

### 5. Outstanding Cheques
Cheques issued by the business (recorded in the CPJ) but not yet presented to the bank for payment. These appear in the **Bank Reconciliation Statement** as a **Debit** (reducing the bank balance).

### 8. Post-dated Cheques: Received and Deposited
When a cheque is received that is dated for a future date, it should not be deposited immediately. If it is deposited inadvertently, it will be dishonoured by the bank.

**Example:** B Bud (tenant) paid R157. The cheque was dated 21 July 2014 but deposited in May 2014. It was dishonoured.

| Journal | Details | Amount | Contra Account |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **CPJ (May 2014)** | B Bud (RD cheque) | 157 | Rent Income |

---

### 9. Post-dated Cheques: Issued
If the business issues a cheque dated for a future date, it is not yet a valid payment for the bank. It must be recorded as an outstanding cheque in the Bank Reconciliation Statement until the date on the cheque is reached.

**Example:** Cheque no. 303 for R6 000 issued to a Factory, dated 25 July 2014.

| Bank Reconciliation Statement (May 2014) | Debit | Credit |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Debit outstanding cheques: 303 (25 July 2014) | 6 000 | |

---

### 10. Stale Cheques: Issued
A cheque is considered "stale" if it is older than 6 months. If a business issued a cheque that was never cashed and is now stale, the original entry must be reversed.

**Example:** Cheque no. 120 issued to Shezi Stat on 20 November 2013 for R100.

| Journal | Details | Amount | Contra Account |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **CRJ (May 2014)** | Shezi Stat (Cancel stale cheque) | 100 | Stationery |

---

### 11. Stale Cheques: Received and Dishonoured
If a cheque received from a debtor is stale, it cannot be processed by the bank and will be dishonoured.

**Example:** Cheque R160 from J Nel, dated 25 May 2013, deposited in May 2014.

| Journal | Details | Amount | Contra Account |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **CPJ (May 2014)** | J Nel (RD cheque) | 160 | Debtors control |

---

### 12. Lost Cheques: Issued
If a cheque is lost, the business must cancel the original cheque and issue a replacement.

**Procedure:**
1. **Cancel** the lost cheque in the CRJ.
2. **Issue** the new cheque in the CPJ.
3. **Record** the new cheque as a "debit outstanding cheque" in the Bank Reconciliation Statement.

**Example:** Cheque no. 255 (R500) lost by PNA. Replaced by cheque no. 365.

| Journal | Details | Amount | Contra Account |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **CRJ (May 2014)** | PNA (Cancel lost cheque) | 500 | Printing |
| **CPJ (May 2014)** | PNA (Issue new cheque) | 500 | Printing |

| Bank Reconciliation Statement (May 2014) | Debit | Credit |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Debit outstanding cheques: 365 | 500 | |

# Chapter 5: Bank Reconciliations - Handling Errors

## 1. Errors Made by the Business in the CRJ and CPJ

When the business records an incorrect amount in the journals, the difference must be corrected by making an entry in the opposite journal.

### Case 13: Amount recorded is less than it should be
If the business recorded an amount that is too low, the difference must be recorded in the same journal to increase the total.
*   **Example:** Cheque no. 253 was recorded as R646 in the CPJ, but the Bank Statement shows R664.
*   **Correction:** Record the difference ($664 - 646 = 18$) in the CPJ.

| Journal | Cheque No. | Details | Amount | Difference | Account |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| CPJ - May 2014 | 453 | Makro | | 18 | Trading stock |

### Case 14: Amount recorded is more than it should be
If the business recorded an amount that is too high, the difference must be recorded in the opposite journal to decrease the total.
*   **Example:** Cheque no. 244 was recorded as R258 in the CPJ, but the Bank Statement shows R250.
*   **Correction:** Record the difference ($258 - 250 = 8$) in the CRJ.

| Journal | Cheque No. | Details | Amount | Difference | Account |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| CRJ - May 2014 | 244 | Investec | | 8 | Rent Expense |

## 2. Errors Made by the Bank on the Bank Statement

These errors are corrected in the **Bank Reconciliation Statement**, not in the business journals.

### Case 15: Cheque drawn by another client erroneously debited
If the bank mistakenly debits your account for someone else's cheque, you must "reverse" this by crediting the Bank Reconciliation Statement.
*   **Example:** Cheque no. 2230 for R1 500 (ZITHA Stores) was wrongly debited to our account.

### Case 16: Deposit wrongly credited to our account
If the bank mistakenly credits your account with a deposit that belongs to someone else, you must "reverse" this by debiting the Bank Reconciliation Statement.
*   **Example:** A deposit of R2 000 was wrongly credited to our account.

| Bank Reconciliation Statement | Debit | Credit |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |

# 5.2 Debtors' Reconciliation

Debtors' reconciliation is the process of ensuring that the balance of the Debtors' Control account in the General Ledger matches the total of the individual balances in the Debtors' List (extracted from the Debtors' Ledger).

## Steps to Correct Differences
When a discrepancy arises between the Debtors' Control account and the Debtors' List, follow these steps:
1. **Verify Source Documents:** Check entries in the subsidiary journals against the original source documents.
2. **Verify Casting:** Check the casting (totalling) of all subsidiary journals.
3. **Verify Posting:** Check the posting of amounts from the journals to both the General Ledger and the Debtors' Ledger.

## Procedure for Reconciliation
When the Debtors' Control account balance does not correlate with the Debtors' List total:
1. **Identify the Location of the Error:** Determine if the error exists in the Debtors' Control account or the Debtors' List.
2. **Identify the Nature of the Error:** Determine if the error occurred in the original entry or in the calculation of the journal total.
   * *Note:* If the original entry is incorrect, the total will also be incorrect. Both the Control account and the List must be corrected.

## Posting Rules
The following rules govern the flow of information in the accounting system:
* **Daily Posting:** Individual entries are posted daily from the journals to the specific accounts in the **Debtors' Ledger** (which forms the Debtors' List).
* **Monthly Posting:** The total of the journal is posted to the **Debtors' Control account** at the end of the month.



![StudyGuide_Accounting_Gr12_Calculations-involving-percentages.pdf-0004-07.png](images/StudyGuide_Accounting_Gr12_Calculations-involving-percentages.pdf-0004-07.png)


### Example: Posting a Transaction
If goods are sold to "Monki" for $R10$ and an invoice is issued:

| Journal | Amount |
| :--- | :--- |
| Monki | $10$ |
| **Total** | **$10$** |

* **Debtors' Ledger Entry:** The $10$ is posted to Monki's account in the Debtors' Ledger.
* **Control Account Entry:** The total of $10$ is posted to the Debtors' Control account in the General Ledger.



![StudyGuide_Accounting_Gr12_Calculations-involving-percentages.pdf-0004-09.png](images/StudyGuide_Accounting_Gr12_Calculations-involving-percentages.pdf-0004-09.png)


## Common Types of Errors and Omissions
Errors that typically cause a reconciliation discrepancy include:
* Errors on source documents.
* Recording errors in subsidiary journals.
* Incorrect posting from journals to the General Ledger or Debtors' Ledger.
* Incorrect additions (casting) in the lists of debtors.
* Incorrect addition of journal totals:
    * **Overcast:** The total is calculated as too much.
    * **Undercast:** The total is calculated as too little.

# Chapter 5: Reconciliations - Debtors Ledger and Control Account

## Basic Rules for Posting
To maintain accurate financial records, follow these fundamental rules when posting from the Debtors Journal (DJ) to the Debtors Ledger and the General Ledger:

1.  **Individual Entries:** Each individual transaction must be posted to the specific debtor's account in the **Debtors Ledger**.
2.  **Totals:** The total of the journal is posted to the **Debtors Control Account** in the General Ledger at the end of the month.

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

| Debtors Journal | Debtors Ledger (Debtors List) | General Ledger: Debtors Control (A) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Entry: 50 | Entry: 50 (Dr) | Dr: Total (Sales) 110 |
| Entry: 60 | Entry: 60 (Dr) | |
| **Total: 110** | | |

---

## Identifying and Correcting Errors
When an error occurs, you must determine whether the mistake happened at the **Entry level** or the **Total level**.

### 1. Error in the Entry
If the individual entry is incorrect, both the Debtors Ledger and the Debtors Control account will be incorrect. You must correct the entry in the Debtors Journal first.

**Example:** An entry was recorded as $56$ instead of $50$.

| Debtors Journal | Debtors Ledger (Debtors List) | General Ledger: Debtors Control (A) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Entry: 56 | Entry: 56 (Dr) | Dr: Total 116 |
| Entry: 60 | Entry: 60 (Dr) | |
| **Total: 116** | | |

*   **Correction:** To fix this, you must adjust the entry by $-6$ in the Debtors Ledger and adjust the total in the Debtors Control account by $-(116 - 6) = 110$.

### 2. Error in the Total
If the total was added incorrectly, but the individual entries are correct, the error only exists in the Debtors Control account. The Debtors Ledger remains correct.

**Example:** The total of the DJ was incorrectly calculated as $100$ instead of $110$.

| Debtors Journal | Debtors Ledger (Debtors List) | General Ledger: Debtors Control (A) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Entry: 50 | Entry: 50 (Dr) | Dr: Total 100 |
| Entry: 60 | Entry: 60 (Dr) | |
| **Total: 100** | | |

*   **Correction:** Since the individual entries are correct, you do not touch the Debtors Ledger. You only correct the Debtors Control account by adding the difference: $100 + 10 = 110$.

---

### Reconciling Errors in Debtors Ledgers

When the total of the Debtors Journal (DJ) matches the Debtors Control Account but individual debtor balances do not reconcile, the error lies in the **posting** to the individual debtor's account in the Debtors Ledger.

---

#### 1. Omitted Entry (Not Posted)
If an entry was recorded in the DJ but not posted to the specific debtor's account, you must post the missing amount to correct the balance.

*   **Example:** An amount of $R50$ was recorded in the DJ but not posted to the debtor's account.
*   **Correction:** Post the $R50$ to the debit side of the debtor's account.



![StudyGuide_Accounting_Gr12_Calculations-involving-percentages.pdf-0006-13.png](images/StudyGuide_Accounting_Gr12_Calculations-involving-percentages.pdf-0006-13.png)


---

#### 2. Under-casting (Amount Recorded Too Low)
If an entry was posted, but the amount recorded in the debtor's account is less than the actual amount, the account is under-cast.

*   **Example:** An amount of $R50$ in the DJ was posted as $R30$.
*   **Calculation of difference:** $R50 - R30 = R20$.
*   **Correction:** Debit the debtor's account with the difference of $R20$.

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

---

#### 3. Over-casting (Amount Recorded Too High)
If an entry was posted, but the amount recorded in the debtor's account is more than the actual amount, the account is over-cast.

*   **Example:** An amount of $R50$ in the DJ was posted as $R59$.
*   **Calculation of difference:** $R59 - R50 = R9$.
*   **Correction:** Credit the debtor's account with the difference of $R9$ to reduce the balance.

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

---

#### 4. Posting to the Wrong Side
If an entry was posted to the credit side of a debtor's account instead of the debit side (or vice versa), the error must be reversed.

*   **Correction Procedure:**
    1.  **Cancel the error:** Make an entry on the opposite side of the account (e.g., if wrongly credited, debit the account with the same amount).
    2.  **Record correctly:** Make the entry on the correct side (debit) with the original amount.
    *   *Note:* This effectively doubles the entry on the correct side to offset the incorrect entry.

---

### Posting Errors and Reconciliations

#### Concept: Posting Errors
When an entry from the Debtors Journal (DJ) is posted to the Debtors Ledger, it must be recorded on the **Debit (Dr)** side. If an entry is incorrectly recorded on the **Credit (Cr)** side, it creates a discrepancy between the Debtors Ledger and the Debtors Control Account.

#### Key Definitions
*   **Overcast:** The journal total has been added up incorrectly, resulting in an amount that is too high.
*   **Undercast:** The journal total has been added up incorrectly, resulting in an amount that is too small.

# 5.3 Debtors’ Age Analysis

## Introduction
Businesses must maintain strict control over the Debtors Ledger. The credit controller is responsible for monitoring credit ratings, ensuring debtors do not exceed their credit limits, and ensuring payments are received according to agreed terms.

Before despatching an order, the debtor’s clerk must verify the account balance and the agreed credit limit.

### Definition: Debtors’ Age Analysis
Age analysis is a process used to break down a debtor’s ledger account into specific time periods to identify how long an amount has been outstanding.

### Rules for Calculating Age Analysis
When performing an age analysis, apply these two fundamental rules:
1. **Returns:** Subtracted from the latest sale.
2. **Payments:** Subtracted from the oldest outstanding balance.



![Textbook_Accounting_Gr12_Manufacturing.pdf-0009-01.png](images/Textbook_Accounting_Gr12_Manufacturing.pdf-0009-01.png)


*Note: A credit note is subtracted from the most recent invoice because goods can usually be returned within a specific period (e.g., 7 days). A receipt is issued for payments and discounts allowed; this total is subtracted from the oldest amount owed.*

## Reasons for Debtors’ Age Analysis
*   To ensure debtors honour their credit agreements (e.g., 30 days).
*   To charge interest on overdue balances as per the agreement.
*   To initiate legal action if accounts are not settled within a set period (e.g., > 90 days).
*   To minimize bad debts that must be written off.
*   To ensure business liquidity and healthy cash flow.
*   To identify which debtors honour agreements and which do not.
*   To follow up on debtors who have signed payment plans due to previous late payments.
*   To identify and rectify potential errors on a debtor’s statement.

## Example: Debtors’ Statement and Age Analysis
To calculate the age analysis for "Moja and Sons," the account is broken down into time periods (February to May).

**Application of Rules:**
*   **Returns:** Subtracted from the most recent invoice.
*   **Payments:** Subtracted from the oldest outstanding balance.

### Calculating Age Analysis
**The Golden Rule:** Always subtract payments from the longest outstanding balance first.

#### Steps for Calculation:
1.  **Template:** Create a table with columns for the current month and preceding months.
2.  **Invoices:** Record invoices in the month the stock was received.
3.  **Payments:** Apply payments to the oldest debt first.
    *   *Example:* A R500 payment in March reduces the February balance of R800 to R300.
    *   *Example:* A R400 receipt in April first clears the remaining R300 from February, then applies the remaining R100 to the March debt.

#### Age Analysis Calculation Table
| Transactions | May | April | March | February |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Balance | | | | 800 |
| Invoices | 1 200 + 700 | 2 400 | 2 500 | |
| Returns | | (600) | | |
| **Total purchases per month** | **1 900** | **1 800** | **2 500** | **800** |
| *Receipts and discounts* | | | | |
| Mar: payment by debtor R500 | - | - | - | (500) |
| Apr: payment by debtor R400 | - | - | (100) | (300) |
| May: R1 500 + 150 | - | - | (1 650) | - |
| **Balance** | **1 900** | **1 800** | **750** | **0** |

**Verification:**
The sum of the age analysis must equal the total balance on the statement:

### 5.4 Creditors Reconciliation with the Creditor's Statement

#### Key Concepts
* **Definition:** A creditors reconciliation is a process performed at the end of each month when a statement is received from a creditor. It involves comparing the creditor's statement against the business's own records in the Creditors Ledger.
* **Purpose:** To ensure that all details of transactions with the creditor are correct before any payments are processed.

#### Important Note on Perspective
When performing a reconciliation, remember that you are a **debtor** in the books of the creditor. 
* When you interpret the statement received from the creditor, read it as if you are reading a **Debtors Control Account**.
* **Rule of thumb:** Every debit entry in your books will appear as a credit entry in the creditor's books.
* **Requirement:** You must have a thorough understanding of the format of both **Control Accounts** and **Source Documents**.

# Chapter 5: Creditors Reconciliations

## Key Concepts
When dealing with creditors, it is essential to understand that the business's records (Creditor's Ledger) and the creditor's records (Creditors Statement) are mirror images of each other.

*   **Creditor's Ledger (In the books of the business):** The business records the debt as a liability (Credit +). Payments and returns decrease the liability (Debit -).
*   **Creditors Statement (In the books of the creditor):** The creditor records the amount owed by the business as an asset (Debit +). Payments and returns received from the business decrease the amount owed (Credit -).

### Transaction Flow
1.  **Invoice:** The creditor sends an invoice to the business.
2.  **Debit Note:** If goods are returned, the business issues a debit note to the creditor.
3.  **Credit Note:** The creditor acknowledges the return by issuing a credit note.
4.  **Payment:** The business pays via cheque, and the creditor issues a receipt.

## Mathematical Reconciliation Logic
The balances reconcile because the entries are inverse:
*   A **Credit** in the business's ledger corresponds to a **Debit** in the creditor's statement.
*   A **Debit** in the business's ledger corresponds to a **Credit** in the creditor's statement.

For any transaction amount $x$:
$$\text{Business Balance} = \text{Opening Balance} + \text{Credit}_x - \text{Debit}_x$$
$$\text{Creditor Balance} = \text{Opening Balance} + \text{Debit}_x - \text{Credit}_x$$

# Creditors Reconciliation: Analysis of Errors

When reconciling a Creditors Statement with the Creditors Ledger, discrepancies often arise due to timing differences or errors made by either the business or the supplier.

## Information: Investigation Findings
The following discrepancies were identified between the records of Ace Traders and the statement received from Kairo Suppliers:

1. A cheque for $R3\ 000$ issued by Ace Traders has not yet been recorded on the supplier's statement.
2. A discount of $R500$ recorded by Ace Traders was disallowed by Kairo Suppliers because payment was not received within the 7-day settlement period.
3. Goods returned were recorded as $R810$ in the Creditors Ledger, but the supplier statement shows $R900$.
4. An invoice for $R7\ 700$ was recorded correctly by Ace Traders, but the supplier statement incorrectly recorded it as $R770$.
5. An invoice for $R3\ 500$ received from Kairo Suppliers was incorrectly recorded as a credit note by Ace Traders.

---

## Explanation of Transactions and Adjustments

| No. | Error Description | Explanation and Adjustment |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1.** | Cheque for $R3\ 000$ not on statement. | The payment was made but not yet processed by the supplier. Deduct $R3\ 000$ from the balance on the Creditors Reconciliation Statement. |
| **2.** | Discount of $R500$ disallowed. | The discount must be cancelled. This increases the amount owed to the creditor. Add $R500$ to the Creditors Ledger. |
| **3.** | Returns recorded as $R810$ vs $R900$. | Ace Traders undercast the return by $R90$ ($R900 - R810 = R90$). The Creditors Ledger must be adjusted to reflect the full return. Subtract $R90$ from the Creditors Ledger. |
| **4.** | Invoice $R7\ 700$ recorded as $R770$ on statement. | The statement is undercast by $R6\ 930$ ($R7\ 700 - R770 = R6\ 930$). The statement balance must be increased by $R6\ 930$. |

## Key Accounting Principles for Reconciliations

*   **Creditors Control Account:** This is a liability account. It increases on the **Credit** side and decreases on the **Debit** side.
*   **Reconciliation Statement:** Used to align the balance as per the Creditors Ledger with the balance as per the Creditors Statement.
*   **Errors on Statement:** If the error is on the supplier's statement, the adjustment is made on the **Reconciliation Statement**.
*   **Errors in Ledger:** If the error is in the business's own books (Creditors Ledger), the adjustment is made in the **Creditors Ledger** (and subsequently the Creditors Control Account).

### Creditors Reconciliation: Error Correction

When reconciling a Creditors Ledger with a Creditors Statement, errors must be identified and corrected using double-entry principles.

#### Worked Example: Error Correction
**Scenario:** An invoice for $R3\ 500$ received from Kairo Suppliers was incorrectly recorded as a credit note by Ace Traders.

*   **Analysis:**
    *   The Creditors Statement is correct.
    *   The Creditors Ledger is incorrect because the invoice was recorded as a return (credit note).
*   **Correction Procedure:**
    1.  Cancel the incorrect entry: The original entry was a debit of $R3\ 500$. To cancel this, a credit of $R3\ 500$ is required.
    2.  Record the correct entry: The invoice of $R3\ 500$ must be recorded as a credit.
    3.  **Total Adjustment:** $R3\ 500 + R3\ 500 = R7\ 000$ to be recorded on the credit side of the Creditors Ledger.
