# Chapter 9: Longitudinal Waves

## Introduction and Key Concepts
In previous studies, we examined transverse pulses and waves. In this chapter, we explore a different type of wave known as a **longitudinal wave**.

*   **Transverse waves:** The motion of the particles in the medium is perpendicular to the direction of the wave.
*   **Longitudinal waves:** The particles in the medium move **parallel** (in the same direction as) to the motion of the wave.

**Examples:**
*   **Transverse:** Water waves.
*   **Longitudinal:** Sound waves.

---

## What is a Longitudinal Wave?

### Definition
> A **longitudinal wave** is a wave where the particles in the medium move parallel to the direction of propagation of the wave.

### Understanding Wave Motion
Similar to our study of transverse waves, we must distinguish between two types of motion:
1.  The motion of the particles in the medium.
2.  The motion of the wave itself.

To visualize the construction of a longitudinal wave, it is best observed using a slinky spring.

## Amplitude

> **DEFINITION: Amplitude**
> The **amplitude** is the maximum displacement from equilibrium. For a longitudinal wave, which is a pressure wave, this would be the maximum increase (or decrease) in pressure from the equilibrium pressure that is caused when a compression (or rarefaction) passes a point.

The amplitude is the distance from the equilibrium position of the medium to a compression or a rarefaction.

---

## Period and Frequency

> **DEFINITION: Period**
> The **period** of a wave is the time taken by the wave to move one wavelength.

> **DEFINITION: Frequency**
> The **frequency** of a wave is the number of wavelengths per second.

# Chapter 9: Longitudinal Waves

## Period and Frequency

### Definitions
*   **Period ($T$):** The time taken for a longitudinal wave to move one wavelength. It is measured in seconds ($s$).
*   **Frequency ($f$):** The number of wavelengths that pass a point per second.

### Mathematical Relationship
The frequency and period are inversely proportional to each other:

$$f = \frac{1}{T}$$

Alternatively, the period can be expressed as:

$$T = \frac{1}{f}$$

---

## Speed of a Longitudinal Wave

### Definition: Wave Speed
Wave speed is defined as the distance a wave travels per unit time.

| Quantity | Unit Name | Unit Symbol |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Wave speed ($v$) | speed | $m \cdot s^{-1}$ |

### Deriving the Wave Speed Formula
The distance between two successive compressions is equal to one wavelength ($\lambda$). Therefore, in a time equal to one period ($T$), the wave travels a distance of one wavelength ($\lambda$).

The speed of the wave ($v$) is calculated as:

$$v = \frac{\text{distance travelled}}{\text{time taken}} = \frac{\lambda}{T}$$

Since we know that $f = \frac{1}{T}$, we can substitute this into the equation to express wave speed in terms of frequency:

$$v = f \cdot \lambda$$

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

---

### 9.5 The Wave Equation

The speed of a wave can be derived from the relationship between wavelength, period, and frequency:

$$v = \frac{\lambda}{T}$$
$$v = \lambda \cdot \frac{1}{T}$$
$$v = \lambda \cdot f$$

We call this equation the **wave equation**.

#### Summary of Variables
*   $v$ = speed in $\text{m} \cdot \text{s}^{-1}$
*   $\lambda$ = wavelength in $\text{m}$
*   $f$ = frequency in $\text{Hz}$

---

### Example 1: Speed of longitudinal waves

**QUESTION**
The musical note "A" is a sound wave. The note has a frequency of $440\text{ Hz}$ and a wavelength of $0,784\text{ m}$. Calculate the speed of the musical note.

**SOLUTION**

**Step 1: Determine what is given and what is required**
Using:
$$f = 440\text{ Hz}$$
$$\lambda = 0,784\text{ m}$$
We need to calculate the speed ($v$) of the musical note "A".

**Step 2: Determine how to approach based on what is given**
We are given the frequency and wavelength of the note. We can therefore use:
$$v = f \cdot \lambda$$

**Step 3: Calculate the wave speed**
$$v = f \cdot \lambda$$
$$v = (440\text{ Hz})(0,784\text{ m})$$
$$v = 345\text{ m} \cdot \text{s}^{-1}$$

## Example 2: Speed of Longitudinal Waves

### Question
A longitudinal wave travels into a medium in which its speed increases. How does this affect its:
1. Period?
2. Wavelength?

---

### Solution

**Step 1: Determine what is required**
We need to determine how the period and wavelength of a longitudinal wave change when its speed increases.

**Step 2: Determine how to approach based on what is given**
We need to find the link between period, wavelength, and wave speed.

**Step 3: Discuss how the period changes**
The frequency ($f$) of a longitudinal wave is dependent on the frequency of the vibrations that create the wave. Therefore, the frequency is always unchanged, irrespective of any changes in speed. Since the period ($T$) is the inverse of the frequency ($T = \frac{1}{f}$), the period remains the same.

**Step 4: Discuss how the wavelength changes**
The frequency remains unchanged. According to the wave equation:

$$v = f \cdot \lambda$$

If $f$ remains the same and $v$ (speed) increases, then $\lambda$ (the wavelength) must also increase.

# Chapter 9 | Summary: Longitudinal Waves

## Key Concepts and Definitions

*   **Longitudinal Wave:** A wave where the particles in the medium move parallel to the direction in which the wave is travelling.
*   **Compressions:** Areas of higher pressure where the particles in the medium are closest together.
*   **Rarefactions:** Areas of lower pressure where the particles in the medium are furthest apart.
*   **Wavelength ($\lambda$):** The distance between two consecutive compressions or two consecutive rarefactions.

## Mathematical Relationships

### Period and Frequency
The relationship between the period ($T$) and frequency ($f$) is defined as:
$$T = \frac{1}{f} \quad \text{or} \quad f = \frac{1}{T}$$

### Wave Speed
The relationship between wave speed ($v$), frequency ($f$), and wavelength ($\lambda$) is:
$$v = f\lambda$$

### Question 3
A longitudinal wave has a compression to compression distance of $10\text{ m}$. It takes the wave $5\text{ s}$ to pass a point.

**a. What is the wavelength of the longitudinal wave?**
*   **Concept:** The distance between two consecutive compressions is defined as the wavelength ($\lambda$).
*   **Answer:** $\lambda = 10\text{ m}$

**b. What is the speed of the wave?**
*   **Formula:** $v = \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t}$
*   **Calculation:** 
    $$v = \frac{10\text{ m}}{5\text{ s}}$$
    $$v = 2\text{ m}\cdot\text{s}^{-1}$$

### Question 4
A flute produces a musical sound travelling at a speed of $320\text{ m}\cdot\text{s}^{-1}$. The frequency of the note is $256\text{ Hz}$. Calculate:

**a. The period of the note**
*   **Formula:** $T = \frac{1}{f}$
*   **Calculation:**
    $$T = \frac{1}{256\text{ Hz}}$$
    $$T \approx 0,0039\text{ s}$$

**b. The wavelength of the note**
*   **Formula:** $v = f \cdot \lambda$ or $\lambda = \frac{v}{f}$
*   **Calculation:**
    $$\lambda = \frac{320\text{ m}\cdot\text{s}^{-1}}{256\text{ Hz}}$$
    $$\lambda = 1,25\text{ m}$$

### Worked Example: Wave Calculations

This example demonstrates how to calculate the period ($T$) and wavelength ($\lambda$) of a wave given the frequency ($f$) and wave speed ($v$).

**Given values:**
*   Frequency ($f$) = $256\text{ Hz}$
*   Wave speed ($v$) = $320\text{ m/s}$

---

#### a) Calculating the Period ($T$)
The period is the time taken for one complete wave cycle, calculated using the formula:
$$T = \frac{1}{f}$$

**Calculation:**
$$T = \frac{1}{256} \approx 0,0039\text{ s}$$

---

#### b) Calculating the Wavelength ($\lambda$)
The wavelength is the distance between two consecutive points in phase, calculated using the wave equation:
$$\lambda = \frac{v}{f}$$

**Calculation:**
$$\lambda = \frac{320}{256} = 1,25\text{ m}$$
