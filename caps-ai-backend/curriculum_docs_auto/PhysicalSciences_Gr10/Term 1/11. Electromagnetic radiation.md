# Chapter 11: Electromagnetic Radiation

## What is electromagnetic radiation?

Electromagnetic (EM) radiation is a form of energy that consists of oscillating electric and magnetic fields. The most common example of EM radiation is visible light, which allows us to see objects when light bounces off them and enters our eyes.

### Key Properties of EM Radiation

In everyday experience, EM radiation exhibits several unique and important properties:

*   **A huge spectrum:** The light visible to the human eye is only a small fraction of the entire electromagnetic spectrum.
*   **Nature's speed limit:** Nothing in the universe travels faster than the speed of light.
*   **Wave nature:** EM radiation exhibits wave-like behaviour.
*   **Particle nature:** EM radiation exhibits particle-like behaviour.
*   **No medium required:** Unlike mechanical waves (such as sound), EM radiation can propagate through a vacuum; it does not require a physical medium to travel.

### Summary of Fundamental Characteristics

Two critical concepts to remember regarding EM radiation are:

1.  It can travel through a vacuum (it does not require a medium).
2.  It exhibits a dual nature, behaving as both a particle and a wave.

## Wave-like Nature of EM Radiation

While electromagnetic (EM) radiation appears to be a continuous wave, experiments demonstrate that it possesses both wave-like and particle-like properties. This phenomenon is known as **wave-particle duality**.

*   **Wave-type experiments:** Reveal the wave nature of light.
*   **Particle-type experiments:** Reveal the particle nature of light.
*   **Constraint:** One cannot observe both the wave and particle nature simultaneously.

### Definition: Photon
A **photon** is a quantum (energy packet) of light.

---

## Fields

Electromagnetic waves are generated by accelerating charges. Unlike sound waves, EM waves do not require a medium to travel through.

### The Principle of Propagation
The propagation of EM waves is based on the principle of **mutual induction**:
1. A changing electric field generates a magnetic field.
2. A changing magnetic field generates an electric field.
3. This cycle continues, allowing the wave to propagate through space.

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

*   **Mechanism:** An electric field oscillating in one plane produces a magnetic field oscillating in a plane at right angles to it. This, in turn, produces an oscillating electric field, and the process repeats.

## 1. Key Concepts
*   **Electric Fields ($E$):** Represented by the symbol $E$.
*   **Magnetic Fields ($B$):** Represented by the symbol $B$.
*   **Electromagnetic (EM) Waves:** These consist of mutually regenerating electric and magnetic fields that are self-propagating.

## 2. Properties of Electromagnetic Waves
*   **Propagation:** EM waves can travel through empty space (a vacuum) and through various media (such as water or air).
*   **Speed in a Vacuum:** In empty space, EM waves travel at a constant speed of approximately $3 \times 10^{8} \text{ m} \cdot \text{s}^{-1}$. This constant is denoted by the symbol $c$.
*   **Speed in a Medium:** When an EM wave travels through a medium, it travels slower than it does in a vacuum.

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->
*Figure 11.2: A diagram showing the mutually regenerating electric field (red solid line) and magnetic field (blue dashed line).*

## 3. The Wave Equation for EM Radiation
Since electromagnetic radiation behaves as a wave, the standard wave equation applies:

$$v = f \cdot \lambda$$

Where:
*   $v$ is the velocity of the wave.
*   $f$ is the frequency.
*   $\lambda$ is the wavelength.

When dealing specifically with electromagnetic radiation in a vacuum, we replace the velocity $v$ with the constant $c$:

$$\boxed{c = f \cdot \lambda}$$

## Example 1: EM Radiation I

### Question
Calculate the frequency of an electromagnetic wave with a wavelength of $4,2 \times 10^{-7}\text{ m}$.

### Solution

**Step 1: Wave equation**
We use the formula: $c = f\lambda$ to calculate frequency. The speed of light is a constant $c = 3 \times 10^8\text{ m}\cdot\text{s}^{-1}$.

**Step 2: Calculate**
$$c = f\lambda$$
$$3 \times 10^8\text{ m}\cdot\text{s}^{-1} = f \times 4,2 \times 10^{-7}\text{ m}$$
$$f = 7,14 \times 10^{14}\text{ Hz}$$

**Step 3: Quote the final answer**
The frequency is $7,14 \times 10^{14}\text{ Hz}$.

## Example 2: EM Radiation II

### Question
An electromagnetic wave has a wavelength of $200\text{ nm}$. What is the frequency of the radiation?

### Solution

**Step 1: What do we know?**
Recall that all radiation travels at the speed of light ($c$) in a vacuum. Since the question does not specify through what type of material the wave is traveling, we assume it is in a vacuum.

---

### Worked Example: Calculating Frequency

When electromagnetic radiation is travelling through a vacuum, we can identify its properties using the wave equation.

**Given:**
*   Wavelength ($\lambda$) = $200\text{ nm} = 200 \times 10^{-9}\text{ m}$
*   Speed of light ($c$) = $3 \times 10^{8}\text{ m}\cdot\text{s}^{-1}$

**Step 1: Apply the wave equation**
$$c = f\lambda$$

**Step 2: Substitute and solve for frequency ($f$)**
$$3 \times 10^{8}\text{ m}\cdot\text{s}^{-1} = f \times 200 \times 10^{-9}\text{ m}$$
$$f = 1.5 \times 10^{15}\text{ Hz}$$

**Step 3: Final Answer**
The frequency is $1.5 \times 10^{15}\text{ Hz}$.

## Penetrating Ability of EM Radiation

Different frequencies of electromagnetic (EM) radiation possess varying degrees of penetration. 

*   **Visible light:** Reflected off the surface of the human body.
*   **X-rays:** Able to penetrate skin and bone, allowing for internal imaging of the human body.

### Relationship Between Frequency, Energy, and Penetration
There is a direct correlation between the frequency of EM radiation and its penetrating power:
*   Higher frequency radiation carries higher energy.
*   Higher energy radiation generally has a higher degree of penetration compared to low-frequency radiation.

### Ionising Radiation
Certain types of EM radiation, such as ultra-violet (UV) radiation, X-rays, and gamma rays, are classified as **ionising radiation**.

*   **Definition:** Ionising radiation is radiation that transfers enough energy to matter to break molecular bonds and create ions.
*   **Biological Impact:** Excessive exposure to this radiation can cause the destruction of biological tissue.
*   **Protection:** The Earth's atmosphere acts as a shield, protecting living beings from most harmful EM radiation.

---

## Ultraviolet (UV) Radiation and the Skin

UV light is categorised into different frequency ranges, primarily UVA and UVB, both of which can damage collagen fibres and accelerate skin ageing.

### UVA Radiation
*   **Characteristics:** Generally considered the least harmful of the UV types.
*   **Effects:** Penetrates deeply into the skin, contributes to skin ageing, causes DNA damage, and may lead to skin cancer. It does not typically cause sunburn.

### UVB Radiation
*   **Characteristics:** Higher energy than UVA.
*   **Effects:** Can cause sunburn and skin cancer.
*   **Mechanism:** The radiation excites DNA molecules in skin cells, which can result in cancerous mutations.
*   **Environmental Protection:** The ozone layer in the atmosphere is critical as it absorbs and blocks most UVB radiation. The depletion of the ozone layer is a significant concern regarding increased risks of skin cancer.

### Biological Defences and Protection
The human body and external products have developed methods to mitigate UV damage:

1.  **Melanin:** When exposed to moderate levels of UV radiation, the body produces a brown pigment called melanin. This pigment acts as a natural shield to block UV penetration and protect deeper, vulnerable skin tissues.
2.  **Sunscreen/Sunblock:** These products are designed to block UV radiation.
3.  **Sun Protection Factor (SPF):** Products are rated with an SPF, which indicates the level of protection the product provides specifically against UVB radiation.

## Gamma-rays
Gamma-rays possess very high energy levels. When absorbed by living cells, they can cause significant biological damage.

*   **Mechanism of Damage:** Gamma-rays are highly penetrating and are not stopped by human skin.
*   **Biological Impact:** They can induce DNA alteration by interfering with the genetic material of cells.
*   **Health Consequences:** DNA double-strand breaks caused by this ionising radiation are linked to the development of cancer and hereditary diseases.
*   **Evidence:** Studies of nuclear workers exposed to high doses of external whole-body gamma radiation have shown a statistical link to increased mortality from leukaemia, as well as lung, liver, and other solid cancers.

## Cellphones and Microwave Radiation

Cellphones operate using electromagnetic waves within the microwave range. Due to the widespread use of these devices, there has been extensive research into potential health risks.

### Particle-like nature of EM radiation

When we talk of electromagnetic radiation as a particle, we refer to **photons**, which are packets of energy. The energy of the photon is related to the wavelength of electromagnetic radiation.

#### Definition: Planck's constant
Planck's constant is a physical constant named after Max Planck.
$$h = 6,63 \times 10^{-34} \text{ J} \cdot \text{s}$$

#### Energy of a photon
The energy of a photon can be calculated using the following formulas:

$$E = hf$$

or

$$E = h\frac{c}{\lambda}$$

**Where:**
*   $E$ = Energy of the photon
*   $h$ = Planck's constant
*   $f$ = Frequency of the radiation
*   $c$ = Speed of light
*   $\lambda$ = Wavelength of the radiation

## Example 3: Calculating the energy of a photon I

### Question
Calculate the energy of a photon with a frequency of $3 \times 10^{18} \text{ Hz}$.

### Solution

**Step 1: Analyse the question**
We are asked to determine the energy of a photon given the frequency. The frequency is provided in standard SI units.

**Step 2: Apply the equation for the energy of a photon**
The relationship between energy and frequency is given by:

$$E = hf$$

Substituting the known values (where $h \approx 6,63 \times 10^{-34} \text{ J}\cdot\text{s}$):

$$E = 6,63 \times 10^{-34} \text{ J}\cdot\text{s} \times 3 \times 10^{18} \text{ Hz}$$
$$E = 2 \times 10^{-15} \text{ J}$$

**Step 3: Quote the final result**
The energy is $2 \times 10^{-15} \text{ J}$.

### Chapter 11: Electromagnetic Radiation

#### Worked Example: Calculating Photon Energy

**Question:**
What is the energy of an ultraviolet photon with a wavelength of $200\text{ nm}$?

---

#### Solution

**Step 1: Analyse the question**
To determine the energy of a photon when given the wavelength, we use the relationship between energy, frequency, and wavelength. We utilize the constant for the speed of light ($c$) and Planck's constant ($h$).

**Step 2: Apply principles**
1. Determine the frequency ($f$) in terms of wavelength ($\lambda$) using the wave speed equation:
   $$c = f \cdot \lambda$$
   $$f = \frac{c}{\lambda}$$

2. Substitute this into the photon energy equation ($E = hf$):
   $$E = h \frac{c}{\lambda}$$

**Step 3: Do the calculation**
Given:
* $h = 6,63 \times 10^{-34}\text{ J}\cdot\text{s}$
* $c = 3 \times 10^{8}\text{ m}\cdot\text{s}^{-1}$
* $\lambda = 200\text{ nm} = 200 \times 10^{-9}\text{ m}$

Calculation:
$$E = h \frac{c}{\lambda}$$
$$E = (6,63 \times 10^{-34}\text{ J}\cdot\text{s}) \cdot \frac{3 \times 10^{8}\text{ m}\cdot\text{s}^{-1}}{200 \times 10^{-9}\text{ m}}$$
$$E = 9,939 \times 10^{-19}\text{ J}$$

*(Note: The original text contains a typographical error in the exponent of the final result; based on the provided values, the calculation yields $10^{-19}\text{ J}$.)*

**Step 4: Quote the final result**
The energy of the photon is $9,939 \times 10^{-19}\text{ J}$.

#### Animal Behaviour and Natural Disasters

There is a common belief that animals possess a "sixth sense" regarding natural disasters. For example, it is often claimed that elephants will trumpet and flee to higher ground before a tsunami arrives, attributed to their high sensitivity to vibrations on the Earth's surface.

**Scientific Perspective:**
*   **Early Detection:** Researchers suggest that animals may detect natural signals (such as early tremors of an earthquake) before humans do. This allows them to react sooner, though this is likely a survival instinct rather than a "special understanding" of the event.
*   **Critical Analysis:** Much of the evidence for "psychic" animal behaviour is anecdotal. People often recall animal behaviours only *after* a disaster has occurred. If an animal behaves normally (e.g., a dog crossing a road) and a disaster happens shortly after, humans may incorrectly link the two events as a warning sign.

### Question 2: Frequency Calculation
Calculate the frequency of an EM wave with a wavelength of $400\text{ nm}$.

**Formula:**
The relationship between speed ($c$), frequency ($f$), and wavelength ($\lambda$) is:
$$\lambda = \frac{c}{f}$$

Rearranging to solve for frequency ($f$):
$$f = \frac{c}{\lambda}$$

**Calculation:**
Given:
* $c = 3 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}$
* $\lambda = 400\text{ nm} = 400 \times 10^{-9}\text{ m}$

$$f = \frac{3 \times 10^8}{400 \times 10^{-9}}$$
$$f = 7,5 \times 10^{14}\text{ Hz}$$

### Exercise 11-3: Energy of a Photon

**1. How is the energy of a photon related to its frequency and wavelength?**

*   **Solution:** The energy of a photon is directly proportional to its frequency and indirectly proportional to its wavelength. This relationship is expressed by the following formula:

$$E = hf = \frac{hc}{\lambda}$$

Where:
*   $E$ = Energy of the photon
*   $h$ = Planck's constant
*   $f$ = Frequency
*   $c$ = Speed of light
*   $\lambda$ = Wavelength

#### Question 4: Wavelength Calculation
**Problem:** What is the wavelength of a photon of light with a frequency of $101,3\text{ kHz}$?

**Solution:**
Using the wave equation $c = f\lambda$, we rearrange to solve for $\lambda$:
$$\lambda = \frac{c}{f}$$
$$\lambda = \frac{3 \times 10^8}{101,3 \times 10^3}$$
$$\lambda = 3,0 \times 10^3\text{ m}$$

#### Question 5: Comparing Photon Energy and Wavelength
**Problem:** What is the energy of a photon of light with a wavelength of $532\text{ nm}$ and one with a frequency of $13\text{ GHz}$? Which has the longer wavelength?

**Solution:**

**Part A: Photon with $\lambda = 532\text{ nm}$**
Using $E = \frac{hc}{\lambda}$:
$$E = \frac{(6,6 \times 10^{-34})(3 \times 10^8)}{532 \times 10^{-9}}$$
$$E = 3,7 \times 10^{-19}\text{ J}$$

**Part B: Photon with $f = 13\text{ GHz}$**
Energy calculation:
$$E = hf = (6,6 \times 10^{-34}) \times (13 \times 10^9)$$
$$E = 8,6 \times 10^{-24}\text{ J}$$

Wavelength calculation:
$$\lambda = \frac{c}{f} = \frac{3 \times 10^8}{13 \times 10^9}$$
$$\lambda = 2,3 \times 10^{-2}\text{ m}$$

**Conclusion:**
Comparing the wavelengths, $13\text{ GHz}$ light has a longer wavelength.

### Electromagnetic Radiation: Applications and Safety

#### 7. Uses of Electromagnetic Waves
The electromagnetic spectrum has various applications based on the properties of different wave types.

| Wave Type | Main Uses |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Radio waves** | Radio and television broadcasts |
| **Infrared** | Night vision, heat sensors, laser metal cutting |

#### 8. Protection from Ultraviolet (UV) Radiation
It is essential to protect the skin from the Sun's UV radiation due to the following biological effects:
*   **UVA Radiation:** Penetrates deeply into the skin and damages collagen fibres, which accelerates skin ageing.
*   **UVB Radiation:** Can cause direct damage to skin cells, which may lead to skin cancer.

---

#### 9. Advantages and Disadvantages of X-rays
X-rays are a powerful diagnostic tool, but they carry risks associated with high-energy radiation.

*   **Advantages:**
    *   Non-invasive method to view internal human anatomy.
    *   Essential for identifying broken bones.
    *   Used to detect tumours and other internal abnormalities.
*   **Disadvantages:**
    *   Prolonged or excessive exposure can cause cellular damage.
    *   Increased risk of developing cancer with high levels of exposure.

---

#### 10. Safety Precautions for Cell Phone Use
To minimise exposure to electromagnetic radiation emitted by mobile devices, the following precautions are recommended:
1.  **Use hands-free kits:** This increases the distance between the phone and the head, reducing radiation absorption.
2.  **Keep the phone away from the body:** Avoid carrying the phone directly against the skin for extended periods.
3.  **Use external antennas:** When using a phone in a car, an external antenna helps maintain a better signal with less power, reducing the radiation emitted by the handset itself.

### 11. Electromagnetic Waves: Infrared Radiation

**Question:** Write a short essay on a type of electromagnetic waves. You should look at uses, advantages, and disadvantages of your chosen radiation.

**Solution:**
Infrared radiation is defined as radiation with a wavelength of approximately $700\text{ nm}$ to $3000\text{ nm}$. 

*   **Nature:** Thermal radiation falls within this range and is emitted by most objects at room temperature.
*   **Uses:** Infrared radiation is used to study stars that are cooler than our sun, known as red dwarfs. These stars have temperatures of approximately $2800\text{ K}$ and emit most of their light in the infrared region of the electromagnetic (EM) spectrum.
*   **Advantages:** The primary advantage of using infrared radiation for studying space is its ability to penetrate interstellar dust. This allows astronomers to observe objects that would be invisible if studied using only visible light.
*   **Disadvantages:** A significant disadvantage is that water vapour in the atmosphere strongly absorbs infrared radiation. This makes the sky practically opaque in certain wavelength bands within the infrared region, hindering ground-based observations.

### 12. Penetrating Ability of Electromagnetic Radiation

**Question:** Explain why some types of electromagnetic radiation are more penetrating than others.

**Solution:**
The penetrating ability of electromagnetic radiation is directly related to its energy level.

*   **Relationship:** Radiation with higher energy levels (which corresponds to higher frequency and shorter wavelengths) possesses a greater penetrating ability than radiation with lower energy.
*   **Comparison:** 
    *   **High-penetration radiation:** Ultraviolet, X-rays, and gamma rays have high frequencies and high energy, allowing them to penetrate matter more effectively.
    *   **Low-penetration radiation:** Microwaves, radio waves, and infrared radiation have lower frequencies and lower energy, resulting in less penetrating power.
