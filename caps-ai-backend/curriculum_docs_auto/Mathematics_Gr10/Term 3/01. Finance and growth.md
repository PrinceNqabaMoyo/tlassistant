## Key Concepts

### 1. Simple Interest
Simple interest is calculated only on the original principal amount. The formula is generally expressed as:
$$A = P(1 + i \times n)$$
Where:
*   $A$ = Accumulated amount
*   $P$ = Principal amount
*   $i$ = Interest rate per period
*   $n$ = Number of periods

### 2. Compound Interest
Compound interest is calculated on the principal amount plus any interest accumulated from previous periods. The formula is:
$$A = P(1 + i)^n$$

### 3. Financial Calculations
This section involves applying the above formulas to solve real-world problems, including determining time periods, interest rates, or principal amounts required to reach a specific financial goal.

### 4. Foreign Exchange Rates
This section covers the conversion of one currency to another based on current market exchange rates, expressed as:
$$\text{Amount in Currency B} = \text{Amount in Currency A} \times \text{Exchange Rate}$$

## 9.2 Simple Interest

> **DEFINITION: Simple interest**
> Simple interest is interest calculated only on the initial amount (the principal) that you invested or borrowed.

### Worked Example: Calculating Simple Interest

**Problem:** Calculate the interest earned by investing R 1000 for 1 year at a rate of $5\%$ p.a. (per annum) simple interest.

**Solution:**

To find the interest, multiply the initial amount by the percentage rate:

$$\text{Interest} = R\,1000 \times 5\%$$

Convert the percentage to a decimal or fraction:

$$\text{Interest} = R\,1000 \times \frac{5}{100}$$
$$\text{Interest} = R\,1000 \times 0,05$$

**Result:**

$$\text{Interest} = R\,50$$

At the end of the year, you would have earned R 50 in interest.

---

### Simple Interest Calculations

#### Basic Concepts
When an investment or loan is made, the initial amount is known as the **principal ($P$)**. The cost of borrowing or the reward for investing is the **interest ($I$)**. The interest rate is denoted by $i$ (expressed as a decimal), and the time period in years is denoted by $n$.

For a one-year period:
*   **Interest:** $I = P \times i$
*   **Closing Balance:** $\text{Closing balance} = P + I = P(1 + i)$

#### The Simple Interest Formula
For investments or loans spanning a period longer than one year, we use the general simple interest formula to calculate the accumulated amount ($A$):

$$A = P(1 + in)$$

**Where:**
*   $A$ = accumulated amount (final balance)
*   $P$ = principal amount (initial investment/loan)
*   $i$ = interest rate (written as a decimal)
*   $n$ = number of years

### Worked Example 3: Determining the investment period

**Question:**
Prashant deposits R 30 000 into a bank account that pays a simple interest rate of 7,5% p.a. How many years must he invest for to generate R 45 000?

**Solution:**

**Step 1: Write down the known variables**
*   Accumulated amount ($A$) = $45\,000$
*   Principal amount ($P$) = $30\,000$
*   Interest rate ($i$) = $0,075$

**Step 2: Write down the formula**
$$A = P(1 + in)$$

**Step 3: Substitute the values and solve for $n$**
$$45\,000 = 30\,000(1 + 0,075 \times n)$$
$$\frac{45\,000}{30\,000} = 1 + 0,075 \times n$$
$$\frac{45\,000}{30\,000} - 1 = 0,075 \times n$$
$$\frac{(\frac{45\,000}{30\,000}) - 1}{0,075} = n$$
$$n = 6\frac{2}{3}$$

**Step 4: Write the final answer**
It will take 6 years and 8 months to make R 45 000 from R 30 000 at a simple interest rate of 7,5% p.a.

## 9.3 Compound Interest

**Definition:** Compound interest is the interest earned on the principal amount and on its accumulated interest.

Unlike simple interest, where only the original principal earns interest, compound interest allows the interest earned in previous periods to also earn interest. This is advantageous for investments but disadvantageous for loans.

### Worked Example: Compound Interest Calculation
Consider R 1 000 invested for 3 years at $5\%$ p.a. compound interest.

At the end of the first year, the accumulated amount ($A_1$) is:

$$A_1 = P(1 + i)$$
$$A_1 = 1000(1 + 0,05)$$
$$A_1 = 1050$$

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

---

### Deriving the Compound Interest Formula

Compound interest is calculated by using the accumulated amount from the previous period as the new principal for the next period.

#### Step-by-Step Progression

**1. Second Year Calculation**
The amount $A_1$ becomes the new principal for the second year:
$$A_2 = P(1 + i)$$
$$A_2 = 1050(1 + 0,05)$$
$$A_2 = 1000(1 + 0,05)(1 + 0,05)$$
$$A_2 = 1000(1 + 0,05)^2$$

**2. Third Year Calculation**
Similarly, $A_2$ is used as the new principal for the third year:
$$A_3 = P(1 + i)$$
$$A_3 = 1000(1 + 0,05)^2(1 + 0,05)$$
$$A_3 = 1000(1 + 0,05)^3$$

---

### Generalizing the Formula

By observing the pattern above, we can derive a general formula for compound interest. If $P$ is the opening balance and $i$ is the interest rate:

*   **After 1 year:** $A = P(1 + i)$
*   **After 2 years:** $A = [P(1 + i)] \times (1 + i) = P(1 + i)^2$
*   **After 3 years:** $A = [P(1 + i)^2] \times (1 + i) = P(1 + i)^3$

The power of the term $(1 + i)$ corresponds exactly to the number of years ($n$).

#### The Compound Interest Formula

The general formula for calculating the accumulated amount is:

$$A = P(1 + i)^n$$

**Where:**
*   $A =$ accumulated amount
*   $P =$ principal amount
*   $i =$ interest rate (written as a decimal)
*   $n =$ number of years

---

### The Power of Compound Interest

"Interest on interest" creates a significant difference in growth compared to simple interest. 

**Example:** Invest $R\,10\,000$ for 10 years at $9\%$ p.a.

#### 1. Simple Interest Calculation

$$A = P(1 + in)$$
$$A = 10\,000(1 + 0,09 \times 10)$$
$$A = R\,19\,000$$

#### 2. Compound Interest Calculation
The formula for compound interest is:
$$A = P(1 + i)^n$$
$$A = 10\,000(1 + 0,09)^{10}$$
$$A = R\,23\,673,64$$

The provided graphs illustrate the difference in growth between simple and compound interest over time.

### 9.4 Calculations using simple and compound interest

#### Hire Purchase
Hire purchase is a financial agreement where a customer pays for a product over time. Because items like appliances are expensive, many people use this method to pay in installments rather than upfront.

**Key Concepts:**
*   **Interest Type:** Hire purchase agreements always use **simple interest**.
*   **Principal Amount:** The amount borrowed is the *Cash Price* minus the *Deposit*.
*   **Calculation Process:**
    1. Calculate the principal amount: $P = \text{Cash Price} - \text{Deposit}$
    2. Calculate the total amount to be repaid using the simple interest formula: $A = P(1 + i \times n)$
    3. Divide the total amount ($A$) by the number of months to determine the monthly installment.

**IMPORTANT:** Always use the simple interest formula for hire purchase problems.

#### Worked Example: Hire Purchase
**Question:** Troy wants to buy a computer screen for R 2500. He pays a 10% deposit and settles the balance over 24 months using a hire purchase agreement at 7,5% p.a. simple interest. Calculate the monthly payments.

**Solution:**

**Step 1: Identify the known variables**
First, calculate the deposit and the remaining balance ($P$):
*   Deposit: $10\% \text{ of } 2500 = 250$
*   Principal amount ($P$): $2500 - 250 = 2250$
*   Interest rate ($i$): $7,5\% = 0,075$
*   Time period in years ($n$): $n = \frac{24}{12} = 2 \text{ years}$

**Step 2: State the simple interest formula**
$$A = P(1 + in)$$

**Step 3: Substitute the values**
$$A = 2250(1 + 0,075 \times 2)$$
$$A = 2587,50$$

**Step 4: Calculate the monthly repayments**
Divide the total amount ($A$) by the number of months:
$$\text{Monthly payment} = \frac{2587,50}{24}$$
$$\text{Monthly payment} = 107,81$$

**Step 5: Final Answer**
Troy's monthly payment is **R 107,81**.

### Inflation

Inflation represents the average increase in the price of goods over time. Because inflation compounds year-on-year, it is calculated using the compound interest formula:

$$A = P(1 + i)^n$$

Where:
*   $A$ = Future cost
*   $P$ = Present cost
*   $i$ = Inflation rate (as a decimal)
*   $n$ = Number of years

**Step 1: Write down the known variables**
*   $P = 14$
*   $i = 0,09$
*   $n = 4$

**Step 2: Write down the formula**
$$A = P(1 + i)^n$$

**Step 3: Substitute the values**
$$A = 14(1 + 0,09)^4$$
$$A = 19,76$$

**Step 4: Write the final answer**
In four years time, two litres of milk will cost $R\,19,76$.

### Population Growth

Population growth is calculated using the compound interest formula because populations increase exponentially as each new generation has the potential to start a new family.

#### The Formula
The formula used for population growth is:
$$A = P(1 + i)^n$$

Where:
*   $A$ = The future population size.
*   $P$ = The current population size.
*   $i$ = The average growth rate (expressed as a decimal).
*   $n$ = The number of time periods (e.g., years).

City planners can expect Johannesburg's population to be $4\,786\,343$ in ten years' time.

### Simple Interest: Calculating the Interest Rate

When dealing with simple interest problems where the final amount ($A$), the principal amount ($P$), and the time period ($n$) are known, you can calculate the interest rate ($i$) by rearranging the simple interest formula.

#### The Formula
The standard simple interest formula is:
$$A = P(1 + in)$$

To solve for the interest rate ($i$), rearrange the formula as follows:
1. Divide by $P$: $\frac{A}{P} = 1 + in$
2. Subtract 1: $\frac{A}{P} - 1 = in$
3. Divide by $n$: $i = \frac{\frac{A}{P} - 1}{n}$

### Calculating the Interest Rate ($i$) in Compound Interest

To determine the interest rate when the principal ($P$), accumulated amount ($A$), and time period ($n$) are known, we rearrange the compound interest formula $A = P(1 + i)^n$ to make $i$ the subject:
