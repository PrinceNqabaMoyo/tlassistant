# 7 Euclidean Geometry

## Overview
Geometry originates from the Greek words "geo" (earth) and "metria" (measure). It is the field of knowledge dealing with spatial relationships.

*   **Analytical Geometry:** Deals with space and shape using algebra and a coordinate system.
*   **Euclidean Geometry:** Deals with space and shape using a system of logical deductions.

### Applications
Euclidean geometry is used in:
*   **Surveying:** Measuring land and distances.
*   **Architecture:** Designing buildings.
*   **Art and Packing:** Determining the most efficient arrangement for objects.

## 7.1 Introduction

### Fundamental Measurements
In Euclidean geometry, we rely on two fundamental types of measurement:
1.  **Angles**
2.  **Distances**

### Angles
An angle is formed when two straight lines meet at a point, known as a **vertex**.

#### Notation and Labelling
*   **Caret notation:** Angles can be labelled with a caret on a letter, for example, $\hat{B}$.
*   **Line segment notation:** Angles can be labelled according to the line segments that form them, for example, $\angle C\hat{B}A$ or $\angle A\hat{B}C$.
*   **Symbols:** The $\angle$ symbol is a shorthand for "angle". For example, "sum of $\angle s$ in $\Delta$".
*   **Units:** Angles are measured in degrees, denoted by the symbol $^\circ$ (a small circle raised above the text).

# Properties and Notation

When two straight lines intersect at a point, they form four angles: $\hat{a}, \hat{b}, \hat{c},$ and $\hat{d}$.

## Types of Angles

| Term | Property | Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Acute angle | $0^\circ < \text{angle} < 90^\circ$ | $\hat{a}; \hat{c}$ |
| Right angle | $\text{Angle} = 90^\circ$ | |
| Obtuse angle | $90^\circ < \text{angle} < 180^\circ$ | $\hat{b}; \hat{d}$ |
| Straight angle | $\text{Angle} = 180^\circ$ | $\hat{a} + \hat{b}; \hat{b} + \hat{c}$ |
| Reflex angle | $180^\circ < \text{angle} < 360^\circ$ | $\hat{a} + \hat{b} + \hat{c}$ |
| Adjacent angles | Angles that share a vertex and a common side. | $\hat{a} \text{ and } \hat{d}; \hat{c} \text{ and } \hat{d}$ |
| Vertically opposite angles | Angles opposite each other when two lines intersect. They share a vertex and are equal. | $\hat{a} = \hat{c}; \hat{b} = \hat{d}$ |
| Supplementary angles | Two angles that add up to $180^\circ$ | $\hat{a} + \hat{b} = 180^\circ; \hat{b} + \hat{c} = 180^\circ$ |
| Complementary angles | Two angles that add up to $90^\circ$ | |
| Revolution | The sum of all angles around a point. | $\hat{a} + \hat{b} + \hat{c} + \hat{d} = 360^\circ$ |

**Note:** Adjacent angles on a straight line are supplementary.

#### Properties of Angles
The following table summarizes the properties of the angles formed by these intersecting lines:

| Name of angle | Definition | Examples | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Interior angles | Angles that lie in between the parallel lines. | $\hat{a}, \hat{b}, \hat{c}$ and $\hat{d}$ are interior angles. | Interior means inside. |
| Exterior angles | Angles that lie outside the parallel lines. | $\hat{e}, \hat{f}, \hat{g}$ and $\hat{h}$ are exterior angles. | Exterior means outside. |
| Corresponding angles | Angles on the same side of the lines and the same side of the transversal. If the lines are parallel, the corresponding angles will be equal. | $\hat{a}$ and $\hat{e}$, $\hat{b}$ and $\hat{f}$, $\hat{c}$ and $\hat{g}$, $\hat{d}$ and $\hat{h}$ are pairs of corresponding angles. $\hat{a} = \hat{e}, \hat{b} = \hat{f}, \hat{c} = \hat{g}$ and $\hat{d} = \hat{h}$. | F shape |
| Co-interior angles | Angles that lie in between the lines and on the same side of the transversal. If the lines are parallel, the angles are supplementary. | $\hat{a}$ and $\hat{d}$, $\hat{b}$ and $\hat{c}$ are pairs of co-interior angles. $\hat{a} + \hat{d} = 180^\circ, \hat{b} + \hat{c} = 180^\circ$. | C shape |
| Alternate interior angles | Equal interior angles that lie inside the lines and on opposite sides of the transversal. If the lines are parallel, the interior angles will be equal. | $\hat{a}$ and $\hat{c}$, $\hat{b}$ and $\hat{d}$ are pairs of alternate interior angles. $\hat{a} = \hat{c}, \hat{b} = \hat{d}$ | Z shape |

---

#### Proving Parallel Lines
If two lines are intersected by a transversal such that:
*   Corresponding angles are equal; or
*   Alternate interior angles are equal; or
*   Co-interior angles are supplementary ($180^\circ$);

Then the two lines are parallel.

#### Notation Note
*   $EF$ refers to the line passing through points $E$ and $F$.
*   $\overline{EF}$ refers to the line segment from point $E$ to point $F$.

#### 2. Triangles (Section 7.2)

**Definition:**
A triangle is a three-sided polygon. We use the notation $\triangle ABC$ to refer to a triangle with vertices labelled $A$, $B$, and $C$.

**Classification of Triangles:**
Triangles are classified based on two criteria:

**A. Classification by Sides:**
*   **Equilateral:** All three sides are equal in length.
*   **Isosceles:** At least two sides are equal in length.
*   **Scalene:** All three sides have different lengths.

**B. Classification by Angles:**
*   **Acute-angled:** All three interior angles are less than $90^\circ$.
*   **Obtuse-angled:** One interior angle is greater than $90^\circ$.
*   **Right-angled:** One interior angle is exactly $90^\circ$.

### Congruency

Two triangles are congruent if one fits exactly over the other. This means that the triangles have equal corresponding angles and sides. We indicate congruency using the symbol $\equiv$.

#### Requirements for Congruency

| Rule | Description | Diagram |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **RHS** or $90^\circ\text{HS}$ ($90^\circ$, hypotenuse, side) | If the hypotenuse and one side of a right-angled triangle are equal to the hypotenuse and the corresponding side of another right-angled triangle, then the two triangles are congruent. | <!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram --> |
| **SSS** (side, side, side) | If three sides of a triangle are equal in length to the corresponding sides of another triangle, then the two triangles are congruent. | <!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram --> |
| **SAS** or $\text{S}\angle\text{S}$ (side, angle, side) | If two sides and the **included angle** of a triangle are equal to the corresponding two sides and included angle of another triangle, then the two triangles are congruent. | <!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram --> |
| **AAS** or $\angle\angle\text{S}$ (angle, angle, side) | If one side and two angles of a triangle are equal to the corresponding one side and two angles of another triangle, then the two triangles are congruent. | <!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram --> |

#### Labelling Congruent Triangles
The order of letters when labelling congruent triangles is very important. For example:
$$\triangle ABC \equiv \triangle DEF$$

This notation indicates the following properties of the two triangles:
*   $\hat{A} = \hat{D}$
*   $\hat{B} = \hat{E}$
*   $\hat{C} = \hat{F}$
*   $AB = DE$
*   $AC = DF$
*   $BC = EF$

> **NOTE:** You might see $\cong$ used to show that two triangles are congruent. This is the internationally recognised symbol for congruency.

# Similarity

Two triangles are similar if one triangle is a scaled version of the other. This means that their corresponding angles are equal in measure and the ratio of their corresponding sides are in proportion. The two triangles have the same shape, but different scales. 

*   **Note:** Congruent triangles are similar triangles, but not all similar triangles are congruent.
*   **Symbol:** We use $|||$ to indicate that two triangles are similar. You may also see $\sim$ used internationally.

## Requirements for Similarity

| Rule | Description | Diagram |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| AAA (angle, angle, angle) | If all three pairs of corresponding angles of two triangles are equal, then the triangles are similar. | <!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram --> $\hat{A} = \hat{D}, \hat{B} = \hat{E}, \hat{C} = \hat{F}$ <br> $\therefore \triangle ABC \ ||| \ \triangle DEF$ |
| SSS (side, side, side) | If all three pairs of corresponding sides of two triangles are in proportion, then the triangles are similar. | <!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram --> $\frac{MN}{RS} = \frac{ML}{RT} = \frac{NL}{ST}$ <br> $\therefore \triangle MNL \ ||| \ \triangle RST$ |

**Important:** The order of letters for similar triangles is very important. Always label similar triangles in corresponding order.
*   Example: $\triangle MNL \ ||| \ \triangle RST$ is correct; but $\triangle MNL \ ||| \ \triangle RTS$ is incorrect.

# The Theorem of Pythagoras

If $\triangle ABC$ is right-angled with $\hat{B} = 90^\circ$, then:
$$b^2 = a^2 + c^2$$

**Converse of the Theorem of Pythagoras:**
If $b^2 = a^2 + c^2$, then $\triangle ABC$ is right-angled with $\hat{B} = 90^\circ$.

### Worked Example: Congruency and Pythagoras

**Question:** Determine if the two triangles ($\triangle CDE$ and $\triangle CBA$) are congruent. Use the result to find the values of $x$, $\hat{y}$, and $z$.

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

---

#### Solution

**Step 1: Examine the information given**
We are given two triangles with specific side lengths and angles. We need to prove congruency to solve for the unknowns.

**Step 2: Determine whether $\triangle CDE \equiv \triangle CBA$**

First, calculate the missing angle in $\triangle CDE$:
$$\hat{D} + \hat{C} + \hat{E} = 180^\circ \quad (\text{sum of } \angle s \text{ in } \triangle)$$
$$90^\circ + 35^\circ + \hat{E} = 180^\circ$$
$$\therefore \hat{E} = 55^\circ$$

Now, compare $\triangle CDE$ and $\triangle CBA$:
*   $D\hat{E}C = B\hat{A}C = 55^\circ$ (Proved above)
*   $C\hat{D}E = C\hat{B}A = 90^\circ$ (Given)
*   $DE = BA = 3$ (Given)

Therefore, $\triangle CDE \equiv \triangle CBA$ by the **AAS** (Angle-Angle-Side) congruency condition.

**Step 3: Determine the unknown angles and sides**

Using the properties of congruent triangles:

1.  **Finding $x$:**
    Apply the Theorem of Pythagoras in $\triangle CDE$:
    $$CE^2 = DE^2 + CD^2$$
    $$5^2 = 3^2 + x^2$$
    $$25 = 9 + x^2$$
    $$x^2 = 16$$
    $$\therefore x = 4$$

2.  **Finding $\hat{y}$ and $z$:**
    Because $\triangle CDE \equiv \triangle CBA$, corresponding parts are equal:
    *   $\hat{y} = D\hat{C}E = 35^\circ$
    *   $z = AC = CE = 5$

---

### Worked Example: Triangle Calculations

In $\triangle CBA$:

$$\hat{B} + \hat{A} + \hat{y} = 180^\circ \quad (\text{sum of } \angle\text{s in } \triangle)$$
$$90^\circ + 55^\circ + \hat{y} = 180^\circ$$
$$\therefore \hat{y} = 35^\circ$$

$$\triangle CDE \equiv \triangle CBA \quad (\text{proved})$$
$$\therefore CE = CA$$
$$\therefore z = 5$$

A parallelogram is a quadrilateral with both pairs of opposite sides parallel.

### Worked Example 3: Properties of a Parallelogram

**Question:**
$ABCD$ is a parallelogram with $AB \parallel DC$ and $AD \parallel BC$. Show that:
1. $AB = DC$ and $AD = BC$
2. $\hat{A} = \hat{C}$ and $\hat{B} = \hat{D}$

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

**Solution:**

**Step 1: Connect $AC$ to form $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle CDA$**
Redraw the diagram and draw line $AC$.

**Step 2: Use properties of parallel lines to indicate all equal angles on the diagram**
On your diagram, mark all the equal angles.

**Step 3: Prove $\triangle ABC \equiv \triangle CDA$**
In $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle CDA$:

| Statement | Reason |
| :--- | :--- |
| $\hat{A}_2 = \hat{C}_3$ | (alt $\angle s$; $AB \parallel DC$) |
| $\hat{C}_4 = \hat{A}_1$ | (alt $\angle s$; $BC \parallel AD$) |
| $AC = AC$ | (common side) |
| $\therefore \triangle ABC \equiv \triangle CDA$ | (AAS) |
| $\therefore AB = CD$ and $BC = DA$ | |

$\therefore$ Opposite sides of a parallelogram have equal length.

### Properties of a Parallelogram

From the geometric proofs, we establish that:
*   $\hat{A}_2 = \hat{C}_3$ and $\hat{A}_1 = \hat{C}_4$
*   Therefore, $\hat{A} = \hat{A}_1 + \hat{A}_2 = \hat{C}_3 + \hat{C}_4 = \hat{C}$
*   Furthermore, $\hat{B} = \hat{D}$ (since $\triangle ABC \equiv \triangle CDA$)

**Conclusion:** Opposite angles of a parallelogram are equal.

#### Summary of Properties
*   Both pairs of opposite sides are parallel.
*   Both pairs of opposite sides are equal in length.
*   Both pairs of opposite angles are equal.
*   Both diagonals bisect each other.

### Worked Example 4: Proving a Quadrilateral is a Parallelogram

**Question:** Prove that if both pairs of opposite angles in a quadrilateral are equal, the quadrilateral is a parallelogram.

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

**Solution:**

**Step 1: Find the relationship between $\hat{x}$ and $\hat{y}$**
In quadrilateral $WXYZ$:
1. $\hat{W} = \hat{Y} = \hat{y}$ (given)
2. $\hat{Z} = \hat{X} = \hat{x}$ (given)
3. $\hat{W} + \hat{X} + \hat{Y} + \hat{Z} = 360^\circ$ (sum of $\angle s$ in a quad)
4. $\therefore 2\hat{x} + 2\hat{y} = 360^\circ$
5. $\therefore \hat{x} + \hat{y} = 180^\circ$
6. $\hat{W} + \hat{Z} = \hat{x} + \hat{y} = 180^\circ$

Since these are co-interior angles between lines $WX$ and $ZY$, it follows that $WX \parallel ZY$.

**Step 2: Find parallel lines**
Similarly, $\hat{W} + \hat{X} = 180^\circ$. These are co-interior angles between lines $XY$ and $WZ$. Therefore, $XY \parallel WZ$.

**Conclusion:** Both pairs of opposite sides of the quadrilateral are parallel, therefore $WXYZ$ is a parallelogram.

To prove that a quadrilateral is a parallelogram, you must demonstrate that one of the following conditions is met:
1. Both pairs of opposite sides are equal.
2. The diagonals bisect each other.
3. One pair of opposite sides is both equal and parallel.

---

### Summary: Properties of a Parallelogram
A quadrilateral is classified as a parallelogram if any of the following are true:
* Both pairs of opposite sides are parallel.
* Both pairs of opposite sides are equal.
* Both pairs of opposite angles are equal.
* The diagonals bisect each other.
* One pair of opposite sides is both equal and parallel.

# Rectangle

### Definition
A **rectangle** is a parallelogram that has all four interior angles equal to $90^\circ$.

### Properties of a Rectangle
A rectangle inherits all the properties of a parallelogram, with additional specific properties:
*   Both pairs of opposite sides are parallel.
*   Both pairs of opposite sides are equal in length.
*   Both pairs of opposite angles are equal.
*   Both diagonals bisect each other.
*   **Special Property:** The diagonals are equal in length.
*   All interior angles are equal to $90^\circ$.

---

### Worked Example: Proving Diagonals are Equal
**Question:** $PQRS$ is a rectangle. Prove that the diagonals are of equal length.

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

**Solution:**

**Step 1:** Connect $P$ to $R$ and $Q$ to $S$ to form $\triangle PSR$ and $\triangle QRS$.

**Step 2:** Use the definition of a rectangle to identify equal angles and sides.

**Step 3:** Prove $\triangle PSR \equiv \triangle QRS$.

In $\triangle PSR$ and $\triangle QRS$:

| :--- | :--- |

### Proving Properties of a Rhombus

#### Step 2: Proving Diagonals Intersect at Right Angles
To prove that the diagonals of a rhombus intersect at $90^\circ$, we use congruent triangles:

*   $XT = ZT$ (sides of a rhombus)
*   $TO = TO$ (common side)
*   $XO = OZ$ (diagonals of a rhombus)
*   $\therefore \Delta XTO \equiv \Delta ZTO$ (SSS)
*   $\therefore \hat{O}_1 = \hat{O}_4$
*   But $\hat{O}_1 + \hat{O}_4 = 180^\circ$ ($\angle$s on a straight line)
*   $\therefore \hat{O}_1 = \hat{O}_4 = 90^\circ$

We can further conclude that $\hat{O}_1 = \hat{O}_2 = \hat{O}_3 = \hat{O}_4 = 90^\circ$.
**Conclusion:** The diagonals of a rhombus bisect each other perpendicularly.

#### Step 3: Proving Diagonals Bisect Interior Angles
Using the properties of congruent triangles:

*   $\hat{X}_2 = \hat{Z}_1$ ($\Delta XTO \equiv \Delta ZTO$)
*   and $\hat{X}_2 = \hat{Z}_2$ (alt $\angle$s; $XT \parallel YZ$)
*   $\therefore \hat{Z}_1 = \hat{Z}_2$

**Conclusion:** Diagonal $XZ$ bisects $\hat{Z}$. Similarly, $XZ$ bisects $\hat{X}$, and diagonal $TY$ bisects $\hat{T}$ and $\hat{Y}$. Therefore, the diagonals of a rhombus bisect the interior angles.

#### Worked Example: Proving Kite Properties

**Objective:** Prove that the diagonal between the equal sides of a kite bisects the interior angles and acts as an axis of symmetry.

In $\triangle ADC$ and $\triangle ABC$:
* $AD = AB$ (given)
* $CD = CB$ (given)
* $AC = AC$ (common side)

Since $\triangle ADC \equiv \triangle ABC$:
* $\hat{A}_1 = \hat{A}_2$
* $\hat{C}_1 = \hat{C}_2$

Therefore, diagonal $AC$ bisects $\hat{A}$ and $\hat{C}$. We conclude that the diagonal between the equal sides of a kite bisects the two interior angles and is an axis of symmetry.

#### 2. The Mid-point Theorem (EMA66)

**Investigation: Proving the Mid-point Theorem**
To understand the relationship between the mid-points of a triangle's sides, follow these steps:
1.  Draw a large scalene $\triangle ABC$.
2.  Mark mid-points $D$ on $AB$ and $E$ on $AC$. Connect $D$ and $E$.
3.  Cut out $\triangle ABC$ and cut along line $DE$.
4.  Rotate $\triangle ADE$ and place vertex $E$ on vertex $C$.
5.  Observe the alignment of the segments.
6.  **Observation:** You will notice that $DE$ is parallel to $BC$ and that the length of $DE$ is exactly half the length of $BC$.

**Conjecture:**
The line segment joining the mid-points of two sides of a triangle is parallel to the third side and is half the length of the third side.

---

#### 3. Worked Example 8: Mid-point Theorem

**Question:**
Prove that the line joining the mid-points of two sides of a triangle is parallel to the third side and equal to half the length of the third side.

**Formal Statement of the Theorem:**
If $D$ is the mid-point of $AB$ and $E$ is the mid-point of $AC$ in $\triangle ABC$, then:
1.  $DE \parallel BC$
2.  $DE = \frac{1}{2}BC$

| Step | Reasoning |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Given** | $\triangle ABC$ with $AD = DB$ and $AE = EC$. |
| **To Prove** | $DE \parallel BC$ and $DE = \frac{1}{2}BC$. |
| **Construction** | Extend $DE$ to $F$ such that $DE = EF$. Join $FC$. |
| **Proof** | In $\triangle ADE$ and $\triangle CFE$: |
| | $AE = EC$ (Given) |
| | $\hat{E}_1 = \hat{E}_2$ (Vertically opposite angles) |
| | $DE = EF$ (By construction) |
| | Therefore, $\triangle ADE \equiv \triangle CFE$ (SAS) |
| | Thus, $AD = FC$ (Congruent triangles) |
| | Since $AD = DB$, then $DB = FC$. |
| | Also, $\hat{A} = \hat{C}_1$ (Alternate angles), so $AB \parallel FC$. |
| | Since $DB \parallel FC$ and $DB = FC$, $DBCF$ is a parallelogram. |
| | Therefore, $DF \parallel BC$ (Opposite sides of parallelogram). |
| | Since $DF \parallel BC$, then $DE \parallel BC$. |
| | Also, $DF = BC$ (Opposite sides of parallelogram). |
| | Since $DF = DE + EF$ and $DE = EF$, then $2DE = BC$. |
| | Thus, $DE = \frac{1}{2}BC$. |

---

### The Mid-point Theorem

The Mid-point Theorem states that the line segment joining the mid-points of two sides of a triangle is parallel to the third side and is half the length of the third side.

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

#### Proof of the Mid-point Theorem

**Given:** $\triangle ABC$ with $D$ as the mid-point of $AB$ and $E$ as the mid-point of $AC$.
**To prove:** $DE \parallel BC$ and $DE = \frac{1}{2}BC$.

---

**Step 1: Construction**
Extend $DE$ to $F$ such that $DE = EF$ and join $FC$.

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

**Step 2: Prove $BCFD$ is a parallelogram**
Consider $\triangle EAD$ and $\triangle ECF$:

| Statement | Reason |
| :--- | :--- |
| $\hat{E}_1 = \hat{E}_2$ | Vertically opposite angles |
| $AE = CE$ | Given ($E$ is the mid-point) |
| $DE = EF$ | By construction |
| $\therefore \triangle EAD \equiv \triangle ECF$ | SAS (Side-Angle-Side) |
| $\therefore \hat{A} = \hat{C}_2$ | Congruency |

Since $\hat{A} = \hat{C}_2$ are alternate interior angles, it follows that $BD \parallel FC$.

Now, compare sides:
* $BD = DA$ (Given)
* $DA = FC$ ($\triangle EAD \equiv \triangle ECF$)
* $\therefore BD = FC$

Since one pair of opposite sides is both equal and parallel ($BD \parallel FC$ and $BD = FC$), **$BCFD$ is a parallelogram**.

Therefore, $DE \parallel BC$.

**Step 3: Use properties of parallelogram $BCFD$ to prove $DE = \frac{1}{2}BC$**

| Statement | Reason |
| :--- | :--- |
| $DF = BC$ | Opposite sides of a parallelogram |
| $DF = 2(DE)$ | By construction ($DE = EF$) |
| $\therefore 2DE = BC$ | Substitution |
| $\therefore DE = \frac{1}{2}BC$ | Division |

---

#### Conclusion
1. The line joining the two mid-points of two sides of a triangle is parallel to the third side.
2. The line joining the mid-point of two sides of a triangle is equal to half the length of the third side.

---

### The Converse of the Mid-point Theorem

**Definition:**
The converse of the mid-point theorem states: If a line is drawn through the mid-point of one side of a triangle and is parallel to a second side, then it will bisect the third side.

# 7.5 Chapter Summary: Euclidean Geometry

## Definitions of Quadrilaterals

A **quadrilateral** is a closed shape consisting of four straight line segments.

### 1. Parallelogram
A quadrilateral with both pairs of opposite sides parallel.
*   Both pairs of opposite sides are equal in length.
*   Both pairs of opposite angles are equal.
*   Both diagonals bisect each other.

### 2. Rectangle
A parallelogram that has all four angles equal to $90^{\circ}$.
*   Both pairs of opposite sides are parallel.
*   Both pairs of opposite sides are equal in length.
*   The diagonals bisect each other.
*   The diagonals are equal in length.
*   All interior angles are equal to $90^{\circ}$.

### 3. Rhombus
A parallelogram that has all four sides equal in length.
*   Both pairs of opposite sides are parallel.
*   All sides are equal in length.
*   Both pairs of opposite angles are equal.
*   The diagonals bisect each other at $90^{\circ}$.
*   The diagonals of a rhombus bisect both pairs of opposite angles.

### 4. Square
A rhombus that has all four interior angles equal to $90^{\circ}$.
*   Both pairs of opposite sides are parallel.
*   The diagonals bisect each other at $90^{\circ}$.
*   All interior angles are equal to $90^{\circ}$.
*   The diagonals are equal in length.
*   The diagonals bisect both pairs of interior opposite angles (i.e., all are $45^{\circ}$).

### 5. Trapezium
A quadrilateral with one pair of opposite sides parallel.

### 6. Kite
A quadrilateral with two pairs of adjacent sides equal.
*   One pair of opposite angles are equal (the angles between unequal sides).
*   The diagonal between equal sides bisects the other diagonal.
*   The diagonal between equal sides bisects the interior angles.
*   The diagonals intersect at $90^{\circ}$.

The mid-point theorem states that the line joining the mid-points of two sides of a triangle is parallel to the third side and equal to half the length of the third side.

4. We are given that $EC \parallel FG$.
5. Since both pairs of opposite sides are parallel ($FD \parallel GC$ and $EC \parallel FG$), $FECG$ is a parallelogram by definition.

#### b) Prove that $FE = ED$

**Proof:**
1. According to the **Mid-point Theorem**, the segment connecting the mid-points of two sides of a triangle is half the length of the third side. Thus:
   $$ED = \frac{1}{2}BC$$
2. Since $G$ is the mid-point of $BC$, the segment $GC$ is half the length of $BC$:
   $$GC = \frac{1}{2}BC$$
3. By substitution, we find:
   $$ED = GC$$
4. Because $FECG$ is a parallelogram (proven in part a), opposite sides are equal. Therefore:
   $$FE = GC$$
5. Since both $ED$ and $FE$ are equal to $GC$, it follows that:
   $$ED = FE$$
