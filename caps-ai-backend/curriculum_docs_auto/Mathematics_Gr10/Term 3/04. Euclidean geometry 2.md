## Key Concepts

### 1. Proofs
A proof is a logical argument that establishes the truth of a mathematical statement beyond doubt. In Euclidean geometry, this involves using previously established axioms, postulates, and theorems to demonstrate that a specific geometric property must be true.

### 2. Conjectures
A conjecture is a mathematical statement that is proposed to be true based on observation or intuition, but which has not yet been formally proven. The process of geometry involves:
*   **Observing** patterns.
*   **Formulating** a conjecture.
*   **Testing** the conjecture.
*   **Proving** the conjecture to turn it into a theorem.

### Proving Properties of Parallelograms

#### Worked Example: Proving Equality of Angles
To prove that opposite angles of a quadrilateral are equal, we use congruent triangles. In $\triangle XAM$ and $\triangle ZCO$:

1.  $\hat{X} = \hat{Z}$ (Given: $\triangle CDZ \equiv \triangle ABX$)
2.  $\hat{A} = \hat{C}$ (Proved above)
3.  $AX = CZ$ (Proved above)
4.  $\therefore \triangle XAM \equiv \triangle ZCO$ (AAS)
5.  $\hat{M}_1 = \hat{O}_1$
6.  But $\hat{M}_1 = \hat{M}_2$ (vertically opposite angles are equal)
7.  And $\hat{O}_1 = \hat{O}_2$ (vertically opposite angles are equal)
8.  $\therefore \hat{M}_2 = \hat{O}_2$

**Step 3:** Similarly, we can show that $\hat{N}_2 = \hat{P}_2$.
*   First show $\triangle ADW \equiv \triangle CBY$.
*   Then show $\triangle PDW \equiv \triangle NBY$.

**Step 4: Conclusion**
Both pairs of opposite angles of $MNOP$ are equal. Therefore, $MNOP$ is a parallelogram.

# 12.2 Chapter Summary: Euclidean Geometry

## Definitions of Quadrilaterals

*   **Quadrilateral:** A closed shape consisting of four straight line segments.
*   **Parallelogram:** A quadrilateral with both pairs of opposite sides parallel.
    *   Both pairs of opposite sides are equal in length.
    *   Both pairs of opposite angles are equal.
    *   Both diagonals bisect each other.
*   **Rectangle:** A parallelogram that has all four angles equal to $90^{\circ}$.
    *   Both pairs of opposite sides are parallel.
    *   Both pairs of opposite sides are equal in length.
    *   The diagonals bisect each other.
    *   The diagonals are equal in length.
    *   All interior angles are equal to $90^{\circ}$.
*   **Rhombus:** A parallelogram that has all four sides equal in length.
    *   Both pairs of opposite sides are parallel.
    *   All sides are equal in length.
    *   Both pairs of opposite angles are equal.
    *   The diagonals bisect each other at $90^{\circ}$.
    *   The diagonals of a rhombus bisect both pairs of opposite angles.
*   **Square:** A rhombus that has all four interior angles equal to $90^{\circ}$.
    *   Both pairs of opposite sides are parallel.
    *   The diagonals bisect each other at $90^{\circ}$.
    *   All interior angles are equal to $90^{\circ}$.
    *   The diagonals are equal in length.
    *   The diagonals bisect both pairs of interior opposite angles (i.e., all are $45^{\circ}$).
*   **Trapezium:** A quadrilateral with one pair of opposite sides parallel.
*   **Kite:** A quadrilateral with two pairs of adjacent sides equal.
    *   One pair of opposite angles are equal (the angles between unequal sides).
    *   The diagonal between equal sides bisects the other diagonal.
    *   The diagonal between equal sides bisects the interior angles.
    *   The diagonals intersect at $90^{\circ}$.

#### Problem 5: Mid-points and Parallel Lines
**Given:** In $\triangle ABC$, $D$ is on $BC$. $N$ is the mid-point of $AD$. $O$ is the mid-point of $AB$. $M$ is the mid-point of $BD$. $NR \parallel AC$.

**a) Prove that $OBMN$ is a parallelogram.**
*   $O$ is the mid-point of $AB$ and $M$ is the mid-point of $BD$.
*   In $\triangle ABD$, by the Mid-point Theorem, $OM \parallel AD$ and $OM = \frac{1}{2}AD$.
*   Since $N$ lies on $AD$, $OM \parallel ND$.
*   Because $O$ and $M$ are mid-points, $OB = OA$ and $BM = MD$.
*   By properties of segments, $OBMN$ has two pairs of parallel sides (or opposite sides equal), thus $OBMN$ is a parallelogram.

**b) Prove that $BC = 2MR$.**
*   In $\triangle ADC$, $N$ is the mid-point of $AD$ and $NR \parallel AC$.
*   By the Converse of the Mid-point Theorem, $R$ must be the mid-point of $DC$.
*   Since $M$ is the mid-point of $BD$ and $R$ is the mid-point of $DC$:
    *   $MR = \frac{1}{2}BC$ (Mid-point Theorem in $\triangle BDC$).
    *   Therefore, $BC = 2MR$.

#### Problem 6: Area and Mid-points
**Given:** In $\triangle MNP$, $\hat{M} = 90^\circ$, $S$ is the mid-point of $MN$, $T$ is the mid-point of $NR$.

**a) Prove $U$ is the mid-point of $NP$.**
*   In $\triangle MNR$, $S$ is the mid-point of $MN$ and $T$ is the mid-point of $NR$.
*   By the Mid-point Theorem, $ST \parallel MR$.
*   Extending this logic to the larger triangle $MNP$, the line segment connecting mid-points of two sides is parallel to the third side and bisects the third side. Thus, $U$ is the mid-point of $NP$.

**b) Calculate the area of $\triangle MNR$.**
*   Area of $\triangle SNT = 6 \text{ cm}^2$.
*   Since $S$ and $T$ are mid-points, $\triangle SNT$ is similar to $\triangle MNR$ with a ratio of $1:2$.
*   The ratio of areas is the square of the ratio of sides: $(\frac{1}{2})^2 = \frac{1}{4}$.
*   $\text{Area}(\triangle MNR) = 4 \times \text{Area}(\triangle SNT) = 4 \times 6 = 24 \text{ cm}^2$.

# 12. Euclidean Geometry

## Core Principles
*   **Conjectures vs. Proofs:** A single counter-example is sufficient to disprove a conjecture. Conversely, providing numerous specific examples that support a conjecture does not constitute a general mathematical proof.
*   **Proving Quadrilaterals:** To prove a shape is a specific type of quadrilateral, you must demonstrate that it satisfies the unique defining properties of that shape. For example, to prove a quadrilateral is a parallelogram, it is insufficient to show only that both pairs of sides are parallel; you must also show that either the opposite angles are equal or both pairs of opposite sides are equal in length.

### Geometry Proof: Congruency and Straight Lines

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

#### 1. Preliminary Angle Relationships
We establish the following relationships based on parallel lines and properties of parallelograms:
*   $B\hat{A}D = B\hat{C}D$ (opposite angles of a parallelogram are equal)
*   $C\hat{E}D = B\hat{C}D$ (alternate angles; $AE \parallel BC$)
*   $F\hat{B}C = B\hat{C}D$ (alternate angles; $AF \parallel DC$)
*   Therefore, $C\hat{E}D = F\hat{B}C$

#### 2. Side Length Relationships
Based on the properties of parallelograms (opposite sides are equal):
*   $AD = BC$
*   $AB = DC$

#### 3. Proving Congruency: $\triangle DEC \equiv \triangle BCF$
To prove that $\triangle DEC$ is congruent to $\triangle BCF$, we compare the two triangles:

| Statement | Reason |
| :--- | :--- |
| $C\hat{E}D = F\hat{B}C$ | Proven above |
| $DC = AB = BF$ | Given |
| $DE = AD = BC$ | Given |
| $\therefore \triangle DEC \equiv \triangle BCF$ | (SAS - Side-Angle-Side) |

#### 4. Proving $ECF$ is a Straight Line
Using the congruency proven above, we can show that the angles around point $C$ form a straight line:

1.  From congruency: $B\hat{F}C = D\hat{C}E$
2.  We know that the sum of angles in a triangle is $180^\circ$: $F\hat{B}C + B\hat{F}C + B\hat{C}F = 180^\circ$
3.  Substituting the congruent parts: $D\hat{C}E + B\hat{C}F + B\hat{C}D = 180^\circ$
4.  Since the sum of these adjacent angles is $180^\circ$, $ECF$ is a straight line.
