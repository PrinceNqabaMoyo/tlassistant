## 3.2 Describing Sequences

### Definition
A **sequence** is an ordered list of items, usually numbers. Each individual item within a sequence is called a **term**.

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

*   **Terms:** The numbers in the sequence are referred to as the $1^{st}$ term, $2^{nd}$ term, $3^{rd}$ term, and so on.
*   **Ellipsis (...):** The three dots at the end of a sequence indicate that the sequence continues indefinitely (forever).

Sequences are formed by specific rules or patterns, which we will examine to understand how they are constructed.

---

### Number Patterns: Examples and Analysis

#### Examples of Number Sequences
A sequence is a list of numbers that follow a specific rule.

1.  **$1; 4; 7; 10; 13; 16; 19; 22; 25; \dots$**
    *   There is a constant difference of $3$ between successive terms.
    *   Rule: Add $3$ to the previous term.

2.  **$13; 8; 3; -2; -7; -12; -17; -22; \dots$**
    *   There is a constant difference of $-5$ between successive terms.
    *   Rule: Add $-5$ (or subtract $5$) from the previous term.

3.  **$2; 4; 8; 16; 32; 64; 128; 256; \dots$**
    *   There is a constant factor of $2$ between successive terms.
    *   Rule: Multiply the previous term by $2$.

4.  **$3; -9; 27; -81; 243; -729; 2187; \dots$**
    *   There is a constant factor of $-3$ between successive terms.
    *   Rule: Multiply the previous term by $-3$.

5.  **$9; 3; 1; \frac{1}{3}; \frac{1}{9}; \frac{1}{27}; \dots$**
    *   There is a constant factor of $\frac{1}{3}$ between successive terms.
    *   Rule: Multiply the previous term by $\frac{1}{3}$ (which is equivalent to dividing by $3$).

### Describing Sequences

#### Notation
To describe terms in a number pattern, we use the following notation:
*   The first term is $T_1$.
*   The fourth term is $T_4$.
*   The tenth term is $T_{10}$.
*   The $n^{th}$ term is written as $T_n$.

#### General Formula
A sequence follows a pattern if we can write a general formula to calculate any term. 

**Example:**
Consider the linear sequence: $1; 3; 5; 7; 9; \dots$
The $n^{th}$ term is given by the general formula: $T_n = 2n - 1$

We can verify this by substituting the position ($n$) into the formula:
*   $T_1 = 2(1) - 1 = 1$
*   $T_2 = 2(2) - 1 = 3$
*   $T_3 = 2(3) - 1 = 5$
*   $T_4 = 2(4) - 1 = 7$
*   $T_5 = 2(5) - 1 = 9$

---

### Common Difference

When a sequence changes by a constant amount, this is called a **common difference**.

**Example:**
Consider the sequence: $6; 1; -4; -9; \dots$
Each term decreases by $5$. We can represent this using a table:

| Term number | $T_1$ | $T_2$ | $T_3$ | $T_4$ | $T_n$ |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Term** | $6$ | $1$ | $-4$ | $-9$ | $T_n$ |
| **Formula** | $6 - 0 \times 5$ | $6 - 1 \times 5$ | $6 - 2 \times 5$ | $6 - 3 \times 5$ | $6 - (n - 1) \times 5$ |

#### Key Concepts
*   **Common Difference ($d$):** The difference between any term and the term before it.
*   **Linear Sequence:** A sequence with a common difference.
*   **General Formula:** For sequences with a common difference, the formula is always in the form:
    $$T_n = dn + c$$
    Where $d$ is the common difference and $c$ is a constant.

**NOTE:** The difference between successive terms is always the coefficient of $n$ in the general formula.

### Calculating the Common Difference

In a number sequence, the common difference ($d$) is the constant value obtained by subtracting a term from the term that follows it.

**General Formula:**
$$d = T_n - T_{n-1}$$

**Example:**
Consider the sequence: $10; 7; 4; 1; \dots$

*   **Between the first two terms:**
    $$d = T_2 - T_1 = 7 - 10 = -3$$
*   **Between the third and fourth terms:**
    $$d = T_4 - T_3 = 1 - 4 = -3$$

Since the result is the same, $d$ is constant.

**IMPORTANT NOTE:**
The order of subtraction matters. You must subtract the previous term from the current term.
$$d = T_2 - T_1 \neq T_1 - T_2$$

### Working with Number Patterns

#### Step 2: Describe the pattern
The number of people seated at $n$ tables is given by the general formula:
$$T_n = 4 + 2(n - 1)$$

#### Step 3: Calculate the $12^{th}$ term
To find the number of people at 12 tables, substitute $n = 12$ into the formula:
$$T_{12} = 4 + 2(12 - 1)$$
$$T_{12} = 4 + 2(11)$$
$$T_{12} = 4 + 22$$
$$T_{12} = 26$$
**Conclusion:** 26 people can be seated at 12 tables.

#### Step 4: Calculate the number of tables ($n$) for a specific value ($T_n$)
To find the number of tables needed to seat 20 people, set $T_n = 20$ and solve for $n$:
$$T_n = 4 + 2(n - 1)$$
$$20 = 4 + 2(n - 1)$$
$$20 = 4 + 2n - 2$$
$$20 - 4 + 2 = 2n$$
$$18 = 2n$$
$$\frac{18}{2} = n$$
$$n = 9$$
**Conclusion:** 9 tables are needed to seat 20 people.

### Worked Example 3: Data Plans

**Question:**
Raymond subscribes to a data plan:
*   1 GB = R 120
*   2 GB = R 135
*   3 GB = R 150
Assume this pattern continues indefinitely.

**1. Table of the pattern:**

| Data ($n$ in GB) | 1 | 2 | 3 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Cost ($T_n$ in R) | 120 | 135 | 150 |

**2. Find the general formula:**
The constant difference ($d$) is $135 - 120 = 15$.
Using the formula $T_n = a + (n - 1)d$, where $a = 120$ and $d = 15$:
$$T_n = 120 + (n - 1)15$$
$$T_n = 120 + 15n - 15$$
$$T_n = 15n + 105$$

**3. Determine the cost for a 30 GB data plan:**
Substitute $n = 30$:
$$T_{30} = 15(30) + 105$$
$$T_{30} = 450 + 105$$
$$T_{30} = 555$$
The cost for 30 GB is **R 555**.

**4. Determine data usage for the unlimited plan (R 520):**
Set $T_n = 520$ and solve for $n$:
$$520 = 15n + 105$$
$$520 - 105 = 15n$$
$$415 = 15n$$
$$n = \frac{415}{15}$$
$$n = 27,67 \text{ GB}$$
Raymond would need to use more than **27,67 GB** for the unlimited plan to be cheaper.

### Worked Example: Analyzing Data Costs as a Number Pattern

This example demonstrates how to use number patterns to determine costs and make financial decisions.

#### Step 1: Identify the Pattern
Create a table to observe how the cost changes as the number of GB ($n$) increases.

| Number of GB ($n$) | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cost (in Rands)** | 120 | 135 | 150 | 165 |
| **Pattern** | 120 | $120 + (1)(15) = 135$ | $120 + (2)(15) = 150$ | $120 + (3)(15) = 165$ |

#### Step 2: Determine the General Formula
Based on the observed pattern, the general formula for the price of $n$ GB of data is:
$$T_n = 120 + 15(n - 1)$$

#### Step 3: Determine the cost of 30 GB of data
To find the cost of 30 GB, we calculate the $30^{th}$ term ($T_{30}$) by substituting $n = 30$ into the general formula:

$$T_n = 120 + 15(n - 1)$$
$$\therefore T_{30} = 120 + 15(30 - 1)$$
$$= 120 + 15(29)$$
$$= 120 + 435$$
$$= 555$$

**Conclusion:** The cost of a 30 GB data package is R 555.

#### Step 4: Determine when it is cheaper to purchase an unlimited data plan
To determine when it is cheaper to purchase an unlimited plan (priced at R 520) instead of the limited plan, we find $n$ where $T_n$ is less than R 520.

We set the formula equal to 520 to find the threshold:
$$T_n = 120 + 15(n - 1)$$
$$520 = 120 + 15(n - 1)$$

**Solving for $n$:**
$$520 = 120 + 15n - 15$$
$$520 = 105 + 15n$$
$$405 = 15n$$
$$\frac{405}{15} = n$$
$$n = 27$$

**Conclusion:** It is cheaper for Raymond to purchase the unlimited data plan if he uses more than 27 GB per month.

# Revision Notes: Linear Number Patterns

## Core Concepts
A linear sequence is a list of numbers where the difference between consecutive terms is constant. This constant is called the **common difference ($d$)**.

The general formula for a linear sequence is:
$$T_n = dn + c$$
Where:
*   $T_n$ is the $n^{\text{th}}$ term.
*   $d$ is the common difference.
*   $n$ is the position of the term.
*   $c$ is a constant determined by substituting a known term.

---

## Worked Examples and Exercises

### 1. Arithmetic Sequences
**Example (Q12):** Study the sequence: $15; 23; 31; 39; \dots$
*   **a) Next 3 terms:** The common difference $d = 23 - 15 = 8$.
    *   $39 + 8 = 47$
    *   $47 + 8 = 55$
    *   $55 + 8 = 63$
*   **b) General formula:** $T_n = 8n + c$. Using $T_1 = 15$:
    *   $15 = 8(1) + c \implies c = 7$.
    *   Formula: $T_n = 8n + 7$.
*   **c) Find $n$ if $T_n = 191$:**
    *   $191 = 8n + 7 \implies 184 = 8n \implies n = 23$.

### 2. Sequences with Variables
**Example (Q14):** List: $-z - 5; -4z - 5; -6z - 2; -8z - 5; -10z - 5$
*   **a) Common difference:** Calculate differences between terms:
    *   $(-4z - 5) - (-z - 5) = -3z$
    *   $(-6z - 2) - (-4z - 5) = -2z + 3$
    *   Since $-3z \neq -2z + 3$, there is **no common difference**.

### 3. Solving for Unknowns in Linear Sequences
**Example (Q16):** Terms: $\frac{k}{3} - 1; -\frac{5k}{3} + 2; -\frac{2k}{3} + 10$
For a linear sequence, the difference between the first two terms must equal the difference between the second and third terms:
$$(-\frac{5k}{3} + 2) - (\frac{k}{3} - 1) = (-\frac{2k}{3} + 10) - (-\frac{5k}{3} + 2)$$
$$-2k + 3 = k + 8 \implies -3k = 5 \implies k = -\frac{5}{3}$$

## Strategy for Periodic Sequences (Q18)
To find the $n^{\text{th}}$ term of a repeating pattern:
1. Identify the length of the repeating block ($L$).
2. Divide the position number ($n$) by the length ($L$).
3. The remainder indicates the position within the repeating block.
   *   If the remainder is $1$, it is the $1^{\text{st}}$ letter.
   *   If the remainder is $0$, it is the last letter of the block.

*Example:* For "PATTERN", $L = 7$. To find the $649^{\text{th}}$ letter:
$$649 \div 7 = 92 \text{ remainder } 5$$
The $649^{\text{th}}$ letter is the $5^{\text{th}}$ letter of the word "PATTERN", which is **R**.

### Lesson: Analyzing Triangular Number Patterns

In this lesson, we examine a geometric pattern formed by dots and lines. We use algebraic formulas to determine the number of components in the pattern for any given figure number $n$.

#### 1. Core Concepts
*   **Dots Pattern:** The number of dots in a triangular arrangement follows the formula:
    $$T_n = \frac{n(n + 1)}{2}$$
*   **Lines Pattern:** The number of lines connecting these dots follows the formula:
    $$T_n = \frac{3n(n - 1)}{2}$$
*   **Total:** The total number of components is the sum of the dots and the lines for a specific figure $n$.

#### 2. Worked Example
To find the values for Figure 1 ($n=1$):
*   **Dots:** $T_1 = \frac{1(1 + 1)}{2} = \frac{2}{2} = 1$
*   **Lines:** $T_1 = \frac{3(1)(1 - 1)}{2} = \frac{0}{2} = 0$
*   **Total:** $1 + 0 = 1$

To find the values for Figure 2 ($n=2$):
*   **Dots:** $T_2 = \frac{2(2 + 1)}{2} = \frac{6}{2} = 3$
*   **Lines:** $T_2 = \frac{3(2)(2 - 1)}{2} = \frac{6}{1} = 3$
*   **Total:** $3 + 3 = 6$

The total is calculated by adding the two formulas:

### Identifying Common Differences in Number Patterns

To determine if a sequence is linear, we calculate the difference ($d$) between consecutive terms ($T_n$). If the difference between all consecutive terms is the same, the sequence has a **common difference**.

#### Worked Examples: Determining Common Differences

**b) Sequence: $5; 12; 19; 26; 33; \dots$**

*   $d = T_2 - T_1 = 12 - 5 = 7$
*   $d = T_3 - T_2 = 19 - 12 = 7$
*   $d = T_4 - T_3 = 26 - 19 = 7$

**Result:** Since all results are the same, the common difference is $d = 7$.

**c) Sequence: $2,93; 1,99; 1,14; 0,35; \dots$**

*   $d = T_2 - T_1 = 1,99 - 2,93 = -0,94$
*   $d = T_3 - T_2 = 1,14 - 1,99 = -0,85$

**Result:** The differences are not the same; therefore, there is no common difference.

**d) Sequence: $2,53; 1,88; 1,23; 0,58; \dots$**

*   $d = T_2 - T_1 = 1,88 - 2,53 = -0,65$
*   $d = T_3 - T_2 = 1,23 - 1,88 = -0,65$

**Result:** The common difference is $d = -0,65$.

$$d = T_2 - T_1$$

### Arithmetic Sequences: Deriving the General Formula

In an arithmetic sequence, each successive term is found by adding a constant difference ($d$) to the previous term.

#### Worked Example 1: Sequence $2; 5; 8; 11; \dots$

To find the general formula ($T_n$), we express each term based on the first term ($a = 2$) and the common difference ($d = 3$):

*   $T_1 = a = 2$
*   $T_2 = a + d = 2 + 3 = 2 + 1(3)$
*   $T_3 = T_2 + d = 2 + 3 + 3 = 2 + 2(3)$
*   $T_4 = T_3 + d = 2 + 3 + 3 + 3 = 2 + 3(3)$
*   $T_n = T_{n-1} + d = 2 + 3(n - 1)$

**General Formula:**
$$T_n = 3n - 1$$

**Calculating specific terms:**

#### Concept: Arithmetic Sequences
An arithmetic sequence is a sequence where the difference between consecutive terms is constant. This constant is called the common difference ($d$).

**Formula for the general term:**
$$T_n = a + (n - 1)d$$
Where:
*   $a$ is the first term ($T_1$)
*   $d$ is the common difference ($T_2 - T_1$)
*   $n$ is the position of the term

---

#### Worked Examples

**Example 8a: Find the sixth term of the sequence $4; 13; 22; 31; \dots$**

1. **Find the common difference ($d$):**
   $$d = T_2 - T_1 = 13 - 4 = 9$$

2. **Determine the general formula ($T_n$):**
   $$T_1 = a = 4$$
   $$T_2 = a + d = 4 + 9$$
   $$T_n = T_{n-1} + d = 4 + 9(n - 1)$$
   $$T_n = 9n - 5$$

3. **Calculate $T_6$:**
   $$T_6 = 9(6) - 5$$
   $$T_6 = 54 - 5 = 49$$

**Example 8b: Find the sixth term of the sequence $5; 2; -1; -4; \dots$**

1. **Find the common difference ($d$):**
   $$d = T_2 - T_1 = 2 - 5 = -3$$

2. **Determine the general formula ($T_n$):**
   $$T_1 = a = 5$$
   $$T_n = T_{n-1} + d = 5 + (-3)(n - 1)$$
   $$T_n = 5 - 3n + 3$$
   $$T_n = 7 - 3n$$

3. **Calculate $T_6$:**
   $$T_6 = 7 - 3(6)$$
   $$T_6 = 7 - 18 = -11$$

To find the general term ($T_n$) of a linear sequence, we identify the constant difference ($d$) between consecutive terms and the first term ($a$). The general formula is derived by adding the common difference repeatedly.

### 15. Arithmetic Sequences (Stadium Seating)
**Problem:** A sports stadium has 15 seats in the first row, 19 in the second, and 23 in the third. Calculate the number of seats in the $25^{th}$ row.

**Solution:**
1. **Identify the sequence:** $15; 19; 23; \dots$
2. **Calculate the common difference ($d$):**
   $$d = T_2 - T_1 = 19 - 15 = 4$$
3. **Determine the general formula ($T_n$):**
   * $T_1 = a = 15$
   * $T_2 = a + d = 15 + 4(1)$
   * $T_3 = a + 2d = 15 + 4(2)$
   * $T_n = a + (n - 1)d = 15 + 4(n - 1)$
   * $T_n = 15 + 4n - 4$
   * $T_n = 4n + 11$
4. **Calculate for $n = 25$:**
   $$T_{25} = 4(25) + 11$$
   $$T_{25} = 100 + 11 = 111$$

There are **111 seats** in the $25^{th}$ row.

### Algebraic Derivation of a Sequence

The general term $T_n$ for the sequence is derived by summing the components of the pattern:

$$T_n = \frac{n(n + 1)}{2} + \frac{3n(n - 1)}{2}$$

**Step-by-step simplification:**

1. Expand the numerators:
   $$T_n = \frac{n^2 + n + 3n^2 - 3n}{2}$$

2. Combine like terms:
   $$T_n = \frac{4n^2 - 2n}{2}$$

3. Divide by the denominator:
   $$T_n = 2n^2 - n$$
