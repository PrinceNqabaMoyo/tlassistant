# Term 1: Business Studies Key Concepts

## Business Functions and Management
*   **General management:** Overseeing everything that goes on in a company and making sure that all departments are working together to turn a profit.
*   **Administration function:** The process of collecting and storing information that will be used by management in decision making.
*   **Financial function:** Involves the acquiring and utilisation of funds necessary for efficient operations.
*   **Purchasing function:** The act of buying the goods and services that a company needs to operate and manufacture products.
*   **Production function:** The action of making or manufacturing a complete product.
*   **Marketing function:** The act of promoting and selling products or services.
*   **Public relations:** The continuous maintenance of a public image by the business.
*   **Human resources:** The department of a business that deals with the hiring and training of staff.
*   **Risk management:** The process of identifying, assessing, and controlling threats to a business.

## Management Levels and Processes
*   **Top management:** Responsible for controlling and overseeing the entire organisation.
*   **Middle management:** Responsible for executing organisational plans, which comply with the company's policies and lower management.
*   **Organising:** The process of identifying and grouping work that needs to be done, defining and delegating responsibility and authority.
*   **Leadership (business):** Providing direction or guidance.
*   **Controlling:** A function that helps to measure the progress towards organisational goals, highlights any deviations, and indicates corrective actions.
*   **Planning:** The process of thinking about the activities required to achieve a desired goal.
*   **Directing:** Activities which are designed to encourage the subordinates to work effectively and efficiently.

## Information and Data
*   **Information:** Facts that are provided to learn about something.
*   **Data:** Facts and statistics collected together for reference and analysis.

## Consumer Protection and Redress
*   **Consumer Tribunal:** An independent entity whose mandate is to hear and decide on cases involving consumers.
*   **National Consumer Commission:** Promotes compliance with the Consumer Protection Act through advocacy and enforcement.
*   **Consumer Court:** Court that deals with cases regarding consumer disputes, conflicts, and grievances.
*   **Ombudsman:** An official appointed to investigate individuals' complaints against a company.

# Unit 2.1: Understanding Business Functions

## Introduction
Business functions are the specific activities carried out by an enterprise to ensure its success. These functions are categorized into two main types:

*   **Core Business Functions:** Activities that directly generate income for the enterprise (e.g., the production function, which creates the final goods or services for customers).
*   **Support Business Functions:** Activities that facilitate the production of goods and services (e.g., the marketing function).

---

## 2.1 The Eight Business Functions
All business functions are distinct, yet they must work together to contribute to the overall success of the enterprise. General management is responsible for overseeing all other business functions.

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

The eight functions are:
1.  General Management
2.  Marketing Function
3.  Administration Function
4.  Production Function
5.  Financial Function
6.  Human Resources Function
7.  Public Relations Function
8.  Purchasing Function

> **Did you know?**
> While the eight business functions are all different, they work together to contribute to the success of the business. General management usually oversees all other business functions.

---

## The Purpose of the Eight Business Functions
The business functions are interdependent; they rely on each other to achieve the business's goals, ensuring the survival and sustainability of the enterprise.

*   **Specific Responsibilities:** Each function supports specific aims and objectives. For example, the **marketing function** focuses on developing new markets or increasing sales.
*   **Departmentalization:** In large businesses, these functions are often organized into separate departments (e.g., the finance department).
*   **Flexibility:** Roles and tasks within these functions may change depending on the size, type, and stage of growth of the business.

> **Tip**
> Do not confuse the **purpose** of business functions with the **importance** of business functions. The purpose provides a general overview of what they do, while the importance focuses on the value of each specific function to the business.

## 2.2 The Differences Between Management and Leadership

### Key Definitions
*   **Charisma:** A quality of charm that can inspire devotion in others.
*   **Instinctive:** Actions performed without conscious thought.

## 2. The Levels of Management

The level of management determines the chain of command, the amount of authority, and the status of a managerial position.

### A. Top Level Management
*   **Role:** Oversees the activities of other functions to ensure business objectives are achieved.
*   **Composition:** Consists of the CEO and directors.
*   **Responsibilities:**
    *   Develop long-term **strategic** plans and business policies.
    *   Determine the vision, mission, objectives, and strategy of the business.
    *   Ensure that people work together to accomplish specific goals.

### B. Middle Level Management
*   **Role:** Responsible for specific departments within the business.
*   **Responsibilities:**
    *   Take medium-term **tactical** decisions.
    *   Execute organisational plans in conformance with company policies.
    *   Responsible for achieving goals and objectives set for specific departments.
    *   **Implement** plans made by top-level management.
    *   Work with managers in other departments to acquire necessary resources.

### C. Lower Level Management
*   **Role:** Act as role models for employees by providing supervision and performance feedback.
*   **Responsibilities:**
    *   Focus on controlling and directing.
    *   Responsible for high levels of productivity, technical assistance, and motivating employees.
    *   Take short-term routine/operational decisions.
    *   **Implement** instructions given by middle management.
    *   Known as the "first management level" because it is the entry point for promotion.

# Term 1: Management Tasks

## Controlling
Controlling is a management function focused on ensuring that the business achieves its goals. Key aspects include:
*   Establishing performance standards and ensuring they are met.
*   Ensuring activities are carried out as planned.
*   Enabling the business to take corrective measures if objectives are not achieved.
*   Identifying risks during the control process.
*   Comparing actual results with the goals set by management.
*   Taking corrective action when discrepancies exist between actual results and set goals.
*   Ensuring the business runs smoothly through continuous control.

## Risk Management
Risk management involves identifying and mitigating potential threats to the business:
*   **Identification:** Finding risk-bearing activities that could negatively impact the organisation.
*   **Analysis:** Assessing the likelihood of each risk occurring.
*   **Evaluation:** Determining the potential financial liability of risks.
*   **Monitoring:** Studying reports and environmental trends to prevent risks.
*   **Handling:** Determining actions to take if an event occurs, utilizing available resources, communication with stakeholders, and implementing a **contingency plan**.

---

# Unit 2.3: Organisational Structure

## 1. The Meaning of Organisational Structure
An organisational structure is a system that outlines how specific activities are directed to achieve the goals of an organisation. This system encompasses:
*   **Rules:** The guidelines governing conduct.
*   **Roles:** The specific positions held by individuals.
*   **Responsibilities:** The duties assigned to specific roles.
*   **Information Flow:** The structure determines how information is communicated between different levels within the company.

## 2. Factors Influencing Organisational Structure
The design of an organisation's structure is influenced by the following factors:
*   **Size of the organisation:** Larger companies often require more complex hierarchies.
*   **Strategy:** The long-term goals and plans of the business.
*   **Technology:** The tools and systems used to perform work.
*   **Resources:** The availability of human, financial, and physical assets.

## 3. Types of Organisational Structure

### Functional Organisational Structure
*   Employees receive instructions from more than one manager.
*   The plans to be executed determine who provides the instructions.
*   **Disadvantage:** This structure can cause confusion for employees as they report to multiple managers.

### Project Organisational Structure
*   Structured around specific **project teams**.
*   It is a temporary structure where employees are drawn from various departments.
*   Employees are grouped together to carry out a specific project.

---

## 4. Key Terminology

| Term | Definition |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Project** | A planned piece of work that has a specific purpose. |
| **Matrix** | A structure in which there is more than one line of reporting managers. |

## The Administration Function

The administration function is responsible for collecting, processing, and distributing information required by management for decision-making. It utilizes modern technology to store and record information and performs general office duties, such as filing.

### Activities of the Administration Function: Management of Information
Administrative staff must handle information accurately to ensure management makes correct decisions based on reliable data. The function is responsible for:

*   Maintaining accounting records used to generate financial statements and reports.

*   Utilizing **cost accounting** to determine if a product can be sold competitively while accounting for production costs.

*   Collecting and classifying numerical data and **statistics**.

# Unit 2.3: The Eight Business Functions - Financial Function

## Key Concepts & Definitions

*   **Investors:** A person or business that puts money into financial schemes, property, or businesses with the expectation of achieving a profit.
*   **Loan:** A sum of money that is borrowed and expected to be paid back with interest.

---

## The Role of Financial Management
The financial function ensures that the business can generate enough income to cover the cost of raising capital. It also involves preparing financial statements to present to banks or investors to prove that the business is financially healthy.

---

## Reasons Why Businesses Need Finance
Businesses require capital for the following reasons:
1.  **Start-up costs:** Borrowing money to establish a new business.
2.  **Working capital:** Covering running costs while waiting for outstanding payments.
3.  **Capital expenditure:** Replacing machinery, equipment, and computers.
4.  **Expansion:** Growing a successful business.

---

## Sources of Financing

### 1. Bank Loans
*   Money borrowed from a bank to be repaid over an agreed timeframe.
*   Typically used for long-term financing.
*   The money is repaid with interest.
*   Entrepreneurs may be required to attach a fixed asset as surety (collateral) to the value of the loan.

### 2. Bank Overdraft
*   A short-term loan added to the account of an entrepreneur or business.
*   Repaid with a set interest rate over an agreed timeframe.

### 3. Asset-based Loan
*   Lent to successful businesses looking to expand.
*   The loan is used to purchase a specific asset.
*   The lender retains ownership of the asset until the loan is fully paid off.
*   If the loan is not repaid, the lender repossesses the asset.

### 4. Grants
*   Funds provided by the government to small businesses in the development phase.
*   Generally does not need to be repaid if the business benefits the community or environment.

### 5. Receivable Finance
*   A loan provided to businesses while waiting for payment of goods or services.
*   Used to avoid cash flow shortages.
*   The loan amount is typically equal to the value of outstanding invoices.

## Types of Capital

### 1. Fixed Capital
*   **Definition:** Capital invested in fixed assets (e.g., land and buildings).
*   **Purpose:** Finances the long-term capital needs of the business.

*   **Definition:** Capital used for day-to-day business activities (e.g., trading stock, raw materials).
*   **Purpose:** Finances the short-term capital needs of the business.
*   **Examples:** Money market, credit allowed by suppliers, short-term loans.

# The Purchasing Function

## The Meaning of the Purchasing Function
The purchasing function is responsible for acquiring quality raw materials and services for the business. It involves the continuous search for suitable, new, and better suppliers to ensure that ordered products are delivered on time, at the agreed price, in the correct quantities, and of the right quality.

### Key Terminology
| Term | Definition |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Reputable supplier** | A supplier who meets current regulatory requirements and industry standards. |
| **Requisition** | An official order laying claim to the use of property or materials. |
| **Raw materials** | The basic material from which a product is made. |

---

## The Purpose of the Purchasing Function
The primary objectives of the purchasing department include:
*   Managing stock levels to ensure sufficient supply for business operations.
*   Continuously searching for the best and most reputable suppliers.
*   Regularly communicating with other business departments to determine their specific needs.
*   Returning damaged goods to suppliers and ensuring replacements are provided.
*   Verifying that delivered goods match specifications and that the invoiced price matches the quoted price.
*   Negotiating favorable payment terms with suppliers.

---

## Activities of the Purchasing Function
The purchasing department performs the following operational tasks:
*   Developing expert knowledge of the products required and the relevant market.
*   Identifying the needs of other departments within the business.
*   Sourcing suitable, new, and improved suppliers.
*   Ensuring sufficient stock availability for continuous production and sales.
*   Following up on orders placed with suppliers.
*   Ensuring timely delivery of products.
*   Maintaining accurate records of stock levels.
*   Recording the cost prices and selling prices of stock.

---

## Purchasing Procedure
To maintain efficiency, the business should follow a structured purchasing procedure:

### 1. Determine the need for the product (Requisition)
*   Liaise with the financial department to establish a budget for purchasing goods and services.
*   Identify the specific product, material, or resource needs of the business.
*   Source the right quality and quantity of goods and services at the right price and time.

### 2. Determine the price of the product
*   Identify the best price by obtaining quotes, tenders, or making formal enquiries.

### 3. Select/Choose a suitable supplier
*   Choose reliable suppliers for raw materials and products.
*   Apply evaluation criteria based on quality, price, and delivery time to select the best supplier.
*   Conduct a thorough investigation into potential suppliers regarding their reputation and reliability.

# Unit 2.3: The Purchasing Function

The purchasing function is responsible for acquiring the necessary goods and services for a business to operate effectively. This process involves several key stages to ensure efficiency and accountability.

## The Purchasing Process

1.  **Place an order:**
    *   Orders must be placed in writing to allow for comparison between delivered goods and the original order.
    *   Confirm prices beforehand to prevent unexpected costs during payment.
2.  **Collect or receive the order:**
    *   Ensure the correct items are received and recorded.
    *   Verify the quality and quantity of stock against the original order.
    *   Retain a copy of the delivery note for record-keeping.
3.  **Pay the supplier:**
    *   The purchasing department instructs the financial department to process payment after delivery.
    *   The supplier must provide copies of the requisition form.
    *   The purchasing department provides the delivery note to the financial department.
    *   The supplier sends an invoice to the financial department for final payment once delivery is confirmed as satisfactory.
4.  **Distribute stock:**
    *   Ensure raw materials or stock are distributed to the relevant departments.
    *   Distribution must align with pre-requisite orders to prevent stock loss.
5.  **Complete the order:**
    *   Ensure all documentation is correctly filed for future reference.

# Term 1: Cash and Credit Payments

## Differences Between Cash and Credit Payments

| CASH PAYMENTS | CREDIT PAYMENTS |
| :--- | :--- |
| Cash payment refers to all payments made by cash/cheque for business purchases immediately. | Credit payment refers to all payments on a future date. |
| Cash payments have an immediate effect on the cashflow of the business. | Credit payments results in the creation of assets (receivable)/liabilities of the business (payable). |
| Cash payments can qualify for cash discounts. | The credit payer can pay more for goods because of the interest added onto credit payments. |

---

## The National Credit Act (NCA)

### New Words
*   **Credit providers:** Companies that offer a range of financial solutions to consumers, including loans.
*   **Reckless:** Without consideration for the consequences of one's actions.

### Definition of the NCA
The National Credit Act (NCA) was introduced to provide both credit providers and credit applicants with clear guidelines regarding their rights and responsibilities. This act applies to all businesses that sell on credit.

### Purpose of the NCA
*   Promotes the development of a credit market that is accessible to all South Africans.
*   Encourages responsible buying.
*   Avoids over-indebtedness and ensures the fulfilment of obligations by credit providers and consumers.
*   Addresses and corrects imbalances in negotiating power between consumers and credit providers.
*   Discourages **reckless** credit granting by credit providers.
*   Educates consumers on making the right choice when applying for credit.
*   Gives guidelines within which the different kinds of credit transactions must take place in South Africa.

### Consumer Rights Outlined in the NCA
Consumers have a right to:
*   Apply for credit.
*   Receive information in their official language.
*   Be protected from unfair discrimination in granting credit.
*   Be given reasons why a credit application is refused.
*   Be informed about the interest rate and any other costs of the proposed credit transaction.
*   Receive a copy of a credit contract and a replacement copy when the consumer asks for one.
*   Apply for **debt counselling** if a customer has too much debt.

### New Words
*   **Debt counselling:** The activity of giving people financial help and advice about managing debt, for example, helping them on a new repayment plan.

## Responsibilities of Credit Providers
Credit providers must perform a thorough credit assessment to ensure consumers can afford the debt. This includes:
*   Verifying income via the most recent payslip or bank statement.
*   Checking the consumer's existing monthly debt-repayment obligations.
*   Considering the consumer's other living expenses.
*   Reviewing the consumer's historical debt repayment record.

---

## Remedies of the National Credit Act (NCA)
When disputes or issues arise, the following bodies provide remedies:

*   **The Consumer Tribunal:** Responsible for reviewing decisions made by the National Credit Regulator (NCR) and the National Consumer Commission (NCC).
*   **National Consumer Commission (NCC):** Responsible for promoting compliance with the NCA and the Consumer Protection Act (CPA) through **advocacy** and enforcement. It also protects the economic welfare of consumers.
*   **Ombudsman:** An independent person with the authority and responsibility to receive, investigate, and formally address complaints from consumers.

### Key Terminology
| Term | Definition |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Advocacy** | Public support for or recommendation of a particular cause or policy. |

---

## The Impact of the National Credit Act on Businesses

### Positives/Advantages
*   **Improved Cash Flow:** Lowers bad debts.
*   **Protection:** Protects businesses against non-paying consumers.
*   **Increased Cash Sales:** Credit is restricted to qualifying customers only.
*   **Responsible Lending:** Prevents reckless lending by financial institutions.
*   **Credit Ratings:** Ensures businesses settle debts on time to maintain good credit scores.
*   **Transparency:** The credit process is clear, ensuring both businesses and customers understand their responsibilities.

### Negatives/Disadvantages
*   **Budgetary Pressure:** Businesses are forced to keep more cash on hand for stock purchases.
*   **Quality Control:** Businesses cannot risk selling poor-quality goods at high prices.
*   **Marketing Restrictions:** Businesses can no longer carry out aggressive credit marketing.
*   **Loss of Sales:** Many businesses may no longer qualify to buy on credit.
*   **Limited Stock:** Businesses may be restricted in stock purchases if credit is unavailable, leading to a loss of customers.
*   **Administrative Burden:** The purchasing process is complicated by increased administrative requirements in the credit-providing process.

## The Consumer Protection Act (CPA)

### Definition
The CPA was introduced to prevent consumers from being exploited by businesses. It ensures the full participation of previously disadvantaged individuals in the economy and applies to all businesses that sell goods and services to consumers.

### The Purpose of the CPA
*   Promotes responsible consumer behaviour.
*   Strengthens a culture of consumer rights and responsibilities.
*   Establishes national standards to protect consumers.
*   Establishes a National Consumer Commission (NCC).
*   Ensures that consumers have access to information needed to make informed choices.
*   Provides guidelines for better consumer information and prohibits unfair business practices.
*   Empowers consumers to take legal action if their rights are not upheld.
*   Promotes consumer safety by protecting them from hazardous products/services.
*   Promotes fair, accessible, and sustainable places for people to sell their products.
*   Protects consumers against contracts that include unfair terms which limit the liability of suppliers.

---

## The Impact of the Consumer Protection Act on Businesses

### Positives/Advantages
*   Businesses may be safeguarded from dishonest competitors.
*   Businesses may be protected if they are regarded as consumers.
*   Prevents larger businesses from undermining smaller ones.
*   May gain consumer loyalty if they comply with the CPA.
*   Enables businesses to resolve disputes fairly through the National Consumer Commission, Consumer Court, or Industrial Ombudsmen.
*   Businesses may build a good image if they ensure that they do not violate consumer rights.

### Negatives/Disadvantages
*   Confidential business information may become available to competitors.
*   Penalties for non-compliance may be very high.
*   Businesses may feel unnecessarily burdened by legal processes.

---

### Additional Contextual Notes
*   The purchasing department must be aware of the terms and conditions of credit granting and the National Credit Act (NCA).
*   Compliance may increase the time taken to purchase goods, which can influence the overall efficiency of the business.
*   The Act compels businesses to sell quality products, or they may be forced to reimburse consumers.

### 1.3.2 Impact of the National Credit Act (NCA) on Businesses

The National Credit Act significantly influences how businesses manage credit and interact with consumers.

#### Positives/Advantages
*   **Improved Cash Flow:** Lowers bad debts, resulting in better cash flow.
*   **Consumer Protection:** Protects businesses against non-paying consumers.
*   **Targeted Sales:** Increases cash sales as credit is only granted to qualifying customers.
*   **Financial Stability:** Prevents reckless lending by financial institutions.
*   **Credit Reputation:** Ensures businesses settle debts on time to maintain good credit scores.
*   **Transparency:** Ensures the credit process is transparent, clarifying responsibilities for both businesses and customers.

#### Negatives/Disadvantages
*   **Liquidity Requirements:** Businesses are forced to budget to keep more cash on hand for stock purchases.
*   **Quality Standards:** Businesses can no longer take the risk of selling poor-quality goods at high prices.
*   **Marketing Restrictions:** Businesses can no longer carry out aggressive credit marketing.
*   **Sales Volume:** Leads to a loss of sales as some customers no longer qualify to buy on credit.
*   **Inventory Constraints:** Businesses can only buy limited stock if credit is not available, potentially resulting in a loss of customers.
*   **Administrative Burden:** The Act complicates the purchasing process due to increased administrative work.
*   **Compliance:** The purchasing department must be fully aware of the terms and conditions of credit granting and the NCA.
*   **Efficiency:** It may take longer to purchase goods, which could influence the overall efficiency of the business.
*   **Liability:** The Act compels businesses to sell quality products or face being forced to reimburse the consumer.

### 1.4 Purpose of the Consumer Protection Act (CPA)

The CPA is designed to regulate the relationship between suppliers and consumers. Its primary purposes include:

*   **Responsible Behaviour:** Promotes responsible consumer behaviour.
*   **Rights and Responsibilities:** Strengthens a culture of consumer rights and responsibilities.
*   **Standardization:** Establishes national standards to protect consumers.
*   **Oversight:** Establishes the National Consumer Commission (NCC).
*   **Informed Choices:** Ensures consumers have access to information needed to make informed choices.
*   **Fair Practices:** Provides guidelines for better consumer information and prohibits unfair business practices.
*   **Legal Empowerment:** Empowers consumers to take legal action if their rights are not upheld.
*   **Safety:** Promotes consumer safety by protecting them from hazardous products or services.
*   **Accessibility:** Promotes fair, accessible, and sustainable places for people to sell their products.
*   **Contractual Fairness:** Protects consumers against contracts that include unfair terms that limit the liability of suppliers.
