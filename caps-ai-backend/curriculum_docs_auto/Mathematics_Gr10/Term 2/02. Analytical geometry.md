## Key Mathematical Concepts

### 1. Distance Between Two Points
To calculate the distance $d$ between two points $A(x_1, y_1)$ and $B(x_2, y_2)$, we use the distance formula derived from the Pythagorean theorem:
$$d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}$$

### 2. Gradient of a Line
The gradient ($m$) represents the steepness of a line passing through points $(x_1, y_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2)$:
$$m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$$

### 3. Mid-point of a Line
The mid-point $M(x, y)$ of a line segment connecting points $(x_1, y_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2)$ is the average of the coordinates:
$$M = \left( \frac{x_1 + x_2}{2} ; \frac{y_1 + y_2}{2} \right)$$

### Naming Conventions
The order of the letters for naming a figure is important. It indicates the order in which points must be joined:
*   $A$ to $B$
*   $B$ to $C$
*   $C$ to $D$
*   $D$ back to $A$

# 8.2 Distance between two points

### Definitions
*   **Point:** A point is an ordered pair of numbers written as $(x; y)$.
*   **Distance:** Distance is a measure of the length between two points.

---

### Investigation: Distance between two points
Given points $P(2; 1)$, $Q(-2; -2)$, and $R(2; -2)$:

1.  **Can we assume that $\hat{R} = 90^\circ$?** Yes, because the line segment $PR$ is vertical (constant $x$-coordinate) and the line segment $QR$ is horizontal (constant $y$-coordinate).
2.  **Apply the theorem of Pythagoras in $\triangle PQR$ to find the length of $PQ$:**

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

In $\triangle PQR$:
*   Length of $PR = |1 - (-2)| = 3$ units.
*   Length of $QR = |2 - (-2)| = 4$ units.

Using the theorem of Pythagoras ($PQ^2 = PR^2 + QR^2$):
$$PQ^2 = 3^2 + 4^2$$
$$PQ^2 = 9 + 16$$
$$PQ^2 = 25$$
$$PQ = \sqrt{25} = 5 \text{ units}$$

---

### Deriving the General Distance Formula
To derive a general formula for the distance between two points $A(x_1; y_1)$ and $B(x_2; y_2)$, we use the theorem of Pythagoras.

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

1.  Construct a right-angled triangle $\triangle ABC$ where $C$ is the point $(x_2; y_1)$.
2.  The length of the horizontal side $AC = |x_2 - x_1|$.
3.  The length of the vertical side $BC = |y_2 - y_1|$.
4.  By the theorem of Pythagoras:
    $$AB^2 = AC^2 + BC^2$$
    $$AB^2 = (x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2$$
    $$AB = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}$$

---

### The Distance Formula

The distance formula is derived from the Pythagorean theorem ($AB^2 = AC^2 + BC^2$).

Given two points $(x_1; y_1)$ and $(x_2; y_2)$, the horizontal and vertical distances are:
*   $AC = x_2 - x_1$
*   $BC = y_2 - y_1$

Substituting these into the Pythagorean theorem gives the general distance formula:

$$d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}$$

**Note:** Because the terms are squared, the order of subtraction does not affect the result, as $(x_1 - x_2)^2 = (x_2 - x_1)^2$.

---

### Worked Example 1: Using the distance formula

**Question:**
Find the distance between $S(-2; -5)$ and $Q(7; -2)$.

**Solution:**

**Step 1: Draw a sketch**
<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

**Step 2: Identify the coordinates**
*   $(x_1; y_1) = (-2; -5)$
*   $(x_2; y_2) = (7; -2)$

**Step 3: Apply the distance formula**
$$d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}$$
$$d = \sqrt{(7 - (-2))^2 + (-2 - (-5))^2}$$
$$d = \sqrt{(7 + 2)^2 + (-2 + 5)^2}$$
$$d = \sqrt{(9)^2 + (3)^2}$$
$$d = \sqrt{81 + 9}$$
$$d = \sqrt{90}$$
$$d = 3\sqrt{10} \approx 9,49$$

The distance between points $S$ and $Q$ is $3\sqrt{10}$ units.

This lesson demonstrates how to find an unknown coordinate when the distance between two points is known, using the distance formula.

$x_1 = 3, \quad y_1 = 9, \quad x_2 = 8, \quad y_2 = y$

The distance $d$ between two points is given by:
$$d = \sqrt{(x_1 - x_2)^2 + (y_1 - y_2)^2}$$

#### Step 4: Substitute values and solve for $y$
Given $d = 13$, substitute the known values into the formula:

$$13 = \sqrt{(3 - 8)^2 + (9 - y)^2}$$
$$13^2 = (-5)^2 + (81 - 18y + y^2)$$
$$169 = 25 + 81 - 18y + y^2$$
$$0 = y^2 - 18y - 63$$

Factorize the quadratic equation:
$$0 = (y + 3)(y - 21)$$

Solving for $y$:
$$\therefore y = -3 \text{ or } y = 21$$

#### Step 5: Check both values for $y$

**Check $y = -3$:**
$$d = \sqrt{(3 - 8)^2 + (9 - (-3))^2}$$
$$d = \sqrt{(-5)^2 + (12)^2}$$
$$d = \sqrt{25 + 144}$$
$$d = \sqrt{169}$$
$$d = 13$$

**Check $y = 21$:**
$$d = \sqrt{(3 - 8)^2 + (9 - 21)^2}$$
$$d = \sqrt{(-5)^2 + (-12)^2}$$
$$d = \sqrt{25 + 144}$$
$$d = \sqrt{169}$$

## 8.3 Gradient of a line (EMA6B)

### Definition: Gradient
The gradient of a line is determined by the ratio of vertical change to horizontal change.

Gradient ($m$) describes the slope or steepness of the line joining two points. In the figure below, line $OQ$ is the least steep and line $OT$ is the steepest.

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

---

### Deriving the Gradient Formula

To derive the formula for the gradient, we consider a right-angled triangle formed by two points $A(x_1; y_1)$ and $B(x_2; y_2)$ on a Cartesian plane. The hypotenuse of this triangle is the line segment $AB$.

The gradient is defined as the ratio of the vertical change (rise) to the horizontal change (run):
*   **Vertical side length:** The difference in $y$-values ($y_2 - y_1$).
*   **Horizontal side length:** The difference in $x$-values ($x_2 - x_1$).

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

#### The Gradient Formula
The gradient ($m$) is calculated as:
$$m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1} \quad \text{or} \quad m = \frac{y_1 - y_2}{x_1 - x_2}$$

> **IMPORTANT!**
> Always be consistent with your order of coordinates. The following is **incorrect**:
> $$m \neq \frac{y_1 - y_2}{x_2 - x_1}$$

---

### Worked Example: Gradient between two points

**Question:**
Find the gradient of the line between points $E(2; 5)$ and $F(-3; 9)$.

**Solution:**

**Step 1: Draw a sketch**
<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

**Step 2: Apply the formula**
Let $(x_1; y_1) = (2; 5)$ and $(x_2; y_2) = (-3; 9)$.

$$m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$$
$$m = \frac{9 - 5}{-3 - 2}$$
$$m = \frac{4}{-5}$$
$$m = -\frac{4}{5}$$

# Straight Lines

## Definition
A straight line is a set of points with a constant gradient between any two of the points.

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

For points $A(x; y)$, $B(x_2; y_2)$, and $C(x_1; y_1)$, the gradient ($m$) is constant:
$$m_{AB} = m_{BC} = m_{AC}$$
$$m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1} = \frac{y_1 - y_2}{x_1 - x_2}$$

## Equations of a Straight Line

### Two-Point Form
The general formula for finding the equation of a straight line given two points is:
$$\frac{y - y_1}{x - x_1} = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$$
where $(x; y)$ is any point on the line.

### Gradient-Point Form
The formula above can also be written as:
$$y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$$

### Standard Form
The standard form of the straight line equation is:
$$y = mx + c$$
Where:
*   $m$ is the gradient.
*   $c$ is the $y$-intercept.

### Finding the Equation of a Straight Line Passing Through Two Points

To find the equation of a straight line when given two points, $P$ and $Q$, follow these logical steps:

#### Step 1: Draw a sketch
Visualizing the points on a Cartesian plane helps to verify the gradient and intercept.

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

#### Step 2: Assign values
Identify the coordinates of the two points as $(x_1; y_1)$ and $(x_2; y_2)$.
For points $P(-1; -5)$ and $Q(5; 4)$:
*   $x_1 = -1$
*   $y_1 = -5$
*   $x_2 = 5$
*   $y_2 = 4$

#### Step 3: Write down the general formula
Use the two-point form of the straight-line equation:
$$\frac{y - y_1}{x - x_1} = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$$

#### Step 4: Substitute values and make $y$ the subject
Substitute the assigned values into the formula and solve for $y$:

1.  **Substitute:**
    $$\frac{y - (-5)}{x - (-1)} = \frac{4 - (-5)}{5 - (-1)}$$

2.  **Simplify the fractions:**
    $$\frac{y + 5}{x + 1} = \frac{9}{6}$$
    *(Note: $\frac{9}{6}$ simplifies to $\frac{3}{2}$)*
    $$\frac{y + 5}{x + 1} = \frac{3}{2}$$

3.  **Cross-multiply:**
    $$2(y + 5) = 3(x + 1)$$

# Parallel and Perpendicular Lines

## Key Concepts

*   **Parallel Lines:** Two lines that run parallel to each other are always the same distance apart and have equal gradients.
    *   If line $1$ is parallel to line $2$, then $m_1 = m_2$.

    *   If line $WX \perp YZ$, then $m_{WX} \times m_{YZ} = -1$.
    *   Perpendicular lines have gradients that are the negative inverses of each other.

### Horizontal and Vertical Lines (EMA6F)

*   **Horizontal Lines:** A line that runs parallel to the $x$-axis. It has a gradient of zero because there is no vertical change.
    $$m = \frac{\text{change in } y}{\text{change in } x} = \frac{0}{\text{change in } x} = 0$$

*   **Vertical Lines:** A line that runs parallel to the $y$-axis. Its gradient is undefined because there is no horizontal change (division by zero is undefined).
    $$m = \frac{\text{change in } y}{\text{change in } x} = \frac{\text{change in } y}{0} = \text{undefined}$$

### 8.5 Chapter Summary: Analytical Geometry

#### Key Concepts and Definitions
*   **Point:** An ordered pair of numbers written as $(x; y)$.
*   **Distance:** A measure of the length between two points.
*   **Gradient ($m$):** The ratio of vertical change to horizontal change between two points.
*   **Straight Line:** A set of points with a constant gradient between any two points.

#### Mathematical Formulas

**1. Distance Formula**
The distance $d$ between any two points $(x_1; y_1)$ and $(x_2; y_2)$ is:

**2. Gradient Formula**
The gradient $m$ of a line passing through two points $(x_1; y_1)$ and $(x_2; y_2)$ is:
$$m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$$

**3. Equation of a Straight Line**
*   **Standard Form:** $y = mx + c$
*   **Two-point Form:** $\frac{y - y_1}{x - x_1} = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$

### Analytical Geometry: Key Concepts

*   **Parallel Lines:** If two lines are parallel, their gradients are equal ($m_1 = m_2$).
*   **Perpendicular Lines:** If two lines are perpendicular, the product of their gradients is equal to $-1$ ($m_1 \times m_2 = -1$).
*   **Horizontal Lines:** The gradient is equal to $0$.
*   **Vertical Lines:** The gradient is undefined.
*   **Mid-point Formula:** The formula for finding the mid-point $M$ between two points $(x_1; y_1)$ and $(x_2; y_2)$ is:
    $$M (x; y) = \left( \frac{x_1 + x_2}{2}; \frac{y_1 + y_2}{2} \right)$$

### 5. Calculating the Length of a Line Segment
To calculate the distance between two points $A(x_1; y_1)$ and $B(x_2; y_2)$ on the Cartesian plane, use the distance formula:

$$d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}$$

**Worked Example:**
Given points $A(-4; 1.5)$ and $B(3.5; -4.5)$:

1.  **Identify coordinates:**
    $x_1 = -4, y_1 = 1.5$
    $x_2 = 3.5, y_2 = -4.5$

2.  **Substitute into the formula:**
    $$AB = \sqrt{(3.5 - (-4))^2 + (-4.5 - 1.5)^2}$$
    $$AB = \sqrt{(7.5)^2 + (-6)^2}$$
    $$AB = \sqrt{56.25 + 36}$$
    $$AB = \sqrt{92.25}$$

3.  **Calculate final value:**
    $$AB \approx 9.60$$

### Analytical Geometry: Practice Exercises

#### 1. Distance Formula Application
**Problem:** Given points $A(-3; 2)$ and $B(x; -4)$, the distance between the points is $8,4853$. Calculate the missing coordinate $x$ of point $B$.

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

**Concept:** The distance formula between two points $(x_1; y_1)$ and $(x_2; y_2)$ is:
$$d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}$$

**Solution Steps:**
1. Substitute the known values into the formula:
   $$8,4853 = \sqrt{(x - (-3))^2 + (-4 - 2)^2}$$
2. Simplify the expression:
   $$8,4853 = \sqrt{(x + 3)^2 + (-6)^2}$$
   $$8,4853 = \sqrt{(x + 3)^2 + 36}$$
3. Square both sides to remove the square root:
   $$(8,4853)^2 = (x + 3)^2 + 36$$
   $$72 = (x + 3)^2 + 36$$
4. Solve for $x$:
   $$36 = (x + 3)^2$$
   $$\pm 6 = x + 3$$
   $$x = 6 - 3 = 3 \quad \text{or} \quad x = -6 - 3 = -9$$

#### 2. Gradient Calculation
**Problem:** Calculate the gradient ($m$) of line $AB$ given the coordinates $A(-1; -3,5)$ and $B(2; 2,5)$.

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

**Concept:** The gradient formula for a line passing through two points is:
$$m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$$

**Solution:**
$$m = \frac{2,5 - (-3,5)}{2 - (-1)}$$
$$m = \frac{2,5 + 3,5}{2 + 1}$$
$$m = \frac{6}{3}$$
$$m = 2$$

#### 3. Finding Missing Coordinates using Gradient
**Problem:** Line $AB$ passes through $A(-2; 0,5)$ and $B(x; 3,5)$. The gradient $m$ is $0,5$. Calculate the missing coordinate $x$ of point $B$.

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

**Solution Steps:**
1. Use the gradient formula:
   $$m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$$
2. Substitute the known values:
   $$0,5 = \frac{3,5 - 0,5}{x - (-2)}$$
3. Simplify:
   $$0,5 = \frac{3}{x + 2}$$
4. Solve for $x$:
   $$0,5(x + 2) = 3$$
   $$x + 2 = \frac{3}{0,5}$$
   $$x + 2 = 6$$
   $$x = 4$$

### Analytical Geometry: Parallel and Perpendicular Lines

#### 12. Parallel Lines
**Concept:** Parallel lines have equal gradients ($m_1 = m_2$).

*   **Problem:** Line $AB$ passes through $A(-2; -1)$ and $B(x; 3)$. It is parallel to the line $y = x - 2$.
*   **Diagram:** <!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->
*   **Solution:**
    1. Identify the gradient of the given line: $m = 1$.
    2. Since $AB$ is parallel, its gradient is also $1$.
    3. Use the gradient formula: $m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$.
    4. Substitute the known values: $1 = \frac{3 - (-1)}{x - (-2)}$.
    5. Simplify: $1 = \frac{4}{x + 2}$.
    6. Solve for $x$: $x + 2 = 4 \implies x = 2$.
    7. The coordinate of point $B$ is $(2; 3)$.

---

#### 13. Perpendicular Lines
**Concept:** The product of the gradients of two perpendicular lines is $-1$ ($m_1 \times m_2 = -1$).

*   **Problem:** Line $AB$ passes through $A(-1; -4)$ and $B(1; y)$. It is perpendicular to the line $y = -0,5x - 2$.
*   **Diagram:** <!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->
*   **Solution:**
    1. Identify the gradient of the given line: $m_1 = -0,5 = -\frac{1}{2}$.
    2. Calculate the gradient of $AB$ ($m_2$): $m_2 = \frac{-1}{m_1} = \frac{-1}{-0,5} = 2$.
    3. Use the gradient formula: $m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$.
    4. Substitute the known values: $2 = \frac{y - (-4)}{1 - (-1)}$.
    5. Simplify: $2 = \frac{y + 4}{2}$.
    6. Solve for $y$: $4 = y + 4 \implies y = 0$.
    7. The coordinate of point $B$ is $(1; 0)$.

#### 14. Determining the Equation of a Line
**Concept:** To find the equation of a line ($y = mx + c$), determine the gradient ($m$) and the $y$-intercept ($c$).

*   **Problem:** Line $AB$ is perpendicular to the blue dashed line with equation $y = x + 0,5$. Point $A$ is at $(-5; 3,5)$. Determine the equation of line $AB$.
*   **Diagram:** <!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->
*   **Solution:**
    1. Identify the gradient of the blue line: $m_1 = 1$.
    2. Calculate the gradient of $AB$ ($m_2$): $m_2 = \frac{-1}{1} = -1$.
    3. Use the point-gradient form or substitute into $y = mx + c$:
       $3,5 = -1(-5) + c$
    4. Solve for $c$:
       $3,5 = 5 + c$
       $c = 3,5 - 5$
       $c = -1,5$
    5. The equation of line $AB$ is $y = -x - 1,5$.

### 18. Determining Equations of Straight Lines
**Formula:** The gradient $m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$ and the equation $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$.

**a) Passing through $P(5; 5)$ and $Q(-2; 12)$:**
1. Calculate gradient: $m = \frac{12 - 5}{-2 - 5} = \frac{7}{-7} = -1$
2. Use point-gradient form: $y - 5 = -1(x - 5)$
3. Simplify: $y = -x + 5 + 5 \implies y = -x + 10$

**b) Parallel to $y = 3x + 4$ and passing through $(4; 0)$:**
1. Parallel lines have equal gradients, so $m = 3$.
2. Use point-gradient form: $y - 0 = 3(x - 4)$
3. Simplify: $y = 3x - 12$

**c) Passing through $F(2; 1)$ and the mid-point of $GH$ where $G(-6; 3)$ and $H(-2; -3)$:**
1. Find mid-point of $GH$: $M = \left( \frac{-6 - 2}{2} ; \frac{3 - 3}{2} \right) = (-4; 0)$
2. Calculate gradient between $F(2; 1)$ and $M(-4; 0)$: $m = \frac{0 - 1}{-4 - 2} = \frac{-1}{-6} = \frac{1}{6}$
3. Use point-gradient form: $y - 0 = \frac{1}{6}(x + 4)$
4. Simplify: $y = \frac{1}{6}x + \frac{4}{6} \implies y = \frac{1}{6}x + \frac{2}{3}$

### Key Formulas for Analytical Geometry

*   **Gradient ($m$):**
    $$m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$$

*   **Mid-point ($M$):**
    $$M = \left( \frac{x_1 + x_2}{2} ; \frac{y_1 + y_2}{2} \right)$$

*   **Distance ($d$):**
    $$d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}$$

*   **Equation of a line:**
    $$y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$$
    or
    $$y = mx + c$$

*   **Parallel Lines:**
    Lines are parallel if their gradients are equal: $m_1 = m_2$.

## Key Mathematical Concepts

### 1. Distance Between Two Points
To calculate the distance $d$ between two points $A(x_1, y_1)$ and $B(x_2, y_2)$, we use the distance formula derived from the Pythagorean theorem:
$$d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}$$

### 2. Gradient of a Line
The gradient ($m$) represents the steepness and direction of a line passing through points $A(x_1, y_1)$ and $B(x_2, y_2)$:
$$m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$$

### 3. Mid-point of a Line
The mid-point $M(x, y)$ of a line segment connecting $A(x_1, y_1)$ and $B(x_2, y_2)$ is the average of the coordinates:
$$M = \left( \frac{x_1 + x_2}{2} ; \frac{y_1 + y_2}{2} \right)$$

### Coordinate Notation
When writing coordinates $(x; y)$, it is standard practice in South Africa to use a semi-colon $(;)$ to separate the $x$ and $y$ values. This avoids ambiguity when dealing with decimal values. 

For example, the point $(5, 5; 5, 5)$ is clear, whereas $(5,5, 5,5)$ is ambiguous as it is unclear if the comma is a separator or a decimal point.

# Analytical Geometry: Gradients of Lines

## Key Concepts
To determine the relationship between two lines, we compare their gradients ($m$):
*   **Parallel Lines:** The gradients are equal ($m_1 = m_2$).
*   **Perpendicular Lines:** The gradients are negative inverses of each other ($m_1 \times m_2 = -1$).
*   **Neither:** If the gradients are not equal and their product is not $-1$.

The gradient formula between two points $(x_1, y_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2)$ is:
$$m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$$

### Analytical Geometry: Parallel, Perpendicular, and Collinear Lines

#### 1. Determining Relationships Between Lines
To determine if two lines are parallel, perpendicular, or neither, we compare their gradients ($m$).

*   **Parallel lines:** $m_1 = m_2$
*   **Perpendicular lines:** $m_1 \times m_2 = -1$ (or $m_1 = -\frac{1}{m_2}$)

---

#### Worked Example: Comparing Lines $AB$ and $CD$
**Given:** $A(3; -4), B(5; 2), C(-1; 3), D(-2; 2)$

$$m_{AB} = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$$
$$m_{AB} = \frac{2 - (-4)}{5 - 3}$$
$$m_{AB} = \frac{6}{2}$$
$$m_{AB} = 3$$

**Step 2: Calculate gradient of $CD$ ($m_{CD}$)**
$$m_{CD} = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$$
$$m_{CD} = \frac{2 - 3}{1 - (-2)}$$
$$m_{CD} = \frac{-1}{3}$$

**Step 3: Conclusion**
*   Since $m_{AB} \neq m_{CD}$, the lines are **not parallel**.
*   Check for perpendicularity: $m_{AB} \times m_{CD} = 3 \times (-\frac{1}{3}) = -1$.
*   Therefore, lines $AB$ and $CD$ are **perpendicular**.

### Finding the Equation of a Line Parallel to a Given Line

When two lines are parallel, they have the same gradient ($m$). To find the equation of a line $AB$ that is parallel to a given line, follow these steps:

1.  **Identify the gradient:** Extract the gradient ($m$) from the given line equation ($y = mx + c$).
2.  **Find the $y$-intercept ($c$):** Substitute the coordinates of a known point $(x, y)$ and the gradient into the slope-intercept form $y = mx + c$ and solve for $c$.
3.  **State the equation:** Write the final equation using the calculated $m$ and $c$.
4.  **Solve for missing coordinates:** Substitute the known $x$ or $y$ value of a second point into your new equation to find the missing coordinate.

---

### Analytical Geometry: Perpendicular Lines

When two lines are perpendicular, the product of their gradients is $-1$. If the gradient of the first line is $m_1$ and the gradient of the perpendicular line is $m_2$, then:
$$m_1 \times m_2 = -1 \quad \text{or} \quad m_2 = \frac{-1}{m_1}$$

---

### Worked Example 1
**Problem:** The equation of the blue dashed line is $y = x + 1$. Point $A$ is at $(-2; 4)$. Determine the equation of line $AB$ which is perpendicular to the blue dashed line.

**Solution:**
1. **Identify the gradient:** The blue line is in the form $y = mx + c$, where $m = 1$.
2. **Find the perpendicular gradient ($m_{AB}$):**
   $$m_{AB} = \frac{-1}{m_{\text{blue line}}} = \frac{-1}{1} = -1$$
3. **Set up the equation:**
   $$y = mx + c$$
   $$y = -1x + c$$
4. **Substitute point $A(-2; 4)$ to find $c$:**
   $$4 = (-1)(-2) + c$$
   $$4 = 2 + c$$
   $$c = 2$$
5. **Final Equation:**
   $$y = -x + 2$$

To verify that a point is the mid-point of a line segment, the distance from the mid-point to each of the two end points must be equal.

$$d = \sqrt{(x_1 - x_2)^2 + (y_1 - y_2)^2}$$

For a mid-point $S(0; -0,5)$ between points $P(-2; 1)$ and $Q(2; -2)$:

$$PS = \sqrt{(x_1 - x_2)^2 + (y_1 - y_2)^2}$$
$$PS = \sqrt{(0 - (-2))^2 + (-0,5 - 1)^2}$$
$$PS = \sqrt{(2)^2 + (-1,5)^2}$$
$$PS = \sqrt{4 + 2,25}$$
$$PS = \sqrt{6,25}$$

The mid-point theorem states that the line joining the mid-points of two sides of a triangle is parallel to the third side and equal to half the length of the third side. To prove this, we must show that $MN \parallel BC$ and $MN = \frac{1}{2}BC$.

We use the gradient formula $m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$ to show the lines have the same gradient:

$$m_{BC} = \frac{-2 - (-2)}{3 - (-5)} = 0$$
$$m_{MN} = \frac{1 - 1}{-4 - 0} = 0$$

Since $m_{BC} = m_{MN}$, it follows that $MN \parallel BC$.

### Analytical Geometry: Properties of Quadrilaterals and Linear Functions

#### 1. Determining if a Quadrilateral is a Rhombus
To determine if a quadrilateral $ABCD$ is a rhombus, one must check if the diagonals intersect at right angles. Two lines are perpendicular if the product of their gradients is $-1$ ($m_1 \times m_2 = -1$).

**Worked Example:**
Given the gradient of diagonal $AC$ ($m_{AC} = -\frac{15}{7}$) and the gradient of diagonal $BD$ ($m_{BD} = \frac{1}{3}$):

$$m_{AC} \times m_{BD} = -\frac{15}{7} \times \frac{1}{3}$$
$$= -\frac{15}{21} \neq -1$$

**Conclusion:** $ABCD$ is not a rhombus because the diagonals do not intersect at right angles.

#### 2. Calculating the Length of a Line Segment
The distance $d$ between two points $(x_1; y_1)$ and $(x_2; y_2)$ is calculated using the distance formula:
$$d = \sqrt{(x_1 - x_2)^2 + (y_1 - y_2)^2}$$

**Worked Example: Find the length of $AB$**
Given points $A(-3; 6)$ and $B(5; 0)$:

$$AB = \sqrt{(-3 - 5)^2 + (6 - 0)^2}$$
$$AB = \sqrt{(-8)^2 + (6)^2}$$
$$AB = \sqrt{64 + 36}$$
$$AB = \sqrt{100}$$
$$AB = 10$$

### Analytical Geometry: Perpendicular Lines, Collinearity, and Mid-points

#### 1. Perpendicular Lines
When two lines are perpendicular, the product of their gradients is $-1$. If line $g(x)$ is perpendicular to $f(x)$, then:
$$m_{g(x)} \times m_{f(x)} = -1$$

**Worked Example: Finding the value of $b$**
Given $g(x)$ passes through $U$ and is perpendicular to $f(x)$, with $V(b; 4)$ lying on $g(x)$:
1. Determine the gradient: $m_{g(x)} = -\frac{2}{3}$
2. Use the general form $g(x) = mx + c$ and substitute point $U$ to find $c$:
   $$5 = -\frac{2}{3}(6) + c$$
   $$5 = -4 + c$$
   $$c = 9$$
3. The equation is $g(x) = -\frac{2}{3}x + 9$. Substitute $V(b; 4)$ to solve for $b$:
   $$4 = -\frac{2}{3}b + 9$$
   $$-5 = -\frac{2}{3}b$$
   $$b = \frac{15}{2} = 7\frac{1}{2}$$

$$c = 8\frac{1}{3}$$

#### c) Proving Parallel Lines ($AB \parallel MN$)
To prove that two lines are parallel, we must show that their gradients are equal ($m_{AB} = m_{MN}$).

**Step 1: Find the coordinates of $A$**
Using the midpoint formula $M = \left( \frac{x_1 + x_2}{2}; \frac{y_1 + y_2}{2} \right)$:
$$M_{OA} = \left( \frac{x_1 + x_2}{2}; \frac{y_1 + y_2}{2} \right)$$
$$(-1, 4) = \left( \frac{x_A + 0}{2}; \frac{y_A + 0}{2} \right)$$

Solving for $x_A$ and $y_A$:
$$-1 = \frac{x_A}{2} \implies x_A = -2$$
$$4 = \frac{y_A}{2} \implies y_A = 8$$
$$\therefore A(x; y) = (-2; 8)$$

**Step 2: Find the coordinates of $B$**
$$M_{OB} = \left( \frac{x_1 + x_2}{2}; \frac{y_1 + y_2}{2} \right)$$
$$(5, 2) = \left( \frac{x_B}{2}; \frac{y_B}{2} \right)$$

Solving for $x_B$ and $y_B$:
$$5 = \frac{x_B}{2} \implies x_B = 10$$
$$2 = \frac{y_B}{2} \implies y_B = 4$$
$$\therefore B(x; y) = (10; 4)$$

**Step 3: Calculate the gradient of $AB$**
Using the gradient formula $m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$:
$$m_{AB} = \frac{8 - 4}{-2 - 10}$$
$$m_{AB} = \frac{4}{-12}$$
$$m_{AB} = -\frac{1}{3}$$

Since $m_{AB} = m_{MN} = -\frac{1}{3}$, it is proven that:
$$\therefore AB \parallel MN$$
