---

#### 2. Content & Cognitive Scope

**A. Patterns, Sequences, and Series**
*   **Core Concepts:** Arithmetic and geometric sequences, Sigma notation ($\sum$), sum of arithmetic series, sum of geometric series, sum to infinity.
*   **Formulas:**
    *   Arithmetic sum: $S_n = \frac{n}{2}[2a + (n-1)d]$ or $S_n = \frac{n}{2}(a + l)$
    *   Geometric sum: $S_n = \frac{a(r^n - 1)}{r - 1}$ ($r \neq 1$)
    *   Sum to infinity: $S_\infty = \frac{a}{1 - r}$ ($-1 < r < 1$)
*   **Skills:** Derivation of formulae (examinable), calculating terms, applying sigma notation, determining convergence conditions.
*   **Cognitive Levels:**
    *   **Knowledge (K):** Identifying sequence types, writing out terms.
    *   **Routine Procedures (R):** Calculating sums, finding specific terms.
    *   **Complex Procedures (C):** Solving for $n$ in sigma notation, working with sum to infinity.

**B. Functions**
*   **Core Concepts:** Inverse functions, one-to-one relations, domain and range restrictions, asymptotes, turning points, intercepts, average gradient, symmetry.
*   **Functions Studied:**
    *   Linear: $y = ax + q$
    *   Quadratic: $y = ax^2$
    *   Exponential: $y = b^x$ ($b > 0, b \neq 1$)
*   **Skills:** Determining inverse functions, sketching graphs, analyzing characteristics (increasing/decreasing intervals), distinguishing between relations and functions.
*   **Cognitive Levels:**
    *   **Knowledge (K):** Identifying domain and range.
    *   **Routine Procedures (R):** Sketching graphs, finding intercepts.
    *   **Complex Procedures (C):** Determining inverse functions, analyzing properties of $f^{-1}(x)$.
    *   **Problem Solving (P):** Proving one-to-one relations, interpreting restricted domains.

---

#### 3. Instructional Clarifications & Cautions

*   **Derivations:** The derivation of sum formulae for arithmetic and geometric series is explicitly examinable.
*   **Notation Caution:** 
    *   The notation $f^{-1}(x)$ is reserved strictly for one-to-one relations.
    *   Many-to-one relations do not have inverse functions in the standard sense.
*   **Reciprocal Confusion:** Do not confuse the inverse function $f^{-1}(x)$ with the reciprocal $\frac{1}{f(x)}$.
    *   Example: For $f(x) = \sqrt{x}$, the reciprocal is $\frac{1}{\sqrt{x}}$, while $f^{-1}(x) = x^2$ for $x \geq 0$.
*   **Sigma Notation Example:** 
    *   Calculate: $\sum_{k=0}^{3} (3k - 1)$
    *   Solve for $n$: $\sum_{i=1}^{n} (3i - 2) < 2000$

#### 2. Content & Cognitive Scope

**Finance, Growth and Decay**
*   **Core Concepts:** Present value annuities, future value annuities, logarithmic application for time periods ($n$), investment/loan decision-making.
*   **Formulas:**
    *   Compound Interest: $A = P(1+i)^n$ or $A = P(1-i)^n$
    *   Future Value Annuity: $F = \frac{x[(1+i)^n - 1]}{i}$
    *   Present Value Annuity: $P = \frac{x[1 - (1+i)^{-n}]}{i}$
    *   Geometric Series: $S_n = \frac{a(r^n - 1)}{r - 1}$ ($r \neq 1$)
*   **Depth of Treatment:** Learners must understand the derivation of formulas and the use of timelines to analyse financial problems. Payment commencement is defined as one period from the present, ending after $n$ periods.

**Trigonometry**
*   **Core Concepts:** Compound angle identities, double angle identities, general solutions, proving trigonometric identities.
*   **Formulas & Identities:**
    *   $\cos(\alpha \pm \beta) = \cos\alpha \cos\beta \mp \sin\alpha \sin\beta$
    *   $\sin(\alpha \pm \beta) = \sin\alpha \cos\beta \pm \cos\alpha \sin\beta$
    *   $\sin 2\alpha = 2\sin\alpha \cos\alpha$
    *   $\cos 2\alpha = \cos^2\alpha - \sin^2\alpha$
    *   $\cos 2\alpha = 2\cos^2\alpha - 1$
    *   $\cos 2\alpha = 1 - 2\sin^2\alpha$
*   **Depth of Treatment:** Mastery of deriving identities and applying them to solve equations (e.g., $\sin 2x + \cos x = 0$) and proving complex identities (e.g., $\frac{1 + \sin 2x}{\cos 2x} = \frac{\cos x + \sin x}{\cos x - \sin x}$).

---

#### 3. Assessment & Clarification Notes

*   **Cognitive Levels:** The curriculum requires progression from knowledge of formulas to complex problem-solving involving financial decision-making and trigonometric proofs.
*   **Clarification on Finance:**
    *   The use of a timeline is a required technique for analysing financial problems.
    *   Example application: Calculating outstanding balances on loans after a specific number of installments.
*   **Clarification on Trigonometry:**
    *   Learners are expected to derive the compound angle identities.
    *   General solutions for trigonometric equations are a core requirement.

#### 2. Content & Cognitive Scope: Investigation Example
The curriculum provides a specific exemplar for the Grade 12 Investigation, focusing on geometric properties and optimization.

**Topic:** Grade 12 Investigation: Polygons with 12 Matches

**Core Concepts & Skills:**
*   **Geometric Properties:** Application of the sine rule, cosine rule, and area rules.
*   **Optimization:** Determining maximum area for a fixed perimeter.
*   **Mathematical Processes:**
    *   Specialising and classifying.
    *   Comparing and inferring.
    *   Estimating and generalising.
    *   Making and validating conjectures.
    *   Proving and communicating mathematical ideas.

**Investigation Tasks:**
1.  Determine how many different triangles can be constructed with a perimeter of $P = 12$ units (where each match represents 1 unit).
2.  Identify which of these triangles yields the greatest area.
3.  Determine what regular polygons can be constructed using all 12 matches.
4.  Investigate the areas of polygons with a perimeter of 12 to establish the maximum area possible.
5.  Formulate extensions or generalisations based on observations, moving beyond trivial results to demonstrate quality of mathematical reasoning.

---

#### 3. Mathematical Formulas & Notation
The investigation requires the application of the following trigonometric principles:

*   **Sine Rule:** 
    $$\frac{a}{\sin A} = \frac{b}{\sin B} = \frac{c}{\sin C}$$
*   **Cosine Rule:** 
    $$a^2 = b^2 + c^2 - 2bc \cos A$$
*   **Area Rule:** 
    $$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}ab \sin C$$

---

#### 4. Pacing & Instructional Guidelines
*   **Pacing:** The investigation is designed to be a substantial task requiring students to move from trivial observations to complex generalisations.
*   **Depth of Treatment:** The focus is not merely on the final answer, but on the *mathematical process*. Teachers are instructed to encourage students to strive for quality in their reasoning and the depth of their generalisations.

### Grade 12 Mathematics: Differential Calculus (Term 2)

#### 1. Annual Teaching Plan (ATP) & Timelines
| No. of Weeks | Topic |
| :--- | :--- |
| 3 | Differential Calculus |

#### 2. Content & Cognitive Scope

**Core Concepts:**
*   **Limit Concept:** Intuitive understanding of limits in the context of approximating the rate of change or gradient of a function at a point.
*   **Definition of Derivative:** Use of limits to define the derivative of a function $f$ at any point $x$:
    $$f'(x) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h}$$
*   **Generalisation:** Defining the derivative function $f'(x)$ for any point $x$ in the domain of $f$.
*   **Geometric Interpretation:** Understanding $f'(a)$ as the gradient of the tangent to the graph of $f$ at the point with $x$-coordinate $a$.
*   **Differentiation Rules:** Application of rules for constants and power functions.

**Required Depth of Treatment:**
*   **First Principles:** Differentiation from first principles will be examined for functions described in sections 3.1, 3.2, and 3.3.
*   **Examples for First Principles:**
    1. $f(x) = x^2 + 2$
    2. $f(x) = \frac{1}{2}x^2 + x - 2$
    3. $f(x) = -x^3$
    4. $f(x) = -\frac{x}{x}$
*   **Differentiation Rules (General):**
    *   $f(x) = ax^2 + bx + c$
    *   $f(x) = ax^3$
    *   $f(x) = \frac{a}{x}$
    *   $f(x) = c$

**Cognitive Levels:**
*   **(R) Routine:** Application of standard differentiation rules.
*   **(C) Complex:** Application of rules to products or quotients requiring algebraic manipulation (e.g., $\frac{d}{dx}((x+1)(x-1))$).
*   **(P) Problem Solving:** Application to more complex expressions (e.g., $f(x) = \frac{(x+2)^3}{\sqrt{x}}$).

#### 3. Instructional Guidelines & Cautions
*   **Sum Rule:** Care should be taken not to apply the sum rule for differentiation in a way that implies the derivative of a product is the product of the derivatives.
    *   *Example:* $\frac{d}{dx}((x+1)(x-1)) \neq \frac{d}{dx}(x+1) \times \frac{d}{dx}(x-1)$
*   **Observation:** Students should be encouraged to write down observations when comparing manual expansion versus rule application.

    $$P(A \text{ and } B) = P(A) \times P(B)$$
*   **Mutually Exclusive Events:** 
    $$P(A \text{ or } B) = P(A) + P(B)$$
*   **General Addition Rule:** 
    $$P(A \text{ or } B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A \text{ and } B)$$
*   **Complementary Rule:** 
    $$P(\text{not } A) = 1 - P(A)$$

### 3. CONTENT & COGNITIVE SCOPE
*   **Depth of Treatment:** The curriculum focuses on the consolidation of Grade 12 content through a 3-week revision period followed by a 6-week formal examination period.
*   **Cognitive Levels:** Assessment is designed to test across the full spectrum of cognitive demand, including:
    *   **Knowledge:** Recall of definitions, formulas, and standard procedures.
    *   **Routine Procedures:** Application of standard algorithms and algebraic manipulation.
    *   **Complex Procedures:** Integration of multiple concepts (e.g., combining Calculus with Functions).
    *   **Problem Solving:** Non-routine applications requiring logical deduction and mathematical modeling.
