## 1. Definition and Explanation: What is Internal Control?

### Concept Definition
*   **Internal:** Refers to activities within the business itself.
*   **Control:** Refers to having full authority over something.

**Internal Control** is the set of actions management takes to exercise authority over all business activities to ensure the business operates efficiently and achieves maximum profit.

### Responsibility
Internal control is not solely the responsibility of managers or owners. Every employee in the business shares the responsibility, and everyone must be involved in implementing successful control measures.

### Processes to Apply Control
To effectively apply internal control, the following steps must be followed:
1.  **Set Objectives:** Decide on both short-term and long-term business objectives.
2.  **Information Gathering:** Gather information regarding the business's strong points and shortcomings.
3.  **Analysis:** Analyse the identified strong points and shortcomings.
4.  **Action:** Take corrective action against any identified shortcomings.

## 2 Basic Internal Control Processes

To maintain effective business operations, internal control must be exercised over various business assets and activities. The primary focus is on **Stock Control**.

### 2.1 Stock Control
Stock control refers to the systems and procedures put in place to manage inventory effectively, prevent losses, and ensure operational efficiency.

#### Key Requirements for Effective Stock Control:

*   **Physical Security:** Storage areas must be protected from natural disasters (e.g., rain, wind) and secured to minimize the risk of burglary.
*   **Insurance:** All stock must be adequately insured against potential loss.
*   **Record Keeping:** 
    *   Stock lists must be kept up-to-date at all times.
    *   All documents related to stock purchases must be completed accurately.
    *   All documents related to stock issued to the sales department must be completed accurately.
*   **Inventory Levels:** Maintain sufficient stock levels—avoiding both overstocking and understocking.
*   **Monitoring and Auditing:** 
    *   Regular stocktaking (physical counts) must be performed.
    *   The sales department must maintain constant communication with the warehouse to ensure stock availability.
*   **Quality and Returns:** The sales department must track returned stock to evaluate packaging quality. If necessary, alternative packaging materials should be implemented.
*   **Order Accuracy:** Ensure that client orders contain the correct items and are packaged correctly.
*   **Seasonal Planning:** The purchasing department must account for seasonal fluctuations (e.g., higher stock requirements during the Christmas season).
*   **Inventory Turnover:** Stock should not be held for too long, as this may lead to losses (e.g., obsolescence or spoilage).
*   **Separation of Duties:** Only one person should be responsible for each specific activity. This prevents "finger-pointing," reduces the risk of theft by staff, and minimizes opportunities for fraud and corruption.

## 2.2 Control over Debtors
To ensure effective management of money owed to the business by customers, the following internal controls must be implemented:

*   **Creditworthiness:** Carefully select debtors by investigating their ability to pay before granting credit.
*   **Record Keeping:** Maintain a complete and accurate record of all transactions.
*   **Individual Accounts:** Every debtor must have an individual account, which must be kept up to date at all times.
*   **Communication:** Send out regular statements of accounts to debtors.
*   **Incentives:** Offer discounts to debtors who settle their accounts regularly.
*   **Penalties:** Levy interest on accounts that remain overdue for a specific period.
*   **Risk Management:** Implement strategies to limit bad debts.

## 2.3 Control over Creditors
Effective control over creditors involves both the selection of suppliers and the verification of incoming goods.

### Supplier Selection Criteria
When deciding on a supplier, the purchasing department must consider:
*   Reliability in providing goods on a regular basis.
*   Availability of trade discounts.
*   The length of the supplier's payment period.
*   Availability of settlement discounts for on-time payments.

### Warehouse Receiving Procedures
All items received from a supplier must be accompanied by the necessary documentation. The warehouse clerk must perform the following checks:
*   **Verification:** Ensure the goods listed on the invoice correspond exactly to the items delivered.
*   **Quality Control:** Inspect all goods for damage.
*   **Supplier Evaluation:** If problems with a specific supplier occur frequently, management must seek an alternative supplier.

## 2.4 Control over Fixed Assets
Fixed assets are items purchased for long-term use within the business. Before any purchase is made, the business must investigate the necessity and purpose of the asset.

**Examples of Fixed Assets:**
*   Land and buildings
*   Vehicles
*   Equipment

## 2.4 Internal Control over Non-Current Assets

### 2.4.1 Land and Buildings
Before starting a business, management must decide whether to buy or rent property based on the nature of the business. Internal controls for land and buildings include:
*   **Location:** Ensure the property is situated in an area where its market value will not depreciate.
*   **Maintenance:** Perform regular maintenance to prevent large, unexpected repair costs in the future.
*   **Insurance:** Ensure the property is adequately insured against natural disasters.

### 2.4.2 Vehicles
A business must conduct an investigation to ensure that any vehicle purchased aligns with the operational needs of the business. Internal controls for vehicles include:
*   **Depreciation:** Apply a depreciation rate and method that accurately reflects the vehicle's useful lifespan.
*   **Maintenance:** Ensure vehicles are serviced and maintained regularly.
*   **Technological Adaptation:** Monitor technological advancements in the transport sector and adapt the fleet accordingly.

### 2.4.3 Equipment
Similar to vehicles, the purpose of equipment must be clearly defined before acquisition. Internal controls for equipment include:
*   **Depreciation:** Determine an appropriate lifespan and depreciation method for the specific equipment.
*   **Technological Updates:** Because technology (e.g., computers) changes rapidly, the business must ensure its equipment remains current and efficient.
*   **Maintenance:** Ensure equipment is maintained to prolong its functional life.

## 2.5 Control over Consumable Goods

Consumable goods include items such as stationery, fuel, and packing material. To manage these effectively, a responsible person must be appointed to oversee the following duties:

1.  **Stocktaking:** Keep an accurate record of all items currently in stock.
2.  **Inventory Levels:** Ensure that sufficient stock levels are maintained to prevent shortages.
3.  **Record Keeping:** Maintain a formal record of all purchases made and all goods issued to departments or employees.

## 2.6 Control over cash
Money is the primary means of exchange in business, taking various forms such as notes, coins, credit cards, and cheques. All participants must be well-informed and understand their responsibilities regarding the handling of money.

---

### 2.6.1 Cash Receipts Journal (CRJ)
Internal control for cash receipts ensures that money received is handled by a responsible person, recorded immediately, and deposited into the bank promptly.

**Required Procedures:**
*   **Documentation:** A source document must be issued for every amount of cash received.
*   **Recording:** All cash received must be recorded in the Cash Receipts Journal.
*   **Security:** Cash must be kept in a safe place until it is deposited into the bank.
*   **Verification:** Monthly bank statements must be reconciled against the books to identify discrepancies and prevent fraud.
*   **Separation of Duties:** Only one person should be responsible for each specific activity. This limits the number of people involved, making it easier to assign accountability and prevent theft, fraud, and corruption.

---

### 2.6.2 Cash Payments Journal (CPJ)
All cash payments (excluding petty cash) must be made by cheque. For safety, cheques usually require two signatures.

**Required Procedures:**
*   **Security:** All issued cheques must be kept in a safe place.
*   **Recording:** All cheques issued must be entered into the Cash Payments Journal in consecutive numerical order.
*   **Authorization:** Blank cheques must never be signed.
*   **Verification:** At the end of the month, the Cash Payments Journal must be compared with the bank statement to ensure accuracy and detect potential fraud.
*   **Separation of Duties:** Similar to cash receipts, separating duties ensures that only one person is responsible for each activity, which helps prevent theft, fraud, and corruption.

## 2.6.3 Petty Cash Journal

Petty cash is used for small, incidental business payments. To ensure these funds are managed securely, a responsible person must be appointed to oversee them.

### Procedures for Petty Cash
To maintain effective internal control, the following procedures must be followed:
*   **Security:** The petty cash must be kept in a locked petty cash box and stored in a safe place.
*   **Recording:** All petty cash payments must be recorded in the Petty Cash Journal.
*   **Authorization:** As far as possible, two people must sign for any petty cash payments.
*   **Documentation:** As far as possible, an external document (proof of purchase) must be attached to the petty cash voucher.
