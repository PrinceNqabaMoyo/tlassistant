## 2. Salaries

### 2.1 Introduction
In business accounting, when a business employs multiple people, it must track payments systematically:
*   **Salary Journal:** Used for employees paid monthly.
*   **Wages Journal:** Used for part-time or weekly-paid employees.

**Definitions:**
*   **Employer:** A person or entity that provides employment to others.
*   **Employee:** A person who works for a business.

### 2.2 Salaries
A salary is typically paid to permanent employees at the end of each month. 

*   **Calculation:** The annual salary is agreed upon by the employer and employee and is divided by $12$ to determine the monthly salary.
*   **Thirteenth Cheque/Bonus:** An additional payment equivalent to one month's salary, determined by the employer (often calculated as a percentage).
*   **Notch/Scale:** Salaried employees are appointed at a specific salary scale.

#### Worked Example: Salary Scale
**Scenario:** R. Smit was employed on 1 January 2005. 
**Salary Scale:** $96\,000 \times 6\,000 - 108\,000 \times 9\,000 - 126\,000$

*   **Interpretation:** 
    *   The starting salary is $96\,000$ per annum.
    *   The employee receives an annual increment of $6\,000$ until they reach $108\,000$.
    *   Once at $108\,000$, the increment increases to $9\,000$ per annum until the maximum of $126\,000$ is reached.

## Understanding Salary Scales
Salary scales represent the progression of an employee's earnings over a period of time. To calculate the monthly salary, the annual salary is divided by 12.

**Example Calculation:**
*   Year 1: $R96\,000 \div 12 = R8\,000$ monthly
*   Year 2: $R96\,000 + R6\,000 = R102\,000 \div 12 = R8\,500$ monthly
*   Year 3: $R102\,000 + R6\,000 = R108\,000 \div 12 = R9\,000$ monthly
*   Year 4: $R108\,000 + R9\,000 = R117\,000 \div 12 = R9\,750$ monthly
*   Year 5: $R117\,000 + R9\,000 = R126\,000 \div 12 = R10\,500$ monthly

## 3. Wages

A wage is a payment made to an employee, typically on a weekly basis. It is calculated based on a fixed hourly rate multiplied by the number of hours worked.

### Key Definitions
*   **Ordinary Time:** The standard working hours per week, which is set at 40 hours.
*   **Overtime:** Any hours worked in excess of the 40-hour ordinary time limit.
*   **Basic Wage:** The payment for ordinary hours worked.
*   **Gross Wage:** The total earnings before any deductions are made.
*   **Net Wage:** The final amount received by the employee after deductions.

### Formulas
$$ \text{Basic wage} + \text{Overtime} = \text{Gross wage} $$
$$ \text{Gross wage} - \text{Deductions} = \text{Net wage} $$

---

### Worked Example
**Scenario:** S. Radebe earns R30 per hour for ordinary time and R45 per hour for overtime. Calculate his gross wage if he works 45 hours in a week.

**Calculation:**
| Description | Calculation | Amount |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Ordinary time | $40 \text{ hours} \times R30$ | $R1\,200$ |
| Overtime | $(45 - 40) = 5 \text{ hours} \times R45$ | $R225$ |
| **Gross wage** | | **$R1\,425$** |

## 4 Deductions
Deductions are amounts subtracted from an employee's gross salary or wage before they receive their net pay. These can be categorized as:
*   **Compulsory deductions:** Required by law or employment contract.
*   **Voluntary deductions:** Requested by the employee (e.g., bond repayments).

---

### 4.1 Pension Fund Contributions
*   **Purpose:** To save money for retirement.
*   **Structure:** Usually compulsory.
*   **Contribution Model:** Both the employee and the employer contribute a percentage of the gross salary to the pension fund on a monthly basis.

---

### 4.2 Income Tax
Income tax is a mandatory contribution paid by citizens to the government.

#### Calculation of Taxable Income
The taxable income is determined by subtracting the pension fund contribution from the gross salary:

$$\text{Taxable income} = \text{Gross salary} - \text{Pension fund contribution}$$

#### Key Administrative Processes
*   **SARS Tables:** The specific tax amount is calculated using tables provided by the South African Revenue Service (SARS), which are updated annually following the Minister of Finance's budget speech.
*   **SARS (PAYE):** The deducted tax is paid to SARS under the Pay-As-You-Earn (PAYE) system.
*   **EMP 201:** A monthly return form that businesses must complete and submit to SARS along with the income tax payment.
*   **IRP 5 Form:** At the end of the financial year (28 February), employees receive an IRP 5 certificate. This document summarizes the employee's annual gross salary and the total amounts deducted for PAYE and SITE.
*   **Income Tax Return:** Employees use the IRP 5 to complete an annual tax return. 
    *   If too much tax was paid, SARS provides a refund.
    *   If too little tax was paid, the taxpayer must pay the outstanding balance to SARS.

## What is SITE?
The **Standard Income Tax on Employees (SITE)** system was introduced on 1 March 1988. 
* **Definition:** A system where income tax is deducted from all income received by employees.
* **Key Characteristic:** SITE is not a separate tax; it is a method of calculation performed at the end of the financial year rather than on a monthly basis.

---

## 4.3 Medical Aid Fund Contributions
In many business environments, membership to a medical aid fund is compulsory for employees.

* **Structure of Contributions:**
    * **Employee Deduction:** A portion is deducted from the employee's salary.
    * **Employer Contribution:** The employer may contribute an additional amount towards the fund.
* **Payment:** Both the employee's deduction and the employer's contribution are paid to the medical aid fund at the end of each month.

---

## Impact of HIV/AIDS on Business
The HIV/AIDS pandemic negatively impacts businesses in South Africa through absenteeism, illness, and death of staff. This affects:
* Productivity and profitability.
* Workplace health and safety.
* Employee morale.

### Financial and Operational Consequences
* **Medical Aid Depletion:** While medical aid funds may pay small amounts for direct AIDS treatment, the weakened immune systems of affected individuals lead to hospitalizations for secondary illnesses (e.g., pneumonia). This results in high costs for medical aid funds.
* **Operational Costs:** 
    * Employees work at a slower pace due to fatigue.
    * Increased absenteeism leads to the depletion of sick leave.
    * Costs rise due to the need to train replacement staff.
    * Overall business productivity and profitability decrease.

### Legal Considerations: Dismissal
* **Non-discrimination:** An employee **cannot** be dismissed simply because they are HIV-positive or have AIDS.
* **Incapacity:** An employer may only dismiss a worker if their health has deteriorated to the point where they are no longer able to perform their duties. In this case, the legal reason for dismissal is **disability due to ill health or injury (incapacity)**.

## Labour Relations and Disability
According to the **Labour Relations Act 66 of 1995 (LRA)**, specifically the *Good Practice Code: Dismissal*, the following procedures must be followed regarding employees with disabilities or serious health conditions:

*   **Investigation:** The extent and seriousness of the disability must be investigated first.
*   **Medical Evidence:** The employer must request a medical certificate from the employee and may request an investigation by an independent doctor to confirm the diagnosis.
*   **HIV Testing:** An employer may suggest an HIV test, but **cannot force** the employee to undergo one. Testing must be voluntary, and results must remain confidential.
*   **Reasonable Accommodation:**
    *   The employer should provide alternative or lighter work where possible.
    *   If the employee is absent for a long period, a temporary worker may be considered.
*   **Dismissal:** An employee may only be discharged if:
    *   A formal hearing has been conducted.
    *   Sufficient time has elapsed.
    *   Counselling has been provided.
    *   Serious attempts have been made to adapt the work to the employee's abilities.
*   **Non-Discrimination:** HIV-positive workers must be allowed to work under normal circumstances as long as they are medically fit. They must not be discriminated against.
*   **Support:** Employers should refer employees to available health, welfare, and psycho-social services.

## 4.4 Unemployment Insurance Fund (UIF)

The Unemployment Insurance Fund is a mandatory contribution system for both employees and employers.

### Key Principles:
1.  **Mandatory Contributions:** Both the employee and the employer must contribute to the UIF.
2.  **Payment to SARS:** The total contributions (employee portion + employer portion) must be paid to the South African Revenue Service (SARS) at the end of every month.
3.  **Documentation:** The business must complete an **EMP 201** form to facilitate the payment of these contributions to SARS.

## 4.5 Skills Development Levy (SDL)

The Skills Development Levy (SDL) is a tax imposed by the government to fund national skills development. The primary objective is to improve the skills of the workforce by encouraging employers to invest in employee training.

### Key Rules for SDL
*   **Registration:** Employers must register with SARS for the levy.
*   **Exemption:** Employers who are not registered for PAYE and whose total annual salary expenses do not exceed $R5\,000\,000$ are not required to pay the levy, though they may still need to register.
*   **Calculation:** Employers are required to pay $1\%$ of their total gross salaries/wages to SARS.

## Key Calculations

### Gross Earnings
*   **Wages:** $(\text{Ordinary Hours} \times \text{Rate}) + (\text{Overtime Hours} \times \text{Overtime Rate})$
*   **Salaries:** Fixed monthly amount.

### Net Pay Calculation
The amount actually received by the employee is calculated as:
$$\text{Net Pay} = \text{Gross Earnings} - \text{Total Deductions}$$

### Employer Contributions
These are costs incurred by the employer over and above the gross salary/wage:
*   **SDL:** Skills Development Levy
*   **UIF:** Unemployment Insurance Fund (Employer portion)
*   **Medical/Pension:** Employer's contribution towards employee benefits.

Internal control procedures are essential to safeguard assets and ensure accurate financial reporting. To control cash received, Maria can implement the following five procedures:
1. **Segregation of duties:** Ensure that the person receiving cash is not the same person recording the transaction in the books.
2. **Issuing receipts:** Always issue a pre-numbered official receipt for every amount of cash received.
3. **Daily banking:** Deposit all cash received into the bank account on the same day or the next business day.
4. **Reconciliation:** Regularly reconcile the cash received against the receipts issued and the bank deposit slips.
5. **Safe storage:** Keep cash in a secure, locked safe until it can be deposited at the bank.

# Term 2 Topic 2: Salaries and Wages

In accounting for salaries and wages, the business must record both the employer's contributions (an expense to the business) and the actual payments made to employees and third parties (such as SARS or medical aid funds).

## Accounting Treatment Table

The following table outlines the double-entry bookkeeping requirements for employer contributions and the subsequent payments of these liabilities.

| Category | Description | Account Debited | Account Credited | Assets | Liabilities | Equity |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Employer contributions** | Pension fund contribution | Pension fund | Pension fund | 0 | - | + |
| | Medical aid fund contribution | Medical aid fund | Medical aid fund | 0 | - | + |
| | UIF contribution | SARS - UIF | SARS - UIF | 0 | - | + |
| | Skills Development Levy | SARS - SDL | SARS - SDL | 0 | - | + |
| **Payments** | Salaries | Creditors for salaries | Bank | - | 0 | - |
| | Pension fund | Pension fund | Bank | - | 0 | - |
| | Medical aid fund | Medical aid fund | Bank | - | 0 | - |
| | SARS - UIF | SARS - UIF | Bank | - | 0 | - |
| | SARS - PAYE | SARS - PAYE | Bank | - | 0 | - |
| | SARS - SDL | SARS - SDL | Bank | - | 0 | - |

### Key Concepts
*   **Employer Contributions:** These are additional costs incurred by the employer over and above the gross salary of the employee. These are recorded as expenses, which decrease equity.
*   **Liabilities:** When contributions are calculated but not yet paid, they are recorded as liabilities (e.g., SARS - UIF, Pension Fund).
*   **Payments:** When the business pays the amounts owed to the employees or third parties, the Bank account is credited (decreasing assets) and the respective liability account is debited (decreasing liabilities).

#### Key Concepts & Calculations

*   **Gross Wages/Salary:** The total amount earned by an employee before any deductions.
*   **Deductions:** Amounts subtracted from the gross pay, including:
    *   **PAYE:** Pay As You Earn (Income Tax).
    *   **UIF:** Unemployment Insurance Fund.
    *   **Pension/Medical:** Contributions toward retirement or health funds.
*   **Net Wages/Salary:** The actual amount paid to the employee:
    $$\text{Net Pay} = \text{Gross Pay} - \text{Total Deductions}$$
*   **Employer Contributions:** Additional costs incurred by the employer for the benefit of the employee (e.g., SDL, UIF, Medical Aid contributions).
*   **Ledger References:** The codes (e.g., B1, N1) refer to the General Ledger accounts where these totals are posted:
    *   **B:** Balance Sheet accounts.
    *   **N:** Nominal (Income/Expense) accounts.

### 4.5 Internal Control Measures for Cash
To ensure the security and integrity of cash handling, the following measures must be implemented:
*   **Division of duties:** Segregate responsibilities to prevent fraud.
*   **Reconciliation:** Receipts and cash slips must be agreed to deposit slips.
*   **Physical security:** Ensure the cash till is physically secure.
*   **Surprise audits:** Conduct regular surprise counts of cash.
*   **Recording:** Cash received must be recorded in the books immediately.
*   **Banking:** Cash must be deposited into the bank daily.
*   **Documentation:** A document must be issued for all cash received.
*   **Safe storage:** Cash must be kept in a safe place until it is deposited.
*   **Audit trail:** Cancelled receipts must be investigated.
*   **Environment:** Cash registers should not be near shop exits.
*   **Policy:** No payments can be made directly from the till.
