# Accounting Curriculum Specifications (CAPS)

## 1. Curriculum Overview
Accounting focuses on measuring performance and processing/communicating financial information about economic sectors. The discipline emphasizes ethical behavior, transparency, and accountability through the logical, systematic, and accurate recording of financial transactions, as well as the compilation, analysis, and interpretation of financial statements.

## 2. Content & Cognitive Scope
The curriculum is structured into three primary pillars: **Financial Accounting**, **Managerial Accounting**, and **Managing Resources**.

## 3. Learning Outcomes
Upon completion of the curriculum, learners will be able to:
*   Record, analyze, and interpret financial and other relevant data to make informed decisions.
*   Present and/or communicate financial information effectively using generally accepted accounting practice in line with current developments and legislation.
*   Develop and demonstrate an understanding of fundamental accounting concepts.
*   Relate skills, knowledge, and values to real-world situations to ensure a balance between theory and practice, facilitating entry into the world of work or higher education.
*   Organize and manage personal finances and activities responsibly and effectively.

## 4. Mathematical Foundations
The core of the accounting equation, which underpins the recording of financial information, is defined as:

$$Assets = Equity + Liabilities$$

Where:
*   **Assets ($A$):** Resources controlled by the entity.
*   **Equity ($E$):** The residual interest in the assets of the entity after deducting all its liabilities.
*   **Liabilities ($L$):** Present obligations of the entity arising from past events.

#### 2. CONTENT & COGNITIVE SCOPE
The curriculum is designed to build from foundational knowledge in Grade 10 to complex analytical and interpretive skills in Grade 12.

*   **Knowledge & Comprehension:** Focuses on definitions, GAAP principles, and basic bookkeeping processes.
*   **Routine Procedures:** Application of calculations for VAT, salaries, depreciation, and basic ledger entries.
*   **Complex Procedures:** Preparation of financial statements, reconciliation statements, and production cost statements.
*   **Problem Solving & Analysis:** Interpretation of financial statements, age-analysis, and valuation of inventory using methods such as:
    *   **Specific Identification:** $Cost = \sum (Units \times Unit Price)$
    *   **First-In-First-Out (FIFO):** Valuation based on the oldest stock items.
    *   **Weighted Average:** $\text{Weighted Average Cost} = \frac{\text{Total Cost of Goods Available}}{\text{Total Units Available}}$

The curriculum follows a scaffolded approach to cognitive development:

*   **Grade 10 (Foundational):** Focuses on the **Knowledge** level, requiring learners to identify and describe basic internal control processes.

    *   Critique existing systems (Internal Control/Audit).
    *   Evaluate the role of professional bodies and the application of the King Code.
    *   Analyze the consequences of non-compliance (disciplinary/punitive measures).

    *   Internal control topics should be integrated into the teaching of financial statements and asset management.
    *   Ethics should be integrated into case studies involving corporate governance and professional conduct.
*   **Assessment Focus:** 
    *   **Grade 11:** Assessment should prioritize the ability to identify ethical dilemmas and describe internal control measures in specific business contexts.
    *   **Grade 12:** Assessment should prioritize the ability to provide recommendations for improving internal controls and explaining the impact of the King Code on organizational accountability.

## 3. Mathematical Formulas (Key Ratios)

The following formulas are required for the analysis and interpretation of financial statements:

*   **Gross Profit on Sales:** 
    $$\frac{Gross\ Profit}{Sales} \times 100$$
*   **Gross Profit on Cost of Sales (Markup %):** 
    $$\frac{Gross\ Profit}{Cost\ of\ Sales} \times 100$$
*   **Net Profit on Sales:** 
    $$\frac{Net\ Profit}{Sales} \times 100$$
*   **Current Ratio:** 
    $$\frac{Current\ Assets}{Current\ Liabilities}$$
*   **Acid Test Ratio:** 
    $$\frac{Current\ Assets - Inventory}{Current\ Liabilities}$$
*   **Solvency Ratio:** 
    $$\frac{Total\ Assets}{Total\ Liabilities}$$
*   **Return on Equity:** 
    $$\frac{Net\ Profit}{Average\ Owner's\ Equity} \times 100$$

# SECTION 4: Assessment Specifications

## 1. Definition and Purpose of Assessment
Assessment is defined as a process that measures individual learners' attainment of knowledge (content, concepts, and skills) in a subject. The data and information obtained are used to:
*   Inform learners about their strengths, weaknesses, and progress.
*   Assist teachers, parents, and stakeholders in making informed decisions regarding the learning process and learner progress.

**Core Principle:** Assessment must be mapped directly against the content and intended objectives of the subject.

---

## 2. Assessment Categories

### A. Informal (Daily) Assessment
*   **Nature:** Planned teaching and learning activities integrated into daily lessons.
*   **Methods:** Question and answer sessions, short written classwork, homework exercises, and open-book tests.
*   **Purpose:** 
    *   To provide immediate feedback to learners to improve learning and teaching.
    *   To scaffold the acquisition of knowledge and skills.
    *   To serve as "stepping stones" to formal assessment tasks.
*   **Administration:** Can be marked by teachers, learners (self-assessment), or peers.
*   **Recording:** Not formally recorded for promotion or certification purposes. Teachers may use simple checklists for tracking progress if desired.

### B. Formal Assessment
*   **Nature:** A systematic evaluation of learner progress within a grade and subject.
*   **Examples:** Projects, oral presentations, demonstrations, performances, tests, examinations, and practical tasks.
*   **Administration:** These tasks constitute the year-long **Formal Programme of Assessment**.
*   **Weighting:** Formal assessment tasks are school-based and weighted according to specific grade-level requirements.

### Recording and Reporting Specifications

#### Recording
Recording is the formal process of documenting learner performance. Teachers must:
*   Document actual raw marks against each assessment task.
*   Utilize a formal record sheet.
*   Use recorded data to verify learner progress and inform future instructional planning.

#### Reporting
Reporting is the process of communicating learner performance to stakeholders (learners, parents, schools, districts, employers, and tertiary institutions). 
*   **Standard:** All reporting for Grades R – 12 must be expressed as a percentage.
*   **Scale:** Performance must be aligned with the official CAPS assessment scale.

### 2. Assessment Policy: Mandatory Projects (FET Band)
In the FET band, a project is a mandatory component of the Programme of Assessment.

#### Instructional Guidelines for Projects
*   **Guidance & Monitoring:** Teachers must provide necessary guidance prior to commencement. Progress must be monitored, and aspects of the project should be completed in class.
*   **Transparency:** All assessment criteria must be discussed with learners before the project begins. The assessment instrument (rubric/checklist) must be provided to learners alongside the instructions.
*   **Nature of Work:** Projects generally involve research, consolidation of information, and the preparation of a written document. In specific subjects (e.g., Computerised Accounting), the focus may shift to the development of technical skills rather than traditional research.
*   **Timeline:** Projects are typically initiated towards the end of Term 1 for submission during Term 2.

#### Technical Specifications for Written Projects
*   **Length:** Between $4$ and $8$ typed pages (or $6$ to $10$ written pages). This excludes the cover page, table of contents, graphs, and pictures.
*   **Formatting:** 
    *   Font size: $12$ pt.
    *   Line spacing: $1.5$.
*   **Structure:**
    *   Must include a cover page.
    *   Must include a table of contents (where applicable).
    *   Text must be divided into logical paragraphs/sections.
    *   References and bibliography must be clearly indicated.
    *   Visuals (pictures, photos, diagrams, graphs) may be included.
    *   The final document must be bound or stapled neatly.

---

### 3. Administrative Requirements
*   **Quarterly Feedback:** Schools are mandated to provide quarterly feedback to parents regarding the Programme of Assessment via a formal reporting tool (e.g., a report card).
*   **Reporting:** The report card must clearly indicate the learner's overall level of performance based on the rating codes provided in the table above.

### Assessment Task Specifications: Presentation, Case Study, and Report

This document outlines the instructional and assessment requirements for three core task types within the CAPS framework.

#### 1. Presentation
Presentations may be conducted in written or oral formats, provided that tangible evidence of the presentation is maintained.

**Guidelines for Setting Presentations:**
*   **Structure:** Must include a cover page, appropriate headings, and a table of contents (where applicable).
*   **Formatting:** Text must be organized into logical paragraphs or sections.
*   **Visuals:** Inclusion of pictures, photos, diagrams, and graphs is encouraged.
*   **Technology:** Use of electronic presentation tools is encouraged where resources permit.
*   **Presentation:** Evidence must be bound or stapled appropriately.
*   **Transparency:** Assessment instruments (rubrics/checklists) and instructions must be provided to learners prior to the commencement of the task.

#### 2. Case Study
Case studies utilize real-life situations, problems, or incidents to allow learners to assume specific roles and apply prior knowledge to interpret, analyze, and solve problems.

**Guidelines for Setting Case Studies:**
*   **Source Material:** Utilize newspaper articles, magazine articles, TV, or radio presentations to ensure relevance and currency.
*   **Cognitive Scope:** Questions must range from lower-order (direct quotes/retrieval) to higher-order (analysis of comments and synthesis of suggestions).
*   **Structure:** Must include a cover page, appropriate headings, and a table of contents (where applicable).
*   **Formatting:** Text must be organized into logical paragraphs or sections.
*   **Visuals:** Inclusion of pictures, photos, diagrams, and graphs is encouraged.
*   **Presentation:** Evidence must be bound or stapled appropriately.
*   **Transparency:** Assessment instruments and instructions must be provided to learners alongside the task.

#### 3. Report
A report serves as written evidence of a survey, analysis, or investigation. It is typically shorter than a project and focuses on specific problem-solving scenarios (e.g., advising a business on liquidity problems).

**Guidelines for Setting Reports:**
*   **Focus:** Strong emphasis on problem-solving and expert consultation.
*   **Structure:** Must include a cover page, appropriate headings, and a table of contents (where applicable).

### Assessment Specifications: Grades 10–12

#### 1. Formal Assessment: Tests
Two formal tests are prescribed per annum, to be written under controlled conditions in **Term 1** and **Term 3**.

**Requirements:**
*   **Standardization:** Must be completed by all classes in the same grade on the same day.
*   **Environment:** Must be completed under formal examination conditions.
*   **Alignment:** Questions must comply with year-end examination standards and cover a range of integrated topics as per the work schedule.
*   **Collaboration:** Where multiple teachers are involved, agreement must be reached on scope, date, and time.
*   **Duration & Weighting:** Minimum duration of one hour for a total of 100 marks.
*   **Cognitive Scope:** Must include a range of cognitive levels and "problem-solving" questions.

---

#### 2. Examination Construction Guidelines

Examinations must adhere to the following content and cognitive distribution frameworks:

**Content Weighting**
| Content Source | Weighting |
| :--- | :--- |
| Content stipulated specifically for the grade | 80% |
| Content stipulated in previous grades (impacts subsequent assessment) | 20% |

**Cognitive Levels (Bloom’s Revised Taxonomy)**
| Cognitive Level Category | Description | Weighting |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Basic thinking skills** | Factual recall, low-level comprehension, low-level application | 30% |
| **Moderately high thinking skills** | Advanced application, interpretation, low-level analysis | 40% |
| **Higher-order thinking skills** | Advanced analytical skills, evaluation, creative problem-solving | 30% |

---

#### 3. Problem Solving Requirements
*   **Allocation:** Approximately 10% of all examinations must address problem-solving questions.
*   **Focus:** Requires critical and creative thinking applied to real-life scenarios within the accounting curriculum.
*   **Integration:** These questions must incorporate a mix of cognitive skills (lower, middle, and higher order) to ensure accessibility for all learners.

---

#### 4. Project/Task Presentation Standards
For formal tasks (projects), the following presentation requirements apply:
*   **Structure:** Text must be divided into paragraphs/sections.
*   **Visuals:** Inclusion of pictures, photos, diagrams, and graphs is permitted.
*   **Submission:** Evidence must be bound or stapled in an acceptable manner.
*   **Transparency:** The assessment instrument (rubric/marking grid) must be provided to learners alongside the task instructions.

### 1. Annual Teaching Plan (ATP) & Timelines
The curriculum pacing is governed by the work schedule, which dictates the proportional allocation of time to specific topics. 

*   **Mid-year Examinations:** Conducted as a single paper (Paper 1) with time and mark allocations varying by grade.
*   **Trial Examination (Grade 12 only):** A comprehensive assessment covering the full curriculum scope, modeling the final examination structure.
*   **End-of-year Examinations:** A standardized 3-hour, 300-mark examination for Grades 10–12.

### 2. Content & Cognitive Scope
The curriculum emphasizes the integration of problem-solving skills across all topics. 

**Guidelines for 'Deep' Problem-Solving:**
*   **Identification:** Learners must identify accounting problems from provided information.
*   **Evidence:** Learners must quote relevant data to support their opinions.
*   **Resolution:** Learners must provide valid and appropriate solutions.

**Cognitive Levels:**
*   **Recall/Comprehension:** Focuses on familiar information.
*   **Deep Problem-Solving:** Focuses on creative solutions in new or unfamiliar scenarios within the Accounting curriculum.
