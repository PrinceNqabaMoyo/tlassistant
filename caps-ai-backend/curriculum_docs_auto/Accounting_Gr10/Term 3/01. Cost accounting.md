# Term 3 Topic 3: Cost Accounting

## 1. Introduction to Concepts

### 1.1 Introduction
To understand cost accounting, it is essential to distinguish between different types of business enterprises:

*   **Trading Enterprise:** An enterprise that buys finished products and sells them at a profit. They deal with "purchased and sold trading stock."
*   **Service Enterprise:** An enterprise where individuals use their skills to provide a service to the community.
*   **Manufacturing Enterprise:** An enterprise that produces finished goods by converting raw materials into completed products.

### 1.2 Cost Concepts
The following table defines the fundamental terminology used in cost accounting:

| CONCEPT | EXPLANATION |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Cost** | The monetary value required to manufacture a product or render a service. |
| **Fixed costs** | Costs that remain constant regardless of the quantity of products produced. |
| **Variable costs** | Costs that fluctuate in direct proportion to the quantity of products produced. |
| **Direct costs** | Costs that can be traced directly to a specific product, production process, or section. |
| **Indirect costs** | Costs that cannot be directly linked to a specific product; these are often referred to as overheads. |
| **Labour costs** | The total amount paid to workers involved in the manufacturing process, including worker contributions. |
| **Direct labour costs** | Remuneration paid to employees directly involved in the manufacturing of a product. |
| **Indirect labour costs** | Remuneration paid to employees not directly involved in the manufacturing of a product. |

## Key Concepts and Definitions

| Concept | Definition |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Direct material costs** | Costs of all raw materials and parts used in the production process that form part of the final product. |
| **Indirect material costs** | Material and parts used in small quantities that form part of indirect costs. It is not possible to determine the exact amount used for individual products. |
| **Work-in-process** | Incomplete products. As units are manufactured, there will always be units at various stages of completion (e.g., 1% - 99%) at the end of a period. |
| **Completed products** | Goods that are 100% complete and ready to be sold. |
| **Cost of sales** | The cost associated with completed units that have been sold. |

## Cost Formulas

The following mathematical relationships are used to calculate production costs:

*   **Total costs:**
    $$\text{Total costs} = \text{Fixed costs} + \text{Variable costs}$$

*   **Primary costs:**
    $$\text{Primary costs} = \text{Direct material} + \text{Direct labour}$$

*   **Manufacturing costs:**
    $$\text{Manufacturing costs} = \text{Primary costs} + \text{Overheads}$$

## Questions

### 2.1 What is Thembi’s Total direct material cost per item?
To calculate the total direct material cost, sum the costs of the specific materials used per bag:
*   **Material:** $0,75\text{m} \times R24 = R18,00$
*   **Cotton:** $(\frac{50\text{m}}{1000\text{m}}) \times R10 = R0,50$
*   **Webbing:** $0,75\text{m} \times R20 = R15,00$
*   **Beads:** $(\frac{48}{12}) \times R7 = R28,00$

**Total Direct Material Cost:** $R18,00 + R0,50 + R15,00 + R28,00 = R61,50$

### 2.2 What is Thembi’s Total direct labour cost per item?
*   **Labour:** $2,5\text{ hours} \times R15/\text{hour} = R37,50$

**Total Direct Labour Cost:** $R37,50$

### 2.3 What is Thembi’s Total primary cost per item?
Primary cost (or Prime cost) is the sum of direct material costs and direct labour costs.
*   **Formula:** $\text{Primary Cost} = \text{Direct Material} + \text{Direct Labour}$
*   **Calculation:** $R61,50 + R37,50 = R99,00$

**Total Primary Cost:** $R99,00$

### 2.4 What is the total overhead cost involved in making the bags?
Overhead costs are the indirect costs associated with the manufacturing process (fixed monthly costs).
*   **Rent:** $R1\,500$
*   **Cleaning material:** $R50$
*   **Electricity:** $R200$

**Total Overhead Cost:** $R1\,500 + R50 + R200 = R1\,750$

## 2.5 Fixed Costs vs. Variable Costs
*   **Fixed Costs:** These are costs that remain constant regardless of the level of production or sales volume. Examples include rent, insurance, and salaries of permanent administrative staff.
*   **Variable Costs:** These are costs that change in direct proportion to the level of production. As more units are produced, the total variable cost increases. Examples include raw materials and direct labour.

## 2.6 Calculation of Total Profit
To calculate the total profit for 100 bags, use the following formula:

$$\text{Total Profit} = (\text{Selling Price} \times \text{Quantity}) - (\text{Total Fixed Costs} + \text{Total Variable Costs})$$

Given:
*   Quantity = $100$
*   Selling Price per bag = $R150$

$$\text{Total Revenue} = 100 \times R150 = R15\,000$$

*Note: To find the final profit, subtract the specific total costs (fixed and variable) associated with the production of the 100 bags from the Total Revenue of $R15\,000$.*

## 1. Concepts and Definitions

In manufacturing, costs are categorized based on their relationship to the production process. Understanding these categories is essential for accurate product costing.

### Cost Classifications
*   **Direct Material Costs:** Raw materials that become a physical part of the finished product and can be easily traced to a specific unit (e.g., denim fabric).
*   **Direct Labour Costs:** Wages paid to employees who are directly involved in the physical manufacturing of the product (e.g., machinists).
*   **Indirect Material Costs:** Materials used in the factory that are necessary for production but are not part of the final product or are too insignificant to trace (e.g., cotton for sewing machines).
*   **Indirect Labour Costs:** Wages or salaries of factory staff who support production but do not work directly on the product (e.g., factory cleaners or quality checkers).
*   **Fixed Costs:** Expenses that remain constant regardless of the number of units produced (e.g., rent, insurance).
*   **Variable Costs:** Expenses that change in direct proportion to the level of production output (e.g., raw materials).

## 3. Conceptual Understanding

**Task:** Explain the difference between Fixed costs and Variable costs in manufacturing.

### Fixed Cost
These are costs that do not change when the volume of production increases or decreases. Even if the factory produces zero units, these costs must still be paid.
*   *Example:* Factory rent or insurance premiums.

### Variable Cost
These are costs that fluctuate directly with the level of production. If you produce more units, your total variable costs will increase; if you produce fewer units, these costs will decrease.
*   *Example:* The cost of raw materials like denim fabric or zips.
