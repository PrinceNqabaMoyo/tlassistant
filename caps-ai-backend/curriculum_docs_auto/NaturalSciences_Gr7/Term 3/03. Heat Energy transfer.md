## 3.1 Heating as a transfer of energy

In the last chapter we looked at thermal systems. The thermal energy of an object is the amount of energy it has inside of it, in other words, its internal energy. In a thermal system, thermal energy is transferred from one object to another. Heat is the transfer of thermal energy from a system to its surroundings or from one object to another. This transfer of energy is from the object at a higher temperature to the object at a lower temperature.

It is very important to know that, in science, heat and temperature are not the same thing.

* **Heat** is the transfer of thermal energy from a system to its surroundings or from one object to another as a result of a difference in temperature. Heat is measured in joules ($J$). This is because heat is a transfer of energy.
* **Temperature** is a measure of how hot or cold a substance feels and it is measured in degrees Celsius ($^{\circ}\text{C}$). Temperature is a measure of the average kinetic energy of the particles in an object or system. We use a thermometer to measure the temperature of an object or substance.

Heat is the transfer of energy. During energy transfer, the energy moves from the hotter object to the colder object. This means that the hotter object will cool down and the colder object will warm up. The energy transfer will continue until both objects are at the same temperature.

# Methods of Thermal Energy Transfer

There are $3$ main ways in which thermal energy can be transferred from one object or substance to another, or from a system to its surroundings:

1. **Conduction**
2. **Convection**
3. **Radiation**

---

## 3.2 Conduction

### Introduction
Have you noticed that when you put a cold, metal teaspoon into a hot cup of tea, the teaspoon handle also warms up after a while? Have you ever wondered how this warmth "moved" from the hot tea to the cold teaspoon and warmed it up? This is one way in which energy is transferred, and it is called **conduction**.

---

### New Words
* **Conduction:** The process by which thermal energy is transferred through a substance via particle collisions.
* **Conductor:** A material that allows thermal energy (or electricity) to flow through it easily.
* **Insulator:** A material that does not allow thermal energy to flow through it easily.

---

### How Conduction Works

When energy is transferred to an object, the energy of its particles increases. 

* This means the particles gain **kinetic energy** and start to move and vibrate faster.
* As these particles move faster, they "bump" into neighbouring particles.
* Through these collisions, they transfer some of their kinetic energy to those neighbouring particles.
* In this way, energy is transferred step-by-step through the substance from one end to the other.

This entire process is called **conduction**. The particles conduct the energy through the substance.

Conduction is the transfer of thermal energy between objects that are touching. In the teaspoon example, the particles of the tea are touching the particles of the metal spoon, which in turn are touching each other, and this is how heat is conducted from one object to the other.

Do all materials conduct heat in the same way? Let's find out.

***

# Revision Notes: Thermal Conductivity

## 1. Core Concepts

### Thermal Conductivity
* **Definition:** **Thermal conductivity** is the rate at which heat is conducted from one object to another.
* **How we perceive temperature:** When we touch an object, we do not actually feel its absolute temperature. Instead, we feel the **rate at which heat is either conducted away from or towards our hand**.

---

## 2. Key Explanations

### Why materials feel colder or warmer
* **Metal vs. Plastic:** 
  * Metals are better thermal conductors than plastics.
  * When touching a metal block, it conducts heat away from your hand rapidly, making it feel **colder**.
  * When touching a plastic block, it conducts heat slower, making it feel **warmer** (even if both are at the exact same room temperature).
* **Melting Ice Cubes:** 
  * An aluminium or metal block melts an ice cube faster because it conducts heat to the ice cube at a higher rate.
  * A plastic block is a worse thermal conductor, so less heat is transferred to the ice cube, causing it to melt more slowly.

# Thermal Conductivity and Heat Transfer

## Core Concepts

* **Thermal Conductivity:** The rate at which thermal energy (heat) is conducted through a substance. Different materials conduct heat at different rates. For example, a metal tin will conduct heat much faster to your hand than a cake, even if both are at the same temperature.
* **Conductors:** Substances that allow thermal energy to be conducted through them easily (e.g., metals).
* **Insulators:** Substances that do not allow thermal energy to be conducted through them easily.

## 3.3 Convection

Think of a pot of water on a stove. Only the bottom of the pot touches the stove plate, but all of the water inside the pot, even the water not touching the sides, becomes warmer. How does the energy transfer throughout the water in the pot? The transfer of energy is because of convection.

Let's do an activity that will help us to visualise how convection occurs.

**NEW WORDS**
* convection
* convection current

### ACTIVITY: Convection in water

**MATERIALS:**
* 200 ml glass beaker
* potassium permanganate
* Bunsen or spirit burner, tripod stand, wire gauze

**INSTRUCTIONS:**
1. Half fill the beaker with cold tap water.
2. Carefully put a small amount of potassium permanganate on one side of the beaker. DO NOT STIR.
3. Heat the water directly under the side of the beaker with potassium permanganate with a Bunsen/spirit burner and observe what happens.

Let's now explain what we observed in the last activity. **Convection** is the transfer of thermal energy from one place to another by the movement of gas or liquid particles. How does this happen?

As a gas or liquid is heated, the substance expands. This is because the particles in liquids and gases gain kinetic energy when they are heated and start to move faster. They therefore take up more space as the particles move further apart. This causes the heated liquid or gas to move upwards and the colder liquid or gas moves downwards. When the warm liquid or gas reaches the top it cools down again and therefore moves back down again.

In the last activity, the water particles gained kinetic energy and moved apart from each other, therefore taking up more space. This water then moves upwards as it is less dense than the cold water, meaning it is lighter than the cold water. We were able to observe this as the potassium permanganate dissolved in the water and moved with the water particles, and then moved downwards again as the water cooled.

This movement of liquid or gas, is called a **convection current**, and energy is transferred from one area in the liquid or gas to another. Have a look at the diagram which shows a convection current.

## Review of Convection Currents

* Convection currents can only form in **gases** and **liquids**.
* In gases and liquids, particles are free to move around and are not held in fixed positions.
* In solids, particles are held together too tightly to move when heated; solid particles will only vibrate faster when heated but will not move from their positions.

# Lesson Revision Notes: Heat Energy Transfer - Radiation

## 3.4 Radiation

We have previously explored how energy is transferred through different materials via **conduction** (in solids) and **convection** (in liquids and gases). However, thermal energy can also travel through empty space where no particles exist. 

### Core Concepts

* **Radiation:** The transfer of thermal energy through space without requiring objects to touch each other or the movement of particles. 
* **Etymology Note:** Radiation comes from the Greek word *radius*, meaning a beam of light.
* **Key Vocabulary:**
  * **Radiation:** Energy transfer via electromagnetic waves.
  * **Matt:** A dull, non-shiny surface texture.
  * **Reflect:** To bounce off a surface.
  * **Absorb:** To take in energy.

# SUMMARY: Heat: Energy Transfer

## Key Concepts

* **Heat:** Energy that is transferred from a hotter to a cooler object.
* **Temperature:** A measure of how hot or cold a substance feels.
* **Heat Transfer:** Occurs in three ways: conduction, convection, and radiation.
* **Conduction:** 
  * The objects must be touching each other for energy transfer to take place.
  * **Conductors:** Some materials, such as metals, conduct heat well.
  * **Insulators:** Some materials, such as plastics and wood, slow down or prevent conduction.
* **Convection:** 
  * The transfer of energy within liquids or gases.
  * **Convection Current:** Refers to the movement of a liquid or gas during energy transfer. The liquid or gas moves upwards from the heat source (as it expands) and then downwards when the liquid or gas cools (as it contracts).
* **Radiation:** 
  * The transfer of energy where objects do not have to be physically touching. 
  * Radiation does not require a medium and can take place through empty space.
  * The Sun's energy is transferred to Earth by radiation.
  * **Dark, matt surfaces:** Good absorbers of radiant heat.
  * **Light and shiny surfaces:** Poor absorbers of radiant heat. Light, shiny surfaces reflect more radiant heat than they absorb.

## Discussion: Why Do Carpets Feel Warmer Than Tiles in Winter?

* **Initial Observation:** In winter, a carpet feels warmer to stand on than tiles.
* **Common Misconception:** People often think the tiles are at a lower temperature than the carpet because they feel colder to the touch.
* **Scientific Reality:** Both the tiles and the carpet are in the same room environment for a long time, meaning **they are at the exact same temperature**.
* **Explanation:** Tiles are better conductors of thermal energy than carpets. Therefore, tiles conduct heat away from your feet at a *faster rate* than the carpet, making the tiles feel colder even though both surfaces share the same temperature.

   * *Role:* This is the **dependent variable**.

   * Distance of the drawing pin from the heat source must remain constant.

---

* A spirit burner can be used instead of a Bunsen burner.

* Paper clips can be used as an alternative to drawing pins.
* The specific metals used are not strictly prescribed, as long as different metals of the **same length** are compared.

---

## Important Scientific Notes
* **Insulation:** The cardboard acts as an insulator to stop heat from the rods transferring directly to the tripod stand itself. Preventing heat loss from the rods ensures accurate experimental results.
* **Mechanism of Heat Transfer:** Heat is transferred by **conduction** through the metal rod to the Vaseline, causing an increase in its temperature and a subsequent change of state (from solid to liquid).
* **Fair Testing:** Keeping variables constant ensures a fair test so that drawing pins are not stuck on with varying amounts of adhesive, which would lead to inaccurate results.

---

1. **Axis Allocation:**
   * The **horizontal axis (x-axis)** represents the type of material (Independent Variable).

2. **Graph Type:** 
   * Since the independent variable (type of material) is non-numerical (categorical) and non-continuous data, a **bar graph** must be used instead of a line graph.
3. **Graph Requirements:**
   * Both axes must be explicitly labelled with units of measurement.
   * The graph must have an appropriate descriptive heading.

### INVESTIGATION: Which surfaces absorb the most radiation? (LB page 215)

#### Aim
To investigate the way in which different materials and surfaces absorb or reflect thermal radiation.

#### Important Controlled Conditions
* The surface area of each material must be kept constant to ensure reliable and valid results.
* The investigation is best conducted on a hot, sunny day in the sunniest location available.

#### Underlying Principle
When a paper envelope absorbs heat, the energy is transferred to the air trapped inside the envelopes, causing a rise in temperature that is recorded by a thermometer. 
* More energy absorbed $\rightarrow$ more energy transferred to the interior $\rightarrow$ higher temperature recorded.
* More energy reflected $\rightarrow$ smaller increase in temperature recorded.

#### Analysis Questions & Answers

1. **What is being measured in this investigation?**  
   The temperature of the substance (or air inside the envelope).
2. **What type of variable is the measured temperature?**  
   The dependent variable.
3. **What is being changed intentionally in this investigation?**  
   The type of material (or color/surface of the envelope).
4. **What type of variable is the type of material?**  
   The independent variable.
5. **What factors must be kept constant (fixed variables) to ensure a fair test?**  
   * The surface area of each substance exposed to the Sun (i.e., identical envelope sizes).
   * The length of time that the materials are exposed to the Sun.

# Investigation Guide: Absorption and Reflection of Radiant Heat

## 1. Hypothesis Formulation
* **Sample Hypothesis:** "The shiny surface will absorb the least heat, and the black/dark-coloured paper will absorb the most."
* **Extensions:** The investigation can be extended by testing additional colours, such as red and yellow, to compare their thermal absorption rates.

## 2. Experimental Variables & Considerations
* **Dependent Variables:** The results depend on the size of the paper envelope constructed by the learners, as well as the intensity of sunlight falling on the envelopes.
* **Environmental Factors:** Readings may fluctuate over time due to cloud cover.

## 3. Data Representation (Graphing Guidelines)
* **Axes Allocation:** 
  * **Horizontal Axis ($x$-axis):** Time
  * **Vertical Axis ($y$-axis):** Temperature
* **Data Series:** Draw three distinct graphs corresponding to the three different materials tested.
* **Graph Title:** The graph must include an appropriate descriptive title, such as: *"A comparison of the rate of temperature increase of different surfaces."*

### Interpreting Graph Slopes and Trends
* **Steepest Slope:** The black paper increases in temperature the fastest, resulting in the steepest curve. This occurs because the black, matte colour absorbs the most radiation.
* **Intermediate Slope:** The white paper displays moderate temperature change.
* **Shallowest Slope:** The aluminium envelope increases in temperature the slowest and has the shallowest curve, as shiny surfaces reflect heat.

## 4. Analysis and Activity Questions
1. **Trend Observation:** Values obtained depend on envelope size and sunlight exposure. Learners should observe a distinct increasing trend in all graph lines.
2. **Material Performance:** The graph representing the black paper should be the steepest, indicating that the envelope increased in temperature the fastest due to maximum radiation absorption.
3. **Reflective Surfaces:** The envelope made out of aluminium foil should show the smallest increase in temperature because shiny surfaces reflect heat rather than absorbing it.

## 5. Evaluation and Conclusion
* **Method Discussion:** Learners should evaluate the quality of their method and whether their results met expectations. Suggestions for improvement may include repeating the experiment three times to calculate an average increase over time.
* **Dealing with Anomalies:** Some learners may encounter outliers while others record clear patterns.
* **Scientific Conclusion:** Black surfaces absorb the most radiation and exhibit the biggest and fastest increase in temperature, whereas shiny surfaces absorb the least because they reflect the most.

## 6. Practical Application
* **Car Colour Selection:** The best colour to buy for keeping a car cool is **white**. Light colours absorb less heat than dark colours, allowing a light-coloured car to remain cooler on the inside.
* **Reflective Windshields:** Using a reflective windshield sunshade helps keep the interior cooler because shiny surfaces are more reflective, ensuring radiant heat is reflected rather than absorbed.

## Thermal Energy Transfer and Insulation in Everyday Life

7. **Ceramic Cups vs. Styrofoam:**
   * **Normal ceramic cups** are good conductors of heat. Therefore, energy from the hot coffee is transferred quickly through the cup to the surrounding environment.
   * **Styrofoam** is a poor conductor of heat (an insulator). It does not allow energy from the coffee to move quickly to the surrounding air, helping the coffee stay warmer for a longer period.

8. **Insulation Using Blankets:**
   * When air is trapped between two blankets, this trapped air acts as a very poor conductor of heat (insulator). 
   * This forms an extra insulating layer that slows down the loss of thermal energy from your body. 
   * *Note:* Using only one blanket cannot trap as much air, resulting in less insulation and warmth compared to using two blankets.

9. **Animal Adaptations (Birds):**
   * Birds fluff up their feathers to trap more air between them. 
   * Since air is a poor conductor of heat, this trapped air prevents thermal energy from the bird's body from being transferred to the colder surroundings.

10. **Air Circulation and Air Conditioning:**
    * Cold air is denser and moves downwards, which cools the room. 
    * Conversely, hot air rises and can be efficiently removed by an air conditioner placed near the ceiling at the top of the room.

11. **Painting Houses for Temperature Control:**
    * **Dark-coloured paint** is the best choice to keep the inside of a house as warm as possible during winter.
    * **Reason:** Dark colours absorb more radiant heat from the Sun during the day, whereas light colours reflect heat. The dark paint absorbs the thermal energy and transfers it to the air inside the house, keeping the interior warmer.
