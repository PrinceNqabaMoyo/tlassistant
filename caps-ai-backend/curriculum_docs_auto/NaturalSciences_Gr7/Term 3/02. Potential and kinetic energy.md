# 2 Potential and kinetic energy

## KEY QUESTIONS:
* What is potential energy?
* What is kinetic energy?
* Where do we get energy from?
* How much energy do I need?
* Can energy be created or destroyed?
* What is a system?

Renewable and non-renewable sources are where we get our energy **from** but what **types** of energy do we find in the world?

All energy can be placed into two main groups:
1. Potential energy
2. Kinetic energy

So what are these different types of energy and what does it mean if an object has potential energy or kinetic energy? Let's investigate!

---

## 2.1 Potential energy

Throughout our investigation and learning about the concepts surrounding energy, we will be talking about systems and how energy is transferred within a system. A system is a set of parts that work together as a whole. A change in one part of the system will affect the other parts. This will become more clear as we see some examples throughout this term.

We are going to find the difference between potential and kinetic energy. Look at the following diagram which shows a ramp with a marble rolling down into a foam cup. The marble will knock the cup and make it move.

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

*A marble rolling down a ramp.*

When the marble is released, it rolls down the ramp and transfers some of its energy to the cup. This transfer of energy is what makes the cup move. But where did the marble get energy from? Do you think you can make the cup move more or less depending on how far up the ramp you start the marble? Let's do an investigation to find out.

# Chapter 2. Potential and Kinetic Energy

## Energy in a Stretched Elastic Band

When an elastic band is stretched, it gains **potential energy**. This happens because work must be done by your hands to stretch it. The elastic band can then snap back and move objects, such as a matchbox, by transferring its energy.

* **Relationship:** The further you stretch the elastic band, the more work you do, and the more potential energy it stores. Consequently, it can push the matchbox further.
* **Energy Transfer within the System:** Energy moves from your hand $\rightarrow$ to the elastic band $\rightarrow$ to the matchbox $\rightarrow$ to the air and the table surface (which ends up slightly warmer as energy disperses and becomes unavailable for further work).



![Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr7_Energy-transfer-to-surroundings.pdf-0007-00.png](images/Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr7_Energy-transfer-to-surroundings.pdf-0007-00.png)

*A stretched elastic band has potential energy.*

---

## What is Potential Energy?

Objects can gain potential energy in different ways, such as **lifting** (raising an object against gravity) or **stretching** (deforming an elastic object). 

> **Definition:** **Potential energy** is energy that is **stored within a system**. It gives an object the potential or the ability to change something or make **something move**.

---

## Other Examples of Potential Energy

### 1. Fossil Fuels
Fossil fuels such as coal and oil contain potential energy. For example, coal is burned in power stations to generate electricity. The chemical energy stored inside the coal is a form of potential energy.

### 2. Batteries
Batteries used in electric circuits act as a source of stored energy. They store energy chemically, meaning **batteries have potential energy**.

## 2.2 Kinetic energy

Think back to the last activity where we used elastic bands to move matchboxes. The stretched elastic band had potential energy. When the elastic band was released, it moved and snapped back and then hit the matchbox and caused it to move. So what do we call this energy that the moving elastic band and moving matchbox have? We call it **kinetic energy**.

**Kinetic energy is the energy that an object or system has because it is moving.**

## What have we learnt so far?

* **Potential energy** is the energy that an object has because of its position in a system. In the brick activity, the brick had potential energy when it was lifted away from the surface of the Earth. The brick and the Earth attract each other so they are a system. The higher you lift the brick, the more potential energy you give it.
* We know that moving objects also have energy, we call the energy of moving objects **kinetic energy**.

But, we have also seen something else. Think again of the marble activity:

* The marble at the top of the ramp has potential energy.
* When the marble was released, it rolled down the ramp and knocked the cup causing it to move.
* The marble therefore **transferred** energy to the cup.

We also saw this in the match box activity:

* The stretched elastic band had potential energy.
* When the elastic band was released, it moved and snapped back and then hit the matchbox and caused it to move. This means that the match box now has energy.
* Energy was therefore transferred from the stretched elastic band to the matchbox.

So, the potential energy in the elastic band is not lost. It is transferred to the matchbox. This brings us to our next section.

## 2.3 Law of conservation of energy

The **Law of Conservation of Energy** states that energy cannot be created or destroyed, it can only be transferred from one part of the system to other parts. This means that we keep recycling all the energy in the universe all the time!

Why are we talking about laws in science? Did you think laws were just for lawyers? Well, you would be wrong. In science we talk about **laws** and **theories**.

Scientific laws predict **what** will happen in a particular situation. The law has been tested repeatedly (often) and the results do not change. A law does not explain **why** something happens, it just says what **should** happen. Theories explain **how** or **why** things happen. Theories are also tested over and over again to make sure that they are valid.

## 2.4 Potential and kinetic energy in systems

Remember, energy cannot be created or destroyed. It is transferred from one part of the system to other parts. When it is transferred it can be stored or used to make something move and so potential energy can be transferred to kinetic energy in a system.

We can look at how energy is transferred within different systems to show that energy is conserved. There are many different types of systems that we can look at to see how energy is transferred through the systems.

### Mechanical systems

A **mechanical system** is one which is based on mechanical principles and the different parts interact in a mechanism. A mechanical system usually involves movement of some kind. It is often a group of simple machines working together.

Do you remember the elastic bands pushing the matchboxes? Do you think that was a system? You are right. It is a mechanical system. The hand, elastic band and matchbox all form part of a mechanical system. Your hand transfers potential energy to the elastic band, this is the input energy. The potential energy of the elastic band was transferred to the matchbox as kinetic energy. No energy was created or destroyed. We experienced the Law of Conservation of Energy without even realising it.

Another simple example is a pulley and rope system, such as at a construction site where the builders want to lift heavy objects up to a higher floor. The construction worker will pull on the rope which goes up over a pulley and to lift the heavy object higher.

## Thermal systems

Did you know that the particles that make up a substance or object, such as atoms or molecules, also have kinetic energy? Particles which have more kinetic energy will move faster than particles which have less kinetic energy. When the particles are moving very fast, we feel the substance and say "That's hot!". This is because the temperature of a substance depends on the kinetic energy of the particles.

The thermal energy can be transferred from one object to another in a thermal system. When thermal energy is transferred, this is called heat. We will look more at this in the next chapter, but for now let's look at some simple examples of energy transfers within thermal systems (heating).

## Chapter 2. Potential and kinetic energy

* So, what have we discovered? The temperature of the water increased. This means that the water particles must have been given more **kinetic energy**. The energy must have come from the Bunsen burner flame. The flame is there because we are burning gas so the energy must have been stored in the gas. If it is stored energy then it is **potential energy**.
* So, we have discovered that the potential energy stored in the gas has been transferred to the water particles as kinetic energy. No energy has been **created**, it has been **transferred** from the gas to the water. The energy of the system has been **conserved**.

## Energy Transfer and Conservation in Biological Systems

When an animal eats a plant, it uses the **potential energy** in the food which is released during respiration. This energy is then used by the animal to move and for all its life processes. Consequently, the potential energy in the food eaten by the animal is transferred to **kinetic energy**. Energy has thus been transferred from the Sun to the plant, and then to the animal.



![Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr7_Heat-Energy-transfer.pdf-0026-00.png](images/Textbook_NaturalScience_Gr7_Heat-Energy-transfer.pdf-0026-00.png)


*An impala eats the grass and stores the energy in its muscles. When the impala runs, the stored energy becomes kinetic energy.*

When humans eat plants or animals, we are able to use the stored potential energy to make our bodies function.

*Our food provides the input energy for our bodies to work and move. The food contains potential energy.*

### Is Energy Conserved in a Biological System?

Yes, it is! 

1. Plants change the Sun's energy into **potential energy** which they store inside themselves.
2. Animals eat the plants, and the stored potential energy is transferred to them.
3. Animals use the stored energy to enable them to move. This means the potential energy within the animal has been transferred to **kinetic energy**.
4. As the animal moves and performs its functions, this kinetic energy is transferred to the surroundings.

No energy has been created or destroyed; it has simply been transferred from the Sun to the plant and subsequently to the animal.

### Lesson Notes: Hydropower Systems and Energy Transfer

#### 1. Introduction to Hydropower
* **Concept:** Hydropower is a renewable energy source that harnesses the natural flow or fall of water to generate electricity.
* **Core Principle:** Water stored at a height possesses stored energy due to its position in a gravitational field. As it moves, this energy is converted through various mechanical and electrical systems to power cities and homes.

---

#### 2. The Hydropower System Diagram Analysis

A typical hydroelectric power plant located at the edge of a dam consists of several interconnected components:
* **Dam:** Holds back water, creating a high-level reservoir.
* **Turbine:** A mechanical device placed in the water flow path that rotates as rushing water hits its blades.
* **Generator:** Connected to the turbine, it converts mechanical energy into electrical energy.
* **Powerhouse:** The building that houses the turbine and generator equipment.
* **Power Lines:** Conductors that transmit the generated electricity across long distances to cities.

---

#### 3. Step-by-Step Activity and Questions

Study the hydropower plant diagram and answer the following conceptual questions:

##### a) Potential Energy in Stored Water
* **Question:** The water in the dam on the left is high up. It has the ability to fall down. What kind of energy does the water have?
* **Explanation:** Because of its elevated position, the water possesses **gravitational potential energy** ($E_p$).

##### b) Energy Transfer During Flow
* **Question:** As the water flows down the outlet from the dam, describe the transfer of energy.
* **Explanation:** As gravity pulls the water downward, its height decreases and its speed increases. Therefore, gravitational potential energy is converted into **kinetic energy** ($E_k$).

##### c) Turbine Mechanics
* **Question:** The flowing water then turns the turbine. This is a mechanical system. What energy does the turbine have?
* **Explanation:** As the moving water forces the turbine blades to spin, the turbine gains **mechanical kinetic energy** ( rotational kinetic energy).

##### d) Generator and Electrical Systems
* **Question:** The generator then transfers the energy between two systems. The kinetic energy in the mechanical system is transferred to kinetic energy in the electrical system as it generates electricity. What parts make up the electrical system in the diagram?
* **Explanation:** Based on the diagram, the electrical system comprises the **generator**, **power lines**, and the connected electrical grid/city distribution infrastructure.

##### e) System Output
* **Question:** What is the output from this whole system? In other words, what does the city get?
* **Explanation:** The final useful output delivered to the city is **electrical energy** (electricity used for lighting, heating, appliances, and industry).

# Experimental Investigation: Ramps, Marbles, and Friction

## 1. Controlling Variables
To ensure a fair test during the experiment, certain factors must remain constant:
* The size of the marble must stay the same.

---

## 2. Formulating Hypotheses
A hypothesis should state how the independent variable is expected to affect the dependent variable. 

**Examples of valid hypotheses:**
* *"The higher the marble is on the ramp, the further the cup will move."*
* *"The higher the marble is on the ramp, the less the cup will move."*

Both examples correctly link the height of the marble (independent variable) to the distance the cup moves (dependent variable).

---

## 3. Setting Up the Ramp
* The height of the ramp at each step depends on the objects used to prop it up.
* Use blocks or books of equal thickness so that the height increases by a consistent, measurable amount at each step.
* Creating larger ramps allows for more measurements to be taken at different positions up the ramp.

---

## 4. Identifying Variables
1. **Independent Variable:** The height of the marble on the ramp (plotted on the horizontal $x$-axis; e.g., using $5\text{ cm}$ increments).
2. **Dependent Variable:** The distance the cup moves (always plotted on the vertical $y$-axis).

---

## 5. Drawing Conclusions
1. The conclusion should state the relationship observed: *As the height of release increases, the distance that the cup moves increases.*
2. Evaluating the hypothesis: Compare the final results against the initial prediction. If the hypothesis predicted that a greater height causes the cup to move further, and the data supports this, the hypothesis is **true**. If the data shows the opposite, the hypothesis is **false**.

---

## 6. Scientific Concepts

### Friction
* **Definition:** A force that opposes motion when surfaces are in contact and move against each other.
* **Effect on Motion:** Causes moving objects (like the cup) to slow down and eventually come to a standstill.
* **Energy Transformation:** When surfaces rub together, friction transfers kinetic energy into **heat** (demonstrated by rubbing hands together until they feel warm).
* *Note on Curriculum:* Friction is covered in greater detail in Grade 8, including static electricity and how rubbing objects transfers electrons to create a charge.

### Energy Conversions and Transfers
* **Potential Energy:** The initial potential energy of the lifted marble originates from the muscular energy of the arm that lifted it.
* **Kinetic and Potential Energy Relationship:** An object at a higher point possesses more potential energy. When released, this converts into higher kinetic energy, meaning it hits the ground moving faster.
* **Energy Dissipation (Sound and Heat):** 
  * When an impact occurs (such as sand flying up or a loud bang), energy is transferred to the surrounding air and detected by eardrums as sound.
  * Friction and impact also increase thermal energy, making surrounding materials (like sand) slightly warmer.

# Boiling Point Curves and Energy Systems

## 1. Boiling Point Curves

### Purpose of a Boiling Point Curve
The primary reason for plotting a boiling point curve is to observe how the curve flattens out, indicating that the temperature remains constant. This is how we experimentally determine the boiling point of a liquid. 

* **Comparative Liquids:** It is useful to plot boiling point curves for other liquids, such as Coca-Cola or orange/apple juice. Learners will observe a similarly shaped curve, demonstrating that at the boiling point of that liquid, the temperature remains constant for a period of time.
* **Extended Heating:** If heated longer, the temperature will eventually begin to fall as more particles move into the atmosphere. This links back to concepts covered in *Matter and Materials* (Chapter 1: Properties of Materials).

---

## 2. Activity Analysis & Questions

### Question 1 & 2: Observations and Heat Transfer
1. **Learner-dependent answer:** Learners should conclude that the longer water is kept over a flame, the higher its temperature rises until it reaches its boiling point. At this point, the temperature remains constant as the water changes state from liquid to gas (or vapour).
2. **Learner-dependent answer.**
3. **Source of Energy:** The energy for the temperature increase came from the burning gas in the Bunsen burner.
4. **Thermal Energy Transfer:** The thermal energy (kinetic and potential energy) of the flame from the Bunsen burner or candle is transferred to the water. Consequently, the thermal energy of the water increases, causing the temperature to rise.
   * **Key Definition:** *Temperature is a measure of the average kinetic energy of the particles.*
5. **Cooling Down:** After turning off the heat source, the water cooled down because no more energy was supplied to the system. The loss of energy became greater than the gain, causing the temperature to decrease.
6. **Surrounding Transfer:** The thermal energy was transferred from the water to the surrounding air.
