## 4.1 Variation within a species

In the last chapters we looked at how to classify organisms on Earth. Do you remember what the classification levels are? What is the smallest group in the classification system?

A **species** is a group of organisms that can interbreed with each other to produce fertile offspring. In this section you will learn why the ability to create a fertile offspring is the single most defining characteristic of a species.

Wherever organisms in a species live, they need to survive in those conditions. We say they are **adapted** to their environment. Those individuals of a species, which have characteristics that make them more successful at surviving, will reproduce more and pass on their characteristics to their offspring. However, environments change over periods of time. This means that the species needs to constantly change over time to better survive the conditions of their changing habitat. If the organisms do not adapt to their environment, they may not survive, and the species will die out. But how do species adapt? Does it happen quickly or over a long time?

What does variation mean when we use it in Natural Sciences? Let's take a look at some animals with which we are all familiar to find out what variation means.

# Lesson Revision Notes: Representing and Analyzing Data

## Core Concepts

### 1. Drawing a Bar Graph
When representing collected data (such as measurements or survey results) visually, a bar graph is often used. Follow these key steps:
* **Axes Assignment:** 
  * The horizontal axis ($x$-axis) represents the independent variables (e.g., categories, individuals, or groups).
  * The vertical axis ($y$-axis) represents the dependent variable (e.g., measurements, frequencies, or values).
* **Graph Elements:** Every graph must include a clear, descriptive heading.

---

### 2. Calculating an Average (Mean)
To find the average of a set of numerical measurements:
1. Add up all the individual measurements to find the sum.
2. Divide the total sum by the total number of items or learners measured.

Expressed as a mathematical formula:

$$\text{Average} = \frac{\sum \text{Measurements}}{\text{Total number of learners}}$$

---

## Questions and Activities

### Question 1
* Who is the tallest and who is the shortest in your class?
  * *Instructions:* Record your class measurements and identify the maximum and minimum values from your dataset.

### Question 2
* What is the average height of all the learners in your class? Use the space below to show your working for this calculation.
  * *Procedure:* 
    1. List all measured heights: $h_1, h_2, h_3, \dots, h_n$.
    2. Sum the heights: $\text{Total Height} = h_1 + h_2 + h_3 + \dots + h_n$.
    3. Count the total number of learners ($n$).
    4. Compute the final average using the formula:

$$\text{Average Height} = \frac{h_1 + h_2 + h_3 + \dots + h_n}{n}$$

## 4.2 Inheritance in humans

We have seen that there is huge variation between all the people on Earth, and even in your class. However, there are also lots of similarities, especially between family members, such as height and skin colour. These characteristics (or traits) are passed down from one generation to the next in a family. We say they are **inherited traits**.

Traits are passed down from generation to generation over many years, from parent organisms to their offspring. This is called **inheritance**.

There are some traits which are very easy to see how they are inherited, such as skin colour or height. Did you know that some people are able to wink with one eye but not with both? Or that others can only see some colours but not all the colours — this is called **colour blindness**. Let's find out a bit more about some of these inherited traits.

# How does variation in a species help it to survive?

## Natural selection

Have you ever heard the saying: "survival of the fittest or strongest"? This refers to the way in which the organisms are able to survive in their environments because they have adapted the best or they have certain characteristics which allow them to survive better than others.

**Survival** refers to the characteristics that allow the members of a species to thrive and reproduce successfully so their advantageous characteristics are passed on successfully. Thus over generations the entire species can survive as the majority of its members happen to have inherited the advantageous characteristics.

For example, imagine some impala in one group in a game reserve started to develop a characteristic, which was passed down from the parents to the offspring, allowing them to run faster for longer. The impala in this group can then run faster than the impala in another group. Over time, the faster impala will be able to escape the predators such as cheetahs and lions more often and so they will live long enough to reproduce and raise their offspring successfully. They will therefore pass on the swift running characteristic. The slower impala will get caught more often and so they will not survive to produce offspring. The slower impala are slowly removed over time. The characteristic making some of the impala faster allows those impala to survive and pass this trait on to their offspring. This is the principle of **natural selection**.

Let's look at a famous and interesting example of how variation in the peppered moth allowed for natural selection.

# Chapter 4. Variation

## The Industrial Revolution and Natural Selection

During the Industrial Revolution in England, there was a huge increase in the number of factories. These factories mainly burnt coal as an energy source, which increased the amount of pollution and soot in the air. The pollution caused the lichen on the trees to die off. The soot coated the trees in the peppered moths' habitat. These trees now did not have any lichen and they were a dark grey colour because of the soot covering them.

The light coloured moths were therefore not camouflaged anymore and could be seen easily by predators when they rested on the trees. As a result, more of the light-coloured moths were eaten by birds and didn't have a chance to mate and lay eggs. Therefore the number of light-coloured moths decreased. In comparison, the few moths that were a dark grey colour were now at an advantage as they were now the same colour as the soot covered trees and could hide. These darker-coloured moths could therefore go on to have more offspring. Over time, this resulted in more and more of the moths being dark-coloured.

# SUMMARY:

## Key Concepts

* A species is a category within the classification system.
* Living organisms of the same type belong to the same species.
* Organisms from the same species can reproduce sexually and produce offspring that are fertile and can reproduce.
* People belong to the species *Homo sapiens*.
* Variation is the difference between individuals from the same species.
* This variation can be inherited from one generation to the next.
* The individuals that are better suited (adapted) to their environment will survive to reproduce. This is called **natural selection**.
* Small changes can take place in a species over shorter periods, like from one generation to the next.
* Over very long periods these small changes can accumulate so that big changes occur over time.

# 4 Variation

## Chapter Overview

**Duration:** 1 week

In this chapter, we explore variation within a species and what it means. While learners already know how to classify organisms using shared characteristics down to the species level, it is important to understand that even within a single species, individuals are different. 

These differences are called **variation**. This chapter focuses on the observable differences between individuals in the same species—some of which are inherited (passed down from one generation to the next)—without delving deeply into the genetic basis (cells and DNA), which is covered in later grades. We also introduce the concept of **natural selection**, in which a particular variation can make an organism better suited (adapted) to a specific environment. This is crucial to the survival of species as environments change.

## 4.1 Variation within a species

The biological classification system moves from the largest group to the smallest group in the following order:
$$\text{Kingdoms} \rightarrow \text{Phyla} \rightarrow \text{Classes} \rightarrow \text{Orders} \rightarrow \text{Families} \rightarrow \text{Genera} \rightarrow \text{Species}$$

The smallest and most specific group in this classification system is the **species**.

---

### ACTIVITY: Small, big, long-haired, short-haired, black, white, brown or spotty?! (LB page 82)

1. **Kingdom:** The animal kingdom.
2. **Phylum:** Dogs belong to the phylum *Chordata* because they are vertebrates (they possess a backbone and an internal bony skeleton).
3. **Class:** Dogs are mammals because they are endothermic (warm-blooded), have fur, possess mammary glands, and give birth to live young.
4. **Common Characteristics:** Some common characteristics of dogs include four legs, fur, a tail, sharp teeth, an elongated snout, pointy ears, and paws with claws.
5. **Definition of a Species:** All different types of domestic dogs can reproduce with each other to create offspring. **The ability to reproduce and have fertile offspring is the definition of a species.**
6. **Differences within a Species:** Variations among dogs include fur colour, fur length, body shape, body size, length of legs, length of tails, length of snouts, and shape of ears.
7. **Cross-breeding and Species Distinction:** Horses and donkeys are not the same species because they are unable to produce fertile offspring. Although both belong to the family *Equidae* and the genus *Equus*, they are distinct species:
   - The domestic horse is classified as *Equus caballus*.
   - The domestic donkey is classified as *Equus asinus*.
   
   This example reinforces the core biological concept that a species consists of organisms capable of reproducing to produce **fertile offspring**.

Humans all belong to a single species. The scientific name for humans is *Homo sapiens*.

#### Scientific Concept: Reliability
This activity introduces the concept of **reliability** in scientific investigations:
* **The Problem:** Mistakes sometimes creep into scientific measurements, leading to incorrect results.
* **The Solution:** We overcome unreliability by repeating the same measurement multiple times. If we obtain the same or very similar results, we can trust that our data is reliable.
* **Classroom Application:** Set up measuring stations where learners take turns being measured. To ensure reliability, each learner should be measured $3$ times, after which an average can be calculated.
* **Large Classes:** If you have a large class, divide learners into groups of $10$. Each group can record their members' heights and use the data to draw a graph.
