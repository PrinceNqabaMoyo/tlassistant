# CHAPTER 4: HUMAN REPRODUCTION

## Introduction

In this chapter we will be studying human reproduction. All organisms must reproduce to ensure the survival of the species. We will look at the structure of the male and female reproductive systems, puberty, how gametes are produced, and hormonal changes that occur in the menstrual cycle. We will also briefly study fertilisation and the development of the zygote, including implantation and gestation.

The understanding of this topic necessitates understanding the role of meiosis, mitosis and fertilisation in the human life cycle as illustrated in Figure 1 below.

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

**Figure 1:** Human life cycle

---

All the body cells (somatic cells) of a human being are **diploid ($2n$)** i.e. have two sets of each chromosome. For humans to grow or to repair damaged tissues, the somatic cells divide by **mitosis**. The new cells produced by mitosis are identical to the original cells which divided.

Sexual reproduction requires two parents. Both the male and the female produce **gametes** (egg and sperm) by a reduction division referred to as **meiosis**. Meiosis ensures that the gametes are **haploid** i.e. they have only one set of chromosomes. When two gametes fuse (a sperm and an egg) as a result of **fertilization**, a diploid **zygote** is formed. The zygote then divides by mitosis to form a human.

## Key terminology

| Term | Definition |
| :--- | :--- |
| **gamete** | an egg or sperm cell with half the number of chromosomes |
| **gametogenesis** | the process in which gametes are produced in the testes and ovaries through meiosis |
| **oogenesis** | the process that occurs when egg cells are made in the ovary through meiosis |
| **spermatogenesis** | the process that takes place when sperm cells are made in the testes through meiosis |
| **germinal epithelium** | cuboidal epithelium found on the surface of the testes and ovaries which gives rise to the cells which mature to form sperm cells and egg cells respectively |

# Temperature Regulation of the Testes

- It is vitally important that the testes are suspended on the outside of the body, as this allows for temperature regulation to occur.
- The optimum temperature for sperm production is $2^\circ\text{C}$ to $3^\circ\text{C}$ lower than normal body temperature.
- If the temperature in the scrotum is high, it interferes with the quality of the sperm resulting in male infertility.
- By having the testes suspended in the scrotum, the temperature of the testes can be adjusted by moving the testes closer to the body in cold conditions or further away from the body during warm conditions.

# Spermatogenesis

## Introduction to Spermatogenesis
* **Definition:** Spermatogenesis is the production of male gametes (sperm cells) in the testes of the male.
* **Location:** It occurs in the germinal epithelium of the seminiferous tubules in the testes.
* **Hormonal Control:** This process happens under the influence of testosterone.
* **Cellular Process:** During puberty, the germinal epithelium contains a diploid number of chromosomes ($46$). These cells go through the process of meiosis, forming haploid sperm cells with $23$ chromosomes. The gametes may have $(22 + \text{X})$ or $(22 + \text{Y})$ chromosomes.

## The Process of Spermatogenesis
Spermatogenesis takes place as follows:
* Under the influence of testosterone, the diploid germinal epithelial cells ($2n$) lining the seminiferous tubules go through meiosis.
* Each cell that goes through meiosis produces $4$ haploid spermatids ($n$).
* Each spermatid matures to form a haploid sperm cell.

---

## Structure of a Human Sperm Cell
Each sperm cell is made up of a head, a middle portion (neck), and a long tail.

### Components of a Sperm Cell:
* **Head:** 
  * Mostly made up of the nucleus, which contains $22$ autosomes and one sex chromosome ($\text{X}$ or $\text{Y}$).
  * Contains the **acrosome**, which holds enzymes that dissolve the outer layer of the egg to allow fertilisation to occur.
* **Middle portion:** Contains **mitochondria**, which provide energy for the movement of the sperm cell.
* **Long tail:** Allows the sperm cell to propel itself forward (to swim) through fluid.

# Oogenesis

**Oogenesis** is the production of female gametes (ova / egg cells) in the ovaries of a female. It occurs when the diploid germinal epithelium of the ovaries starts to produce follicles by mitosis.

## Process of Oogenesis

Oogenesis takes place as follows:
* The diploid germinal epithelium cells ($2n$) of the ovaries go through the process of mitosis to form many follicles.
* Every 28 days, the follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) stimulates one follicle. Only one cell inside of that follicle enlarges and goes through the process of meiosis.
* Out of the 4 (four) haploid cells produced through meiosis, only one cell will survive to form a mature ovum.
* The other three cells from meiosis will degenerate.

# Revision Notes: The Menstrual Cycle

The menstrual cycle is made up of two separate cycles that happen at the same time:
1. **Ovarian cycle**
2. **Uterine cycle**

---

## 1. Ovarian Cycle

The ovarian cycle refers to the development and release of an ovum (or egg cell) inside the ovary. 

### Core Process and Hormones

1. **Role of FSH:**
   * The cycle begins when **FSH (Follicle Stimulating Hormone)** is secreted by the pituitary gland and transported to the ovary by the blood.
   * FSH stimulates a primary follicle to develop into a **Graafian follicle**, which contains a mature ovum (egg cell).

2. **Production of Oestrogen:**
   * As the Graafian follicle develops, it produces the hormone **oestrogen**, which increases oestrogen levels in the blood.

3. **Ovulation (Around Day 14):**
   * The Graafian follicle ruptures and releases an ovum in a process called **ovulation**.
   * Ovulation is stimulated by the **Luteinising Hormone (LH)**, which is released by the pituitary gland.

4. **Role of the Corpus Luteum:**
   * LH causes the ruptured Graafian follicle to transform into a structure called the **corpus luteum**.
   * The corpus luteum secretes the hormone **progesterone**, which increases progesterone levels in the blood.

### Revision Notes: The Hormonal Control of the Menstrual Cycle

---

#### 1. The Hormonal Control of the Menstrual Cycle

The regulation of the menstrual cycle involves a sequence of interactions between the pituitary gland and the ovaries, driven by various hormones:

*   **Follicle Stimulating Hormone (FSH):** Released by the pituitary gland, FSH stimulates the development and maturation of a primary follicle in one of the ovaries.
*   **Oestrogen:** As the follicle develops into a mature Graafian follicle, it releases oestrogen.
*   **Luteinising Hormone (LH):** Increasing levels of oestrogen stimulate the pituitary gland to release luteinising hormone (LH). The surge in LH causes **ovulation** to occur.
*   **Progesterone:** After ovulation, the ruptured Graafian follicle transforms into the **corpus luteum**, which secretes progesterone.
*   **Inhibition by Progesterone:** An increased amount of progesterone prevents the release of both follicle stimulating hormone (FSH) and luteinising hormone (LH) through an inhibitory mechanism.
*   **Menstruation:** If fertilization does not occur, the corpus luteum breaks down, causing progesterone levels to decrease. This drop causes the endometrium (uterine lining) to break down. The endometrium and unfertilised ovum are discharged through the vagina as blood during menstruation.
*   **Cycle Restart:** Due to the decreased level of progesterone, FSH and LH are no longer inhibited. They are once again produced by the pituitary gland, allowing a new cycle to begin.

---

#### 2. Negative Feedback Mechanism Between Progesterone and FSH

A **negative feedback system** is a regulatory mechanism where an interaction between two hormones helps restore balance:
*   An increase in one hormone stimulates or inhibits processes to adjust the level of another hormone.
*   In the menstrual cycle, high levels of progesterone exert a negative feedback effect on the pituitary gland, inhibiting the secretion of follicle stimulating hormone (FSH). This prevents any further follicular development during the luteal phase.

**In the event of fertilization:**
*   If the ovum is fertilized, the corpus luteum remains active and continues to secrete progesterone.
*   Persistently high levels of progesterone maintain the inhibition of FSH secretion, ensuring that no new follicles develop during pregnancy. Consequently, ovulation does not take place.

## Fertilisation & development of zygote to blastocyst

During copulation (sexual intercourse) the penis is inserted into the vagina and sperm cells are released through ejaculation close to the cervix. The sperm cells swim through the cervix up into the uterus and through the fallopian tubes. 

The haploid ovum released during ovulation enters the fallopian tubes. If an ovum (haploid) is present in the fallopian tubes, one sperm cell (haploid) may penetrate through the jelly layer and fertilise the ovum resulting in a diploid zygote. The nucleus of the ovum and the nucleus of the sperm cell fuse resulting in fertilisation. 

The zygote divides by mitosis as it moves down the fallopian tube towards the uterus. Mitosis continues and a solid ball of cells known as the morula is formed. The morula develops into a hollow fluid-filled ball of cells called the blastocyst. Once the ovum is fertilised it takes approximately $5$ days to form the blastocyst.

# Implantation of the blastocyst and gestation

The blastocyst moves from the fallopian tube into the uterus where it embeds itself into the endometrium. This is known as **implantation** and takes place as follows:

* The outer cells of the blastocyst secrete enzymes which break down a small portion of the thickened uterine wall causing it to become softer
* The blastocyst sinks into this softer area and the outer layers develop into two extra-embryonic membranes called the **amnion** and the **chorion**
* The chorion extends finger-like outgrowths called the chorionic villi into the endometrium and form part of the placenta which secretes progesterone.
* The blastocyst is now called the **embryo**

The gestation period, also known as pregnancy, is the time in which the embryo develops inside the uterus. Gestation and the development of the embryo lasts for about $40$ weeks or $280$ days. After $12$ weeks the embryo is known as a foetus.

## The Chorion and Amniotic Sac

* **Chorion:** The outer membrane that forms the **chorionic villi**.
* **Amnion:** The inner membrane that becomes filled with **amniotic fluid** to form the **amniotic sac**.

### Functions of Amniotic Fluid
* Protects the foetus against mechanical injury (acts as a shock-absorber).
* Prevents dehydration.
* Maintains a stable temperature for the foetus.
* Allows for free movement of the foetus as it grows and develops.

---

## The Umbilical Cord

The umbilical cord attaches the foetus to the placenta and contains umbilical blood vessels:

* **Two umbilical arteries:** Carry deoxygenated blood and waste products from the foetus to the placenta.
* **One umbilical vein:** Carries oxygenated blood, nutrients, water, and other substances from the placenta to the foetus.

---

## The Placenta

* **Definition:** A temporary organ that forms in the area where the blastocyst implants.
* **Function:** Allows for substances to be transferred by diffusion between the mother and the foetus without any direct blood contact. 
* **Note:** The blood of the mother and the foetus never actually mixes.

## Functions of the Placenta

The placenta has the following functions:

* It is the point of attachment of the foetus to the mother.
* It allows for diffusion of nutrients from the mother to the foetus.
* It allows for the diffusion of oxygen from the mother to the foetus and for the diffusion of carbon dioxide from the foetus to the mother (gas exchange).
* It allows for the diffusion of waste products from the foetus to the mother.
* After 12 weeks, the placenta secretes progesterone to maintain the pregnancy.

   * a) Protects the sperm cell from the acidic environment of the vagina, provides a fluid medium in which sperm cells can swim, and increases the mobility of sperm.

   * To keep the testes at a temperature that is lower than body temperature, providing the optimum temperature necessary for the production of healthy sperm and ensuring that healthy sperm can survive.

#### 2.3.1. Developmental Changes from Fertilised Ovum to Implantation
**Question:** Describe the developmental changes in the fertilised ovum until implantation occurs in the uterus. (4)

**Answer (Any four of the following):**
* The zygote undergoes mitosis.
* Continues dividing until a solid ball of cells is formed, called a **morula**.
* The morula continues to divide and forms a mass of cells with a hollow cavity, called a **blastocyst**.
* The outer membrane of the blastocyst forms chorionic villi / attachment villi.
* The blastocyst attaches to the endometrium (implantation).

### Part 1: Fetal Health and Blood Flow

#### 3.1.3 Consequences of Blood Vessel Blockage
If blood vessel **D** (such as the umbilical cord vessels) becomes blocked, blood flow is disrupted, leading to the following consequences:
* Waste products, nitrogenous wastes, and carbon dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$) accumulate in the foetus's body.
* This accumulation can ultimately cause the death of the foetus.

#### 3.1.4 Mixing of Maternal and Fetal Blood
If the blood of the mother and the blood of the foetus come into direct contact with each other, it can lead to the death of the foetus due to:
* Harmful substances or bacteria passing directly from the mother's blood to the blood of the foetus.
* Incompatibility between the blood types or other proteins of the mother and the baby.

### Part 2: Factors Affecting Human Fertility

#### Case Extract
> "Several recent studies have suggested a gradual decline in sperm production in men. Endocrine disruptions as well as life style have been suggested as risk factors. One life style factor that may affect human fertility is driving a vehicle for a prolonged period. It is suggested that the driving position may increase the scrotal temperature."

#### 3.2.1 Risk Factors Identified by Researchers
Based on the extract, the risk factors identified are:
* Endocrine disruptors / malfunctioning of endocrine glands.
* Life style variations (e.g., driving a vehicle for a prolonged period of time).

#### 3.2.2 Explanation: Prolonged Driving and Sperm Count
Regular long-distance driving with no breaks can possibly lower the sperm count in healthy males through the following mechanism:
* Long-distance driving extends the period of sitting in a fixed position, causing the male reproductive organ to remain tightly squeezed between the thighs for a considerable period.
* As the temperature rises in the area, the scrotum is unable to pull the testis away from the body due to a lack of spacing and movement.
* Consequently, the testes remain in contact with the body and maintain a higher temperature, causing a disruption in spermatogenesis (sperm production).
