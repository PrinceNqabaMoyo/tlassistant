#### 2. Content & Cognitive Scope
**Core Concepts & Skills:**
*   **System Definition:** Define a "system" in physics; identify internal vs. external forces.
*   **Isolated Systems:** Define an isolated system as one where the net external force is zero ($\sum F_{net} = 0$).
*   **Conservation of Momentum:** State the law: The total linear momentum of an isolated system remains constant.
*   **Collision Types:** Distinguish between elastic and inelastic collisions.
*   **Energy Principles:** Understand that kinetic energy is only conserved in an elastic collision.
*   **Problem Solving:** Apply the conservation of momentum to collisions of two objects moving in one dimension along a straight line, utilizing an appropriate sign convention.

**Cognitive Levels:**
*   **Knowledge:** Defining systems, isolated systems, and stating the law of conservation of momentum.
*   **Routine Procedures:** Identifying internal/external forces and applying sign conventions.
*   **Complex Procedures:** Distinguishing between collision types and applying conservation laws to one-dimensional motion.

#### 4. Resource Requirements
| Category | Required Materials |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Prescribed Experiment** | Air-track with blower, two trolleys, pulley, two photo-gates, two retort stands, dual timer, metre-stick, black card, set of equal weights |

#### 5. Mathematical Foundations
The core principle of this unit is governed by the Law of Conservation of Linear Momentum. For an isolated system, the total momentum before a collision equals the total momentum after:

$$\sum p_{before} = \sum p_{after}$$

Where momentum ($p$) is defined as:
$$p = mv$$

For a two-body system ($m_1, m_2$) moving in one dimension:
$$m_1v_{i1} + m_2v_{i2} = m_1v_{f1} + m_2v_{f2}$$

*   **Elastic Collision:** Kinetic energy is conserved ($K_{before} = K_{after}$).
*   **Inelastic Collision:** Kinetic energy is not conserved ($K_{before} \neq K_{after}$).

### 2. Content & Cognitive Scope

#### Core Concepts & Skills
*   **Definition of Impulse:** Defined as the product of the net force and the contact time.
    *   Formula: $Impulse = F_{net}\Delta t$
*   **Vector Nature:** Understanding that impulse is a vector quantity.
*   **Impulse-Momentum Theorem:**
    *   Relationship: $F_{net}\Delta t = \Delta p$
    *   Conceptual understanding that impulse is equal to the change in momentum.
*   **Calculations:** Use the impulse-momentum theorem to calculate force, time, or change in momentum for objects in one-dimensional motion.
*   **Application:** Application of impulse concepts to everyday safety considerations (e.g., airbags, seatbelts, and arrestor beds).

#### Cognitive Levels & Depth of Treatment
*   **Knowledge:** Recall definitions of impulse and momentum.
*   **Routine Procedures:** Applying the formula $F_{net}\Delta t = \Delta p$ to standard one-dimensional problems.
*   **Complex Procedures:** Analyzing safety scenarios (e.g., why bending knees when landing or using airbags reduces injury) by relating the increase in time ($\Delta t$) to a decrease in net force ($F_{net}$) for a constant change in momentum ($\Delta p$).

---

### 3. Guidelines for Teachers
*   **Pedagogical Focus:** Emphasize that in many safety applications, an object must be brought to rest from a specific initial velocity.
*   **Conceptual Link:** Since the change in momentum ($\Delta p$) is fixed, increasing the time interval ($\Delta t$) over which the force acts results in a decrease in the magnitude of the net force ($F_{net}$), thereby reducing potential damage or injury.
*   **Practical Examples:** Use real-world examples such as:
    *   Arrestor beds for trucks.
    *   Deployment of airbags.
    *   Bending knees when jumping from a height.

*   **Motion Description:** Using $x$ vs. $t$ and $v$ vs. $t$ graphs to describe complex motion scenarios, including:
    *   Bouncing balls.
    *   Objects thrown vertically upwards.
    *   Objects thrown vertically downwards.

#### 3. Mathematical Foundations
The analysis of these graphs relies on the fundamental kinematic relationships defined by the gradient and area under the curve:

*   **Velocity from Position:** The velocity is the gradient of the position-time graph:
    $$v = \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t}$$
*   **Acceleration from Velocity:** The acceleration is the gradient of the velocity-time graph:
    $$a = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}$$
*   **Displacement from Velocity:** The displacement is the area under the velocity-time graph:
    $$\Delta x = \int v \, dt \quad \text{(or area under the curve)}$$

#### 4. Mathematical & Scientific Context
The analysis of physical properties relies on the relationship between molecular mass ($M$), intermolecular force strength ($F_{IMF}$), and energy required for phase transitions:

*   **Boiling Point Trend:** As chain length increases, the surface area for Van der Waals forces increases, leading to higher boiling points:
    $$T_{boiling} \propto \text{Surface Area} \propto \text{Chain Length}$$
*   **Branching Effect:** Increased branching reduces the effective surface area for intermolecular contact, resulting in lower boiling points compared to straight-chain isomers:
    $$\text{Branching} \uparrow \implies \text{Surface Area} \downarrow \implies T_{boiling} \downarrow$$
*   **Esterification Constraint:**
    $$\text{Alcohol} + \text{Carboxylic Acid} \xrightarrow{H^+} \text{Ester} + H_2O$$
    *Constraint:* $n(C)_{alcohol} \leq 8$ and $n(C)_{acid} \leq 8$.

# Lesson: Elimination Reactions in Organic Chemistry

## 1. Core Concepts
Elimination reactions involve the removal of atoms or groups of atoms from adjacent carbon atoms in a saturated molecule to form an unsaturated molecule (containing a double or triple bond).

*   **Substrates:** Saturated compounds such as haloalkanes and alcohols undergo these reactions.
*   **Major Product Rule:** If more than one elimination product is possible, the **major product** is the one where the hydrogen ($H$) atom is removed from the carbon ($C$) atom that has the **least number of hydrogen atoms**.

---

## 2. Dehydrohalogenation
Dehydrohalogenation is a specific type of elimination reaction where a hydrogen halide ($HX$) is removed from a haloalkane.

### Reaction Conditions
To successfully perform dehydrohalogenation, the following conditions must be met:
1.  **Reagent:** A concentrated solution of sodium hydroxide ($NaOH$) or potassium hydroxide ($KOH$).
2.  **Solvent:** Pure ethanol (making it an **ethanolic** solution).
3.  **Heat:** The reaction must be heated under **reflux** (vapours condense and return to the reaction vessel during heating).

### General Equation
The general chemical equation for the dehydrohalogenation of a haloalkane is:
$$CH_2Cl-CH_2Cl \rightarrow CH_2=CHCl + HCl$$

| **10 Hours** | Work, Energy & Power | Definition of Work; Net Work |

#### 2. Content & Cognitive Scope
**Core Concepts:**
*   **Definition of Work:** Work done on an object by a force.
*   **Mathematical Representation:** $W = F\Delta x \cos\theta$
*   **Scalar Nature:** Work is a scalar quantity measured in Joules ($J$).
*   **Net Work Calculation:** 
    *   Applying the definition of work to each force acting on an object during displacement.
    *   Summing the scalar contributions of each force.
*   **Energy Implications:**
    *   Positive net work increases system energy.
    *   Negative net work decreases system energy.
*   **Methodology for Net Work:**
    *   Construction of force diagrams showing only forces acting along the plane (ignoring perpendicular forces).

#### 3. Guidelines for Teachers (Pedagogical Focus)
*   **Conceptual Clarification:** Explicitly distinguish between the colloquial use of "work" and the scientific definition.
*   **Force Components:** Emphasize that only the component of the applied force parallel to the motion performs work.
*   **Angular Relationships ($\theta$):**
    *   **Perpendicular forces ($\theta = 90^\circ$):** No work is done ($\cos 90^\circ = 0$).
    *   **Parallel forces ($\theta = 0^\circ$):** Positive work is done ($\cos 0^\circ = 1$).
    *   **Anti-parallel forces ($\theta = 180^\circ$):** Negative work is done (e.g., friction) ($\cos 180^\circ = -1$).

#### 4. Mathematical Formulas
The fundamental equation for work done by a constant force is defined as:
$$W = F\Delta x \cos\theta$$

Where:
*   $W$ = Work done (Joules, $J$)
*   $F$ = Magnitude of the force (Newtons, $N$)
*   $\Delta x$ = Magnitude of the displacement (meters, $m$)
*   $\theta$ = The angle between the force vector and the displacement vector

#### 2. Content & Cognitive Scope
The following core concepts and skills are required for mastery of the application of equilibrium principles:

*   **Core Concepts:**
    *   Le Chatelier’s Principle.
    *   Factors affecting equilibrium: Pressure, Temperature, and Concentration (including the common ion effect).
    *   The role of catalysts in equilibrium systems.
    *   Industrial applications (e.g., The Haber Process).
*   **Skills to be Mastered:**
    *   Defining Le Chatelier’s principle.
    *   Identifying and explaining the effects of changes in system conditions.
    *   Predicting changes in concentrations and amounts of substances in an equilibrium mixture.
    *   Explaining the influence of a catalyst on an equilibrium mixture.
    *   Interpreting simple equilibrium graphs.
    *   Applying equilibrium principles to industrial processes.
*   **Cognitive Levels:**
    *   **Knowledge:** Recall of the definition of Le Chatelier’s principle.
    *   **Routine Procedures:** Identifying factors that shift equilibrium.
    *   **Complex Procedures:** Interpreting equilibrium graphs and predicting shifts based on changes in $P$, $T$, and concentration.
    *   **Problem Solving:** Applying principles to industrial contexts like the Haber process.

#### 3. Guidelines for Teachers
*   **Definition:** Le Chatelier’s principle states that a change in any of the factors that determine equilibrium conditions of a system will cause the system to change in such a manner as to reduce or counteract the effect of the change.
*   **Instructional Focus:** Ensure students understand that the system "counteracts" the change. Emphasize that while a catalyst increases the rate of both forward and reverse reactions, it does not change the position of the equilibrium.

#### 4. Mathematical & Conceptual Representation
While the provided text focuses on qualitative application, the underlying equilibrium constant ($K_c$) principles for these applications are governed by the law of mass action:

For a general reaction:
$$aA + bB \rightleftharpoons cC + dD$$

The equilibrium constant expression is:
$$K_c = \frac{[C]^c [D]^d}{[A]^a [B]^b}$$

*   **Note:** When applying Le Chatelier's principle, students must distinguish between changes that alter the value of $K_c$ (only temperature) and changes that shift the equilibrium position without altering $K_c$ (concentration, pressure, and catalysts).

#### 4. Guidelines for Teachers (Pedagogical Focus)
*   **Conceptual Linkage:** Emphasize that both motors and generators rely on the interaction between a coil and a magnetic field.
*   **Generator Mechanics:** Explain that an AC generator uses slip rings to maintain contact, resulting in alternating current.
*   **Motor Mechanics:** Explain that a current-carrying coil in a magnetic field experiences a force on both sides, creating a torque that induces rotation.
*   **Assessment Note:** Ensure students can use both words and diagrams to explain the basic principles of operation for both devices.

#### 5. Mathematical & Physical Principles
The underlying physics of these systems is governed by the interaction of magnetic fields and current. While specific formulas are applied in broader contexts, the fundamental principles involve:

*   **Faraday's Law of Induction:** The induced EMF ($\varepsilon$) is proportional to the rate of change of magnetic flux ($\Phi$):
    $$\varepsilon = -N \frac{\Delta \Phi}{\Delta t}$$
*   **Magnetic Force (Motor Effect):** The force ($F$) on a current-carrying conductor is given by:
    $$F = BIl \sin(\theta)$$
    *Where:*
    *   $B$ = Magnetic field strength
    *   $I$ = Current
    *   $l$ = Length of the conductor
    *   $\theta$ = Angle between the conductor and the magnetic field

*   **Knowledge:** Recall of standard conditions ($1\text{ mol/dm}^3$), definitions of the standard hydrogen electrode, and conventions for cell notation.
*   **Routine Procedures:** Calculating the standard emf ($E^\circ_{cell}$) using the Table of Standard Reduction Potentials.
*   **Complex Procedures:** Representing galvanic cells using standard notation and explaining the function of individual components (salt bridge, electrodes).
*   **Problem Solving:** Predicting the spontaneity of redox reactions based on calculated $E^\circ_{cell}$ values.

### 4. Physics Experimental Context
The prescribed experiment for Term 3 requires the determination of the internal resistance ($r$) of a battery. This typically involves the use of Ohm's Law and the relationship between Electromotive Force ($\varepsilon$), terminal potential difference ($V$), and current ($I$):

$$\varepsilon = I(R + r)$$
$$V = \varepsilon - Ir$$

Where:
*   $\varepsilon$ = Electromotive force (V)
*   $V$ = Terminal potential difference (V)
*   $I$ = Current (A)
*   $R$ = External resistance ($\Omega$)
*   $r$ = Internal resistance ($\Omega$)

#### 4. Mathematical Formulas & Notation
*   **Equilibrium Constant:** The expression for the equilibrium constant $K_c$ for a general reaction $aA + bB \rightleftharpoons cC + dD$ is defined as:
    $$K_c = \frac{[C]^c [D]^d}{[A]^a [B]^b}$$
*   **Le Chatelier’s Principle:** Qualitative prediction of the shift in equilibrium position when stress (concentration, pressure, or temperature) is applied to a system at equilibrium.
