# Economic and Management Sciences (Grades 7-9) Curriculum Specifications

## 1. Subject Overview
Economic and Management Sciences (EMS) focuses on the efficient and effective use of private, public, and collective resources to satisfy human needs and wants. The subject emphasizes critical reflection on the impact of resource exploitation on the environment and society, while teaching the management of scarce resources to maximize profit. It is a practical subject designed to equip learners with real-life skills for personal development and community growth.

## 2. Content Scope and Weighting

## 3. Instructional Planning Notes
*   **Pacing Guidelines:** The curriculum is designed to be delivered across four terms. Teachers must ensure that the 30% weighting for "The Economy" and 40% weighting for "Financial Literacy" are reflected in the depth of treatment and time allocation throughout the academic year.
*   **Cognitive Scope:** Instruction should progress from foundational knowledge to complex problem-solving, specifically regarding the management of scarce resources and the application of financial accounting principles.
*   **Assessment Integration:** Assessment tasks must align with the specified weightings to ensure learners are evaluated proportionately across both theoretical economic concepts and practical financial literacy skills.

### 2. Content & Cognitive Scope

The Economic and Management Sciences (EMS) curriculum for Grades 7-9 encompasses the following core conceptual areas:

*   **Needs and Wants:** Analysis of the needs and wants of different communities in societies.
*   **Production and Business Activities:** The nature, processes, and production of goods and services, and business activities within different economic sectors.
*   **Financial Management:** Accounting as a tool for business management and record-keeping.
*   **Market Dynamics:** The influence of demand, supply, and pricing.
*   **Economic Flow:** The circular flow of money, goods, and services between households, businesses, and government; rights and responsibilities of economic role players.
*   **Socio-Economic Development:** Strategies to achieve sustainable growth, poverty reduction, and fair wealth distribution while maintaining profitability.
*   **Entrepreneurial Competencies:** Skills and knowledge required for effective self-management and environmental management.
*   **Leadership and Management:** Basic aspects of leadership and management principles.
*   **Macro-Economic Factors:** The role of savings in sustainable economic growth and development; the influence of trade unions in the economy.
*   **Resource Management:** The importance of sustainable, effective, and efficient resource utilization.
*   **Business Structures:** The functioning of both formal and informal businesses.

---

### 3. Instructional Planning Notes
*   **Scope:** The curriculum is designed to integrate theoretical knowledge with practical application, focusing on the transition from personal financial management to broader economic participation.
*   **Depth of Treatment:** Instruction must progress from foundational definitions (e.g., needs vs. wants) to complex analytical tasks (e.g., evaluating the impact of trade unions or designing a business plan).
*   **Cognitive Progression:** Teachers are expected to facilitate learning across the cognitive spectrum, moving from *Knowledge* (identifying sectors) to *Problem Solving* (developing business plans and managing resources).

# Economic and Management Sciences (EMS) Grades 7-9: Curriculum Overview

## 1. Overview of Topics and Content Scope

The EMS curriculum is structured around three core pillars: **The Economy**, **Financial Literacy**, and **Entrepreneurship**. The following table outlines the progression of content across the Senior Phase.

| Topic | Grade | Content Scope |
| :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **The Economy** | 7 | History of money; needs and wants; goods and services; the production process; inequality and poverty |
| | 8 | Government; the National Budget; standard of living; markets |
| | 9 | Economic systems; the circular flow; price theory; trade unions |
| **Financial Literacy** | 7 | Savings; budgets; income and expenses; accounting concepts |
| | 8 | Accounting concepts; accounting cycle; source documents; Cash Receipts Journal (CRJ) and Cash Payments Journal (CPJ) of a service business; effects of cash transactions on the accounting equation; General Ledger and Trial Balance |
| | 9 | CRJ and CPJ of a sole trader; posting to the General Ledger; preparing a Trial Balance; recording transactions in the Debtors Journal, Creditors Journal; posting to the Debtors Ledger and Creditors Ledger |
| **Entrepreneurship** | 7 | The entrepreneur; starting a business; businesses; Entrepreneur's Day |
| | 8 | Factors of production; forms of ownership; levels of management; functions of management |
| | 9 | Sectors of the economy; functions of a business; business plan |

---

## 2. Content & Cognitive Scope

### Core Concepts and Skills
*   **The Economy:** Focuses on macro-economic indicators, government fiscal policy, and the interaction between economic participants.
*   **Financial Literacy:** Focuses on the systematic recording of financial data. The fundamental accounting equation is central to this pillar:
    $$\text{Assets} = \text{Owner's Equity} + \text{Liabilities}$$
*   **Entrepreneurship:** Focuses on the practical application of business management principles, from conceptualization (business plan) to operational execution (Entrepreneur's Day).

### Cognitive Levels
The curriculum requires learners to progress through the following cognitive domains:
1.  **Knowledge:** Recall of definitions (e.g., factors of production, accounting concepts).
2.  **Routine Procedures:** Application of standard accounting practices (e.g., posting to journals, balancing ledgers).
3.  **Complex Procedures:** Analysis of economic data and business scenarios (e.g., interpreting the National Budget or circular flow).
4.  **Problem Solving:** Synthesis of knowledge to create business plans or evaluate economic systems.

---

## 3. Instructional Planning Notes
*   **Pacing:** The curriculum is designed to be delivered over four terms. Teachers must ensure that Financial Literacy topics are taught sequentially, as skills in Grade 8 (e.g., the accounting cycle) are foundational for Grade 9 (e.g., Debtors and Creditors journals).
*   **Integration:** While topics are categorized, teachers are encouraged to integrate Entrepreneurship projects (such as Entrepreneur's Day) with Financial Literacy record-keeping to provide a holistic view of business operations.

# Economic and Management Sciences: Grade 8, Term 1

## 1. Annual Teaching Plan (ATP) & Timelines
*   **Total Duration:** 10 Weeks
*   **Instructional Time:** 2 hours per week

| Week | Topic | Content |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Revision | Revise Grade 7 work; overview of Term 1 curriculum. |
| 2 – 3 | The economy: Government | Meaning of government; levels of government; roles regarding households and businesses (consumers/producers). |
| 4 – 5 | The economy: National Budget | Government revenue (direct/indirect tax); expenditure (education, health, housing, grants, transport, security); influence on growth and inequality. |
| 6 | The economy: Standard of living | Lifestyles; self-sufficient, modern, and rural societies; environmental impact; unemployment; productive resource use for a healthy environment. |
| 7 – 8 | Financial literacy: Accounting concepts | Sole trader; debit; credit; capital; owner’s equity; income; expenses; profit; losses; liability; assets; banking; cash receipts; cash payments; subsidiary journals; accounting equation. |
| 9 – 10 | Financial literacy: Source documents | Receipts; deposit slips; cash register slips (till slips); cheques; cheque counter foils; bank statements; cash invoices. |

## 2. Content & Cognitive Scope
*   **Core Concepts:** Economic systems, government fiscal policy, standard of living, and fundamental accounting principles.
*   **Skills to be Mastered:**
    *   Analysis of government roles and budgetary impacts.
    *   Application of accounting concepts to business scenarios.
    *   Interpretation of financial source documents.
*   **Mathematical Application:**
    *   The fundamental accounting equation:
        $$A = OE + L$$
        *(Where $A$ = Assets, $OE$ = Owner's Equity, and $L$ = Liabilities)*

## 3. Assessment Matrix
*   **Formal Assessment Requirements:** Two tasks per term.
*   **Weighting:**

| Assessment Task | Weighting |
| :--- | :--- |
| Data Response | 40% |
| Controlled Test | 60% |

### Assessment Notes:
1.  The **Controlled Test** must cover all topics taught during Term 1.
2.  Completion of all topics as per the ATP is compulsory.
3.  Instructional delivery must be contextualized to the specific environment of the school.

*   **Cognitive Depth:** Learners are expected to progress from knowledge recall (definitions of factors of production) to routine procedures (recording in CRJ) and complex application (effect of transactions on the accounting equation).

| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Week 1** | Revision | Revise Term 2 work; overview of Term 3 curriculum. |
| **Weeks 2–3** | Financial Literacy: Cash Receipts Journal (CRJ) | Entering cash transactions in the CRJ; closing off the CRJ; effect of cash transactions on the accounting equation. |
| **Weeks 4–10** | Financial Literacy: Cash Payments Journal (CPJ) | Concept of CPJ (service business); formats and column usage; source documents; entering transactions; closing off the CPJ; effect on accounting equation; entering combined transactions in CRJ and CPJ. |
| **Weeks 4–10** | Entrepreneurship: Forms of ownership | Sole traders; partnerships; close corporations; private and public companies; characteristics, advantages, and disadvantages; role in sustainable job creation and natural resource use. |

*Note: During weeks 4–10, the 2-hour weekly allocation is split into 1 hour for Accounting and 1 hour for Entrepreneurship.*

## 2. Content & Cognitive Scope
*   **Core Concepts:** Accounting cycle for service businesses (CRJ/CPJ), Accounting Equation, Business Ownership structures.
*   **Skills:** Journalizing cash transactions, balancing/closing journals, analyzing business structures, evaluating socio-economic impacts of business ownership.
*   **Accounting Equation:** All transactions must be analyzed according to the fundamental equation:
    $$Assets = Owners' Equity + Liabilities$$
*   **Depth of Treatment:** Learners must demonstrate the ability to process source documents into journals and understand the conceptual differences between various legal forms of business entities.

## 3. Assessment Matrix & Exam Weightings

| :--- | :--- | :--- |

**Assessment Notes:**
1. The controlled test must cover the full breadth of the Term 3 syllabus.
2. Teachers are advised to contextualize assessments based on the specific school environment.

# Economic and Management Sciences: Grade 8, Term 4

## 1. Annual Teaching Plan (ATP) & Timelines
**Total Time Allocation:** 2 hours per week

| Week | Topic | Content |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Week 1** | Revision | Revise Term 3 work; overview of Term 4 curriculum. |
| **Weeks 2–6** | **Entrepreneurship:** Levels and functions of management | Management levels, tasks (planning, organising, leading, controlling), characteristics of good management, management styles (autocratic, permissive/laissez-faire, democratic/participatory). |
| **Weeks 2–9** | **Financial Literacy:** General Ledger and Trial Balance (service) | Double-entry principle, "T" accounts, General Ledger format/sections, opening accounts, posting from CRJ and CPJ, balancing accounts, preparing a Trial Balance for a service business. |
| **Weeks 7–9** | Examination preparations | Revision of annual curriculum, study techniques, examination writing skills. |
| **Week 10** | End-of-year examinations | Formal summative assessment. |

*Note: Entrepreneurship is allocated 1 hour/week (Weeks 2–6). Financial Literacy is allocated 1 hour/week (Weeks 2–9). Examination preparation is allocated 1 hour/week (Weeks 7–9).*

## 2. Content & Cognitive Scope
*   **Core Concepts:** Management theory, business leadership styles, double-entry bookkeeping, financial reporting.
*   **Skills:** 
    *   **Managerial:** Identifying and applying management tasks and styles.
    *   **Accounting:** Recording transactions in the General Ledger, balancing accounts, and constructing a Trial Balance.
    *   **Metacognitive:** Examination preparation and study strategy application.
*   **Depth of Treatment:** Learners must demonstrate the ability to transition from source documents (CRJ/CPJ) to ledger accounts and verify accuracy via the Trial Balance.

## 3. Assessment Matrix & Exam Weightings

### Formal Assessment Structure
The final year mark is calculated based on a weighted combination of School-Based Assessment (SBA) and the End-of-Year Examination.

| Component | Weighting |
| :--- | :--- |
| **SBA (Terms 1, 2, & 3)** | 40% |
| **End-of-Year Examination** | 60% |
| **Total** | **100%** |

### Calculation Formulas
The final year mark is determined by the following calculation:
$$\text{Year Mark} = (\text{SBA Average} \times 0.40) + (\text{Exam Mark} \times 0.60)$$

### Assessment Requirements
1.  **SBA:** Comprises 100 marks per term for Terms 1, 2, and 3.
2.  **Examination:** The end-of-year examination is marked out of 150.
3.  **Scope:** The end-of-year examination must be comprehensive, covering all topics taught across Terms 1, 2, 3, and 4.
4.  **Contextualization:** Teaching and assessment must consider the specific context of the school environment.
