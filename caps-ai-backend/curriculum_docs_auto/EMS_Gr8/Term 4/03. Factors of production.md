# Topic: Entrepreneurship and the Factors of Production

## 1. Overview of Entrepreneurship and Production

In this topic, learners explore the concept of **entrepreneurship** and the foundational elements required to start and run a business. The core focus areas include:
- Capital (borrowed capital and own capital)
- Labour (unskilled, semi-skilled, and skilled labour)
- The role of workers in the business
- Fair employment practices
- Natural resources
- Entrepreneurship as a factor of production
- The remuneration of the factors of production

---

## 2. Capital

Capital refers to the money, assets, or resources used to start, operate, and grow a business. Start-up capital can be categorized into two main types:
* **Own Capital:** Money invested directly by the owner(s) from personal savings or personal assets.
* **Borrowed Capital:** Money obtained from external sources, such as bank loans, family members, or other financial institutions, which must be repaid with interest.

---

## 3. Labour

Labour is the physical and mental effort exerted by humans to produce goods and services. Without labour—whether skilled, semi-skilled, or unskilled—there can be no production.

### Types of Labour
* **Unskilled Labour:** Work that requires little to no specialized training, formal education, or previous experience (e.g., basic manual tasks).
* **Semi-Skilled Labour:** Work that requires a moderate amount of specialized training or basic practical skills.
* **Skilled Labour:** Work that requires specialized training, formal education, high-level expertise, and professional qualifications (e.g., engineers, accountants, technicians).

### Preparation for the Workplace
Workers acquire specific levels of skill through various pathways:
- Apprenticeships and learnerships
- On-the-job training
- Formal studies at colleges, technikons, or universities

### Skills Shortages in South Africa
South Africa experiences specific skills shortages in critical sectors. Addressing these shortages requires focusing on education and training in:
- Mathematics
- Engineering and Science
- Information and Communication Technology (ICT) services
- Project management

---

## 4. The Role of Workers and Labour Relations

### The Value of Workers
* Labour is one of a business's greatest assets, regardless of skill level.
* Workers bring ideas, creativity, individuality, and uniqueness that drive innovation within a business.

### Labour Relations and Fair Employment Practices
* **Fair Employment Practices:** Ensuring safe working conditions, fair wages, non-discrimination, and legal protection for all workers.
* **South African Labour Legislation:** Relevant laws ensuring fair labour practices include:
  * The Compensation for Occupational Injuries and Diseases Act (COIDA)
  * The Labour Relations Act (LRA)
  * The Basic Conditions of Employment Act (BCEA)
  * The Occupational Health and Safety Act (OHSA)
* *Resource Note:* Further information and resources can be accessed via the [Department of Labour website](http://www.labour.gov.za/).

---

## 5. Natural Resources and Entrepreneurship

### Natural Resources
* Gifts of nature used in the production of goods and services (e.g., land, minerals, water, and forests). These resources are often scarce and must be managed sustainably.

### Entrepreneurship
* The factor of production that brings together the other three factors (capital, labour, and natural resources) to produce goods and services for profit.
* Involves taking risks, identifying business opportunities, and organizing production.

# Human Capital and Labour Legislation

## 1. Human Capital and Economic Value
* Human capital increases as a worker's experience in doing their job increases.
* This makes the worker more valuable to the business.
* It also makes the worker more valuable to other businesses because they can use their acquired skills and experience to secure a higher position elsewhere.
* Therefore, human capital holds distinct economic value for both workers and businesses.

## 2. Key South African Labour Legislation
* **Labour Relations Act (LRA):** Promotes social justice, peace, and democracy in the workplace.
* **Basic Conditions of Employment Act (BCEA):** Protects employees from exploitation by their employers by regulating aspects such as working hours, leave, pay, and retrenchment.
* **Occupational Health and Safety Act (OHS Act):** Specifies safe and healthy workplace standards and outlines how employers must ensure their operations do not compromise employee health.
* **Compensation for Occupational Injuries and Diseases Act (COIDA):** Specifies how employers must compensate employees and their families if workers fall ill, get injured, or die while performing their jobs.

## 3. Factors of Production and Remuneration

### The Four Factors of Production
The production process uses four primary factors to produce goods and services:
1. **Labour**
2. **Capital**
3. **Land**
4. **Entrepreneurship**

### Remuneration for Factors of Production
Factors of production receive remuneration (rewards) for their contribution to the production process:
* **Land:** Receives **rent**. 
  * *Example:* A development company owns an office complex, and businesses use the office space in return for a monthly rental payment.
* **Labour:** Receives **salaries or wages**. 
  * *Example:* A manager receives a salary for running a call centre, while a farm worker receives wages for harvesting fruit.
* **Capital:** Receives **interest**. 
  * *Example:* A farmer borrows money to buy a combine harvester for a commercial farm.

## 4. Importance of Fair Employment Practices
* **Definition:** Human capital is the knowledge and skills that workers acquire through working. As experience increases, human capital grows, making it an essential asset to any business.
* **Fair Treatment:** Businesses must treat workers fairly. In South Africa, unfair employment practices are illegal.
* **Business Impact:** Treating employees unfairly is detrimental to business success:
  * Mistreated employees will not perform quality work or remain loyal.
  * They fail to develop professionally into better assets for the business.
  * They may treat customers poorly, which can ultimately cause the business to lose money.

## 5. FAIR LABOUR PRACTICES

Fair employment practices focus on good labour relations. Labour relations are the ways in which employers and employees relate to each other in the workplace.

### Labour Relations Act (LRA)
Promotes social justice, peace and democracy in the workplace. This law protects workers and people looking for employment against unfair labour practices and allows workers to join trade unions.

### Basic Conditions of Employment Act (BCEA)
The BCEA protects employees from exploitation by their employers by regulating working hours, leave, overtime, minimum wages and retrenchment.

### Occupational Health and Safety Act (OHSA)
Specifies what safe and healthy workplaces are and how employers should make sure that their business do not damage their employees' health.

### Compensation for Occupational Injuries and Diseases Act (COIDA)
COIDA specifies how employers should compensate employees and their families if employees get sick or injured or die while doing their jobs.

### Commission for Conciliation, Mediation and Arbitration (CCMA)
The CCMA is an organisation that is independent of the government, any political party or organisation so that it can make unbiased judgements. Their role is to resolve labour disputes referred to it in terms of the Labour Relations Act. (The CCMA attempts to resolve conflict between an employer and employee).

Each factor of production is remunerated in a unique way.

### Remuneration of labour
**Salaries and wages** is the remuneration for the use of labour.

### Remunerations for the use of natural resources
**Rent** is the remuneration for the use of natural resources.

### Remuneration for the use of capital
**Interest** is the remuneration for the use of capital.

### Remuneration for Entrepreneurship
**Profit** is the remuneration for entrepreneurship.
