# THE ECONOMY: TYPES OF MARKETS

## Overview
In this topic, learners will explore **The Economy**, focusing specifically on different types of markets, such as:
* Goods and services market
* Factor market (including labour and financial markets)

Learners should begin to realise that markets are not strictly confined to physical spaces. There are different types of markets where various exchanges take place, such as the **Johannesburg Stock Exchange (JSE)**.

---

## Topic: Types of Markets

### Meaning of a Market
A **market** is a place where buyers and sellers meet to exchange information about prices and quantities of goods and services to be exchanged between them. 

There are **TWO** main types of markets:
1. Goods and services market
2. Factor market

---

### 1. Goods and Services Market
It is a place where products (such as vehicles, clothing, and stationery) or services (such as garden services, banking services, and telephone services) are sold. A market can be a physical place, like a shop, or a virtual place, such as a website on the internet.

#### Different Types of Goods and Services Markets:
* **Wholesale Market:** Wholesalers purchase **large** quantities of goods, repack the goods into smaller quantities, and sell them to customers. A wholesaler’s customers are usually smaller businesses called **retailers**.
* **Retail Market:** Retailers can buy goods directly from manufacturers or wholesalers. They can also buy goods in large quantities and re-sell them in smaller quantities to consumers. Retail stores include supermarkets, department stores, and specialist stores.
* **Farmer, Craft, and Flea Market:** Most of these markets are open once or twice a week (e.g., Boksburg Flea Market). People can sell their craft items/products to the local community. Farmers' markets sell fruit, vegetables, meat, and dairy products directly from the farmer to the local community. Craft markets often sell various types of handmade products like biscuits, bags, jewellery, clothing, and wooden toys.
* **Services Market:** This is where a service, expertise, skills, or experience is sold (e.g., a doctor sells his/her skills, knowledge, and experience of medicine to a patient). A service market can be situated in an office or a shop (e.g., doctors, lawyers, hairdressers) or it can go to the client (e.g., garden services, electricians, plumbers, carpenters). **A service is something intangible.**

---

### 2. Factor Market
The **factor market** is where people buy and sell the **factors of production**:
* Natural resources (Land)
* Labour
* Capital
* Entrepreneurship

---

# Different Types of Factor Markets

## 1. Labour Market

* **Definition of Labour:** Labour is the physical or mental work done by an employee.
* **Nature of the Market:** This is a place where the employer and employee come into contact (note that this is not a specific physical location). Employers advertise job vacancies in newspapers, magazines, or on the internet, or they can pay an employment agency to find someone to fill the position.
* **Exchange:** Employers buy employees' time, knowledge, and experiences, and the business pays them money in the form of salaries and wages.
    * **Salary:** A set amount of money that is paid at the end of every month.
    * **Wage:** An hourly rate paid at the end of the week according to the number of hours worked. Wages are usually paid to semi-skilled and unskilled workers.

---

## 2. Financial Market

* **Definition:** These are markets where people or companies trade financial assets such as stocks and shares. It includes any type of financial transaction that helps a business grow and helps an investor make money. An example of a financial market is the **Johannesburg Stock Exchange (JSE)**.

There are **TWO** types of financial markets:

### A. Capital Market
* **Description:** Money is obtained to start a large business in the form of **shares**. The company uses this money to purchase assets, pay for labour, and cover operating costs. 
* **Stakeholders:** The holders of the shares are **stakeholders**. 
* **Dividend:** Stakeholders receive a dividend, which is the portion of the profit that shareholders receive according to the number of shares they own.

### B. Money Market
* **Description:** A business can obtain cash or credit here. This can take the form of a loan from a bank (cash) or credit from a supplier or bank overdraft.
* **Key Forms of Credit & Funding:**
    * **Bank loan:** The money the bank loans to a business. The business repays a specific amount over a specified number of months until the debt plus interest is repaid.
    * **Trade credit:** When a supplier allows the business to take goods and only pay for them at the end of the month or after 60 days.
    * **Bank overdraft:** A facility where the bank allows a business to withdraw more money than they have in their cheque account up to a stipulated maximum amount. The business pays interest on the amount overdrawn for the number of days it was overdrawn.

## Extension: Understanding Markets

### 'Markets are not confined to a specific place.' Explain this statement.
Markets do not have to be concrete, tangible entities. For example, you can buy airtime online. You do not physically go to a specific place and hand over money to buy a box full of airtime, and you cannot see or touch airtime. This is still a transaction on the **goods and services market**. 

Similarly, you can sell factors of production, such as your labour or entrepreneurship, in exchange for income or profits. You do not always do this at a physical market (as the goods and services market sometimes is); you do this on the **factor market**, which is the market where people buy and sell the services of the factors of production (productive resources). Some productive resources are intangible, like labour.

### Explain how the factor market is different from the goods and services market.
- **Goods and Services Market:** Households are the main buyers, purchasing finished goods and services that businesses and the government produce.
- **Factor Market:** Businesses and the government are the main buyers, purchasing the factors of production from households. 
  - *Example 1:* When you rent a warehouse to a business as business premises, you are not selling the land to the business. You are selling a service of the land—its ability to allow a business to produce goods there.
  - *Example 2:* When entrepreneurship is exchanged on the factor market, people are not buying and selling entrepreneurs; they are buying and selling the entrepreneurs' ideas and the potential of those business ideas to make a profit.

### 5.6 Factors of Production vs. Their Services

**Question (Middle Order):** 
Why do we say that we buy and sell the services of the factors of production on the factor market and not the factors of production themselves? Give an example to explain your answer. (3 marks)

**Model Answer & Explanation:**
* When a business buys your labour on the factor market, it is not buying *you*. 
* Instead, it is buying your **services**—specifically, your ability to work.
