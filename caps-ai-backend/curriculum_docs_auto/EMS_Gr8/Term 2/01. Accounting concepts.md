# Financial Literacy: Introduction to Accounting Concepts

## 1. Core Concepts
Financial literacy is the foundation of Economic and Management Sciences (EMS). Understanding these terms is essential for managing personal and business finances.

*   **Capital:** The money or assets invested by the owner to start or run a business.
*   **Assets:** Items of value that a business or individual owns.
    *   *Fixed Assets:* Long-term items (e.g., buildings, vehicles).
    *   *Current Assets:* Short-term items (e.g., cash, stock).
*   **Liabilities:** Debts or obligations that a business or individual owes to others.
    *   *Long-term Liabilities:* Debts payable over a long period (e.g., bank loans).
    *   *Current Liabilities:* Debts payable in the short term.
*   **Income:** Money received by a business from its operations (e.g., sales).
*   **Expenses:** Costs incurred to operate the business (e.g., rent, electricity, salaries).
*   **Profit:** The financial gain when income exceeds expenses.
    *   Formula: $\text{Profit} = \text{Income} - \text{Expenses}$ (where $\text{Income} > \text{Expenses}$)
*   **Losses:** The financial deficit when expenses exceed income.
    *   Formula: $\text{Loss} = \text{Expenses} - \text{Income}$ (where $\text{Expenses} > \text{Income}$)
*   **Budgets:** A plan for future income and spending.
*   **Savings:** Money set aside for future use.
*   **Banking:** Financial institutions that provide services such as holding deposits, providing loans, and facilitating payments.
*   **Financial Records:** Documents that track financial transactions (e.g., till slips, invoices).

---

## 2. The Importance of Financial Records
Keeping accurate financial records is vital for:
*   **Proof of Purchase:** Serving as evidence for guarantees or warranties (e.g., returning a faulty cellphone).
*   **Accountability:** Tracking where money is spent.
*   **Business Management:** Helping owners make informed decisions about the future of the business.

#### Guidance for Budgeting
When planning your financial future, you must distinguish between **needs** (essential expenses) and **wants** (leisure/dreams). Your budget should be divided into a **Spend** column and a **Save** column.

| Category | Examples of Expenses/Savings |
| :--- | :--- |

#### Financial Best Practices
To manage your finances effectively, consider the following strategies:
1.  **Savings Vehicles:** Utilize savings accounts, unit trusts, or notice deposit accounts.
2.  **Debt Management:** Avoid overusing credit. Be aware that financing purchases (like cars or electronics) incurs interest, which increases the total cost beyond the original capital.
3.  **Core Principles:**
    *   Never spend more than you earn.
    *   Prioritize buying what you **need** before what you **want**.
    *   Pay off all debt to remain debt-free.
    *   Always maintain a cash reserve for basic needs and emergencies.

### Financial Literacy and Accounting Concepts

#### 1. Services Provided by Banks
Banks offer a variety of services to assist individuals and businesses in managing their finances. Ten key services include:
1. Cellphone and Internet banking
2. Providing bank balances
3. Issuing bank statements
4. Facilitating payments to third parties
5. Providing exchange rate enquiries
6. Opening and closing accounts
7. Processing loan applications
8. Drafting wills
9. Financial planning
10. Retirement planning

#### 2. Importance of Financial Records
It is essential for businesses to maintain accurate financial records for the following reasons:
* **Fraud Prevention:** Records act as a control mechanism to prevent theft or mismanagement of funds.
* **SARS Compliance:** Businesses must provide documentation to the South African Revenue Services (SARS) to prove that declared earnings are accurate.
* **Legal Requirement:** It is illegal for a business to fail to keep records of its earnings.

#### 3. Financial Records vs. Financial Transactions
* **Financial Records:** These are the documented history of a business's financial transactions.
* **Financial Transactions:** These are the specific events where buyers and sellers exchange assets (goods or services) for money.
* **Note:** Every time an action occurs between a person and a business involving an exchange, a **transaction** takes place.

#### 4. Examples of Financial Records
Common examples of documents used to record financial activity include:
* Till slips
* Bank deposit slips
* Payslips
* Payrolls
* Credit notes
* Electronic payment notifications

#### 5. Budgeting
Budgeting is the process of planning how to spend, save, or borrow money. It allows individuals and businesses to learn from past financial performance and plan for future long-term goals.

# Financial Concepts and Terminology

## Assets
**Assets** are possessions of the business that could be converted into cash.

1. **Fixed assets**
   - Possessions that will not change for more than one year.
   - Examples:
     - Land and buildings
     - Vehicles
     - Equipment
     - Financial assets

2. **Current assets**
   - Possessions that change during the course of the year.
   - Examples:
     - Trading stock
     - Bank account
     - Petty cash
     - Debtors control
     - Cash floats

---

## Liabilities
**Liabilities** are the debts that a business or individual has to pay. 
- Can be created in different ways: 
  1. Bank loan 
  2. Lay buy 
  3. Overdraft

- **Long-term liabilities**: Debts that will be paid back within 12 months (mortgage bonds, vehicle repayments).
- **Current liabilities**: Debts that will be paid back within one year (clothing accounts, bank overdrafts).

---

## Income
**Income** is the payment you receive for selling goods, your time, services, or investing your money.

- **Service business**: Business that generates income through providing a service.
- **Retail business**: Business that generates income through selling or trading goods.

---

## Expenses
**Expenses** are the money you spend to buy in goods or services provided by someone else.
- To get an income, the business must pay for certain things.

---

## Profit
**Profit** occurs when the income is more than the expenses (outcome).

---

## Losses
A **Loss** occurs when a business's expenses are higher than its income.

# Financial Literacy and Business Concepts

## 1. Budgets
* **Definition:** The best way to prevent a loss is to plan how to spend the money that comes into the business through a careful estimate of all costs and expenses.

## 2. Savings
* **Definition:** Any money left over after you have paid all your expenses.
* **Banking (Historical Context):** Long ago, people kept their money in a safe place in their house, but this was risky as anyone could steal it.
* **Banking (Present Day):** There are many secure places where people can keep their money safe, known as **financial institutions** (such as banks).

## 3. Financial Records
* **Definition:** A diary or record kept of financial events which helps to understand the financial position or performance of a person or the business.
* **Example:** A budget is an example of a financial record.

## 4. Transactions
* **Definition:** An exchange between parties.
* **Note:** In a business, it is important to have proof of every transaction in the form of a source document.
