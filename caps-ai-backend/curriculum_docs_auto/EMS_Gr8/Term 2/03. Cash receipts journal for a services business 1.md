# Financial Literacy: Cash Receipts Journal (CRJ) of a Services Business

## Introduction to the Cash Receipts Journal (CRJ)
In this topic, learners will look at:
* Cash Receipts Journal (CRJ) of a services business
* Formats and uses of the columns in the CRJ
* Source documents used to complete the CRJ
* Entering of cash transactions in the CRJ
* Balancing of the CRJ
* The effect of cash transactions on the accounting equation

---

## Formats and Uses of Columns in the CRJ
* **Document Number:** Records the source document number (e.g., receipt number, deposit slip number).
* **Day:** Records the date of the transaction.
* **Details:** Records the source of the cash received (e.g., customer name, bank name).
* **Folio:** Used for referencing to the general ledger.
* **Analysis of Receipts:** Records every amount received per source document. This column shows the breakdown of cash inflows, not the total cash received.
* **Bank:** Records the total amount of cash deposited into the bank for the day.
* **Current Income:** Records cash received from current income sources, such as sales or services rendered.
* **Sundry Accounts:** Records cash received for other items or transactions not classified under current income or sales.

---

## How It Works: Step-by-Step Guide
1. **Record Transactions:** Each cash receipt is recorded in a separate row of the CRJ.
2. **Document the Source:** The details column should include the name of the person or entity providing the cash, or the type of transaction.
3. **Analyze Receipts:** The analysis of receipts column breaks down the cash received from different sources (e.g., sales, capital contributions, loan).
4. **Bank the Receipts:** The bank column records the total cash deposited into the bank for the day.
5. **Update General Ledger:** The CRJ is then used to update the general ledger accounts.

---

## Balancing the CRJ
* The CRJ is balanced when the total of the bank column equals the sum of all other columns (analysis of receipts, sundry accounts, etc.).
* The same total amount should appear on both sides of the CRJ.

---

Example Transactions:

* **Capital Contribution:**
  When the owner invests money into the business, it's recorded as capital or owner's capital in the sundry accounts column.

* **Cash Sales:**
  Sales of goods or services for cash are recorded in the current income and bank columns.

* **Loan Received:**
  When a business receives a loan, it's recorded in the sundry accounts and bank columns.

* **Interest Received:**
  Interest earned from bank accounts or investments is recorded in the sundry accounts and bank columns.

The **Cash Receipts Journal (CRJ)** is the special book of first entry where a business records **all money it receives**. For a service business, this is mainly money from providing services, but also includes things like capital from the owner or loan money. 💵 ➡️

---

## Format and Columns of the CRJ

The CRJ is a table with several columns, each with a specific job.

* **Doc (Document):** The source document number for proof.
* **Day:** The date the cash was received.
* **Details:** The name of the person or business you received the money from and reason.
* **Analysis of Receipts:** Records every amount received per source document. This column shows the breakdown of cash inflows, not the total cash received.
* **Bank:** This is the most important column! It shows the total amount of money received and deposited into the bank.
* **Current Income:** A special column used only for money earned from providing your normal services (e.g., fees for haircuts, lawn mowing, etc.).
* **Sundry Accounts:** This means "various other accounts." It's a general-purpose column for all other types of money received that are **not** for your normal services.

---

This includes things like capital contributions from the owner or receiving money from a loan.

## Source Documents for the CRJ

You can't record anything without proof! The source documents for the CRJ are:

* **Duplicate Receipts:** The copy you keep after giving the original to a customer who paid you.
* **Cash Register Roll / Till Slip:** The paper roll from the cash register that summarizes all the cash sales for the day.
* **Duplicate Deposit Slip:** The stamped copy you get from the bank as proof that you deposited money into your account.
* **Bank Statement:** Used to record money that went directly into your account, like interest earned.

## Entering Transactions in the CRJ

Let's record a transaction. **On the 5th of the month, you received $\$150$ from T. Moyo for services provided. Your receipt number was $001$.**

1. **Doc:** Write the receipt number, $001$.
2. **Day:** Write the date, $5$.
3. **Details:** Services rendered to T. Moyo
4. **Analysis of Receipts:** $150$.
5. **Bank:** Write the total amount received, $150$.
6. **Current Income:** Since this money is for your normal service, you also write $150$ in this column. The Sundry Accounts column is left blank.

## "Details" Column

The **Details** column is used to write the **name of the specific account** that is affected.

---

Since the **Sundry Accounts** column is a catch-all for many different types of transactions (anything that isn't Current Income), you need this column to specify exactly what the transaction was for.

* **Example (in a CRJ):** If the owner puts $\$5,000$ of their own money into the business, the "Details" column would say **Capital**.
* **Example (in a CPJ):** If the owner takes $\$200$ for personal use, the "Details" column would say **Drawings**.

Without this column, you would just have a list of amounts with no explanation of what they were for.

---

## "Fol." (Folio) Column

The **Fol.** column stands for **Folio**, which means page number or reference.

Its job is to create a link between the journal and the General Ledger. This process is called **cross-referencing**.

When you post an amount from the Sundry Accounts column to its specific T-account in the General Ledger (for example, you post the $\$5,000$ to the "Capital" T-account), you then write the account number of that T-account (e.g., B1) in the Fol. column.

* **Purpose:** It acts like an address. It shows exactly where the amount was posted to in the General Ledger, creating a clear trail that anyone can follow. ✅

## Balancing the CRJ

At the end of the month, you must check that your journal is correct. This is called balancing or proving the journal.

1. **Add up** every column that contains money (Bank, Current Income, and Sundry Accounts).
2. **Check your work** using this simple rule:

$$\text{Total of Bank column} = \text{Total of Current Income column} + \text{Total of Sundry Accounts column}$$

If the left side equals the right side, your journal is balanced! ✅

---

## Effect on the Accounting Equation

Every transaction in the CRJ affects the accounting equation ($\text{Assets} = \text{Owner's Equity} + \text{Liabilities}$).

---

Receiving cash **always** increases your **Assets** because your Bank account balance goes up. 
The other effect depends on *why* you received the money:

* **If you received money for services (Current Income):**
  * **Assets** increase (Bank goes up).
  * **Owner's Equity** increases (because income makes the owner's worth grow).

* **If the owner contributes Capital (Sundry Account):**
  * **Assets** increase (Bank goes up).
  * **Owner's Equity** increases (because the owner has invested more).

* **If you receive a Loan (Sundry Account):**
  * **Assets** increase (Bank goes up).
  * **Liabilities** increase (because you now owe the bank money).

## Example 1: Receiving money for services

This is the most common type of transaction in a service business's CRJ.

**The Transaction:** On the 15th of the month, the business receives $\$350$ from a customer, **L. Zulu**, for services provided. The business issues receipt number **003**.

**Explanation of the Entry:**

* **Doc:** The receipt number, **003**, is entered as proof.
* **Day:** The day the money was received, **15**, is entered.
* **Details:** Services rendered to **L. Zulu**, is written so you know who paid you.
* **Analysis of Receipts:** $350$
* **Bank:** The total amount of money received, $\$350$, goes into this column.
* **Current Income:** Because this money was earned from your normal services, the $\$350$ is also recorded here.
* **Sundry Accounts:** This section is left blank because the transaction fits into the special "Current Income" column.

## Example 2: Receiving money for another reason (Sundry)

This example shows how to record money that is **not** from your normal day-to-day services.

**The Transaction:** On the 28th of the month, the business takes out a **loan** from **ABC Bank** for **$5,000** to buy new equipment. The money is deposited directly into the account. The source document is the bank statement, **BS2**.

### Explanation of the Entry:

* **Doc:** The source document number, **BS2**, is entered.
* **Day:** The day the money appeared in the account, **28**, is entered.
* **Details:** The name of the bank, **ABC Bank**, is written
* **Analysis of Receipts:** You write **5000** to describe the transaction.
* **Bank:** The total amount received, **$5,000**, goes into this column.
* **Current Income:** This column is left blank because a loan is not income you earned from services.
* **Sundry Accounts:** The **$5,000** is entered in the "Amt" (amount) column, and the specific account name, **Loan**, is written in the "Details" column. The "Fo." (Folio) column is left blank until it's posted to the General Ledger.

# Cash Receipts Journal (CRJ) - Instructional Guide

## 1. Introduction and Materials Required
* **Exercise Book:** Learners must each have an exercise book called an 'Eight money column' or 'Cash money column' to complete the Cash Receipts Journal (CRJ).
* **Subsidiary Journals:** There are two main subsidiary journals covered in this topic: the CRJ and the CPJ (Cash Payments Journal). This section focuses exclusively on the **CRJ** for recording receipts only.

---

## 2. Purpose of the CRJ
* The primary purpose of the CRJ is to **reduce entries in the General Ledger**, making bookkeeping more efficient.

---

## 3. Heading Requirements for the CRJ
The heading of each CRJ must include:
1. The name of the business.
2. The month in which the transactions take place.
3. The number of the journal (e.g., since this is the business's first month of trading, the number will be **CRJ1**).

---

## 4. Recording Transactions in the CRJ (Worked Explanations)

* **1 Dec:** 
  * The receipt is issued to a client (e.g., L Marais). Her name is written in the **Details** column. 
  * The amount is deposited directly into the bank; therefore, there is **no entry** in the *Analysis of receipts* column, only in the **Bank** and **Sundry accounts** columns.

* **5, 11, 21 Dec:** 
  * A cash register roll is normally issued, but since this business does not yet have a cash register, they will issue a **cash invoice**. 
  * Because these are cash transactions, they write "Services rendered" in the details. 
  * The amounts appear in the **Analysis of receipts** and **Current income** columns. Since these were the only transactions on those respective days, the amounts appear individually in the **Bank** column as well.

* **28 Dec:** 
  * **First transaction:** An increase of the owner's capital contribution. It is not deposited directly and is therefore entered in the **Analysis of receipts** and **Sundry account** columns.
  * **Second transaction:** For Services rendered; the amount appears in the **Analysis of receipts** and **Current income** columns.
  * **Total Deposit:** The total of the two transactions is deposited as one combined amount (e.g., R740) in the **Bank** column.

---

## 5. Key Rules for Entering Transactions

* **Single Purpose:** This journal is strictly for entering **receipts**.
* **Analysis of Receipts:** 
  * Each transaction is recorded in the *Analysis of receipts* column, **except** when the transaction indicates that the money is deposited directly in the bank.
* **Allocation:** 
  * After the analysis, the transaction is allocated to a specific analytical column (for example, *Current income*). 
  * If there isn't a specific column for it, it is allocated to the **Sundry accounts** column (for example, *Capital* or *Rent income*).
* **Banking (Bank Column):** 
  * Each amount is deposited or banked daily and entered in the *Bank* column. 
  * When more than one transaction happens on the same day, the total of all the transactions of that same day must be added together and entered in the *Bank* column.

---

## 6. Cross-Checking and Skills Development
* **Cross-Checking:** Teachers must instruct learners on how to cross-check the totals of the columns of the CRJ with one another to ensure mathematical accuracy.
* **Curriculum Importance:** The completion of a CRJ is a fundamental accounting skill that learners must master properly, as they will be expected to complete CRJs continuously up to **Grade 12**.

---

- The number of analysis columns for which provision must be made depends on the requirements of each individual business and is determined by the nature and type of business.
- Provision is always made for sundry accounts in the journal because it is not possible to provide individual analysis columns for every type of receipt.
- If a receipt is issued, the name of the person or business to whom it is issued appears in the details.
- If you issue a cash invoice or use the cash register roll as a source document, the details of the transaction appear in the Details column, for example Services rendered or Sales.
- Cross-checking: The total of the Bank column must be equal to the total of the Current income column plus the total of the Sundry accounts column.

### Consolidation

* Complete the following sentences by filling in the correct word.
- In the CRJ we only enter cash **receipts**.
- A transaction in the CRJ will always be entered in the **Analysis of receipts** column and in one other column, except when the transaction indicates that the deposit was **directly** banked.
- Each amount in the CRJ is deposited or banked **daily**. When there is more than one transaction on the same day, the total of all the transactions of the same day must be added together and entered in the **Bank** column.

- If you issued a cash invoice or used a cash register roll as a source document, the details of the **transaction** appear in the Details column as Services rendered or Sales.
- A CRJ summarises **similar** transactions. It also **decreases** the number of transactions entered in the General Ledger.
- **Cross-checking** is done after you have completed the entries for a specific month in order to see whether all the money received, has been banked. You do it by **adding** the totals of all the columns plus the total of the **Sundry** accounts and this must be equal to the total of the Bank column. – The following has to appear in the heading: the **name** of the business, the **month** in which the transactions took place and the **number** of the journal.
