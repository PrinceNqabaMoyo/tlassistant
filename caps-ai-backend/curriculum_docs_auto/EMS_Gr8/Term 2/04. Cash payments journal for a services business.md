## Purpose of the Cash Payments Journal (CPJ)
The purpose of the CPJ is to record **every single payment** made by the business, either with physical cash or by cheque. It is the official "Cash Out" diary. 

* If money leaves the business, its first official entry is in the CPJ.
* The CPJ is exclusively for entering **PAYMENTS** only.

---

## Rules and Procedures for Recording in the CPJ

* **The Bank Column:** Each transaction (each cheque number) must be recorded in the Bank column. Therefore, for each cheque number, there must be an amount in the Bank column.
* **Allocation of Amounts:** The transaction amount is allocated to a specific analysis column, such as **Material costs** or **Wages**. If there is no dedicated column for a specific item, it is allocated to the **Sundry accounts** column (e.g., Telephone, Water, and Electricity).
* **Analysis Columns:** The number of analysis columns required depends on the specific needs of each individual business and is determined by its nature and type.
* **Sundry Accounts Provision:** Provision is always made for Sundry accounts in the journal because it is not possible to provide individual analysis columns for every type of payment.
* **Name of Payee:** In the *Name of Payee* column, write the name of the person or business to whom the cheque was issued. For expenses like Wages or Cash float, a cash cheque is always issued.
* **Cross-Checking Rule:** The total of the Bank column must be equal to the sum of the totals of the analysis columns (such as Material costs and Wages) plus the total of the Sundry accounts column.

$$\text{Total Bank} = \text{Sum of Analysis Columns} + \text{Total Sundry Accounts}$$

## Consolidation and Review Questions

* **Name the source documents used in the CPJ:** 
  Cheque and cheque counterfoil.
* **Name three items that must appear in the heading of a CPJ:** 
  1. Name of the business
  2. Month in which the transactions take place
  3. Number of the journal
* **Explain how you cross-check a CPJ:** 
  By adding together all the totals of the analysis columns (such as Wages, Stationery, and Consumable goods) and the Sundry accounts column, which must equal the total of the Bank column.
* **Name one important rule for completing a CPJ:** 
  The amount for every transaction (and its cheque number) must appear in the Bank column and in at least one other column. If the business bought two different items, the transaction amounts may appear in more than two columns.

### Compare and Contrast: CPJ vs. CRJ

| Feature | Cash Payments Journal (CPJ) | Cash Receipts Journal (CRJ) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Purpose | To record all **payments** (cash going out). | To record all **receipts** (cash coming in). |
| Effect on Bank | **Decreases** the bank balance. | **Increases** the bank balance. |
| Source Documents | Cheque counterfoils, original receipts you receive. | Duplicate receipts you issue, till slips. |
| Main Focus | Recording Expenses, Drawings, and buying Assets. | Recording Income and Capital. |

Export to Sheets

* **Doc (Document):** The cheque number (e.g., 101) or other source document number.
* **Day:** The date the payment was made.
* **Details (Payee):** The name of the person or business you paid.
* **Bank:** The main money column, showing the total amount paid out.
* **Special Columns (e.g., Wages, Consumable goods, Stationery):** These columns are for frequent payments. Instead of writing "Wages" every week in the Sundry column, you have a dedicated column to save time.
* **Sundry Accounts:** The "various others" column for payments that do not have their own special column. This includes things like buying equipment, owner's drawings, or paying for repairs.

## Source Documents for the CPJ

You need proof for every payment. The source documents for the CPJ are:

* **Cheque Counterfoil:** The stub left in the chequebook. This is the most common source document.
* **Original Receipt:** The receipt you get from a shop when you buy something for cash.
* **Original Cash Invoice:** A detailed bill you receive when paying for something in cash.
* **Bank Statement:** Used for payments that go off your account automatically, like bank charges or a debit order.

## Closing Off (Balancing) the CPJ

At the end of the month, you must prove the journal is mathematically correct.
1. Add up every column that contains money (Bank, Wages, Stationery, and Sundry Accounts).
2. Apply the balancing rule: 
   **Total of Bank column = The sum of the totals of ALL other money columns.** (e.g., Total of Wages + Total of Stationery + Total of Sundry Accounts)

If the totals match, the journal is balanced. ✅

## Effect on the Accounting Equation

Every transaction in the CPJ **decreases your Assets** (because your Bank account balance goes down). The second effect depends on the reason for the payment:

| Type of Payment | Effect on the Equation ($\text{Assets} = \text{Owner's Equity} + \text{Liabilities}$) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Paying an Expense**<br>(e.g., Wages, Rent, Stationery) | **Assets** ⬇️<br>**Owner's Equity** ⬇️ (Expenses reduce profit, which reduces the owner's worth) |
| **Buying an Asset**<br>(e.g., Equipment, Vehicle) | **Assets have no overall change!**<br>*(One asset, Bank, decreases while another asset, Equipment, increases by the same amount).* |
| **Owner takes Drawings**<br>(Money for personal use) | **Assets** ⬇️<br>**Owner's Equity** ⬇️ (Drawings reduce the owner's investment) |
| **Paying off a Liability**<br>(e.g., Repaying a loan) | **Assets** ⬇️<br>**Liabilities** ⬇️ (The amount you owe others goes down) |

# Managing Cash Flow: Cash Receipts Journal (CRJ) and Cash Payments Journal (CPJ)

## Overview of Cash Journals

Both the **Cash Receipts Journal (CRJ)** and the **Cash Payments Journal (CPJ)** are essential accounting tools for managing a business's cash flow. 

* **Cash Receipts Journal (CRJ):** Tracks all cash coming into the business.
* **Cash Payments Journal (CPJ):** Tracks all cash going out of the business.

Together, they provide a complete picture of the cash movements for the month.
