## Introduction to Financial Literacy

In this topic, learners explore **Financial Literacy** by studying the complete accounting cycle for a service business. The curriculum covers the following key components:

* Transactions
* Source documents
* Subsidiary journals
* The General Ledger
* The Trial Balance
* The Income Statement
* The Balance Sheet
* The Cash Journals of a service business (their purpose and importance)

### The Accounting Cycle

The accounting cycle is the step-by-step process businesses use to record and understand their financial activities. It represents the story of a business's money from start to finish for a specific period (such as a month or a year).

---

## Transactions and Source Documents

### Definitions

* **Transaction:** Any financial event that affects a business. Examples include making a sale, paying for electricity, or buying equipment.
* **Source Document:** The original paper or digital proof that a transaction happened. It serves as evidence, and no transaction can be recorded without a source document.

---

- **Receipts:** Proof of a cash sale or payment.
- **Invoices:** A request for payment for services you provided.
- **Bank Statements:** A summary of all money that went in and out of the business bank account.

---

## Subsidiary Journals

These are the **first books** where we record transactions from source documents. We sort transactions by type into different journals. For a Grade 8 service business, we focus on the two main cash journals.

- **Cash Receipts Journal (CRJ):** This journal is used to record **all money received** by the business. If cash comes in, it's recorded here.
- **Cash Payments Journal (CPJ):** This journal is used to record **all money paid out** by the business. If cash goes out, it's recorded here.

---

## The General Ledger

The General Ledger is the "main book" of accounts. After recording transactions in the journals, we **post** (transfer) the totals to the General Ledger.

Think of the ledger as a set of folders. Each account (like Bank, Sales, Rent Expense, Capital) has its own "folder," usually in the shape of a **T-account**. This helps us see the total activity for each specific account.

| Bank (Asset) |
|---|
| **Debit (+)** | **Credit (-)** |
| | |

---

## The Trial Balance

The Trial Balance is a list of all the accounts from the General Ledger and their final balances.

Its main purpose is to **check for mathematical errors** and make sure that our accounting equation is in balance. We do this by proving that:

**Total Debits = Total Credits** ✅

If they don't balance, we know there's a mistake somewhere in the journals or ledger that needs to be fixed.

---

## Financial Statements

These are the final reports that summarize the business's performance and financial position.

### Income Statement

The Income Statement shows if the business made a **profit** or a **loss** over a period of time (e.g., a month). It's sometimes called the "Statement of Financial Performance."

It has a simple formula: **Income - Expenses = Net Profit (or Net Loss)**

It tells you if the business's operations were successful during that period. 🏆

### Balance Sheet

The Balance Sheet shows the business's financial position on a **single specific day**. It's a snapshot 📸 of what the business **owns** and what it **owes**. It's sometimes called the "Statement of Financial Position."

It is a statement that proves the accounting equation is true: **Assets = Owner's Equity + Liabilities**

* **Assets:** What the business owns (e.g., Bank, Equipment).
* **Liabilities:** What the business owes to others (e.g., Loans).
* **Owner's Equity:** The owner's investment and worth in the business.

---

## Purpose and Importance of Cash Journals

For a service business (like a hairdresser, lawn-mowing service, or car wash), the **Cash Receipts Journal (CRJ)** and **Cash Payments Journal (CPJ)** are extremely important.

* **Purpose:** Their main purpose is to provide a detailed, day-by-day record of all cash that flows in and out of the business. The CRJ tracks all income, while the CPJ tracks all expenses paid with cash.

* **Importance:**
  1. **Cash Management:** Cash is the lifeblood of a small business. These journals help the owner see exactly where cash is coming from and where it's going, preventing them from running out of money.
  2. **Error Control:** Recording all transactions daily helps reduce the chance of mistakes or forgetting a transaction.

---

3. **Decision Making**: By looking at the journals, an owner can quickly see if they are earning enough cash to cover their payments. This helps them make smart decisions, like whether they can afford to buy new equipment or need to cut costs.

## Balance Sheet Accounts vs. Nominal Accounts

To understand the tables, you first need to know the difference between these two types of accounts.

### Balance Sheet Accounts

These are considered **permanent accounts**. Their balances are carried forward from one year to the next. They represent the financial position of the business.

Balance Sheet accounts include:

* **Assets** (what the business owns, e.g., Bank, Equipment)
* **Liabilities** (what the business owes, e.g., Loans)
* **Owner's Equity** (the owner's investment, e.g., Capital and Drawings)

### Nominal Accounts

---

These are considered **temporary accounts**. Their job is to track the business's performance (profit or loss) **for the current financial period only**. At the end of the period, their balances are closed off to calculate the profit.

Nominal accounts include:

* **Incomes** (money earned, e.g., Current Income)
* **Expenses** (running costs, e.g., Telephone, Rent, Wages)

---

## Applying This to the Examples

Now let's see why the accounts in your tables were classified the way they were.

* **Transaction 1: Owner's Capital Contribution**
  * The General Ledger account is **Capital**.
  * **How to decide:** Is Capital an Asset, Liability, Owner's Equity, Income, or Expense? It's the owner's investment, so it's **Owner's Equity**.
  * **Conclusion:** Because it's an Owner's Equity account, it is a **Balance Sheet** account.

* **Transaction 2: Paid the Telephone Account**
  * The General Ledger account is **Telephone**.
  * **How to decide:** Is Telephone an Asset, Liability, Owner's Equity, Income, or Expense? It's a day-to-day running cost, so it's an **Expense**.
  * **Conclusion:** Because it's an Expense, it is a **Nominal account**.

---

## From Trial Balance to Financial Statements

The rule for this final step is very simple and always the same:

1. All **Nominal accounts** (Incomes and Expenses) are used to create the **Income Statement**. 📝
   * This is because the purpose of the Income Statement is to calculate the profit by comparing all income against all expenses.

2. All **Balance Sheet accounts** (Assets, Liabilities, Owner's Equity) are used to create the **Balance Sheet**. ⚖️
   * This is because the purpose of the Balance Sheet is to show the overall financial position of the business on a specific day.

---

That is why in your examples, the **Nominal account** (Telephone) goes to the **Income Statement**, and the **Balance Sheet account** (Capital) goes to the **Balance Sheet**.
