A **source document** is the original proof that a financial transaction took place. It's the starting point of the accounting cycle because it provides all the details needed to record an event, like the date, the amount, and the reason for the transaction. The rule is simple: **no source document, no entry in the books!** 🚫📝

---

## Receipts

A receipt is a written acknowledgement that a specific amount of money has been received.

* **Purpose:** To provide proof of payment for both the buyer and the seller.
* **The Flow:**
  * When your business **receives cash**, you issue the **original receipt** to the customer and keep a **duplicate (copy)** for your records. This duplicate is your source document for the **Cash Receipts Journal (CRJ)**.
  * When your business **pays cash** for something, you receive an **original receipt**. This is your source document for the **Cash Payments Journal (CPJ)**.

## Deposit Slips

A deposit slip is a form you fill out to deposit cash or cheques into a bank account.

* **Purpose:** To provide proof that money was physically handed over to the bank for safekeeping in your account.

---

* **The Flow:** You fill out the slip and give it to the bank teller with your money. The teller stamps a copy and gives it back to you. This stamped copy is your source document.
* **Journal:** It confirms the "Bank" column entry in the **CRJ**.

## Cash Register Slips (Till Slips)

This is the slip printed by a cash register (or "till") when a customer pays for goods or services.

* **Purpose:** To act as proof of a cash sale for the customer. For the business, the summary of all the day's slips (the till roll) is used to record the total sales for the day.
* **The Flow:** The customer gets the original slip. The business keeps the internal electronic or paper roll.
* **Journal:** The daily total from the till roll is the source document for a single entry for "Cash Sales" in the **CRJ**.

---

## Cheques and Cheque Counterfoils

A **cheque** is a written order from you to your bank, instructing it to pay a specific person or business. A **cheque counterfoil** is the small stub that remains in the chequebook after the cheque is torn out.

* **Purpose:** The cheque is used to make a payment safely without using physical cash. The **counterfoil** is the business's crucial internal record of that payment.
* **The Flow:** The cheque is given to the person you are paying. The business keeps the counterfoil.
* **Journal:** The cheque counterfoil is the source document for recording the payment in the **Cash Payments Journal (CPJ)**.

## Bank Statements

A bank statement is a monthly summary from the bank showing all activity in your account.

* **Purpose:** To check your own records against the bank's records. It also acts as a source document for transactions you might not know about immediately, like bank charges, interest earned, or direct payments from customers.
* **The Flow:** The bank provides it to the business.
* **Journal:** Used to record bank charges in the **CPJ** and interest received in the **CRJ**.

---

## Cash Invoices

An invoice is a document that lists the goods or services provided, including quantities and prices. A **cash invoice** is specifically for a sale where payment is made immediately.

* **Purpose:** It's like a detailed receipt, providing more information than a simple till slip.
* **The Flow:** A seller gives a cash invoice to a buyer when payment is made on the spot.
* **Journal:**
  * If you **sell** something for cash and issue a cash invoice, your copy is a source document for the **CRJ**.
  * If you **buy** something for cash and receive a cash invoice, it's your source document for the **CPJ**.

## Source Documents in Accounting

### 1. Key Concepts and Definitions

Source documents form an integral part of the accounting cycle. They serve as objective proof that a transaction has taken place and provide the necessary information to record transactions in the books of the business.

#### Types of Source Documents

* **2. Deposit Slip:** 
  * A source document that serves as proof that money has been deposited into the bank account.
  * It is completed in duplicate; the bank teller issues the duplicate deposit slip to the depositor and keeps the original.

* **3. Cash Register Slip:** 
  * A source document which shows that money has been received for goods sold or services rendered.

* **4. Electronic Funds Transfers (EFT):** 
  * Money transferred electronically from one person or institution to another, as a payment for buying goods and services, using computers, cellphones, etc. 
  * Buyers and sellers do not meet physically and may not know each other.

* **5. Bank Statements:** 
  * A statement prepared by the bank which shows a list of all the transactions of the business with the bank over a specific period.

* **6. Cash Invoice:** 
  * A source document that shows goods or services have been paid for immediately (used when a business does not have a cash register).

---

### 2. Application and Usage Guide

When working with source documents, keep the practice as realistic as possible. At school level, specific source documents are used for specific types of transactions:

* **Receipts / Cash Register Rolls:** Used for services rendered and sales. If a business does not have a cash register, they use a cash invoice. If the business receives money for rent income or capital contributions, they issue a receipt.

---

### 3. Summary of Source Documents and Transactions

| Source Document | Transaction Type / Purpose |
| :--- | :--- |

| Cash register roll | • Current income<br>• Sales |

| Cheque and cheque counterfoil | • Purchases of fixed assets<br>• Purchases of current assets<br>• Payment of expenses |
| Cash invoice (when a business doesn't have a cash register) | • Current income<br>• Sales |

## Extension

### Scenario 1: Rendering a Service to a Customer
When Business ABC renders a service to a customer, more than one source document is involved:

* **Name the source document(s) that Business ABC will issue to the customer:**
  * Cash (register) slip or Cash invoice (original)
* **Name the source document that will be issued by the customer if he doesn't pay in cash:**
  * Cheque
* **Name the source document that Business ABC will receive from the bank when the money is deposited:**
  * Deposit slip (duplicate)
* **Name the source document that Business ABC will use for the entry in the books of the business for services rendered:**
  * Cash register roll

---

### Scenario 2: Buying Stationery
When Business ABC buys stationery at a stationery shop to use in the business, more than one source document is involved:

* **Name the source document(s) that the stationery shop will issue to Business ABC:**
  * Cash (register) slip or Cash invoice (original)
* **Name the source document that Business ABC will issue when they pay for the stationery:**
  * Cheque
* **Name the source document that Business ABC will use for the entry in the books of the business:**
  * Cheque counterfoil
