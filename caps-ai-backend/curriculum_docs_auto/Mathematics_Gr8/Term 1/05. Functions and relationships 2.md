## 21.1 Calculating Output Values

### Formulae and Tables
A **formula** is a mathematical description of how the values of a dependent variable can be calculated for any given values of the other variable(s) on which it depends.

For example, the statement $y = 2x + 6$ is a formula where $y$ is calculated based on the chosen value of $x$.

### Representing Functions

Functions can be represented in four different ways:
1.  **A formula:** e.g., $y = 2x + 2$
2.  **A table:** Showing input ($x$) and output ($y$) values.
3.  **A flow diagram:** Visualizing the operations performed on the input.
4.  **A verbal representation:** Describing the process in words.

**(b) Calculate the interior angle for:**
*   A polygon with 20 sides: $b = 180^\circ - \frac{360^\circ}{20} = 180^\circ - 18^\circ = 162^\circ$
*   A polygon with 120 sides: $b = 180^\circ - \frac{360^\circ}{120} = 180^\circ - 3^\circ = 177^\circ$

# 12.2 What to Calculate and How: Representing Situations Mathematically

## Teaching Guidelines
When solving "word" sums, learners must translate real-world situations into mathematical formulae. Encourage learners to ask:
1. What do I have to calculate?
2. What information is available to use?
3. Which is the **dependent variable** and which is the **independent variable**? (e.g., the cost of a bus trip depends on the distance travelled, not the other way around).

## Notes on the Questions
Learners should be guided through the process of describing situations mathematically. Help them test different formulae to determine which ones accurately describe the situation and which do not, and why.

### From Patterns to Formulae

#### Key Concepts
*   **Pattern Recognition:** Identifying the constant difference or relationship between terms in a sequence.
*   **Variables:** Using letters (like $x, y, z, n$) to represent changing quantities in a pattern.
*   **Formulae:** Mathematical expressions that allow you to calculate any term in a sequence based on its position or a related variable.

---

#### Teaching Guidelines
*   If learners struggle to find a formula from a geometric representation, encourage them to:
    1.  Write out the sequence of numbers formed by the pattern.
    2.  Create a table to describe the relationship between the position and the value.
*   **Common Misconception:** Learners often confuse the "position number" of the pattern with the "side length" of the shape. Always clarify the difference between these two variables before attempting to derive a formula.
