### Analyzing Relationships and Rates of Change

#### 3. Comparing Output Changes
The input numbers for different relationships may be identical, but the output numbers change in distinct ways depending on the nature of the relationship (e.g., linear, inverse, or exponential).

#### 4. Bar Graph Shapes
The shape of a bar graph provides a visual representation of how the output values change. 
*   **Constant rate of change:** Results in a straight line.
*   **Changing rate of change:** Results in a curve.

#### 5. Calculating Differences in Output Values
To determine how output values change, calculate the difference between consecutive terms.

**(a) Sally’s Savings**
*   Sequence: $4, 8, 12, 16, \dots$
*   Difference: $8 - 4 = 4$; $12 - 8 = 4$
*   **Conclusion:** Sally’s savings grow by **4** every week. This is a **constant rate of change**.

**(b) Chocolates per Friend**
*   Sequence: $24, 12, 8, \dots$
*   Difference: $12 - 24 = -12$; $8 - 12 = -4$
*   **Conclusion:** The number of chocolates per friend decreases rapidly at first and then changes slower. The **rate of change is not constant**.

**(c) Vusi’s Payment per Bag**
*   Sequence: $1, 2, 4, 8, \dots$
*   Difference: $2 - 1 = 1$; $4 - 2 = 2$; $8 - 4 = 4$
*   **Conclusion:** The amount grows slowly at first and then **faster**. The **rate of change increases**.

**(d) Areas of Judy’s Squares**
*   Sequence: $1, 4, 9, 16, \dots$
*   Difference: $4 - 1 = 3$; $9 - 4 = 5$; $16 - 9 = 7$
*   **Conclusion:** The area of the squares **increases faster and faster**.

---

### Key Concepts: Rate of Change

*   **Rate of Change:** Defines how fast or slow a change occurs per unit of time or input.
*   **Constant Rate of Change:** When the difference between consecutive output values remains the same.
*   **Non-Constant Rate of Change:** When the difference between consecutive output values changes (e.g., increasing or decreasing).

#### Relationship Between Shape and Rate
| Rate of Change | Shape of Graph |
| :--- | :--- |
| Constant | Straight line |
| Changing | Curve |

#### 3. Understanding Types of Change

When investigating the growth or change in a relationship, we observe how the output numbers change:

*   **Constant increase:** The value grows by the same amount each time (e.g., Perimeter of a square).
*   **Constant decrease:** The value shrinks by the same amount each time (e.g., Length of a burning candle).
*   **Non-constant increase (accelerating):** The value increases faster and faster (e.g., Area of a square as side length increases).
*   **Non-constant decrease (accelerating):** The value decreases faster and faster (e.g., Amount of money per person as more friends share a cost).

#### Concept: Interpreting Rate of Change
When filling containers, the shape of the container determines the "steepness" of the graph:
*   **Constant width:** The height increases at a constant rate, resulting in a **linear graph** (a straight line).
*   **Varying width:** If the container gets wider as it fills, the height increases more slowly (the graph curves). If it gets narrower, the height increases more quickly.

## Mathematical Background

A graph is a visual tool used to display information and reveal trends. Graphs are essential in business, government, and daily life to represent data and study patterns.

### Key Concepts
*   **The "Story" of a Graph:** The focus is on understanding the narrative or trend a graph conveys, rather than just plotting points or using formulas.
*   **Variables:** Identifying the variables represented in the graph and understanding how they relate to each other.
*   **Rate of Change:** The direction of a graph (increasing or decreasing) is determined by the rate of change of the output values.
    *   **Steepness:** The rate of change is directly related to the steepness of the graph.
    *   **Foundation:** Understanding rate of change in this context prepares learners for the formal concept of a **gradient** in later grades.
*   **Interpreting vs. Drawing:**
    *   **Interpreting:** Making up a story that matches the information shown by the graph.
    *   **Drawing:** Representing a known story in a graph, ensuring all elements of the story are included and accurately reflected.

### Understanding Rate of Change and Graph Shapes

#### Key Concepts
*   **Rate of Change:** This describes how fast or slow a change happens per unit of time (or per input).
*   **Constant Rate of Change:** When the difference between consecutive output values remains the same. On a bar graph, this results in a **straight line**.
*   **Changing Rate of Change:** When the difference between consecutive output values is not the same. On a bar graph, this results in a **curve**.

---

#### Analysis of Relationships

The following table summarizes how output values change based on the input:

| Scenario | Pattern of Change | Rate of Change | Graph Shape |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **(a) Sally's Savings** | Increases by 4 each week | Constant | Straight line (increasing) |
| **(b) Chocolates per friend** | Decreases rapidly, then slows down | Not constant | Curve (sloping downwards) |
| **(c) Vusi's payment** | Increases slowly, then faster and faster | Not constant | Curve (sloping upwards) |
| **(d) Area of squares** | Increases by larger amounts each time | Not constant | Curve (sloping upwards) |

---

#### Worked Examples: Calculating Differences

To determine the rate of change, calculate the difference between consecutive output values:

**(a) Sally's Savings**
Sequence: $4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 28, 32$
Differences: $(8-4)=4, (12-8)=4, (16-12)=4 \dots$
*Result:* The savings grow by a **constant amount** of $4$ every week.

**(b) Chocolates per friend**
Sequence: $24, 12, 8, 6, 4.8, 4, 3.4, 3$
Differences: $(12-24)=-12, (8-12)=-4, (6-8)=-2 \dots$
*Result:* The number of chocolates **decreases rapidly and then the decrease becomes slower**.

**(c) Vusi's payment per bag**
Sequence: $1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64$
Differences: $(2-1)=1, (4-2)=2, (8-4)=4, (16-8)=8 \dots$
*Result:* The amount grows slowly at first and then **increases faster and faster**.

**(d) Areas of squares**
Sequence: $1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49$
Differences: $(4-1)=3, (9-4)=5, (16-9)=7, (25-16)=9 \dots$
*Result:* The area of the squares **increases faster and faster**.

---

#### Summary Rules for Graphs
1.  **Constant Rate:** If the rate of change is constant, the tops of the bars in a graph form a **straight line**.
2.  **Changing Rate:** If the rate of change is changing, the shape of the graph is a **curve**.
    *   If the rate increases, the graph slants upwards.
    *   If the rate decreases, the graph slants downwards.

# Change and Rate of Change

## Key Concepts: Understanding Rate of Change
The rate of change of output values in a relationship directly influences the visual appearance of its graph:

*   **Steepness:** The higher the rate of change, the faster the output numbers change, and the steeper the graph appears.
*   **Linearity:** If the rate of change is constant, the graph is a straight line (linear).
*   **Non-linearity:** If the rate of change is not constant, the graph is a curve.
*   **Direction:** 
    *   If the rate of change is increasing, the graph slopes upward from left to right.
    *   If the rate of change is decreasing, the graph slopes downward from left to right.
*   **Constant Relationships:** If the rate of change is $0$, the relationship is constant, and the graph is a horizontal straight line.

### Understanding Rates of Change and Graphs

When investigating growth or change in a relationship, we analyze how the output numbers change. This change can be categorized into different types of patterns.

#### Types of Change
1.  **Constant Increase:** The value increases by the same amount at each step (e.g., the perimeter of a square as the side length increases).
2.  **Constant Decrease:** The value decreases by the same amount at each step (e.g., the length of a burning candle).
3.  **Non-Constant Increase (Increasing faster and faster):** The value grows at an accelerating rate (e.g., the area of a square as the side length increases).
4.  **Non-Constant Decrease (Decreasing faster and faster):** The value shrinks at an accelerating rate (e.g., the amount of money each friend pays as more friends share a cost).

### Interpreting Graphs: Linear and Non-Linear Relationships

#### Key Concepts
*   **Linear Graph:** A graph where the increase or decrease is constant, resulting in a straight line.
*   **Non-Linear Graph:** A graph where the increase or decrease is not constant, resulting in a curved line.
*   **Constant Values:** If there is no change in the output variable, the graph is a straight horizontal line.

#### Visualizing Trends
*   **Increase:** The graph slopes upward.
    *   Linear: A straight upward slope.
    *   Non-Linear: A curved upward slope.
*   **Decrease:** The graph slopes downward.
    *   Linear: A straight downward slope.
    *   Non-Linear: A curved downward slope.

### Summary of Mathematical Concepts
*   **Independent Variable:** Usually plotted on the horizontal axis ($x$-axis), such as *Time* or *Distance*.
*   **Dependent Variable:** Usually plotted on the vertical axis ($y$-axis), such as *Speed*, *Rainfall (mm)*, or *Water Level (mm)*.
*   **Rate of Change:** The steepness of the line (gradient) indicates how quickly the dependent variable is changing relative to the independent variable. A horizontal line indicates a rate of change of $0$.
