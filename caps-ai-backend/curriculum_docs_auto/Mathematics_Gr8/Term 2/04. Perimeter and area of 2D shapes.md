# Chapter 9: Perimeter and Area of 2D Shapes

## 9.1 Perimeter of Polygons

### Definition
The **perimeter** ($P$) of a shape is the total distance around the outside of the shape. It is calculated by adding the lengths of all its sides together.

**Units of Measurement:**
Perimeter is measured in linear units, such as:
*   Millimetres ($\text{mm}$)
*   Centimetres ($\text{cm}$)
*   Metres ($\text{m}$)

# 9.2 Perimeter formulae

Perimeter is the total distance around the outside of a 2D shape.

### 1. Square
If the sides of a square are all $s$ units long:
*   **Perimeter of square** $= s + s + s + s$
*   $= 4 \times s$
*   or $P = 4s$

### 2. Rectangle
If the length of a rectangle is $l$ units and the breadth (width) is $b$ units:
*   **Perimeter of rectangle** $= l + l + b + b$
*   $= 2 \times l + 2 \times b$
*   $= 2l + 2b$
*   or $P = 2(l + b)$

### 3. Triangle
A triangle has three sides ($s_1, s_2, s_3$):
*   **Perimeter of triangle** $= s_1 + s_2 + s_3$
*   or $P = s_1 + s_2 + s_3$

# 9.3 Area and square units

### Definition
The **area** of a shape is the size of the flat surface surrounded by the border (perimeter) of the shape.

### Measurement
Area ($A$) is measured in square units. Common units include:
*   Square millimetres ($\text{mm}^2$)
*   Square centimetres ($\text{cm}^2$)
*   Square metres ($\text{m}^2$)

### Conversion of Units

The figure on the right shows a square with sides of $1\text{ cm}$. The area of the square is one square centimetre ($1\text{ cm}^2$).



![Textbook_Mathematics_Gr8_Geometry-of-2D-shapes.pdf-0003-19.png](images/Textbook_Mathematics_Gr8_Geometry-of-2D-shapes.pdf-0003-19.png)


#### Converting between $\text{cm}^2$ and $\text{mm}^2$
To change $\text{cm}^2$ to $\text{mm}^2$:
$$1\text{ cm}^2 = 1\text{ cm} \times 1\text{ cm}$$
$$= 10\text{ mm} \times 10\text{ mm}$$
$$= 100\text{ mm}^2$$

To change $\text{mm}^2$ to $\text{cm}^2$:
$$1\text{ mm}^2 = 1\text{ mm} \times 1\text{ mm}$$
$$= 0,1\text{ cm} \times 0,1\text{ cm}$$
$$= 0,01\text{ cm}^2$$

#### Conversion Summary Table

| From $\text{m}^2$ to $\text{cm}^2$ | From $\text{cm}^2$ to $\text{m}^2$ |
| :--- | :--- |
| $1\text{ m}^2 = 1\text{ m} \times 1\text{ m}$ | $1\text{ cm}^2 = 1\text{ cm} \times 1\text{ cm}$ |
| $= 100\text{ cm} \times 100\text{ cm}$ | $= 0,01\text{ m} \times 0,01\text{ m}$ |
| $= 10\ 000\text{ cm}^2$ | $= 0,0001\text{ m}^2$ |

#### Conversion Rules
*   $\text{cm}^2$ to $\text{mm}^2 \rightarrow$ multiply by $100$
*   $\text{mm}^2$ to $\text{cm}^2 \rightarrow$ divide by $100$
*   $\text{m}^2$ to $\text{cm}^2 \rightarrow$ multiply by $10\ 000$
*   $\text{cm}^2$ to $\text{m}^2 \rightarrow$ divide by $10\ 000$

### Calculating Area of Rectangles and Squares

A quick way to calculate the number of squares that fit into a rectangle is to multiply the number of squares along the length by the number of squares along the breadth.



![Textbook_Mathematics_Gr8_Geometry-of-2D-shapes.pdf-0005-01.png](images/Textbook_Mathematics_Gr8_Geometry-of-2D-shapes.pdf-0005-01.png)
> **[Diagram Context - Textbook_Mathematics_Gr8_Geometry-of-2D-shapes.pdf-0005-01.png]**
> This geometry figure depicts a triangle labeled ABC with side lengths of 8 cm for AC and 6 cm for AB, along with angle markings at vertices A and B. For a high school student studying mathematics, the diagram illustrates the properties of an isosceles triangle where equal side lengths imply equal base angles (angle A equals angle B).



#### Formulae

**1. Area of a Rectangle**
The area ($A$) is calculated by multiplying the length ($l$) by the breadth ($b$):
$$A = l \times b$$

**2. Area of a Square**
Since all sides of a square are equal, the area ($A$) is calculated by multiplying the length of the side ($l$) by itself:
$$A = l \times l = l^2$$

---

#### Units of Measurement
When calculating area, all dimensions must be in the same unit. Use the following conversions:

*   $1\text{ m} = 100\text{ cm}$
*   $1\text{ cm} = 10\text{ mm}$
*   $1\text{ cm}^2 = 1\text{ cm} \times 1\text{ cm} = 10\text{ mm} \times 10\text{ mm} = 100\text{ mm}^2$
*   $1\text{ m}^2 = 1\text{ m} \times 1\text{ m} = 100\text{ cm} \times 100\text{ cm} = 10\text{ 000 cm}^2$
*   $1\text{ mm}^2 = 1\text{ mm} \times 1\text{ mm} = 0,1\text{ cm} \times 0,1\text{ cm} = 0,01\text{ cm}^2$
*   $1\text{ cm}^2 = 1\text{ cm} \times 1\text{ cm} = 0,01\text{ m} \times 0,01\text{ m} = 0,0001\text{ m}^2$

---

#### Worked Example

**Question:** Calculate the area of a rectangle with a length of $50\text{ mm}$ and a breadth of $3\text{ cm}$. Give the answer in $\text{cm}^2$.

**Solution:**
First, ensure units are consistent. Convert $50\text{ mm}$ to $\text{cm}$:
$50\text{ mm} = 5\text{ cm}$

Using the formula $A = l \times b$:
$$A = 5\text{ cm} \times 3\text{ cm}$$
$$A = 15\text{ cm}^2$$

### Formula: Area of a Triangle

To understand the area of a triangle, consider a rectangle $ABCD$ with a length ($l$) of $5\text{ cm}$ and a breadth ($b$) of $3\text{ cm}$. When diagonal $AC$ is drawn, it divides the rectangle into two equal triangles: $\Delta ABC$ and $\Delta ADC$.



![Textbook_Mathematics_Gr8_Geometry-of-2D-shapes.pdf-0010-00.png](images/Textbook_Mathematics_Gr8_Geometry-of-2D-shapes.pdf-0010-00.png)


#### Derivation
1. The area of a rectangle is calculated as:
   $$\text{Area} = l \times b$$
2. Since the triangle is exactly half of the rectangle:
   $$\text{Area of } \Delta ABC = \frac{1}{2} \times (\text{Area of rectangle})$$
   $$\text{Area of } \Delta ABC = \frac{1}{2} \times (l \times b)$$
3. In the context of a triangle, the length of the rectangle becomes the **base** ($b$) and the breadth becomes the **perpendicular height** ($h$).

#### The Area Formula
The general formula for the area of any triangle is:
$$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} \times (\text{base} \times \text{height})$$
$$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} \times (b \times h)$$

**Note:** In this formula, $b$ represents the base and $h$ represents the perpendicular height.

---

#### 3. Finding the Base of a Triangle
**Problem:** In $\triangle ABC$, the area is $42\text{ m}^2$ and the perpendicular height is $16\text{ m}$. Find the length of the base.

2.  Substitute the known values:

## Mathematical Background

### 1. Perimeter
The **perimeter** is the distance around the border of a shape. It is calculated by adding the lengths of all sides or by using specific formulae.

*   **Perimeter of a rectangle:** $P = 2 \times \text{length} + 2 \times \text{breadth}$
*   **Perimeter of a square:** $P = 4 \times \text{side length}$

### 2. Area
The **area** is a measure of the surface a shape covers, expressed in square units (e.g., $cm^2$).

*   **Area of a rectangle:** $A = \text{length} \times \text{breadth}$ ($A = l \times b$)
*   **Area of a square:** $A = \text{side} \times \text{side}$ ($A = s \times s$)

### 3. Area of Triangles
Any triangle can fit inside a rectangle that has the same base and height. The area of a triangle is exactly half the area of that rectangle.

*   **Formula:** $A = \frac{1}{2} \times b \times h$
    *   Where $b$ is the base.
    *   Where $h$ is the perpendicular height (altitude).

**Key Concepts:**
*   **Irregular shapes:** These are composite shapes made up of squares and rectangles. Their area is found by dividing them into smaller, regular shapes.
*   **Triangles:** The altitude (height) divides a triangle into two right-angled triangles. By calculating the area of the corresponding rectangles and halving them, we prove the formula $A = \frac{1}{2} \times b \times h$ applies to all triangles, including obtuse-angled triangles.

## Key Concepts and Definitions

Perimeter is the total distance around the outside of a 2D shape. It is calculated by adding the lengths of all sides together.

# 9.3 Area and Square Units

### Definition
**Area** is the size of a flat surface surrounded by the border (perimeter) of a shape.

### Measuring Area
Area ($A$) is measured in square units. Common standard units include:
*   Square millimetres ($\text{mm}^2$)
*   Square centimetres ($\text{cm}^2$)
*   Square metres ($\text{m}^2$)

---

### Teaching Guidelines
To help learners understand the concept of area:
1.  **Visualization:** Encourage learners to imagine covering a surface with a material like paint or paper.
2.  **Distinguishing Concepts:** Ensure learners do not confuse **perimeter** (the distance around the edge) with **area** (the space inside).
    *   Ask: "How long is the perimeter?" (Linear measurement)
    *   Ask: "How many units will cover the shape?" (Area measurement)
3.  **Collaborative Learning:** Encourage class discussions regarding solution methods to reinforce the correct application of these concepts.

#### Conversion Factors
To convert between units of area, use the following rules:

| Conversion | Operation |
| :--- | :--- |
| $cm^2$ to $mm^2$ | Multiply by $100$ |
| $mm^2$ to $cm^2$ | Divide by $100$ |
| $m^2$ to $cm^2$ | Multiply by $10\,000$ |
| $cm^2$ to $m^2$ | Divide by $10\,000$ |

---

#### Worked Examples

**1. Converting $cm^2$ to $mm^2$:**
Since $1\ cm = 10\ mm$:
$$1\ cm^2 = 1\ cm \times 1\ cm$$
$$1\ cm^2 = 10\ mm \times 10\ mm = 100\ mm^2$$

**2. Converting $mm^2$ to $cm^2$:**
$$1\ mm^2 = 1\ mm \times 1\ mm$$
$$1\ mm^2 = 0,1\ cm \times 0,1\ cm = 0,01\ cm^2$$

**3. Converting $m^2$ to $cm^2$:**
Since $1\ m = 100\ cm$:
$$1\ m^2 = 1\ m \times 1\ m$$
$$1\ m^2 = 100\ cm \times 100\ cm = 10\,000\ cm^2$$

# Area of Rectangles and Squares

## Concept Overview
To calculate the area of a rectangle, determine how many square units fit in a row along the length, and how many of these rows fit along the breadth.

### Formulas
*   **Area of a rectangle:** $A = l \times b$
    *   Where $A$ is the area, $l$ is the length, and $b$ is the breadth.
*   **Area of a square:** $A = l \times l = l^{2}$
    *   Where $A$ is the area and $l$ is the length of a side.

When calculating area, ensure all dimensions use the same units. Use the following conversions:

| Area (m to cm) | $1\text{ m}^{2} = 1\text{ m} \times 1\text{ m} = 100\text{ cm} \times 100\text{ cm} = 10\,000\text{ cm}^{2}$ |
| Area (mm to cm) | $1\text{ mm}^{2} = 1\text{ mm} \times 1\text{ mm} = 0,1\text{ cm} \times 0,1\text{ cm} = 0,01\text{ cm}^{2}$ |

### Formula: Area of a Triangle

#### Conceptual Understanding
The area of a triangle is exactly half the area of a rectangle that shares the same base and height. 

**Derivation:**
1. Consider a rectangle with length ($l$) and breadth ($b$).
2. The area of this rectangle is $A = l \times b$.
3. If you draw a diagonal across the rectangle, it splits the rectangle into two equal triangles.
4. Therefore, the area of one triangle is half the area of the rectangle:
   $$A = \frac{1}{2} \times (l \times b)$$



![Textbook_Mathematics_Gr8_Geometry-of-2D-shapes.pdf-0024-02.png](images/Textbook_Mathematics_Gr8_Geometry-of-2D-shapes.pdf-0024-02.png)

#### The Area Formula
In a triangle, we refer to the length as the **base** ($b$) and the breadth as the **perpendicular height** ($h$). The height must always be the line drawn at a right angle ($90^\circ$) from the base to the opposite vertex.

### Teacher Guidelines & Common Misconceptions

*   **Visualizing the concept:** Use grid paper to demonstrate that a triangle occupies half the space of a rectangle with the same dimensions. Have learners cut out rectangles and slice them along the diagonal to physically prove the two resulting triangles are equal.
*   **Identifying the height:** Ensure learners understand that the height ($h$) must be perpendicular to the base. 
*   **Common Error:** In triangles where the height is drawn outside the base (such as in obtuse triangles), learners often mistakenly use the slanted side length instead of the perpendicular height. Always emphasize that $h$ is the vertical distance from the base to the highest point.

#### 3. Finding the Base when Area is Known
**Problem:** In $\Delta ABC$, the area is $42\text{ m}^2$ and the perpendicular height is $16\text{ m}$. Find the length of the base.

**Solution:**
*   Formula: $A = \frac{1}{2}(b \times h)$
*   Substitute known values: $42 = \frac{1}{2}(b \times 16)$
*   Rearrange to solve for $b$:
    $$b = 42 \times 2 \div 16$$
    $$b = 5,25\text{ m}$$

# Perimeter and Area of 2D Shapes

## 1. Concepts and Definitions

*   **Perimeter ($P$):** The total distance around the outside of a 2D shape.
*   **Area ($A$):** The amount of surface covered by a 2D shape.

### Formulas
*   **Square:**
    *   $P = 4 \times \text{side}$
    *   $A = \text{side} \times \text{side}$
*   **Rectangle:**
    *   $P = 2(l + b)$
    *   $A = l \times b$
*   **Triangle:**
    *   $A = \frac{1}{2}(b \times h)$
