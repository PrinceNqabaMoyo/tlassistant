# Square Grid Paper

## Concept Overview
Square grid paper is a mathematical tool used for geometric constructions, plotting coordinates, drawing shapes to scale, and calculating area and perimeter.

### Specifications
*   **Unit Size:** Each individual square on this grid measures $1\text{ cm} \times 1\text{ cm}$.
*   **Area of one square:** The area of a single grid cell is calculated as:
    $$\text{Area} = \text{side} \times \text{side}$$
    $$\text{Area} = 1\text{ cm} \times 1\text{ cm} = 1\text{ cm}^2$$

## Applications
1.  **Geometry:** Drawing polygons (triangles, rectangles, squares) with specific dimensions.
2.  **Cartesian Plane:** Plotting points $(x, y)$ on a coordinate system.
3.  **Measurement:** Estimating the area of irregular shapes by counting the number of squares covered.
4.  **Transformations:** Performing translations, reflections, and rotations of shapes.

# Dotted Paper

Dotted paper is a versatile mathematical tool used for geometric constructions, drawing shapes, and exploring patterns. It provides a grid of equidistant points that act as vertices for polygons or reference points for transformations.



![Textbook_Mathematics_Gr8_Construction-of-geometric-figures.pdf-0003-03.png](images/Textbook_Mathematics_Gr8_Construction-of-geometric-figures.pdf-0003-03.png)
> **[Diagram Context - Textbook_Mathematics_Gr8_Construction-of-geometric-figures.pdf-0003-03.png]**
> This geometric construction diagram illustrates the use of a pair of compasses to draw arcs intersecting a straight line, labeled with points P, A, and B. The visual shows a grey compass instrument positioned above a horizontal line, with pencil marks and arc intersections marked at letters A and B. Conceptually, this demonstrates fundamental Euclidean geometry construction techniques used in CAPS Mathematics for bisecting lines, constructing angles, or transferring lengths.



### Instructional Uses
*   **Geometry:** Drawing 2D shapes such as triangles, quadrilaterals, and polygons by connecting the dots.
*   **Transformations:** Practicing translations, reflections, and rotations by counting the units between dots.
*   **Area and Perimeter:** Calculating the area of shapes by counting the number of unit squares formed by the dots, or using Pick's Theorem:
    $$Area = i + \frac{b}{2} - 1$$
    Where:
    *   $i$ = number of interior dots
    *   $b$ = number of boundary dots
*   **Patterns:** Creating tessellations and exploring symmetry.

### Tips for Learners
1.  **Use a Ruler:** Always use a straight edge to connect dots to ensure accuracy in your geometric constructions.
2.  **Orientation:** You can rotate the paper to explore different perspectives of a shape.
3.  **Scale:** Define the distance between two adjacent dots as $1$ unit to assist in measuring lengths and calculating areas.
