# Chapter 17: Geometry of 3D Objects

## 17.1 Classifying 3D Objects

Three-dimensional (3D) objects possess three dimensions: **length**, **width**, and **height**. These objects are categorized into two main groups based on their surfaces:

1.  **Objects with curved surfaces:** These include shapes like spheres (balls), cylinders, and cones.
2.  **Polyhedra:** These are objects made entirely of flat surfaces.

## What is a Polyhedron?

A **polyhedron** is a 3D object composed exclusively of flat surfaces. It contains no curved surfaces. Every polyhedron is defined by three specific components:

*   **Face:** A flat surface of a 3D object.
*   **Edge:** The line segment where two faces of a polyhedron intersect.
*   **Vertex:** The point where the edges meet.

### Terminology Note
*   **Singular:** One polyhedron; one vertex.
*   **Plural:** Two or more polyhedra; two or more vertices.

---

### Identifying and Describing 3D Objects

3D objects (three-dimensional objects) are defined by their **faces** (flat surfaces), **edges** (where two faces meet), and **vertices** (corners where edges meet).

#### Key Examples
*   **Cube:** A cube has $6$ faces, $12$ edges, and $8$ vertices.
*   **Pyramid:** A square-based pyramid has $5$ faces, $8$ edges, and $5$ vertices.

#### Difference Between Prisms and Pyramids

Prisms and pyramids are two special groups of **polyhedra** (3D objects with flat faces).

##### Prisms
A **prism** is a polyhedron with two faces that are congruent and parallel polygons. 

*   **Bases:** The two congruent and parallel faces.
*   **Lateral faces:** The faces that connect the bases (these are parallelograms).
*   **Congruent:** Means exactly the same shape and size.

**Right Prisms:**
In a right prism, the bases are connected by rectangles that are perpendicular to the base. This means the lateral faces make a $90^{\circ}$ angle with the bases.

**Naming Prisms:**
Prisms are named according to the shape of their base:
*   **Triangular prism:** Base is a triangle.
*   **Rectangular prism:** Base is a rectangle or square.
*   **Pentagonal prism:** Base is a pentagon.
*   **Cube:** A special type of prism where all six faces are congruent; therefore, any face can be a base.

### Pyramids
A **pyramid** is a 3D object with only one base. The lateral faces (the sides) are always triangles that meet at a single common point called the **apex**.

#### Right Pyramids
In a **right pyramid**, the lateral faces are isosceles triangles.

#### Classification of Pyramids
Pyramids are named according to the shape of their base:

| Base Shape | Name of Pyramid |
| :--- | :--- |
| Triangle | Triangular-based pyramid (Triangular pyramid) |
| Square | Square-based pyramid (Square pyramid) |
| Hexagon | Hexagonal-based pyramid (Hexagonal pyramid) |

### Understanding Cube Nets

A **net** is a 2D shape that can be folded to form a 3D object. When folding a net into a cube, each square corresponds to a specific face of the cube.

#### Folding Process
To visualize how a net forms a cube, follow these steps:
1. **Front Face (F):** Establish your base or front face.
2. **Left Face (L):** The square to the left of the front face folds to become the left side.
3. **Back Face (B):** The square to the left of the left face folds to become the back face.
4. **Top Face (T):** The remaining square at the top folds to cover the cube.

## Mathematical Background

### Definitions
*   **3D Object:** An object that has length, breadth, and height, and takes up space.
*   **Polyhedra (singular: polyhedron):** 3D objects that have only flat surfaces.
*   **Cylinder:** An object shaped like a pipe with one curved surface and two flat circular faces.
*   **Net:** A flat 2D arrangement of all the faces that make up a 3D object when joined to each other at one side.

### Prisms
Prisms have identical faces at the top and bottom. All remaining side faces are rectangles or squares. The name of a prism is determined by the shape of its base (the face on which it rests).
*   *Example:* A prism with an octagon as a base is an octagonal prism.
*   *Note:* A cube is a special type of prism where all six faces are identical squares.

Pyramids rest on a 2D shape with straight sides. All remaining faces are isosceles triangles that meet at a single point called the **apex**.

# Chapter 17: Geometry of 3D Objects

## 17.1 Classifying 3D Objects

3D objects are objects that have three dimensions: **length**, **width**, and **height**. They are generally classified into two main groups:
1. Objects with **curved surfaces** (e.g., spheres, cylinders, and cones).
2. Objects with **flat surfaces only** (called **polyhedra**).

### Key Definitions
*   **Face:** A flat surface of a 3D object.
*   **Edge:** The line segment where two faces of a polyhedron meet.
    *   A **straight edge** is formed when two flat surfaces meet.
    *   A **curved edge** is formed when at least one of the two surfaces is curved.
*   **Vertex (plural: vertices):** The point where the edges of a polyhedron meet.

---

## What is a Polyhedron?

A **polyhedron** is a three-dimensional object made entirely of flat surfaces. It has no curved surfaces and consists of faces, edges, and vertices.

### Terminology Note
*   **One polyhedron:** The singular form.
*   **Two or more polyhedra:** The plural form.
*   **One vertex:** The singular point where edges meet.
*   **Two or more vertices:** The plural form.

---

*   **Faces:** The flat sides of the object.
*   **Edges:** The lines where two faces join.
*   **Vertices:** The corners or points where edges meet.
*   **Apex:** The top point of a pyramid (where the triangular faces meet).
*   **Base:** The bottom face upon which the object rests.

# Identifying Prisms and Pyramids

## Concept Overview
To identify 3D objects, it is essential to understand the properties of prisms and pyramids. A common misconception is that the "base" of an object is always the face that is drawn horizontally. In reality, the base is defined by the geometric properties of the shape.

### Key Definitions
*   **Prism:** A 3D object with two identical, parallel ends (bases) and flat sides that are parallelograms (usually rectangles).
*   **Pyramid:** A 3D object with a single base (which can be any polygon) and triangular sides that meet at a common point called the apex.

A net is a two-dimensional shape that can be folded to form a three-dimensional object. In the case of a cube, a net consists of six squares arranged in a specific pattern that, when folded along the edges, creates a closed cube.
