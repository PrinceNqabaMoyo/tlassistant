# Financial Literacy: Recording Payments to Creditors

## Topic Overview

In this topic, learners will explore **Financial Literacy**, focusing specifically on:
* Recording payments to creditors in the **Cash Payments Journal (CPJ)**.
* Posting transactions to the **Creditors Ledger** and **General Ledger**.
* Understanding the effect of credit transactions on the **accounting equation**.

---

## Required Resources

* An exercise book called an **'Eight money column'** or **'Cash money column'** for the CPJ and CJ.
* A **'Double Ledger'** book for the General Ledger.
* A **'Treble Cash'** book for the Creditors Ledger.
* Photocopies of the Cash Payments Journal, Creditors Journal, and Creditors/General Ledger templates (found at the end of the guide if necessary).
* A calculator for each learner.

---

## Teaching Guidelines & Steps

### 1. Revision: Buying on Credit & National Credit Act
* Remind learners briefly about the **National Credit Act (NCA)** and use it to revise the concept of buying on credit.
* Discuss the complete business cycle from the point where the business places an order until the order is paid.
* Talk through the **accounting cycle** and explain each step in sequence.

### 2. Source Document & Journal: Payments to Creditors
* **Source document – Cheque:** Show learners a cheque on the board and remind them how to complete it correctly.
* **Journal – Cash Payments Journal:** Show learners how to allocate that cheque payment within the journal:
  * In the **Bank** column.
  * In the **Creditors Control** column.

---

## Posting to Ledgers

### Posting from Source Documents to Ledgers
1. **Invoice from the Creditors Journal:** Posted to the **credit side** because it increases the creditor's account.
2. **Cheque from the Cash Payments Journal:** Posted to the **debit side** because it decreases the creditor's account.

### Creditors Ledger vs. General Ledger
* **Creditors Ledger:** Updated as soon as a transaction takes place. It shows the balance due after *each* transaction.
* **General Ledger:** Shows only the closing balance at the end of the month.

---

## Posting the Creditors Journal to the General Ledger

Discuss posting the Creditors Journal to the General Ledger for creditors, continuing with the example of **Chelsea Traders**, noting that there are two ways to explain this process.

---

1. The Creditors Control account has been credited because it increased (liabilities increase on the credit side) and according to the double-entry principle, all the other accounts are debited OR
2. Identify each account, for example: Trading Stock is an Asset that increased because we bought some stock or Stationery is an expense that decreased Owner's equity and is therefore debited.

* Emphasise the following:

### 1. Credit purchases of an Asset

### 2. Credit purchase of an Expense

| Creditors Control | Stationery |
| :--- | :--- |
| Liability increased because debt to a creditor increased | Stationery is an expense that decreased Owners' equity |

### 3. Payment of the creditor's account

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

| Creditors Control | Bank |
| :--- | :--- |
| Liability decreased because debt to a creditor decreased | Asset decreased because the Bank decreased by a payment |

---

* Review the Creditors Ledger and emphasise where we get the information from:

| Document | Source Document | Processing Rule | Effect on Balance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| CJ | Invoice | Entry in the credit column | Add amount to the balance |
| CPJ | Cheque | Entry in the debit column | Subtract amount from the balance |

---

* The business can settle creditors' accounts in three ways:
- **Settle accounts on the first of the month**, for example 1 April: Business pays the previous month's account in full to the creditor.
- **Settle accounts in full (up to date)**: Business pays the full amount that he owes up until that day in full.
- **Settle accounts in part**: Business only pays a part of the creditor's account.

## Informal Assessment

### Activity 15.1
* **Task:** Show the CPJ on an overhead/data projector while you work through each transaction. Then show the completed CJ from Activity 13.2 together with the CPJ. Explain step by step on the board how you post to the Creditors Ledger. Highlight the invoices in one colour and use the same colour in the credit column of the Creditors Ledger. Highlight the cheques in another colour and use the same colour in the debit column of the Creditors Ledger. Learners can mark their own work.
* **Marks:** (40)

### Activities 15.2–15.3
* **Task:** Show the CPJ on an overhead/data projector while you work through each transaction. Then show the completed CJ from Activity 13.3 together with the CPJ. Explain step by step how you post to the Creditors Ledger and the General Ledger, using different colours for each journal. 
* *Alternative approach:* Another way to mark Activity 15.3 is by using a handout. This gives learners who are struggling time to work at their own pace and you some time to check learners' books.
* **Marks:** (155)

### Activity 15.4
* **Task:** A good method of marking a General Ledger is by doing it on the board, as you explain where the answers come from. Learners' answers can also be marked by a partner using a handout for informal assessment class marks.
* **Marks:** (24)

### Activity 15.5
* **Task:** Learners do this activity on the board. Call one learner at a time to the board to complete a transaction, while you do corrections, if necessary. This is a good way to assess whether learners still struggle with the accounting equation and in what areas.
* **Marks:** (70)

## Consolidation

### Complete the following sentences:
* A **credit transaction** is a transaction where the business buys items on **credit**.
* A **creditor** is a **Current liability** because the business must pay back the debt within one year.
* After the business orders goods, it receives an **original invoice** with the goods.
* **Creditors** are suppliers to whom the business owes money.
