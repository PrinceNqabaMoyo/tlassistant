# Financial Literacy: Creditors and the Creditors Journal (CJ)

## Overview of Topics
In this topic, learners will explore **Financial Literacy**, focusing on:
* Creditors
* Creditors allowance
* The accounting cycle
* Recording of transactions in the **Creditors Journal (CJ)**

---

## 1. Introduction to Credit Transactions
* Discussion on buying on credit.
* Identifying who is the **debtor** and who is the **creditor** in credit transactions, including creditors, recording credit purchases, and trade discount.
* *Note:* Learners will deal with this concept in more detail in Grade 10.

---

## 2. The Accounting Cycle for Credit Purchases
Discuss the steps in the accounting cycle from the point where the business places the order to the creditor until the point where the business pays for the order. Refer to the accounting cycle and each step as you go along:

1. **Source Document – Invoice:** 
   * Show the invoice and revise how to fill it in.
   * Remind learners that this will be the **original invoice** received, which will need to be renumbered.
2. **Journal – Creditors Journal:** 
   * Show learners how to allocate the invoice in the Creditors Journal across different columns.
   * Items such as *Stationery* and *Packing material* are allocated to the **Sundry accounts** column because there are no dedicated analysis columns for them.

---

## 3. Rules for Recording in the Creditors Journal (CJ)

* **Only CREDIT PURCHASES** are entered into the Creditors Journal.
* **Creditors Control Column:** Each transaction (each invoice number) must be recorded in the Creditors control column. In other words, for each (renumbered) invoice, there must be a corresponding amount in the Creditors control column before the transaction is allocated to a specific column or the Sundry accounts column.
* **Analysis Columns:** The number of analysis columns required depends on the needs of each individual business and is determined by its nature and type.
* **Sundry Accounts:** Provision is always made for Sundry accounts in the journal because it is not practical to provide individual analysis columns for every type of purchase or expense.
* **Creditors Column:** In this column, we write the name of the person or business from whom the invoice is received.
* **Cross-Checking (CJ):** The total of the *Creditors control column* must be equal to the total of the analysis columns plus the total of the *Sundry accounts column*.
