# Economic and Management Sciences (Grades 7-9) Curriculum Specifications

## 1. Subject Overview
Economic and Management Sciences (EMS) is a practical subject focused on the efficient and effective use of private, public, and collective resources to satisfy human needs and wants. The curriculum emphasizes critical reflection on resource exploitation, environmental impact, and the management of scarce resources to maximize profit, fostering personal development and sustainable economic growth.

## 2. Content & Cognitive Scope
The curriculum is structured around two primary pillars, each with specific sub-topics that define the scope of mastery required for learners.

### Weighting of Curriculum Topics

| Pillar | Weighting | Topics |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **The Economy** | 30% | 1. History of money; 2. Need and wants; 3. Goods and services; 4. Inequality and poverty; 5. The production process; 6. Government; 7. The National Budget; 8. Standard of living; 9. Markets; 10. Economic systems; 11. The circular flow; 12. Price theory; 13. Trade unions |
| **Financial Literacy** | 40% | 1. Savings; 2. Budgets; 3. Income and expenditure; 4. Accounting concepts; 5. Accounting cycle; 6. Source documents; 7. Financial management and keeping of records |

*Note: The remaining 30% of the curriculum weighting is allocated to Entrepreneurship (as per standard CAPS framework for this subject).*

## 3. Instructional Planning Guidelines
*   **Pacing:** The curriculum is designed to be delivered across four school terms.
*   **Skill Development:** Instruction must prioritize real-life application, enabling learners to manage personal finances and understand the broader economic environment.
*   **Cognitive Depth:**
    *   **Knowledge:** Recall of economic terminology and accounting definitions.
    *   **Routine Procedures:** Application of basic accounting cycles and source document processing.
    *   **Complex Procedures:** Analysis of the National Budget and economic systems.
    *   **Problem Solving:** Critical evaluation of resource management and the impact of economic decisions on community development.

## 4. Assessment Matrix
Assessment in EMS is continuous and formal, designed to measure the attainment of the specific topics listed above.

| Assessment Type | Focus Area | Weighting |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Formal Assessment** | The Economy & Financial Literacy | 70% (Term marks) |
| **Summative Assessment** | End-of-year Examination | 30% |

*   **Paper 1:** Typically covers *The Economy* and *Entrepreneurship* components.
*   **Paper 2:** Typically focuses on *Financial Literacy* (Accounting cycle, source documents, and financial management).

### 2. Content & Cognitive Scope

The Economic and Management Sciences (EMS) curriculum for Grades 7-9 encompasses the following core conceptual areas:

*   **Needs and Wants:** Analysis of the needs and wants of different communities in societies.
*   **Production and Business Activities:** The nature, processes, and production of goods and services, and business activities within the different sectors of the economy.
*   **Financial Management:** Accounting as a tool for business management and record-keeping.
*   **Market Dynamics:** The influence of demand, supply, and pricing.
*   **Economic Flow:** The flow of money, goods, and services between households, businesses, and government; rights and responsibilities of economic role players.
*   **Socio-Economic Development:** Strategies to achieve sustainable growth, reduce poverty, and distribute wealth fairly while pursuing profitability.
*   **Entrepreneurship:** Skills and knowledge required to manage self and the environment effectively.
*   **Leadership and Management:** Basic aspects of leadership and management principles.
*   **Economic Growth:** The role of savings in sustainable economic growth and development.
*   **Labor Relations:** Trade unions and their influence in the economy.
*   **Resource Management:** The importance of using resources sustainably, effectively, and efficiently.
*   **Business Structures:** Functioning of both formal and informal businesses.

---

### 3. Instructional Specifications

*   **Scope:** The curriculum is designed to integrate theoretical knowledge with practical application in the context of the South African economy.
*   **Depth of Treatment:** Learners are expected to progress from foundational knowledge of economic concepts to the application of management and entrepreneurial skills.
*   **Cognitive Levels:** Instruction should facilitate movement through the following cognitive domains:
    *   **Knowledge:** Recall of definitions, economic terms, and business structures.
    *   **Routine Procedures:** Application of basic accounting and record-keeping processes.
    *   **Complex Procedures:** Analysis of economic flows and the impact of market forces (e.g., $Demand \uparrow \implies Price \uparrow$ under specific conditions).
    *   **Problem Solving:** Developing business plans and strategies for sustainable economic growth.

# Economic and Management Sciences (EMS) Grades 7-9: Curriculum Overview

## 1. Overview of Topics and Content Scope

The EMS curriculum is structured around three core pillars: **The Economy**, **Financial Literacy**, and **Entrepreneurship**. The following table outlines the progression of content across the Senior Phase.

| Topic | Grade | Content Scope |
| :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **The Economy** | 7 | History of money; needs and wants; goods and services; the production process; inequality and poverty |
| | 8 | Government; the National Budget; standard of living; markets |
| | 9 | Economic systems; the circular flow; price theory; trade unions |
| **Financial Literacy** | 7 | Savings; budgets; income and expenses; accounting concepts |
| | 8 | Accounting concepts; accounting cycle; source documents; Cash Receipts Journal (CRJ) and Cash Payments Journal (CPJ) of a service business; effects of cash transactions on the accounting equation; General Ledger and Trial Balance |
| | 9 | CRJ and CPJ of a sole trader; posting to the General Ledger; preparing a Trial Balance; recording transactions in the Debtors Journal, Creditors Journal; posting to the Debtors Ledger and Creditors Ledger |
| **Entrepreneurship** | 7 | The entrepreneur; starting a business; businesses; Entrepreneur's Day |
| | 8 | Factors of production; forms of ownership; levels of management; functions of management |
| | 9 | Sectors of the economy; functions of a business; business plan |

---

## 2. Content & Cognitive Scope

### Core Concepts and Skills
*   **The Economy:** Focuses on macro-economic principles, the role of government in resource allocation, and the socio-economic dynamics of the South African market.
*   **Financial Literacy:** Focuses on the systematic recording of financial data. The fundamental accounting equation is central to this pillar:
    $$\text{Assets} = \text{Owner's Equity} + \text{Liabilities}$$
*   **Entrepreneurship:** Focuses on the practical application of business theory, moving from basic business concepts in Grade 7 to complex business planning and management functions in Grade 9.

### Cognitive Levels
The curriculum requires learners to progress through the following cognitive domains:
1.  **Knowledge:** Recall of definitions (e.g., factors of production, accounting terminology).
2.  **Routine Procedures:** Application of standard accounting practices (e.g., balancing a ledger, recording in journals).
3.  **Complex Procedures:** Analysis of economic data and business scenarios (e.g., interpreting the circular flow model or the impact of the National Budget).
4.  **Problem Solving:** Synthesis of knowledge to create business plans or evaluate economic systems.

---

## 3. Instructional Planning Notes
*   **Pacing:** The curriculum is designed to be delivered over four terms. Teachers are expected to integrate the three pillars throughout the year to ensure a balanced exposure to theory and practical financial recording.
*   **Depth of Treatment:** 
    *   **Grade 7:** Introductory level, focusing on conceptual understanding and basic personal financial management.
    *   **Grade 8:** Introduction to formal bookkeeping and the structural components of the South African economy.
    *   **Grade 9:** Advanced application, requiring learners to handle complex journals and understand the broader economic environment in which businesses operate.

# Economic and Management Sciences: Grade 9, Term 1

## 1. Annual Teaching Plan (ATP) & Timelines
*Total instructional time: 2 hours per week.*

| Week | Topic | Content |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Week 1** | **Revision** | Revise Grade 8 work; overview of Term 1 curriculum. |
| **Weeks 2, 3, 4** | **Financial Literacy** | Cash transactions of a trading business; effect of cash transactions on the accounting equation. |
| **Weeks 2, 3, 4** | **The Economy** | Three major economic systems (planned, market, mixed); origins, advantages, disadvantages, and characteristics of each; the global economy. |
| **Weeks 5–10** | **Financial Literacy** | Posting cash transactions from CRJ and CPJ to the General Ledger; preparing a trial balance of a trading business. |
| **Weeks 5–10** | **The Economy** | Participants in the circular flow of a closed economy; flow of goods, services, money, and factors of production; illustration via flow diagram. |

*Note: Financial Literacy and The Economy are taught concurrently, with 1 hour allocated to each per week.*

## 2. Content & Cognitive Scope
*   **Core Concepts:**
    *   **Accounting Equation:** Understanding the impact of cash transactions on the business entity.
    *   **Economic Systems:** Comparative analysis of planned, market, and mixed economies.
    *   **Ledger & Trial Balance:** Systematic recording and balancing of financial data for a sole trader.
    *   **Circular Flow Model:** Conceptualizing the interaction between participants in a closed economy.
*   **Skills to be Mastered:**
    *   Recording transactions in journals (CRJ/CPJ).
    *   Posting entries to the General Ledger.
    *   Balancing accounts and drafting a Trial Balance.
    *   Diagrammatic representation of economic flows.
*   **Depth of Treatment:**
    *   Focus is on the **sole trader** business entity.
    *   Application of theoretical economic models to real-world contexts.

## 3. Assessment Matrix
*   **Formal Assessment Requirements:** Two tasks per term.
*   **Weighting:**
    *   **Assignment:** 40%
    *   **Controlled Test:** 60%
*   **Scope of Assessment:** The controlled test must cover all topics taught during Term 1.

## 4. Instructional Guidelines
1.  **Compulsory Coverage:** All topics indicated in the teaching plan must be completed within the term.
2.  **Contextualization:** Teachers are required to adapt the delivery of these topics to the specific context of the school and its learners.
3.  **Mathematical Integration:** While specific formulas are not listed in this excerpt, the accounting equation is foundational:
    $$\text{Assets} = \text{Owner's Equity} + \text{Liabilities}$$

# Economic and Management Sciences: Grade 9, Term 2

## 1. Annual Teaching Plan (ATP) & Timelines
*Total instructional time: 2 hours per week.*

| Week | Topic | Content |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Week 1** | Revision | Revision of Term 1 work; overview of Term 2 curriculum. |
| **Weeks 2–7** | **Financial Literacy:** Credit transactions – debtors | Credit sales; debtors; National Credit Act; debtors allowance; accounting cycle; recording transactions to Debtors Journal (DJ); Debtors Allowance Journal (DAJ); recording receipts from debtors in Cash Receipts Journal (CRJ). |
| **Weeks 2–7** | **The Economy:** Price theory | Law of demand, demand schedule and graphical illustration of the demand curve; law of supply, supply schedule and graphical illustration of the supply curve; equilibrium price and quantity; change in quantity demanded; change in quantity supplied; increase and decrease in demand; increase and decrease in supply; graphical illustration of changes. |
| **Weeks 8–9** | **Entrepreneurship:** Sectors of the economy | Primary, secondary, and tertiary sectors; types of businesses in each sector; interrelationship of sectors; sustainable resource use; role of sectors in the economy; skills required per sector. |
| **Week 10** | **Mid-year examination** | Assessment covering Term 1 and Term 2 content. |

*Note: Weeks 2–7 are split into 1 hour per week for Financial Literacy and 1 hour per week for The Economy.*

## 2. Content & Cognitive Scope
*   **Core Concepts:** Credit-based accounting cycles, market equilibrium (Price Theory), and sectoral economic structures.
*   **Skills to be Mastered:**
    *   **Accounting:** Journalizing credit transactions (DJ, DAJ, CRJ) and applying the National Credit Act.
    *   **Economic Analysis:** Constructing and interpreting demand and supply schedules and graphs.
    *   **Entrepreneurial Analysis:** Categorizing business activities into primary, secondary, and tertiary sectors and evaluating sustainability.
*   **Depth of Treatment:** Learners must be able to transition from theoretical definitions to practical application (recording journals) and graphical representation of economic variables.

## 3. Assessment Matrix & Exam Weightings

### Formal Assessment Requirements
The Term 2 formal assessment mark is comprised of two distinct tasks:

| Assessment Task | Weighting | Scope |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Class Test or Data Response | 40% | Term 2 content |
| Mid-year Examination | 60% | Term 1 and Term 2 content |

### Assessment Notes
1.  **Compulsory Completion:** All topics listed in the teaching plan must be covered.
2.  **Contextualization:** Instructional delivery and assessment should consider the specific context of the school environment.
3.  **Examination Scope:** The mid-year examination is cumulative, covering all work completed from the start of the academic year through to the end of Term 2.

## 4. Mathematical & Economic Modeling
The following concepts involve graphical and quantitative modeling:

*   **Market Equilibrium:** The point where quantity demanded ($Q_d$) equals quantity supplied ($Q_s$):
    $$Q_d = Q_s$$
*   **Price Theory:** Graphical representation requires plotting Price ($P$) on the vertical axis and Quantity ($Q$) on the horizontal axis to demonstrate:
    *   Law of Demand: Inverse relationship between $P$ and $Q_d$.
    *   Law of Supply: Direct relationship between $P$ and $Q_s$.

### Economic and Management Sciences: Grade 9, Term 4

#### 1. Annual Teaching Plan (ATP) & Timelines
The term consists of 10 weeks with a total allocation of 2 hours per week.

| Week | Topic | Content |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | Revision | Revision of Term 3 work; overview of Term 4 curriculum. |
| **2–7** | **Financial Literacy:** Transactions (Cash & Credit) | Recording cash/credit transactions for a sole trader; posting to Debtors Journal (DJ), Debtors Allowance Journal (DAJ), Creditors Journal (CJ), Creditors Allowance Journal (CAJ), and General Ledger; preparing a Trial Balance. |
| **2–7** | **Entrepreneurship:** Business Plan | Concept and components of a business plan; product/service description; business goals; production, marketing, and management plans; SWOT analysis; financial planning (fixed/variable costs, break-even points, mark-up, profit percentage). |
| **8–9** | Examination Preparation | Revision of full-year curriculum; study techniques; examination writing skills. |
| **10** | End-of-year Examination | Formal summative assessment. |

*Note: During weeks 2–7, the 2 hours per week are split equally (1 hour for Financial Literacy, 1 hour for Entrepreneurship).*

---

#### 2. Content & Cognitive Scope
*   **Financial Literacy:** Focuses on the application of accounting principles for sole traders. Mastery requires the ability to record transactions in subsidiary journals and reconcile these into the General Ledger and Trial Balance.
*   **Entrepreneurship:** Focuses on the strategic planning of a business. Mastery requires the ability to conduct a SWOT analysis and perform financial calculations.
*   **Mathematical Formulas & Concepts:**
    *   **Break-even point:** The point where total revenue equals total costs.
    *   **Mark-up on sales:** $$\text{Mark-up} = \frac{\text{Selling Price} - \text{Cost Price}}{\text{Cost Price}} \times 100$$
    *   **Profit percentage:** $$\text{Profit \%} = \frac{\text{Net Profit}}{\text{Revenue}} \times 100$$
