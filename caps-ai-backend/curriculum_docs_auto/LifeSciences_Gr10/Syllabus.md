#### 2. Content & Cognitive Scope

**Core Concepts:**
*   **Continuity of Life:** Establishing links between Natural Sciences (GET) and Life Sciences (FET).
*   **Homeostasis:** Defining life, its scope, and its dynamic balance at every level of organisation.
*   **Evolutionary Context:** Life on Earth is characterised by changes over billions of years.
*   **Levels of Organisation:** Living systems exhibit levels of organisation from molecules to biomes.
*   **Nature of Science:** 
    *   Involves contested knowledge and non-dogmatic inferences based on evidence and peer review.
    *   Fundamental knowledge built on scientific evidence and verified findings (journals/conferences).

**Scientific Skills to be Mastered:**
*   **Observing & Investigating:** Developing inquiry-based approaches.
*   **Quantitative Skills:** Making measurements and understanding the importance of scaling.
*   **Data Handling:** Collecting and presenting data via drawings, written descriptions, tables, and graphs.
*   **Analytical Skills:** 
    *   Understanding the limitations of scientific evidence.
    *   Identifying patterns and relationships in data.
    *   Communicating findings.
    *   Taking societal aspects of scientific evidence into account.
*   **Biological Representation:**
    *   Principles of biological drawings (surface area vs. volume/size).
    *   Translating 3D objects/specimens into 2D drawings and photographs.
    *   Interpreting 2D drawings and photographs (transverse and longitudinal sections).
*   **Mathematical Application:** Interpreting graphs and performing calculations.

**Operational & Classroom Skills:**
*   Using equipment and resources.
*   Understanding safety procedures in laboratories and classrooms.
*   Working in groups.
*   Understanding assessment requirements.
*   Career guidance and subject combinations for higher education.

#### 3. Assessment Matrix
*   **Note:** The "Orientation to Life Sciences" unit is **not assessable**. 
*   **Integration:** Relevant aspects of this introduction must be incorporated into the context of specific content where they apply and will be assessed at those later stages.

#### 4. Cognitive Levels & Depth of Treatment
The curriculum requires a progression through the following cognitive levels:
1.  **Knowledge:** Recall of definitions (e.g., homeostasis, levels of organisation).
2.  **Routine Procedures:** Application of safety rules, basic measurement, and standard biological drawing techniques.
3.  **Complex Procedures:** Translating 3D specimens to 2D representations; interpreting transverse and longitudinal sections.
4.  **Problem Solving:** Identifying patterns in data and evaluating the limitations of scientific evidence.

#### 2. Content & Cognitive Scope
**Strand Overview:**
Living organisms are composed of atoms that combine to form molecules. These molecules constitute the basic units of life (cells). Cells are organized into tissues to perform specialized functions, facilitating life processes: movement, nutrition, respiration, excretion, growth, reproduction, and response to stimuli.

**Core Content Requirements:**
*   **Molecules for Life:**
    *   Organic molecules composed of $C$, $H$, and $O$, with additional elements such as $N$ and $P$.
    *   Classes: Carbohydrates, lipids, nucleic acids, proteins, and vitamins.
    *   *Note: Only basic structural detail is required.*
*   **Inorganic Compounds:**
    *   Water: $H_2O$ (Main functions).
    *   Minerals: $Na$, $K$, $Ca$, $P$, $Fe$, $I$, nitrates, and phosphates.
    *   Focus: Macro and micro elements, main functions, and deficiency diseases (links to Grade 9 nutrition).
*   **Environmental/Applied Context:**
    *   The impact of fertilizers on over-utilized soils.
    *   Eutrophication: The process of fertilizer runoff into rivers (links to ecology).

### Life Sciences: Term 1 Curriculum Specifications (Organic Compounds)

#### 1. Content & Cognitive Scope
The curriculum focuses on the molecular basis of life, specifically the structure, function, and identification of organic compounds.

| Category | Core Concepts & Skills |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Carbohydrates** | Monosaccharides (glucose, fructose), disaccharides (sucrose, maltose), polysaccharides (starch, cellulose, glycogen). |
| **Lipids** | Structure (glycerol + 3 fatty acids), types (unsaturated vs. saturated fats), cholesterol, and heart disease links. |
| **Proteins** | Composition (C, H, O, N, and sometimes P, S, Fe), structure, function, and enzyme kinetics (Lock and Key Model). |
| **Enzymes** | Influence of temperature and pH on enzyme action; practical application in biological washing powders. |
| **Nucleic Acids** | Basic composition of DNA and RNA (C, H, O, N, P). |
| **Vitamins** | Roles of A, B-complex, C, D, and E in human health. |

#### 2. Practical & Investigative Requirements
Learners are required to master the following experimental procedures:

| Investigation | Materials Required | Objective |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Food Tests** | Bunsen burners, test tubes, reagents | Identify starch, glucose, lipids, and proteins. |
| **Enzyme Activity** | $H_2O_2$, chicken liver, pineapple juice, egg white, plastic straws | Demonstrate enzyme function and the effect of environmental factors. |
| **Dietary Analysis** | RDA charts, food labels | Compare Recommended Daily Allowance (RDA) with individual dietary intake. |

#### 3. Instructional Guidelines & Pacing
*   **Depth of Treatment:** 
    *   **Nucleic Acids:** Brief introduction to molecular make-up only; detailed structure is deferred to later sections.
    *   **Vitamins:** Focus on the "why" (necessity in plants/animals) rather than complex chemical synthesis.
    *   **Enzymes:** Emphasis on the "Lock and Key" model and practical application in everyday life (e.g., biological detergents).
*   **Cognitive Levels:**
    *   **Knowledge:** Identification of chemical elements ($C, H, O, N, P, S, Fe$).
    *   **Routine Procedures:** Performing standard food tests.
    *   **Complex Procedures:** Measuring and recording results of enzyme experiments under varying temperature conditions.
    *   **Problem Solving:** Analyzing dietary data to discuss implications of specific food types on health.

#### 4. Chemical & Biological Notation
The following chemical elements and compounds are central to the curriculum:
*   **Elements:** Carbon ($C$), Hydrogen ($H$), Oxygen ($O$), Nitrogen ($N$), Phosphorus ($P$), Sulfur ($S$), Iron ($Fe$).
*   **Compounds:** Hydrogen Peroxide ($H_2O_2$).

### 2. CONTENT & COGNITIVE SCOPE

#### Core Concepts
*   **Molecular Make-Up:** Composition of cells (proteins, carbohydrates, lipids, nucleic acids, water).
*   **History of Microscopy:** Evolution from lens to light and electron microscopes; impact on cell theory.
*   **Cell Structure & Function (Organelles):**
    *   **Cell Wall:** Support structure (plant cells only).
    *   **Cell Membrane:** Fluid mosaic model, boundaries, transport (diffusion, osmosis, active transport).
    *   **Nucleus:** Chromatin material, nuclear membrane, nucleopores, nucleolus (control centre, heredity).
    *   **Cytoplasm:** Storage and circulation of materials.
    *   **Mitochondria:** Energy release (cell respiration).
    *   **Ribosomes:** Protein synthesis.
    *   **Endoplasmic Reticulum:** Transport systems (rough and smooth).
    *   **Golgi Body:** Assembles secretions.
    *   **Plastids:** Production/storage of food and pigments.
    *   **Vacuole, Lysosomes, Vesicles:** Storage, digestion, osmoregulation.
*   **Cell Differentiation:** Relationship between structure, location, and function.

#### Skills to be Mastered
*   **Microscopy:** Demonstrate light microscope operation; prepare and observe wet mounts (onion epidermis, cheek cells).
*   **Mathematical Application:** Calculate magnification using the field of view; calculate specimen size using provided scale lines.
*   **Scientific Investigation:** Practical investigation of diffusion and osmosis.

#### Cognitive Levels & Depth
*   **Knowledge:** Recall of organelle names and molecular components.
*   **Routine Procedures:** Microscope operation and slide preparation.
*   **Complex Procedures:** Linking organelle structure to specific physiological functions.
*   **Problem Solving:** Calculating magnification and specimen size; interpreting experimental data from diffusion/osmosis investigations.

---

### 3. MATHEMATICAL FORMULAS & CALCULATIONS

**Magnification Calculation:**
To determine the magnification of a specimen under a microscope:
$$M = \frac{\text{Image Size}}{\text{Actual Size}}$$

**Specimen Size Calculation:**
Using a provided scale line:
$$\text{Actual Size} = \frac{\text{Measured Image Size}}{\text{Magnification}}$$

#### 4. Mathematical & Scientific Notation
*   **Cellular Division Logic:**
    *   If a parent cell undergoes mitosis, the resulting daughter cells ($D_1, D_2$) are defined by:
        $$D_1 = D_2 = \text{Parent Cell}$$
    *   The process maintains the diploid number of chromosomes ($2n$).

### 4. MATHEMATICAL & SCIENTIFIC CONTEXT
The study of transpiration and transport involves the analysis of rates. While specific formulas are applied in the practical investigation, the general relationship for transpiration rate ($R$) can be expressed as:

$$R = \frac{\Delta V}{\Delta t}$$

Where:
*   $R$ = Rate of transpiration
*   $\Delta V$ = Change in volume of water (or distance moved in potometer)
*   $\Delta t$ = Change in time

### Life Sciences Curriculum Specification: Term 4

**Strand:** Diversity, Change and Continuity
**Context:** Life exists in a huge array of forms and modes of life which scientists organise according to man-made classification systems. Modern life forms have a long history, extending from the first bacteria, around 3.5 billion years ago. South Africa has a rich fossil record of some key events in the history of life. Changes in life forms are related to climate changes as well as movements of continents and oceans over long periods of time.

---

#### 1. Annual Teaching Plan (ATP) & Timelines

| Time | Topic | Content | Investigations | Resources |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1 Week** (4 hours) | **Biodiversity and Classification** | Enormous biodiversity on Earth; classification schemes; history of classification; five-kingdom system; binomial nomenclature; prokaryotes vs eukaryotes; diagnostic features of Bacteria, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, and Animalia. | Principles of classification (grouping objects); Classify familiar organisms using identification guides. | Textbooks, Photographs, Micrographs, Identification guides, Keys to groups of organisms. |

---

#### 2. Content & Cognitive Scope

**Core Concepts:**
*   **Biodiversity:** Understanding the large variety of species, ecosystems, and genetic differences, with specific emphasis on biodiversity and endemism in Southern Africa.
*   **Classification Schemes:** The scientific attempt to organise biodiversity based on shared features; understanding that classification systems change as new information emerges.
*   **Taxonomy:** The five-kingdom system (Animalia, Plantae, Fungi, Protista, Monera/Bacteria).
*   **Nomenclature:** The species concept and the binomial system (Carl von Linne/Linnaeus); the rationale for using Latin.
*   **Cellular Biology:** Distinguishing between prokaryotic and eukaryotic cell structures.
*   **Diagnostic Features:** Identifying characteristics of the five major kingdoms.

**Cognitive Levels & Depth of Treatment:**
*   **Knowledge:** Recall of the five-kingdom system and the role of Linnaeus.
*   **Routine Procedures:** Applying classification principles to group everyday objects.
*   **Complex Procedures:** Using identification keys to classify familiar organisms based on visible diagnostic features.
*   **Problem Solving:** Analyzing why classification systems change and the significance of biodiversity in the Southern African context.

### 4. MATHEMATICAL & SCIENTIFIC NOTATION
*   **Radiometric Dating:** The decay of radioactive isotopes follows the exponential decay law:
    $$N(t) = N_0 e^{-\lambda t}$$
    Where:
    *   $N(t)$ is the quantity at time $t$.
    *   $N_0$ is the initial quantity.
    *   $\lambda$ is the decay constant.
    *   $t$ is the time elapsed.
