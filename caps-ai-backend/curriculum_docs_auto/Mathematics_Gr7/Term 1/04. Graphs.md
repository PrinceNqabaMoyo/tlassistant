### Core Concepts: Understanding Rate of Change

* **Constant Rate of Change:** The amount of money that Sally saves per week stays **constant**. The relationship therefore has a **constant rate of change**.
* **Non-Constant Rate of Change (Decreasing):** With every additional friend, the number of chocolates per friend changes. The number of chocolates changes (decreases) **rapidly at first and then it changes slower**. The **rate of change is not constant**.
* **Increasing Rate of Change:** The amount that Vusi would like to earn per bag of acorns grows faster and faster with each bag that he collects. The **rate of change increases**.
* **Increasing Rate of Change (Exponential/Quadratic Growth):** The area of a square also **increases faster and faster** for every centimetre added to the side length.

> #### Key Definition
> The **rate of change** means how fast or slow change happens per unit of time.

> #### Interpreting Graphs
> The **shape** of a bar graph shows the **rate of change** of the relationship.
> * If the rate of change is constant, the shape is a **straight line**.
> * If it is changing, the shape is a **curve**.

# Term 3 — Graphs: Linear and Non-Linear Relationships

## Key Concepts: Graph Directions and Shapes

### 1. Increasing Graphs
In the case of an increase, the graph slopes upwards. This can happen in different ways:



![Textbook_Mathematics_Gr7_Graphs.pdf-0007-02.png](images/Textbook_Mathematics_Gr7_Graphs.pdf-0007-02.png)


* **Constant increase:** A straight line sloping upwards.
* **Non-constant increase:** A curve bending upwards (either getting steeper or flattening out).

---

### 2. Decreasing Graphs
In the case of a decrease, the graph slopes downwards. This can happen in different ways:



![Textbook_Mathematics_Gr7_Graphs.pdf-0007-03.png](images/Textbook_Mathematics_Gr7_Graphs.pdf-0007-03.png)


* **Constant decrease:** A straight line sloping downwards.
* **Non-constant decrease:** A curve bending downwards (either getting steeper or flattening out).

---

> ### Important Definitions
> * When the increase or decrease is constant, the graph is a straight line and it is called a **linear graph**.
> * If the increase or decrease is not constant, the graph is curved and is called a **non-linear graph**.
> * If there is no change in the output variable, the graph is a straight horizontal line (constant).

### Mathematical Background

A graph shows information about a situation and can be used to reveal trends. 

Graphs are used by businesses, governments, organisations, and all kinds of people in many different contexts to represent information and study trends. We therefore need to be able to interpret graphs so that we can make sense of the information offered to us.

#### Key Focus Areas in this Unit:

* **Discovering Trends:** The focus is on being able to discover the trend that a graph shows and understand the "story" it tells, rather than just focusing on the basics (such as using formulae to generate coordinates or plotting points).
* **Identifying Variables:** We focus on identifying the variables in the "story" that a graph represents and how these variables relate to each other at different points, as well as how that relationship changes if the variables change.
* **Direction and Rate of Change:** 
  * The direction of the graph is linked to the increase or decrease of the dependent values. 
  * The increase or decrease is determined by the **rate of change** of the output values in the relationship.
  * Working with the rate of change in this context prepares you to have a better understanding in later, more formal work, of what a **gradient** means.
* **Interpreting Graphs:** This involves making up a story that contains the facts shown by the graph.
* **Drawing Graphs:** This involves another way of thinking. The story is known and has to be represented in a graph while making sure that all the elements of the story are represented, and then checking that the graph actually tells the story that was intended from the start.

# 15.2 Investigating rate of change situations

## COMPARE SITUATIONS AND REPRESENT THEM IN A DIFFERENT WAY

### Teaching Guidelines

It is not the intention that learners should find the rules for these relationships; but there may be learners to whom it is a challenge to find the rule.

* **First Situation (Linear Relationship):** Shows a relationship where the output numbers steadily increase each week. Learners could have predicted this as the output numbers grow by a fixed amount from week to week. Learners may see that the rule in this case is: multiply the number of weeks by $4$, or if the number of weeks is represented by $x$, the rule is $4x$.
* **Second Situation (Non-linear Relationship):** The output is determined by dividing the fixed number of chocolates by the input numbers that increase. This leads to decreasing output numbers that give the shape of the graph. As the number of friends increase, the number of chocolates per friend decreases. The rule in this case is: divide $24$ by the number of friends. If the number of friends is represented by $x$, the rule is $24 \div x$ (or $\frac{24}{x}$).

### Notes on the Questions

The fact that learners represent the output numbers using bars should make it easier for them to relate the shape of the graph to the relationship: **linear increase** in the first case and **non-linear decrease** in the second case.

## Key Concepts & Definitions

### Rate of Change
> **Definition:** The **rate of change** means how fast or slow change happens per unit of time.

### Graph Shapes and Rate of Change
* The shape of a bar graph shows the **rate of change** of the relationship.
* If the rate of change is **constant**, the tops of the bars in the graph form a **straight line**.
* If the rate of change is **changing**, the shape of the graph is a **curve**.
  * If the rate of change **increases**, the graph slants upwards more steeply.
  * If the rate of change **decreases**, the graph slants downwards or becomes flatter.

# Grade 7 Mathematics: Graphs and Rates of Change

## Key Concepts: Understanding Growth and Change

When we investigate the relationship between two quantities, we look at how the **output values** change in response to the **input values**. This rate of change determines the shape of the graph representing the relationship.

The change between variables can be classified into four main types:

1. **Constant Increase:** The output values increase by the same amount each time (e.g., the perimeter of a square as its side length increases). This is represented by a straight line sloping upwards.
2. **Constant Decrease:** The output values decrease by the same amount each time (e.g., the length of a burning candle over time). This is represented by a straight line sloping downwards.
3. **Non-Constant Increase (Faster and Faster):** The output values increase, and the amount of increase grows larger with each step (e.g., the area of a square as its side length increases). This is represented by a curve that bends upwards.
4. **Non-Constant Decrease (Faster and Faster):** The output values decrease, and the rate of decrease changes (e.g., the amount of money each friend has to pay as more friends share a fixed cost). This is represented by a curve that slopes downwards and flattens out.

---

## Investigations and Tables

### Investigation 1: Side Length vs. Area of a Square
Let us investigate the relationship between the length of a side of a square and its area. 

The formula for the area of a square is:
$$A = s^2$$

Where:
* $s$ is the length of a side
* $A$ is the area

| Length of a side of the square ($s$) | $1$ | $2$ | $3$ | $4$ | $6$ | $7$ | $8$ | $9$ |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Area of the square ($A$)** | $1$ | $4$ | $9$ | $16$ | $36$ | $49$ | $64$ | $81$ |

#### Analysis of Change:
* From side length $1$ to $2$: Area increases by $+3$ ($4 - 1 = 3$)
* From side length $2$ to $3$: Area increases by $+5$ ($9 - 4 = 5$)
* From side length $3$ to $4$: Area increases by $+7$ ($16 - 9 = 7$)
* From side length $6$ to $7$: Area increases by $+13$ ($49 - 36 = 13$)

**Conclusion:** The area increases **faster and faster** as the side length increases. This is a non-constant increase.

### Investigation 2: Burning Candle
A tall candle was lit, and its length was measured and recorded every hour while it was burning.

| Number of hours the candle is burning ($t$) | $1$ | $2$ | $3$ | $4$ | $6$ | $7$ | $8$ | $9$ |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Length of candle in cm ($L$)** | $33$ | $31$ | $29$ | $27$ | $23$ | $21$ | $19$ | $17$ |

#### Analysis of Change:
* From hour $1$ to $2$: Length decreases by $-2$ cm ($31 - 33 = -2$)
* From hour $2$ to $3$: Length decreases by $-2$ cm ($29 - 31 = -2$)
* From hour $3$ to $4$: Length decreases by $-2$ cm ($27 - 29 = -2$)

**Conclusion:** The candle decreases in length by a constant amount of $2\text{ cm}$ every hour. This is a **constant decrease**.
