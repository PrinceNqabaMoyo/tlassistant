# Mathematics Grade 7 Teacher Guide

## Resource Page: Square Grid Paper ($1\text{ cm} \times 1\text{ cm}$)

This resource page provides a standard $1\text{ cm} \times 1\text{ cm}$ square grid template. In the CAPS Grade 7 Mathematics curriculum, grid paper is a vital tool used across several content areas, particularly in **Space and Shape (Geometry)** and **Measurement**.

---

### 1. Key Instructional Applications

#### A. Measurement: Perimeter and Area
* **Perimeter:** The distance around a two-dimensional shape. On this grid, the side of each square represents exactly $1\text{ cm}$. Learners can calculate perimeter by counting the number of unit segments along the boundary of a shape.
* **Area:** The amount of two-dimensional space enclosed by a shape. Each individual grid square represents an area of:
  $$\text{Area} = 1\text{ cm} \times 1\text{ cm} = 1\text{ cm}^2$$
  Learners can find the area of regular and irregular shapes by counting the total number of grid squares enclosed.

#### B. Transformational Geometry
Grid paper is used to perform and visualize geometric transformations:
* **Translation:** Sliding a shape a specified number of units horizontally and/or vertically.
* **Reflection:** Flipping a shape across a line of symmetry (mirror line) drawn along the grid lines.
* **Rotation:** Turning a shape about a fixed point (vertex) by a specific angle (e.g., $90^\circ$ or $180^\circ$).
* **Enlargement and Reduction:** Scaling a shape up or down by a scale factor by multiplying or dividing the lengths of its sides.

#### C. Drawing 2D Shapes
* Drawing accurate geometric figures such as triangles, rectangles, squares, and parallelograms with specified dimensions.

### 3. Worked Example: Calculating Area and Perimeter on Grid Paper

**Question:**
A rectangle is drawn on the $1\text{ cm} \times 1\text{ cm}$ grid paper. It is $5\text{ squares}$ long and $3\text{ squares}$ wide. 
1. Calculate its perimeter ($P$).
2. Calculate its area ($A$).

**Solution:**

1. **Perimeter ($P$):**
   $$P = 2 \times (\text{length} + \text{width})$$
   $$P = 2 \times (5\text{ cm} + 3\text{ cm})$$
   $$P = 2 \times 8\text{ cm} = 16\text{ cm}$$

2. **Area ($A$):**
   $$A = \text{length} \times \text{width}$$
   $$A = 5\text{ cm} \times 3\text{ cm}$$
   $$A = 15\text{ cm}^2$$

## 1. Purpose and Use of Graph Paper in Grade 7

Graph paper is an essential mathematical tool used in the CAPS Grade 7 curriculum for several key topics:

1. **Data Handling:** Drawing bar graphs, double bar graphs, histograms, and line graphs to represent collected data.
2. **Geometry of 2D Shapes:** Drawing, measuring, and calculating the perimeter and area of regular and irregular shapes by counting grid squares.
3. **Transformations:** Performing transformations on 2D shapes, including:
   * **Translation:** Sliding a shape along a grid.
   * **Reflection:** Flipping a shape over a line of symmetry.
   * **Rotation:** Turning a shape around a fixed point.
4. **Introduction to the Cartesian Plane:** Plotting coordinates in the first quadrant where both $x$ and $y$ coordinates are positive.

---

## 2. Key Graphing Concepts

* **Grid System:** A network of horizontal and vertical lines spaced uniformly to form squares.
* **Axes:** 
  * The horizontal axis is the **$x$-axis**.
  * The vertical axis is the **$y$-axis**.
* **Origin:** The point where the axes intersect, represented by the coordinates $(0, 0)$.
* **Ordered Pair / Coordinates:** Written in the form $(x, y)$, where:
  * $x$ represents the horizontal distance from the origin.
  * $y$ represents the vertical distance from the origin.
* **Scale:** The value represented by each grid interval (e.g., $1 \text{ grid square} = 1 \text{ unit}$ or $1 \text{ grid square} = 10 \text{ units}$).

## 4. Worked Example: Plotting and Area Calculation

### Problem:
1. On a grid, plot the following coordinate points:
   * $A(2, 1)$
   * $B(6, 1)$
   * $C(6, 4)$
   * $D(2, 4)$
2. Connect the points in alphabetical order and close the shape by connecting $D$ back to $A$.
3. Identify the shape formed.
4. Calculate the perimeter and area of the shape, assuming each grid square represents $1 \text{ cm} \times 1 \text{ cm}$.

### Solution:

1. **Plotting the Points:**
   * Start at the origin $(0,0)$.
   * For $A(2, 1)$, move $2$ units right and $1$ unit up.
   * For $B(6, 1)$, move $6$ units right and $1$ unit up.
   * For $C(6, 4)$, move $6$ units right and $4$ units up.
   * For $D(2, 4)$, move $2$ units right and $4$ units up.

2. **Identifying the Shape:**
   * The shape formed has four right angles and opposite sides of equal length.
   * Therefore, shape $ABCD$ is a **rectangle**.

3. **Calculating Dimensions:**
   * $\text{Length } (l) = 6 - 2 = 4 \text{ units (cm)}$
   * $\text{Breadth } (b) = 4 - 1 = 3 \text{ units (cm)}$

4. **Perimeter Calculation:**
   $$P = 2(l + b)$$
   $$P = 2(4 \text{ cm} + 3 \text{ cm})$$
   $$P = 2(7 \text{ cm})$$
   $$P = 14 \text{ cm}$$

5. **Area Calculation:**
   $$\text{Area} = l \times b$$
   $$\text{Area} = 4 \text{ cm} \times 3 \text{ cm}$$
   $$\text{Area} = 12 \text{ cm}^2$$

# Dotted Paper

This resource page provides a grid of evenly spaced dots (square dot paper) used for various mathematical activities in Grade 7.

## Uses of Dotted Paper in Grade 7 Mathematics

Dotted paper is an essential tool in the CAPS Mathematics curriculum. It is primarily used for:

1. **Geometry of 2D Shapes:**
   - Drawing accurate geometric figures such as triangles, quadrilaterals, and other polygons.
   - Investigating properties of shapes (e.g., parallel lines, right angles, and symmetry).

2. **Transformations:**
   - Performing and visualizing transformations:
     - **Translation:** Sliding a shape a certain number of units horizontally and vertically.
     - **Reflection:** Flipping a shape across a line of reflection.
     - **Rotation:** Turning a shape around a center of rotation.

3. **Area and Perimeter:**
   - Calculating the perimeter by counting the unit distances between dots.
   - Estimating and calculating the area of irregular and regular shapes by counting grid squares.
