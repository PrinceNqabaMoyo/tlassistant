|**Term**|**Meaning**|
|---|---|
|**Gross Salary**|The total amount earned in a month. This includes all types of salary (e.g. salary,<br>overtime, bonuses, etc.)|
|**Deductions**|These are amounts that need to be subtracted from the gross salary_before_<br>money is deposited into the employee’s bank account. These include items such<br>as UIF, Pension, Medical Aid, Trade Union Fees, Loan repayments, Tax, etc.|
|**Net Salary**|Also known as ‘take home pay’. This is the amount that is deposited into an<br>employee’s bank account. It is calculated as follows:<br>**Net Salary = Gross Salary - Deductions**|
|**Income Tax**|This is a tax on all sources of income (e.g. salary, interest income, rental income,<br>etc.). It is calculated on the taxable income.|
|**Taxable Income**|This is different from Net Salary although the calculation looks similar.<br>**Taxable Income = Gross Income – Tax-deductible Deductions**|
|**Gross Income**|This is different from gross salary (above) because it includes all forms of<br>income, e.g. salary, rental income, textbook royalties, etc.|
|**Tax deductible**<br>**Deductions**|These are specific deductions that are subtracted from the gross income before<br>tax is calculated. There are two types of taxable deductions:<br>_Salary-based deductibles_: subtracted from the gross salary by the employer<br>before the salary is paid. These include: UIF, Pension fund contributions, etc.<br>_Non-Salary deductibles_: These may be paid out of an employee’s take-home<br>pay, e.g. donations to charities, certain medical expenses.<br>There are limits placed on deductibles, e.g. the maximum amount that can be<br>deducted for pension is 7,5% of the gross salary.|
|**Non-tax Deductible**<br>**Expenses**|The majority of expenses are not tax deductible. These are generally living<br>expenses, e.g. food, rent, fuel, entertainment, etc. Only tax deductible deductions<br>reduce the amount of taxable income owed.|
|**Taxable Deductions**|Some deductions subtracted from an employee’s payslip are taxable. Although<br>the employee receives less money they still have to pay tax on the larger amount<br>of money that they earned. Examples include: loans from an employer, a<br>garnishee order, monthly payments to the employer for services rendered, etc.|

## **IRP5 forms** 

These contain a summary of: 

- the total amount that an employee has earned 

- any deductions that were made (e.g. pension, UIF, etc.) 

- any tax deducted from the employee’s salary 

The information on the IRP5 will be used by the employee to fill out a tax return at the end of the tax year. 

An employee has to obtain an IRP5 certificate from each employer that they have worked for in any given tax year and total all of the information across all of them.
