### CONTENT & COGNITIVE SCOPE

#### 1. Exponents and Surds
*   **Core Concepts:** Rational exponents, laws of exponents, simplification of surds, solving exponential equations.
*   **Depth of Treatment:** 
    *   Revision of Grade 10 exponential laws and equations.
    *   Application of $x^{\frac{p}{q}} = \sqrt[q]{x^p}$.
    *   Practical application in technical fields (e.g., $C = \frac{Q}{V}$).
*   **Cognitive Levels:**
    *   **Knowledge (K):** Evaluating simple expressions (e.g., $9^{\frac{3}{2}}$).
    *   **Routine Procedure (R):** Simplifying surds, solving equations like $4x^{\frac{5}{2}} = 128$ or $25^{2x} = 5^{x-3}$.
    *   **Complex Procedure (C):** Multi-step simplification involving roots and fractions (e.g., $\frac{5^3 \times \sqrt[3]{625}}{\sqrt{5} \cdot 125 \times 5} + \sqrt[3]{8^{-2}}$).

#### 2. Logarithms
*   **Core Concepts:** Relationship between logs and exponents, conversion between forms, laws of logarithms, solving logarithmic equations.
*   **Depth of Treatment:**
    *   Conversion between logarithmic and exponential form.
    *   Simplification of expressions without a calculator.
    *   Proving identities involving logarithms.
    *   Solving equations of the form $\log_a(x+b) + \log_a(x+c) = d$.
*   **Cognitive Levels:**
    *   **Knowledge (K):** Basic log equations (e.g., $\log_2 \frac{1}{8} = x$).
    *   **Routine Procedure (R):** Applying laws to simplify expressions (e.g., $\log 6 + 2\log 20 - \log 3 - 3\log 2$).
    *   **Complex Procedure (C):** Proving logarithmic identities (e.g., $\frac{\log_x 64 + \log_x 4 - \log_x 8}{\log_x 1024} = \frac{1}{2}$).

### MATHEMATICAL FORMULAS & IDENTITIES

**Exponents:**
$$x^{\frac{p}{q}} = \sqrt[q]{x^p} \quad (x > 0; q > 0)$$

**Logarithms:**
1.  $\log_a xy = \log_a x + \log_a y$
2.  $\log_a \frac{x}{y} = \log_a x - \log_a y$
3.  $\log_a x^n = n \log_a x$
4.  $\log_a b = \frac{\log_c b}{\log_c a}$

**Applied Formula (Technical):**
$$C = \frac{Q}{V}$$
*(Where $Q = 6 \times 10^{-4} \text{ C}$ and $V = 200 \text{ volt}$)*

#### 2. Content & Cognitive Scope
The curriculum requires learners to move from basic plotting to analytical deduction of functional properties.

**Core Concepts:**
*   **Parabolic Functions:** $y = a(x + p)^2 + q$ and $y = ax^2 + bx + c$
*   **Hyperbolic Functions:** $y = \frac{a}{x} + q$
*   **Exponential Functions:** $y = a \cdot b^x + q$ (where $b > 0$ and $b \neq 1$)
*   **Circle Equations:** $x^2 + y^2 = r^2$ and associated semi-circles:
    *   $y = \pm\sqrt{r^2 - x^2}$
    *   $y = +\sqrt{r^2 - x^2}$
    *   $y = -\sqrt{r^2 - x^2}$

**Skills to be Mastered:**
*   **Tabulation & Plotting:** Gaining confidence in graphing through point-plotting.
*   **Parameter Analysis:** Understanding the influence of variables ($a, p, q$) on the form and position of graphs.
*   **Critical Point Calculation:** Determining intercepts, turning points, and asymptotes.
*   **Deduction:** Deriving the equation of a function given specific critical points.
*   **Analysis:** Interpreting information provided by existing graphs.

**Cognitive Levels:**
*   **Knowledge (K):** Identifying graph types and basic parameter effects.
*   **Routine Procedures (R):** Tabulating points, drawing standard graphs, calculating intercepts.
*   **Complex Procedures (C):** Determining equations from given points, analyzing transformations.
*   **Problem Solving (P):** Interpreting complex graphical information and applying functional properties to solve problems.

#### 3. Mathematical Formulas & Notation
The following functional forms are central to the Term 2 syllabus:

*   **Quadratic (Vertex Form):** $$y = a(x + p)^2 + q$$
*   **Quadratic (Standard Form):** $$y = ax^2 + bx + c$$
*   **Hyperbolic:** $$y = \frac{a}{x} + q$$
*   **Exponential:** $$y = a \cdot b^x + q, \quad b > 0, b \neq 1$$
*   **Circle (Center at Origin):** $$x^2 + y^2 = r^2$$
*   **Semi-circles:**
    *   Full circle: $$y = \pm\sqrt{r^2 - x^2}$$
    *   Upper semi-circle: $$y = +\sqrt{r^2 - x^2}$$
    *   Lower semi-circle: $$y = -\sqrt{r^2 - x^2}$$

### 3. MATHEMATICAL FORMULAS & CONVERSIONS

**Circle Equation:**
$$x^2 + y^2 = r^2$$

**Angular Relationships:**
*   Complete revolution: $360^\circ = 2\pi \text{ radians}$
*   Half revolution: $180^\circ = \pi \text{ radians}$
*   Arc length formula: $\theta = \frac{s}{r}$ (where $\theta$ is in radians)

**Conversion Formulas:**
*   Degrees to Radians: $1^\circ = \frac{\pi}{180^\circ} \text{ rad}$
*   Radians to Degrees: $1 \text{ rad} = \frac{180^\circ}{\pi}$

**Sector and Segment Geometry:**
*   Area of a sector: $A = \frac{rs}{2} = \frac{r^2\theta}{2}$
*   Where:
    *   $r = \text{radius}$
    *   $s = \text{arc length}$
    *   $\theta = \text{central angle in radians}$

### Content & Cognitive Scope

#### 1. Core Concepts
*   **Trigonometric Ratios:** Extension to all four quadrants.
*   **Triangle Geometry:** Application of Sine Rule, Cosine Rule, and Area Rule in 2D contexts.
*   **Functions & Graphs:** Transformation of trigonometric functions including amplitude ($k$), frequency ($k$), and horizontal shifts ($p$).
*   **Vector Analysis:** Relationship between rotating vectors and the generation of sine/cosine waves.
*   **Identities:** Fundamental Pythagorean and quotient identities.

#### 2. Mathematical Formulas & Definitions
*   **Segment Geometry:** $h^2 - 4dh + x^2 = 0$ (where $h$ = height, $d$ = diameter, $x$ = chord length).
*   **Angular Velocity:** $\omega = 2\pi n = 360^\circ n$ (where $n$ = revolutions per second).
*   **Linear Velocity:** $v = \pi Dn$ (where $D$ = diameter).
*   **Identities:**
    *   $\tan \theta = \frac{\sin \theta}{\cos \theta}$
    *   $\sin^2 \theta + \cos^2 \theta = 1$
    *   $1 + \tan^2 \theta = \sec^2 \theta$
    *   $\cot^2 \theta + 1 = \csc^2 \theta$

#### 3. Instructional Guidelines
*   **Graphing:** Learners must be able to sketch graphs and extract information from given sketches.
*   **Pacing:** The 4-week block is dedicated to mastering the transition from right-angled trigonometry to general triangle applications and functional analysis.
*   **Assessment Focus:** Assessment should prioritize routine procedures for equations and reduction formulae, ensuring learners can handle horizontal shifts and parameter changes systematically.

### 4. MATHEMATICAL FORMULAS

The following formulas are prescribed for the curriculum:

*   **Simple Decay (Straight-line depreciation):**
    $$A = P(1 + in)$$

*   **Compound Decay (Reducing balance depreciation):**
    $$A = P(1 - i)^n$$

*Where:*
*   $A$ = Final value
*   $P$ = Principal amount
*   $i$ = Interest/Depreciation rate
*   $n$ = Number of time periods
