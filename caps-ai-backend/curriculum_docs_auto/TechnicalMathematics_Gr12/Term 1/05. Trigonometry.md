## Key Concepts

### 1. Similarity of Triangles
Understanding that triangles with the same interior angles are similar, meaning their sides are in proportion.

### 2. Trigonometric Ratios
The study of the relationships between the sides and angles of right-angled triangles. These are defined as:
*   $\sin \theta = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}$
*   $\cos \theta = \frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}}$
*   $\tan \theta = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}$

### 3. Reciprocal Ratios
The reciprocals of the primary trigonometric ratios:
*   $\text{cosec } \theta = \frac{1}{\sin \theta}$
*   $\sec \theta = \frac{1}{\cos \theta}$
*   $\cot \theta = \frac{1}{\tan \theta}$

### 4. Special Angles
Specific values for trigonometric functions at angles such as $30^\circ$, $45^\circ$, and $60^\circ$ which can be derived without a calculator.

### 5. The Cartesian Plane
Extending trigonometric definitions to angles greater than $90^\circ$ using coordinates $(x, y)$ and the radius $r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$.

# 5. Trigonometry

## 5.1 Introduction (EMA3M)

Trigonometry is the study of the relationships between the angles and sides of a triangle. The foundation of trigonometry is built upon trigonometric ratios within right-angled triangles.

### Applications of Trigonometry
Trigonometry is essential in various fields, including:
*   **Astronomy:** Triangulation is used to measure distances to nearby stars.
*   **Geography & Navigation:** Used for measuring distances between landmarks and in GPS (Global Positioning System) technology.
*   **Other Fields:** Acoustics, optics, financial market analysis, electronics, probability theory, statistics, biology, medical imaging (CAT scans and ultrasound), chemistry, cryptology, meteorology, oceanography, land surveying, architecture, phonetics, engineering, computer graphics, and game development.

#### 3. Similar Triangles
Two triangles are considered **similar** if their corresponding angles are equal and their corresponding sides are in the same proportion. 

If $\triangle ABC$ is similar to $\triangle DEF$, we denote this as:
$$\triangle ABC ||| \triangle DEF$$

#### 4. Ratios in Similar Triangles
When triangles are similar, we can deduce the following relationships between their corresponding sides:

*   **Ratio of corresponding sides:**
    $$\frac{AB}{BC} = \frac{DE}{EF}$$
    $$\frac{AB}{AC} = \frac{DE}{DF}$$
    $$\frac{AC}{BC} = \frac{DF}{EF}$$

*   **Combined proportionality:**
    $$\frac{AB}{DE} = \frac{BC}{EF} = \frac{AC}{DF}$$

### Similar Triangles
For similar triangles $ABC$ and $DEF$, the corresponding angles are equal:
*   $\hat{A} = \hat{D}$
*   $\hat{B} = \hat{E}$
*   $\hat{C} = \hat{F}$

**NOTE:** The order of letters for similar triangles is very important. Always label similar triangles in corresponding order.
*   $\triangle ABC \parallel \triangle DEF$ is correct.
*   $\triangle ABC \parallel \triangle DFE$ is incorrect.

#### Identifying Sides in a Right-Angled Triangle
In a right-angled triangle, the sides are named based on their position relative to a chosen angle $\theta$:
1.  **Hypotenuse:** The side opposite the right angle. It is always the longest side.
2.  **Opposite:** The side directly across from the angle $\theta$.
3.  **Adjacent:** The side next to the angle $\theta$ (that is not the hypotenuse).

#### The Trigonometric Ratios
The three fundamental trigonometric ratios are defined as follows:

$$\sin \theta = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}$$

$$\cos \theta = \frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}}$$

$$\tan \theta = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}$$

**IMPORTANT:** These definitions are **only** applicable to right-angled triangles. Always verify that the triangle contains a $90^\circ$ angle before applying these ratios.

### 5.4 Reciprocal Ratios

Each of the three primary trigonometric ratios has a corresponding reciprocal ratio. These are cosecant ($\text{cosec}$), secant ($\text{sec}$), and cotangent ($\text{cot}$).

#### Definitions
The reciprocal ratios are defined as follows:

$$\text{cosec } \theta = \frac{1}{\sin \theta}$$
$$\text{sec } \theta = \frac{1}{\cos \theta}$$
$$\text{cot } \theta = \frac{1}{\tan \theta}$$

#### Ratios in a Right-Angled Triangle
Using the sides of a right-angled triangle, these reciprocals are defined as:

$$\text{cosec } \theta = \frac{\text{hypotenuse}}{\text{opposite}}$$
$$\text{sec } \theta = \frac{\text{hypotenuse}}{\text{adjacent}}$$
$$\text{cot } \theta = \frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{opposite}}$$

#### Important Identities
The product of a trigonometric ratio and its reciprocal is always equal to $1$:

$$\sin \theta \times \text{cosec } \theta = 1$$
$$\cos \theta \times \text{sec } \theta = 1$$
$$\tan \theta \times \text{cot } \theta = 1$$

***

**Note:** The cosecant ratio is often abbreviated as $\text{csc}$.

# 5.5 Calculator Skills

In this section, we focus on using a scientific calculator to determine the values of trigonometric ratios for any given angle.

### Important Concepts
*   **Calculator Mode:** Ensure your scientific calculator is set to **"degrees"** mode before performing calculations.
*   **Reciprocal Ratios:** Scientific calculators typically only have buttons for $\sin$, $\cos$, and $\tan$. To calculate reciprocal ratios ($\sec$, $\csc$, $\cot$), you must convert them into their standard counterparts:
    *   $\sec \theta = \frac{1}{\cos \theta}$
    *   $\csc \theta = \frac{1}{\sin \theta}$
    *   $\cot \theta = \frac{1}{\tan \theta}$
*   **Notation Note:** The notation $\sin^2 \theta$ is equivalent to $(\sin \theta)^2$. This rule applies to all trigonometric ratios.

#### Worked Example: Finding an Unknown Side ($x$)

**Step 1: Identify the sides with reference to the given angle**
To solve for an unknown side in a right-angled triangle, first identify the sides relative to the given angle:
*   **Hypotenuse:** Always opposite the right angle.
*   **Opposite:** The side opposite the angle of interest.
*   **Adjacent:** The remaining side next to the angle.

Using the cosine ratio:
$$\cos \theta = \frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}}$$
$$\cos 25^\circ = \frac{7}{x}$$

**Step 2: Rearrange the equation to solve for $x$**
Multiply both sides by $x$ to move it out of the denominator:
$$\cos 25^\circ \times x = \frac{7}{x} \times x$$
$$x \cos 25^\circ = 7$$

Divide both sides by $\cos 25^\circ$ to isolate $x$:
$$\frac{x \cos 25^\circ}{\cos 25^\circ} = \frac{7}{\cos 25^\circ}$$
$$x = \frac{7}{\cos 25^\circ}$$

**Step 3: Use your calculator to find the answer**
$$x = \frac{7}{0,90630...}$$
$$x = 7,723645...$$
$$x \approx 7,72$$

    Press: `SHIFT` `sin` `(` `2` `.` `4` `÷` `3` `)` `=` $53,1301... \approx 53,13^\circ$

*   **Step 3: Write the final answer**

### Note: Trigonometric Constraints
When solving trigonometric equations, you may encounter an error if you try to calculate $\sin$ or $\cos$ for values greater than $1$. Remember that both the sine and cosine functions have a maximum value of $1$. If the ratio exceeds $1$, there is no solution to the equation.

---

### Worked Example 10: Solving Trigonometric Equations

**Question**
Solve for $\alpha$: $3 \sec \alpha = 1,4$.

**Solution**

*   **Step 1: Convert $\sec$ to $\cos$**
    Since there is no "$\sec$" button on the calculator, we must convert $\sec \alpha$ to $\cos \alpha$ to find $\alpha$:
    $$3 \sec \alpha = 1,4$$
    $$\frac{3}{\cos \alpha} = 1,4$$

*   **Step 2: Rearrange the equation**
    We need to rearrange the equation so that $\cos \alpha$ is isolated:
    $$\frac{3}{\cos \alpha} = 1,4$$
    $$3 = 1,4 \cos \alpha$$
    $$\frac{3}{1,4} = \cos \alpha$$

### Solving Trigonometric Equations: Inverse Functions

#### Concept: Using Inverse Functions
To solve for an unknown angle (e.g., $\alpha$), use the inverse trigonometric functions on your calculator (e.g., $\cos^{-1}$, $\sin^{-1}$, or $\tan^{-1}$). These functions work backwards from a ratio to determine the angle.

**Important Note on Domain:**
The values of $\sin \theta$ and $\cos \theta$ must always fall within the range $[-1, 1]$. If a calculation results in a ratio greater than $1$ or less than $-1$, there is **no solution**.

**Example:**
To solve $\cos \alpha = \frac{3}{1,4}$:
1. Since $\frac{3}{1,4} \approx 2,14$, which is $> 1$, the calculator will display a "math error".
2. **Conclusion:** There is no solution.

# 5.8 Defining Ratios in the Cartesian Plane

Trigonometric ratios, previously defined using right-angled triangles, can be extended to any angle by plotting points on the Cartesian plane. These definitions depend on the size of the angle rather than the specific lengths of the triangle sides.

---

## Finding an Angle for Specific Points

To find an angle for a point $(x; y)$ on the Cartesian plane:
1. Draw a line from the origin $(O)$ to the point.
2. Construct a right-angled triangle by drawing a perpendicular line from the point to the $x$-axis.

### Worked Example: Point $P(2; 3)$
For point $P(2; 3)$, we have $x = 2$ and $y = 3$.
*   The side **opposite** to angle $\hat{A}$ is $3$.
*   The side **adjacent** to angle $\hat{A}$ is $2$.

Using the tangent ratio:
$$\tan \hat{A} = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}} = \frac{3}{2}$$
$$\hat{A} = 56,3^\circ$$

Alternatively, using the Theorem of Pythagoras, we can find the hypotenuse ($r$) and use sine or cosine:
$$\sin \hat{A} = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}} \quad \text{or} \quad \cos \hat{A} = \frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}}$$

---

## Angles in Standard Position

An angle is in **standard position** when it is measured from the positive $x$-axis in an anti-clockwise direction.

### Worked Example: Point $Q(-2; 3)$
For point $Q(-2; 3)$, let $\hat{B}$ be the angle in standard position. Let $\alpha$ be the reference angle formed between the line $OQ$ and the negative $x$-axis.

1.  **Calculate the reference angle $\alpha$:**
    Using the coordinates of $Q$, the opposite side is $3$ and the adjacent side is $2$:
    $$\tan \alpha = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}} = \frac{3}{2}$$
    $$\alpha = 56,3^\circ$$

2.  **Calculate the standard position angle $\hat{B}$:**
    Since $\hat{B} + \alpha = 180^\circ$:
    $$\hat{B} = 180^\circ - \alpha$$
    $$\hat{B} = 180^\circ - 56,3^\circ = 123,7^\circ$$

## Quadrant Analysis
Using the general definitions of trigonometric ratios, we can determine which ratios are positive in each quadrant:

| Quadrant | Positive Ratios | Reasoning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Quadrant I** | All ratios | Both $x$ and $y$ values are positive. |
| **Quadrant II** | $\sin \theta, \csc \theta$ | Only $y$ values are positive. |

### Trigonometric Ratios for $0^\circ$ and $90^\circ$

#### Ratios for $0^\circ$
When $\theta = 0^\circ$, the length of the opposite side is $0$ and the length of the adjacent side is equal to the hypotenuse.
$$\sin 0^\circ = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}} = \frac{0}{\text{hypotenuse}} = 0$$
$$\cos 0^\circ = \frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}} = \frac{\text{hypotenuse}}{\text{hypotenuse}} = 1$$
$$\tan 0^\circ = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}} = \frac{0}{\text{adjacent}} = 0$$

#### Ratios for $90^\circ$
When $\theta = 90^\circ$, the length of the adjacent side is $0$ and the length of the opposite side is equal to the hypotenuse.
$$\sin 90^\circ = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}} = \frac{\text{hypotenuse}}{\text{hypotenuse}} = 1$$
$$\cos 90^\circ = \frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}} = \frac{0}{\text{hypotenuse}} = 0$$
$$\tan 90^\circ = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}} = \frac{\text{opposite}}{0} = \text{undefined}$$

### Worked Example: Proving Trigonometric Identities

This example demonstrates how to prove the identity $\tan^2\theta + 1 = \sec^2\theta$ using the Cartesian plane.

#### Step 2: Use the theorem of Pythagoras to calculate $y$
Given the relationship between coordinates and the radius $r$:
$$r^2 = x^2 + y^2$$
$$y^2 = r^2 - x^2$$
$$y^2 = (13)^2 - (-5)^2$$
$$y^2 = 169 - 25$$
$$y^2 = 144$$
$$y = \pm 12$$

**Note:** Since $\theta$ lies in the third quadrant, $y$ must be negative. Therefore, $y = -12$.

#### Step 3: Substitute values for $x$, $y$, and $r$ and simplify
Given $x = -5$, $y = -12$, and $r = 13$:

**Left-Hand Side (LHS):**
$$\tan^2\theta + 1 = \left(\frac{y}{x}\right)^2 + 1$$
$$= \left(\frac{-12}{-5}\right)^2 + 1$$
$$= \left(\frac{144}{25}\right) + 1$$
$$= \frac{144 + 25}{25}$$
$$= \frac{169}{25}$$

**Right-Hand Side (RHS):**
$$\sec^2\theta = \left(\frac{r}{x}\right)^2$$
$$= \left(\frac{13}{-5}\right)^2$$
$$= \frac{169}{25}$$

**Conclusion:** Since the LHS = RHS, the identity $\tan^2\theta + 1 = \sec^2\theta$ is proven.

*   **Solution:** The sine ratio is defined as $\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}$. In any right-angled triangle, the hypotenuse is the side of the longest length. *   **Solution:** The tangent ratio is defined as $\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}$. Since the opposite and adjacent sides can have any value (as long as the length of the side is less than or equal to the length of the hypotenuse), there is no maximum value for the tangent ratio.

### Trigonometric Ratios in the Cartesian Plane

Given a point $(x, y)$ where $x = 2$ and $y = t$, we first determine the radius $r$ using the Pythagorean theorem:

$$r^2 = x^2 + y^2$$
$$r^2 = (2)^2 + (t)^2$$
$$r^2 = 4 + t^2$$
$$r = \sqrt{4 + t^2}$$

Since $0^\circ \leq \theta \leq 90^\circ$, the angle lies in the first quadrant.

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

#### Determining Trigonometric Ratios

**1. Secant ($\sec \theta$)**
$$\sec \theta = \frac{r}{x} = \frac{\sqrt{t^2 + 4}}{2}$$

**2. Cotangent ($\cot \theta$)**
**Solution:** Using the values $x = 2, y = t, r = \sqrt{t^2 + 4}$:
$$\cot \theta = \frac{x}{y} = \frac{2}{t}$$

**3. Cosine Squared ($\cos^2 \theta$)**
**Solution:**
$$\cos^2 \theta = \left( \frac{x}{r} \right)^2$$
$$\cos^2 \theta = \left( \frac{2}{\sqrt{t^2 + 4}} \right)^2$$
$$\cos^2 \theta = \frac{4}{t^2 + 4}$$

**4. Tangent Squared minus Secant Squared ($\tan^2 \theta - \sec^2 \theta$)**
**Solution:**
$$\tan^2 \theta - \sec^2 \theta = \left( \frac{y}{x} \right)^2 - \left( \frac{r}{x} \right)^2$$
$$\tan^2 \theta - \sec^2 \theta = \left( \frac{t}{2} \right)^2 - \left( \frac{\sqrt{t^2 + 4}}{2} \right)^2$$
$$\tan^2 \theta - \sec^2 \theta = \frac{t^2}{4} - \frac{t^2 + 4}{4}$$
$$\tan^2 \theta - \sec^2 \theta = \frac{t^2 - t^2 - 4}{4}$$
$$\tan^2 \theta - \sec^2 \theta = \frac{-4}{4}$$
$$\tan^2 \theta - \sec^2 \theta = -1$$

### Trigonometry: Solving for Trigonometric Ratios

**Problem Statement:**
Given $10 \cos \beta + 8 = 0$ and $180^\circ < \beta < 360^\circ$. Determine the value of:

---

#### a) $\cos \beta$

**Solution:**
Rearrange the given equation to isolate $\cos \beta$:

$$10 \cos \beta + 8 = 0$$
$$10 \cos \beta = -8$$
$$\cos \beta = \frac{-8}{10}$$
$$\cos \beta = -\frac{4}{5}$$

#### b) $\frac{3}{\tan \beta} + 2 \sin^2 \beta$

**Solution:**
To solve this, we must determine the values of $x$, $y$, and $r$ using the definition $\cos \beta = \frac{x}{r}$.

From part (a), we have:
$$\cos \beta = \frac{x}{r} = \frac{-4}{5}$$

Therefore, $x = -4$ and $r = 5$. We use the Pythagorean identity $r^2 = x^2 + y^2$ to find $y$:

$$y^2 = r^2 - x^2$$
$$y^2 = (5)^2 - (-4)^2$$
$$y^2 = 25 - 16$$
$$y^2 = 9$$
$$y = \pm 3$$

Given the constraint $180^\circ < \beta < 360^\circ$, the angle lies in the third or fourth quadrant. Based on the coordinate geometry and the negative value of $x$, we identify the angle is in the third quadrant, where $y$ is negative. Thus, $y = -3$.

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

Now, we calculate the required expression:

1. Find $\tan \beta = \frac{y}{x} = \frac{-3}{-4} = \frac{3}{4}$
2. Find $\sin \beta = \frac{y}{r} = \frac{-3}{5}$

Substitute these into the expression:

$$\frac{3}{\tan \beta} + 2 \sin^2 \beta = \frac{3}{\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)} + 2 \left(\frac{-3}{5}\right)^2$$
$$= 3 \cdot \left(\frac{4}{3}\right) + 2 \left(\frac{9}{25}\right)$$
$$= 4 + \frac{18}{25}$$
$$= \frac{100}{25} + \frac{18}{25}$$
$$= \frac{118}{25}$$

### Trigonometric Ratios in the Cartesian Plane

#### Worked Example: Evaluating Trigonometric Expressions
Given the expression $\frac{3}{\tan \beta} + 2 \sin^2 \beta$, we substitute the ratios $\tan \beta = \frac{y}{x}$ and $\sin \beta = \frac{y}{r}$:

$$\frac{3}{\tan \beta} + 2 \sin^2 \beta = \frac{3}{\frac{y}{x}} + 2\left(\frac{y}{r}\right)^2$$
$$= \frac{3x}{y} + \frac{2y^2}{r^2}$$

$$= \frac{3(-4)}{-3} + \frac{2(-3)^2}{(5)^2}$$
$$= \frac{-12}{-3} + \frac{18}{25}$$

24. Determine the area of $\triangle ABC$.

---

### Trigonometry: Triangle Area Problem

**Given:**
*   $\angle A = 55^\circ$
*   $\angle C = 75^\circ$
*   Height $BD = 280$
*   $D$ is the right-angled vertex

**Solution:**

1.  **Calculate $AD$:**
    $$\tan 55^\circ = \frac{280}{AD}$$
    $$AD = \frac{280}{\tan 55^\circ} \approx 196,058$$

2.  **Calculate $CD$:**
    $$\tan 75^\circ = \frac{280}{CD}$$
    $$CD = \frac{280}{\tan 75^\circ} \approx 75,026$$

3.  **Calculate base $AC$:**
    $$AC = AD - CD$$
    $$AC = 196,058 - 75,026 = 121,032$$

4.  **Calculate Area of $\triangle ABC$:**
    $$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height}$$
    $$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} \times 121,032 \times 280$$
    $$\therefore \text{Area} = 16\,944 \text{ units}^2$$
