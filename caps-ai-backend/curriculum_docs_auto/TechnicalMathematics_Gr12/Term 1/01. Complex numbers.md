## 1. Introduction to Complex Numbers

A complex number is composed of a real number and an imaginary number.

### Definition
*   **Rectangular Form:** A complex number is expressed in the form $z = a + bi$, where $a$ is the real part and $bi$ is the imaginary part.
*   **Imaginary Unit:** It is important to note that $\sqrt{-1} = i$.

---

## 2. Laws of Exponents
To perform operations with complex numbers, recall the following algebraic laws:

| Law | Formula |
| :--- | :--- |
| Product Rule | $x^m \times x^n = x^{m+n}$ |
| Quotient Rule | $x^m \div x^n = x^{m-n}$ |
| Power of a Power | $(x^m)^n = x^{mn}$ |
| Power of a Product | $(x \cdot y)^n = x^n \times y^n$ |
| Power of a Quotient | $(\frac{x}{y})^m = \frac{x^m}{y^m}$ |
| Negative Exponent | $x^{-m} = \frac{1}{x^m}$ |
| Zero Exponent | $x^0 = 1$ |
| Rational Exponent | $\sqrt[n]{x^m} = x^{\frac{m}{n}}$ |

---

## 3. Roots of Numbers

When dealing with roots, distinguish between the following:

*   **Odd roots of negative numbers:** These are **NOT** imaginary numbers (e.g., $\sqrt[3]{-8} = -2$).
*   **Even roots of negative numbers:** These are **imaginary numbers** (e.g., $\sqrt{-4} = 2i$).

### Powers of the Imaginary Unit ($i$)

The imaginary unit $i$ is defined as $i = \sqrt{-1}$. The powers of $i$ follow a repeating cycle:

| Power | Result | Power | Result |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $i = \sqrt{-1}$ | $i$ | $i^2$ | $-1$ |
| $i^3$ | $-i$ | $i^4$ | $1$ |
| $i^5$ | $i$ | $i^6$ | $-1$ |
| $i^7$ | $-i$ | $i^8$ | $1$ |

---

### Dividing Imaginary Numbers

When dividing complex numbers, you must multiply both the numerator and the denominator by the **conjugate** of the denominator to rationalise the expression.

#### Understanding Conjugates
The conjugate is found by changing the sign of the imaginary part of the complex number.

| Imaginary Number | Conjugate |
| :--- | :--- |
| $i$ | $-i$ |
| $2i$ | $-2i$ |
| $-3i$ | $3i$ |
| $i - 2$ | $-2 - i$ |
| $i + 3$ | $3 - i$ |
| $2i - 7$ | $-7 - 2i$ |

**NB:** Always write complex numbers in the standard form $a + bi$ before determining the conjugate. Note that only the sign of the imaginary term changes. For example, the conjugate of $i + 3$ is $3 - i$.

---

### Multiplication of Complex Numbers

When multiplying a single term by a complex number, distribute the term across the bracket.

**Worked Example:**
$$ -2(-4 + 5i) $$
$$ = (-2 \times -4) + (-2 \times 5i) $$
$$ = 8 - 10i $$

### Complex Numbers: Multiplication, Conjugates, and Division

#### 1. Multiplication of Complex Numbers
To multiply complex numbers, use the distributive property (FOIL method) and remember that $i^2 = -1$.

**Worked Example:**
Calculate $(2 - \sqrt{3}i)(1 - \sqrt{3}i)$

| Step | Calculation | Note |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1. Expand | $= 2(1 - \sqrt{3}i) - \sqrt{3}i(1 - \sqrt{3}i)$ | Term in first bracket multiplying the second bracket |
| 2. Distribute | $= 2 - 2\sqrt{3}i - \sqrt{3}i + 3i^2$ | |
| 3. Substitute | $= 2 - 3\sqrt{3}i + 3(-1)$ | Substitute $i^2$ with $-1$ |
| 4. Simplify | $= -1 - 3\sqrt{3}i$ | |

---

#### 2. The Conjugate
The conjugate of a complex number is used to simplify expressions and perform division.

*   **Definition:** For a complex number $z = a + bi$, the conjugate is denoted as $\bar{z} = a - bi$.
*   **Key Rule:** The conjugate changes the sign of the imaginary part of the complex number.

**Example:**
The conjugate of $4 + 7i$ is $4 - 7i$.

---

#### 3. Division of Complex Numbers
To divide complex numbers, multiply both the numerator and the denominator by the conjugate of the denominator.

**Worked Example:**
Calculate $\frac{2}{3 + i}$

| Step | Calculation | Note |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1. Multiply | $= \frac{2}{3 + i} \times \frac{3 - i}{3 - i}$ | Multiply by the conjugate of the denominator |
| 2. Expand | $= \frac{2(3 - i)}{(3 + i)(3 - i)}$ | Simplify |
| 3. Substitute | $= \frac{6 - 2i}{9 - 3i + 3i - i^2}$ | $i^2 = -1$ |
| 4. Finalize | $= \frac{6 - 2i}{9 - (-1)} = \frac{6 - 2i}{10} = \frac{2}{10} - \frac{2i}{10} = \frac{1}{5} - \frac{i}{5}$ | Simplify |
