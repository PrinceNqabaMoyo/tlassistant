# 6.1 Ratio

Ratios are used to describe everyday situations, such as comparing the total distance covered to the duration (total time taken), which is expressed as the ratio of speed.

### Definition
A ratio is an expression that results when division compares two quantities or two numbers.

In general, for any two numbers, $x$ and $y$, the ratio of $x$ to $y$ is the quotient obtained by dividing $x$ by $y$. This ratio is expressed as:
$$x:y \quad \text{or} \quad \frac{x}{y}$$

## 6.2 Proportion

A proportion is defined as the equality of two ratios.

**Mathematical Representation:**
$$\frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d} \quad (b \neq 0, d \neq 0)$$
Or expressed as:
$$a:b = c:d$$
*In words, this is read as: "$a$ is to $b$ as $c$ is to $d$."*

### Terms of a Proportion
In a proportion, each number is called a term. For example:

| 1st Term | 2nd Term | 3rd Term | 4th Term |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $3$ | $4$ | $9$ | $12$ |

*   **Extremes:** The first and fourth terms ($3$ and $12$).
*   **Means:** The second and third terms ($4$ and $9$).

### Property of Proportions
The product of the means equals the product of the extremes (cross-multiplication).

**Rule:**
If $\frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d}$, then:
$$a \times d = b \times c$$

*Note: In a true proportion, the two cross products are always equal.*

---

### Worked Example: Proportions

A proportion is a statement that two ratios are equal. To determine if two ratios $\frac{a}{b}$ and $\frac{c}{d}$ form a proportion, check if the product of the means equals the product of the extremes ($a \times d = b \times c$).

#### 1. Determine whether each pair of ratios can be written as a proportion:
a) $\frac{3}{4}$ and $\frac{15}{20}$
b) $\frac{8}{12}$ and $\frac{6}{9}$
c) $\frac{6}{7}$ and $\frac{7}{8}$

**Solutions:**
a) $3 \times 20 = 60$ and $4 \times 15 = 60$. Since $60 = 60$, $\frac{3}{4} = \frac{15}{20}$ is a proportion.
b) $8 \times 9 = 72$ and $12 \times 6 = 72$. Since $72 = 72$, $\frac{8}{12} = \frac{6}{9}$ is a proportion.
c) $6 \times 8 = 48$ and $7 \times 7 = 49$. Since $48 \neq 49$, $\frac{6}{7} \neq \frac{7}{8}$ is not a proportion.

#### 2. Use the following set of numbers to write a true proportion:
a) $4; 7; 12; 21$
b) $4; 20; 16; 5$

**Solutions:**
a) $\frac{4}{7} = \frac{12}{21} \Rightarrow \frac{4}{7} = \frac{4}{7}$
b) $\frac{4}{16} = \frac{5}{20} \Rightarrow \frac{1}{4} = \frac{1}{4}$

# Properties of Proportions

Proportions involving segment lengths are essential for solving geometry problems. Often, you must rewrite a given proportion into an equivalent form to find an unknown length.

## Properties of a Proportion
If $\frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d}$ and $a, b, c, d \neq 0$, then the following properties are true:

1. $ad = bc$
2. $\frac{d}{b} = \frac{c}{a}$
3. $\frac{a}{c} = \frac{b}{d}$
4. $\frac{b}{a} = \frac{d}{c}$
5. $\frac{a+b}{b} = \frac{d+c}{c}$
6. $\frac{a-b}{b} = \frac{d-c}{c}$

---

**Solution:**

*   $\frac{20}{16} = \frac{15}{12}$ (Switch the extremes)
*   $\frac{16}{12} = \frac{20}{15}$ (Invert the proportion)

### Euclidean Geometry: Proportionality and Similarity

#### Key Concept: Triangles with Equal Altitudes
The fundamental principle in this section is that the ratio of the areas of two triangles that share the same altitude (height) is equal to the ratio of their bases.

If two triangles have equal altitudes, their areas are in the same proportion as the lengths of their bases.

---

#### Worked Example 1: General Ratio Derivation
Given $\triangle ABC$ with altitude $AE$ drawn to base $BC$, where $D$ lies on $BC$:

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

**Derivations:**

a) $$\frac{\text{area of } \triangle ABD}{\text{area of } \triangle ABC} = \frac{\frac{1}{2} \cdot BD \cdot AE}{\frac{1}{2} \cdot BC \cdot AE} = \frac{BD}{BC}$$

b) $$\frac{\text{area of } \triangle ABD}{\text{area of } \triangle ADC} = \frac{\frac{1}{2} \cdot BD \cdot AE}{\frac{1}{2} \cdot DC \cdot AE} = \frac{BD}{DC}$$

c) $$\frac{\text{area of } \triangle ADC}{\text{area of } \triangle ABC} = \frac{\frac{1}{2} \cdot DC \cdot AE}{\frac{1}{2} \cdot BC \cdot AE} = \frac{DC}{BC}$$

---

#### Worked Example 2: Applying Ratios with Specific Base Proportions
Given $\triangle ABC$ where $D$ is a point on $BC$ such that $BD = DC$ (indicated by markings on the base).

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

**Solutions:**

a) Find $\frac{\text{area of } \triangle ABD}{\text{area of } \triangle ABC}$:
$$\frac{\text{area of } \triangle ABD}{\text{area of } \triangle ABC} = \frac{\frac{1}{2} \cdot BD \cdot AE}{\frac{1}{2} \cdot BC \cdot AE} = \frac{BD}{BC} = \frac{BD}{2BD} = \frac{1}{2}$$

b) Find $\frac{\text{area of } \triangle ABD}{\text{area of } \triangle ADC}$:
$$\frac{\text{area of } \triangle ABD}{\text{area of } \triangle ADC} = \frac{\frac{1}{2} \cdot BD \cdot AE}{\frac{1}{2} \cdot DC \cdot AE} = \frac{BD}{DC} = \frac{BD}{BD} = 1$$

c) Find $\frac{\text{area of } \triangle ADC}{\text{area of } \triangle ABC}$:
$$\frac{\text{area of } \triangle ADC}{\text{area of } \triangle ABC} = \frac{\frac{1}{2} \cdot DC \cdot AE}{\frac{1}{2} \cdot BC \cdot AE} = \frac{DC}{BC} = \frac{DC}{2DC} = \frac{1}{2}$$

#### The Proportionality Theorem
**Note:** The result drawn from Exercise 5 can be expressed as a theorem.

**Theorem:** A line parallel to one side of a triangle divides the other two sides (internally or externally) into segments that have the same proportion.

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

If $MN \parallel KL$, then:
$$\frac{JM}{MK} = \frac{JN}{NL}$$

---

#### Properties of Proportions
Applying the properties of proportions, if $\frac{JM}{MK} = \frac{JN}{NL}$, then the following results are true:

| Property | Mathematical Expression |
| :--- | :--- |
| a) Cross-multiplication | $JM \times NL = MK \times JN$ |
| b) Inversion | $\frac{JM}{JN} = \frac{MK}{NL}$ |
| c) Reciprocal | $\frac{MK}{JM} = \frac{NL}{JN}$ |
| d) Addition | $\frac{JM}{MK} + 1 = \frac{JN}{NL} + 1 \Rightarrow \frac{JM + MK}{MK} = \frac{JN + NL}{NL} \Rightarrow \frac{JK}{MK} = \frac{JL}{NL}$ |

### Worked Example: Proportionality in Triangles

This example demonstrates how to use the **Proportionality Theorem** to find ratios between segments in triangles.

**Given:**
*   $\frac{TU}{UR} = \frac{3}{4}$

**Step-by-step derivation:**
1.  Let $TU = 3k$, then $UR = 4k$.
2.  The total length $TR = TU + UR = 3k + 4k = 7k$.
3.  Therefore, $\frac{TR}{UR} = \frac{7}{4}$.
4.  Using the Proportionality Theorem in $\triangle RTP$ (where $PT \parallel SU$):
    $$\frac{TU}{UR} = \frac{PS}{SR} = \frac{3}{4}$$
5.  Using the Proportionality Theorem in $\triangle PQR$ (where $PQ \parallel TS$):
    $$\frac{PS}{SR} = \frac{QT}{TR} = \frac{3}{4}$$
6.  Let $QT = 3n$, then $TR = 4n$, and $QR = 3n + 4n = 7n$.
7.  Since $\frac{QR}{TR} = \frac{7}{4}$, we substitute $TR$:
    $$\frac{QR}{\frac{7 \times UR}{4}} = \frac{7}{4}$$
8.  Solving for the ratio:
    $$\frac{QR}{UR} = \frac{7}{4} \times \frac{7}{4} = \frac{49}{16}$$
9.  **Result:** $QR : UR = 49 : 16$

### 6.3 Similar Figures

**Definition:** Similar figures are shapes that have the same internal angles and proportional side lengths, but are not necessarily the same size.

*   **Size:** Can differ (enlargements or reductions).
*   **Application:** Used in photography, scale models, and image projection. The relationship between the dimensions is governed by a specific ratio known as the **aspect ratio**.

### Similar Polygons

In geometry, **similar figures** have the same shape. This means they are the same kind of figure, and one is an enlargement or reduction of the other.

#### Note: Conditions for Similarity
Two polygons are similar (denoted by the symbol $|||$) if:
1. All corresponding angles are equal.
2. The ratios of the lengths of corresponding sides are equal.

---

### Properties of Similar Polygons
Similar polygons possess the same number of sides, equal corresponding angles, and proportional corresponding sides.

*   **Dilation:** To dilate an object is to change its size (enlarge or reduce) without changing its form. The resulting object is called a **dilatation** or **scale drawing** of the original.
*   **Notation:** We write $QRST ||| ABCD$ to indicate that polygon $QRST$ is similar to polygon $ABCD$. 
*   **Correspondence:** Vertices must be listed in corresponding order. For example, $QRST ||| ABCD$ is correct, but $TQRS ||| ABCD$ is not.

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

#### Mathematical Relationships
If $QRST ||| ABCD$, then:

**a) Corresponding angles are equal:**
$$\hat{Q} = \hat{A}, \hat{R} = \hat{B}, \hat{S} = \hat{C}, \hat{T} = \hat{D}$$

**b) Corresponding sides are proportional:**
$$\frac{QR}{AB} = \frac{RS}{BC} = \frac{ST}{CD} = \frac{QT}{AD}$$

### Scale Factor
The ratio of the lengths of corresponding sides of two similar polygons is called the **scale factor**.

**Example:**
Given the polygons in the diagram:
*   The ratio of $QR$ to $AB$ is: $\frac{QR}{AB} = \frac{2}{6} = \frac{1}{3}$
    *   The scale factor of $QRST$ to $ABCD$ is $\frac{1}{3}$.
*   The ratio of $AB$ to $QR$ is: $\frac{AB}{QR} = \frac{6}{2} = 3$
    *   The scale factor of $ABCD$ to $QRST$ is $3$.

*Note: For polygons other than triangles, having equal corresponding angles does not guarantee proportional sides, and having proportional sides does not guarantee equal corresponding angles.*

### Similar Triangles
Similar triangles are used extensively in industry, construction, navigation, and land surveying. 

**Key Concept:**
Triangles are similar if they are equiangular. For similar triangles $\Delta A'B'C' ||| \Delta ABC ||| \Delta A''B''C''$, the ratio of corresponding sides is equal:
$$\frac{A'B'}{B'C'} = \frac{AB}{BC} = \frac{A''B''}{A''B''}$$

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

If two triangles are equiangular, their corresponding sides are in the same proportion, and thus the triangles are similar.

**Formal Statement:**
In $\Delta ABC$ and $\Delta PQR$:

$$\frac{AB}{PQ} = \frac{AC}{PR} = \frac{BC}{QR}$$
Therefore, $\Delta ABC ||| \Delta PQR$.

# Applications of Similar Triangles: The Pantograph

## Definition
A **pantograph** is a mechanical linkage instrument used for copying an image on a predetermined scale. It consists of four light, rigid bars joined in a parallelogram form. The movement of one point (the tracing point) produces identical, scaled movements in a second point (the pencil).

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

## The Mathematical Principle
The pantograph operates on the principle of **similar triangles**. 

### Key Concepts
*   **Mechanism:** The device uses a series of pivots and arms to maintain geometric similarity.
*   **Scaling:** If the distance from the anchor point to the pencil ($C$) is $k$ times greater than the distance from the anchor point to the tracing point ($B$), then the figure traced by the pencil will be $k$ times larger than the template figure.
*   **Geometric Proof:**
    *   $DEFG$ is a parallelogram because $\hat{E} = \hat{F}$ (corresponding angles).
    *   Points $A$, $B$, and $C$ always lie on the same straight line.
    *   In $\Delta ADC$ and $\Delta BGC$:
        *   $AD \parallel BG$
    *   Because the lines are parallel, the triangles are similar. Therefore, the ratio of the sides remains constant:
        $$\frac{AC}{AB} = \text{constant}$$

### Practical Applications
The same principle of similarity and proportional division is applied in various fields, including:
*   **Sculpture:** Creating scaled replicas of statues.
*   **Minting:** Producing coins from larger master models.
*   **Engraving and Milling:** Precision duplication of patterns or text.

# Indirect Measurement

## Concept: Proportionality Theorem
The Proportionality Theorem states that if a line is drawn parallel to one side of a triangle, it divides the other two sides proportionally. This principle is used to calculate unknown lengths in structures where direct measurement is not possible.

---

## Worked Example: Beam Structure
In the provided figure, vertical beams $MN$, $OP$, and $RQ$ are parallel. We are given the lengths of segments $MN = 65,5\text{ cm}$, $MO = 56,5\text{ cm}$, and $OQ = 77,5\text{ cm}$. We need to determine the distance $x$ (where $x = PR$).

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

### Solution

**Step 1: Geometric Construction**
Extend lines $NR$ and $MQ$ to intersect at a common point $H$. Let $HN = a$ and $HM = b$.

**Step 2: Setting up Proportions**
Using the Proportionality Theorem:

1. $\frac{a}{65,5} = \frac{b}{56,5}$
2. $\frac{a}{b} = \frac{65,5}{56,5}$
3. $\frac{a}{65,5 + x} = \frac{b}{56,5 + 77,5}$

By equating the ratios:
$$\frac{65,5 + x}{56,5 + 77,5} = \frac{65,5}{56,5}$$

**Step 3: Solving for $x$**
Make $x$ the subject of the formula:

$$65,5 + x = \frac{65,5(56,5 + 77,5)}{56,5}$$

$$x = \frac{65,5(56,5 + 77,5)}{56,5} - 65,5$$

$$x = \frac{65,5 \times 56,5}{56,5} + \frac{65,5 \times 77,5}{56,5} - \frac{65,5 \times 56,5}{56,5}$$

$$x = \frac{65,5 \times 77,5}{56,5}$$

$$x = 89,9\text{ cm}$$

---

## Alternative Method: Ratio of Corresponding Segments
We can also use the property that the ratios of corresponding segments of transversals are equal:

$$\frac{x}{77,5} = \frac{65,5}{56,5}$$

$$x = \frac{65,5 \times 77,5}{56,5}\text{ cm}$$

$$x = 89,9\text{ cm}$$

# Summary: Ratio and Proportion

## 1. Ratio
A ratio is an expression that results when division compares two quantities or two numbers.

## 2. Proportion and Properties
A proportion is the equality of two ratios. Using symbols, this is expressed as:
$$\frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d} \quad (b \neq 0, d \neq 0)$$
In words, this is read as "$a$ is to $b$ as $c$ is to $d$".

### The Product Rule
The product of the means equals the product of the extremes. If $\frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d}$, then:
$$a \times d = b \times c$$

### Properties of Proportions
If $\frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d}$ (where $a, b, c, d \neq 0$), then the following are always true:

1. $ad = bc$
2. $\frac{d}{b} = \frac{c}{a}$
3. $\frac{a}{c} = \frac{b}{d}$
4. $\frac{b}{a} = \frac{d}{c}$
5. $\frac{a+b}{b} = \frac{d+c}{c}$
6. $\frac{a-b}{b} = \frac{d-c}{c}$

---

## 3. Segments Divided Proportionally and Areas of Triangles

### Ratio of Areas
*   The areas of triangles with equal altitudes are in the same proportion as the lengths of their bases.
*   If triangles are on the same base (or equal bases) and are between the same parallel lines, then the triangles are equal in area.

### Theorem: The Proportionality Theorem
A line parallel to one side of a triangle divides the other two sides (internally or externally) into segments that have the same proportion.

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

Given the triangle $JKL$ with line $MN \parallel KL$:
$$\frac{JM}{MK} = \frac{JN}{NL}$$

# Similar Figures

## Definition
Similar figures have the same shape, but not necessarily the same size. Two polygons are considered similar (denoted by the symbol $|||$) if:
1. Corresponding angles are equal.
2. The ratios of the lengths of corresponding sides are equal.
3. They have one angle the same, and two sides adjacent to that angle in the same proportion (SAS criterion for similarity).

---

## Theorem 1: Equiangular Triangles
If two triangles are equiangular, their corresponding sides are in proportion, and thus the triangles are similar.

**Example:**
In $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle PQR$:
If $\hat{A} = \hat{P}$, $\hat{B} = \hat{Q}$, and $\hat{C} = \hat{R}$, then:

$$\frac{AB}{PQ} = \frac{AC}{PR} = \frac{BC}{QR}$$

Therefore, $\triangle ABC ||| \triangle PQR$ (Triangles are equiangular).

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

*Note: If two angles of a triangle are equal to two corresponding angles of another triangle, the triangles are similar.*

---

## Theorem 2: Proportional Sides
If two triangles have their corresponding sides in proportion, then they are equiangular, and thus the triangles are similar.

**Example:**
In $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle PQR$:
If:

$$\frac{AB}{PQ} = \frac{AC}{PR} = \frac{BC}{QR}$$

Then:
$\hat{A} = \hat{P}$, $\hat{B} = \hat{Q}$, and $\hat{C} = \hat{R}$.

Therefore, $\triangle ABC ||| \triangle PQR$ (Corresponding sides are in proportion).

### Mathematical Concepts & Formulas

**Proportionality Theorem:**
If a line is drawn parallel to one side of a triangle, it divides the other two sides proportionally.
$$\frac{PS}{SQ} = \frac{PT}{TR}$$

**Similar Triangles:**
Triangles are similar if their corresponding angles are equal and their corresponding sides are in the same proportion. If $\triangle ABC \parallel \triangle DEF$, then:
$$\frac{AB}{DE} = \frac{BC}{EF} = \frac{AC}{DF}$$

**Scale Factor:**
When triangles are similar, the ratio of their corresponding sides is constant:
$$\frac{\text{Side of large triangle}}{\text{Side of small triangle}} = k$$

#### Exercise 5: Similar Triangles and Shadows
A 12-centimeter rod is held between a flashlight and a wall. Find the length of the shadow on the wall if the rod is 45 cm from the wall and 15 cm from the light.

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

*   **Given:**
    *   Length of rod = $12\text{ cm}$
    *   Distance from light to rod = $15\text{ cm}$
    *   Distance from rod to wall = $45\text{ cm}$
    *   Total distance from light to wall = $15\text{ cm} + 45\text{ cm} = 60\text{ cm}$

*   **Method:** Use the property of similar triangles where the ratio of the shadow height ($h$) to the total distance is equal to the ratio of the rod height to the distance from the light.
    $$\frac{h}{60} = \frac{12}{15}$$

### Similarity and Proportionality

#### Key Concepts
*   **Similarity:** Two polygons are similar if their corresponding sides are in the same proportion. If the ratios are not equal, the shapes are not similar.
    *   Example: $\frac{10}{6} \neq \frac{7}{3}$
*   **Scale Drawings:** Dimensions are calculated using a scale factor.
    *   Example: $\frac{1 \text{ cm}}{1,5 \text{ cm}} \times 27 \text{ m} = 18 \text{ m}$ and $\frac{1 \text{ cm}}{1,5} \times 18 \text{ m} = 12 \text{ m}$
