## Key Mathematical Concepts

### 1. Distance Between Two Points
To calculate the distance $d$ between two points $A(x_1, y_1)$ and $B(x_2, y_2)$, we use the distance formula derived from the Pythagorean theorem:
$$d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}$$

### 2. Gradient of a Line
The gradient ($m$) represents the steepness and direction of a line passing through points $(x_1, y_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2)$:
$$m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$$

### 3. Mid-point of a Line
The mid-point $M(x, y)$ of a line segment connecting points $(x_1, y_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2)$ is the average of the coordinates:
$$M = \left( \frac{x_1 + x_2}{2} ; \frac{y_1 + y_2}{2} \right)$$

The order of the letters for naming a figure is important. It indicates the order in which points must be joined:
*   $A$ to $B$
*   $B$ to $C$
*   $C$ to $D$
*   $D$ back to $A$

# 8.2 Distance between two points

### Definitions
*   **Point:** A point is an ordered pair of numbers written as $(x; y)$.
*   **Distance:** Distance is a measure of the length between two points.

---

### Investigation: Distance between two points

Given points $P(2; 1)$, $Q(-2; -2)$, and $R(2; -2)$:

1.  **Can we assume that $\hat{R} = 90^\circ$?** Yes, because the line segment $PR$ is vertical (constant $x$-coordinate) and the line segment $QR$ is horizontal (constant $y$-coordinate).
2.  **Apply the theorem of Pythagoras in $\triangle PQR$ to find the length of $PQ$:**

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

*   Length of $PR = |1 - (-2)| = 3$ units.
*   Length of $QR = |2 - (-2)| = 4$ units.
*   By the theorem of Pythagoras:
    $$PQ^2 = PR^2 + QR^2$$
    $$PQ^2 = 3^2 + 4^2$$
    $$PQ^2 = 9 + 16$$
    $$PQ^2 = 25$$
    $$PQ = \sqrt{25} = 5 \text{ units}$$

---

### Deriving the General Distance Formula

To derive a general formula for the distance between two points $A(x_1; y_1)$ and $B(x_2; y_2)$, we use the theorem of Pythagoras.

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

By constructing a right-angled triangle $\triangle ABC$ where $C$ is the point $(x_2; y_1)$:
*   The length of $AC = |x_2 - x_1|$
*   The length of $BC = |y_2 - y_1|$

Applying the theorem of Pythagoras ($AB^2 = AC^2 + BC^2$):

$$AB = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}$$

---

### The Distance Formula

The distance formula is derived from the Pythagorean theorem. For a right-angled triangle with sides $AC$ and $BC$ and hypotenuse $AB$:

$$AB^2 = AC^2 + BC^2$$
$$\therefore AB = \sqrt{AC^2 + BC^2}$$

Given the coordinates of two points $(x_1; y_1)$ and $(x_2; y_2)$, the lengths of the sides are:
$$AC = x_2 - x_1$$
$$BC = y_2 - y_1$$

Substituting these into the Pythagorean theorem gives the **distance formula**:

$$d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}$$

**Note:** Because the terms are squared, the order of subtraction does not matter: $(x_1 - x_2)^2 = (x_2 - x_1)^2$.

---

### Worked Example 1: Using the distance formula

**Question:**
Find the distance between $S(-2; -5)$ and $Q(7; -2)$.

**Solution:**

**Step 1: Draw a sketch**

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

**Step 2: Assign coordinates**
Let $(x_1; y_1) = (-2; -5)$
Let $(x_2; y_2) = (7; -2)$

**Step 3: Apply the distance formula**
$$d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}$$
$$d = \sqrt{(7 - (-2))^2 + (-2 - (-5))^2}$$
$$d = \sqrt{(7 + 2)^2 + (-2 + 5)^2}$$
$$d = \sqrt{(9)^2 + (3)^2}$$
$$d = \sqrt{81 + 9}$$
$$d = \sqrt{90}$$
$$d = 3\sqrt{10} \approx 9,49$$

$$0 = (y + 3)(y - 21)$$

Solving for $y$:
$$\therefore y = -3 \text{ or } y = 21$$

## 8.3 Gradient of a line (EMA6B)

### Definition: Gradient
The gradient of a line is determined by the ratio of vertical change to horizontal change.

Gradient ($m$) describes the slope or steepness of the line joining two points. In the figure below, line $OQ$ is the least steep and line $OT$ is the steepest.

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

---

### Deriving the Gradient Formula

To derive the formula for the gradient, we consider a right-angled triangle formed by two points $A(x_1; y_1)$ and $B(x_2; y_2)$. The hypotenuse of this triangle is the line segment $AB$.

The gradient is defined as the ratio of the vertical side length to the horizontal side length:
*   **Vertical side:** The difference in $y$-values ($y_2 - y_1$).
*   **Horizontal side:** The difference in $x$-values ($x_2 - x_1$).

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

#### The Gradient Formula
The gradient ($m$) is calculated as:
$$m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1} \quad \text{or} \quad m = \frac{y_1 - y_2}{x_1 - x_2}$$

> **IMPORTANT!**
> Always be consistent with your order of coordinates. The following is **incorrect**:
> $$m \neq \frac{y_1 - y_2}{x_2 - x_1}$$

---

### Worked Example: Gradient between two points

**Question:**
Find the gradient of the line between points $E(2; 5)$ and $F(-3; 9)$.

**Solution:**

**Step 1: Draw a sketch**
<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

**Step 2: Assign coordinates**
Let $(x_1; y_1) = (2; 5)$ and $(x_2; y_2) = (-3; 9)$.

**Step 3: Apply the formula**
$$m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$$
$$m = \frac{9 - 5}{-3 - 2}$$
$$m = \frac{4}{-5}$$
$$m = -\frac{4}{5}$$

### Worked Example 4: Finding an Unknown Coordinate

**Question:**
Given $G(7; -9)$ and $H(x; 0)$, with a gradient $m_{GH} = 3$, find the value of $x$.

**Solution:**

**Step 1: Draw a sketch**
<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

**Step 2: Assign values to coordinates**
*   $G = (x_1; y_1) = (7; -9)$

$$3 = \frac{9}{x - 7}$$

Multiply both sides by $(x - 7)$:
$$3(x - 7) = 9$$

Divide by 3:
$$x - 7 = 3$$

Add 7 to both sides:
$$x = 10$$

# Straight Lines

## Definition
A straight line is a set of points with a constant gradient between any two of the points.

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

For points $A(x; y)$, $B(x_2; y_2)$, and $C(x_1; y_1)$, the gradient ($m$) is constant:
$$m_{AB} = m_{BC} = m_{AC}$$

The formula for the gradient is:
$$m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1} = \frac{y_1 - y_2}{x_1 - x_2}$$

## Equations of a Straight Line

### Two-Point Form
The general formula for finding the equation of a straight line given two points is:
$$\frac{y - y_1}{x - x_1} = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$$
where $(x; y)$ is any point on the line.

### Gradient-Point Form
The formula above can also be written as:
$$y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$$

### Standard Form
The standard form of the straight line equation is:
$$y = mx + c$$
Where:
*   $m$ is the gradient.
*   $c$ is the $y$-intercept.

### Horizontal and Vertical Lines (EMA6F)

#### Horizontal Lines
A line that runs parallel to the $x$-axis is called a horizontal line. It has a gradient of zero because there is no vertical change:

$$m = \frac{\text{change in } y}{\text{change in } x} = \frac{0}{\text{change in } x} = 0$$

#### Vertical Lines
A line that runs parallel to the $y$-axis is called a vertical line. Its gradient is undefined because there is no horizontal change:

$$m = \frac{\text{change in } y}{\text{change in } x} = \frac{\text{change in } y}{0} = \text{undefined}$$

#### Key Concepts and Definitions
*   **Point:** An ordered pair of numbers written as $(x; y)$.
*   **Distance:** A measure of the length between two points.
*   **Gradient ($m$):** The ratio of vertical change to horizontal change between two points.

$$m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$$

**3. Equations of a Straight Line**
*   **Standard Form:** $y = mx + c$

### 5. Calculating the Length of a Line Segment
To calculate the distance between two points $A(x_1; y_1)$ and $B(x_2; y_2)$ on the Cartesian plane, use the distance formula:

$$d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}$$

**Worked Example:**
Given points $A(-4; 1.5)$ and $B(3.5; -4.5)$:

1.  **Identify coordinates:**
    $x_1 = -4, y_1 = 1.5$
    $x_2 = 3.5, y_2 = -4.5$

2.  **Substitute into the formula:**
    $$AB = \sqrt{(3.5 - (-4))^2 + (-4.5 - 1.5)^2}$$
    $$AB = \sqrt{(7.5)^2 + (-6)^2}$$
    $$AB = \sqrt{56.25 + 36}$$
    $$AB = \sqrt{92.25}$$

3.  **Calculate final value:**
    $$AB \approx 9.60$$

### Analytical Geometry: Parallel and Perpendicular Lines

#### 12. Parallel Lines
**Concept:** Parallel lines have equal gradients ($m_1 = m_2$).

*   **Problem:** Line $AB$ passes through $A(-2; -1)$ and $B(x; 3)$. It is parallel to the line $y = x - 2$.
*   **Diagram:** <!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->
*   **Solution:**
    1. Identify the gradient of the given line: $m = 1$.
    2. Since $AB$ is parallel, its gradient is also $m = 1$.
    3. Use the gradient formula: $m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$
    4. Substitute the known values: $1 = \frac{3 - (-1)}{x - (-2)}$
    5. Simplify: $1 = \frac{4}{x + 2}$
    6. Solve for $x$: $x + 2 = 4 \implies x = 2$.
    7. The coordinate of point $B$ is $(2; 3)$.

#### 13. Perpendicular Lines
**Concept:** The product of the gradients of two perpendicular lines is $-1$ ($m_1 \times m_2 = -1$).

*   **Problem:** Line $AB$ passes through $A(-1; -4)$ and $B(1; y)$. It is perpendicular to the line $y = -0,5x - 2$.
*   **Diagram:** <!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->
*   **Solution:**
    1. Identify the gradient of the given line: $m_1 = -0,5 = -\frac{1}{2}$.
    2. Calculate the gradient of $AB$ ($m_2$): $m_2 = \frac{-1}{m_1} = \frac{-1}{-0,5} = 2$.
    3. Use the gradient formula: $m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$
    4. Substitute the known values: $2 = \frac{y - (-4)}{1 - (-1)}$
    5. Simplify: $2 = \frac{y + 4}{2}$
    6. Solve for $y$: $4 = y + 4 \implies y = 0$.
    7. The coordinate of point $B$ is $(1; 0)$.

### 18. Determining Equations of Straight Lines
The general equation of a straight line is $y = mx + c$, where $m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$.

**a) Passing through $P(5; 5)$ and $Q(-2; 12)$:**
1. Calculate gradient $m$: $m = \frac{12 - 5}{-2 - 5} = \frac{7}{-7} = -1$
2. Solve for $c$: $5 = -1(5) + c \implies 5 = -5 + c \implies c = 10$
**Equation:** $y = -x + 10$

**b) Parallel to $y = 3x + 4$ and passing through $(4; 0)$:**
1. Parallel lines have equal gradients, so $m = 3$.
2. Solve for $c$: $0 = 3(4) + c \implies 0 = 12 + c \implies c = -12$
**Equation:** $y = 3x - 12$

**c) Passing through $F(2; 1)$ and the mid-point of $GH$ where $G(-6; 3)$ and $H(-2; -3)$:**
1. Find mid-point of $GH$: $M = \left( \frac{-6 - 2}{2} ; \frac{3 - 3}{2} \right) = (-4; 0)$
2. Find gradient between $F(2; 1)$ and $M(-4; 0)$: $m = \frac{0 - 1}{-4 - 2} = \frac{-1}{-6} = \frac{1}{6}$
3. Solve for $c$ using $F(2; 1)$: $1 = \frac{1}{6}(2) + c \implies 1 = \frac{1}{3} + c \implies c = \frac{2}{3}$
**Equation:** $y = \frac{1}{6}x + \frac{2}{3}$

### Analytical Geometry: Parallel, Perpendicular, and Collinear Lines

#### 1. Determining Relationships Between Lines
To determine if two lines are parallel, perpendicular, or neither, we calculate their gradients ($m$) using the formula:
$$m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$$

*   **Parallel lines:** Gradients are equal ($m_1 = m_2$).
*   **Perpendicular lines:** The product of the gradients is $-1$ ($m_1 \times m_2 = -1$).

---

#### Worked Example: Comparing Lines $AB$ and $CD$
**Given:** $A(3; -4)$, $B(5; 2)$, $C(-1; 3)$, $D(-2; 2)$

**Step 1: Calculate gradient of $AB$ ($m_{AB}$)**
$$m_{AB} = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$$
$$m_{AB} = \frac{2 - (-4)}{5 - 3}$$
$$m_{AB} = \frac{6}{2} = 3$$

**Step 2: Calculate gradient of $CD$ ($m_{CD}$)**
$$m_{CD} = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$$
$$m_{CD} = \frac{2 - 3}{1 - (-2)}$$
$$m_{CD} = \frac{-1}{3}$$

**Conclusion:**
Since $m_{AB} \neq m_{CD}$, the lines are not parallel.
Since $m_{AB} \times m_{CD} = 3 \times (-\frac{1}{3}) = -1$, the lines $AB$ and $CD$ are **perpendicular**.

### Finding the Equation of a Line Parallel to a Given Line

When two lines are parallel, they have the same gradient ($m$). To find the equation of a line $AB$ that is parallel to a given line, follow these steps:

1.  **Identify the gradient:** Extract the gradient ($m$) from the given line equation ($y = mx + c$).
2.  **Find the $y$-intercept ($c$):** Substitute the coordinates of a known point $(x, y)$ and the gradient into the slope-intercept form equation:
    $$y = mx + c$$
3.  **Determine the full equation:** Write the final equation using the calculated $m$ and $c$.
4.  **Solve for missing coordinates:** Substitute the known $x$ or $y$ value of another point into your new equation to find the missing coordinate.

---

$$m = \frac{y_B - y_A}{x_B - x_A}$$

---

#### Worked Example 1: Finding the $x$-coordinate
**Problem:** Given line $AB$ with points $A(-2; 0,5)$ and $B(x; 3,5)$, and a gradient $m = 0,5$. Calculate the value of $x$.

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

**Solution:**
1. Substitute the known values into the gradient formula:
   $$0,5 = \frac{3,5 - 0,5}{x - (-2)}$$
2. Simplify the expression:
   $$0,5 = \frac{3}{x + 2}$$
3. Multiply both sides by $(x + 2)$:
   $$0,5(x + 2) = 3$$
4. Expand and solve for $x$:
   $$0,5x + 1 = 3$$
   $$0,5x = 2$$
   $$x = 4$$

---

#### Worked Example 2: Finding the $y$-coordinate
**Problem:** Given line $AB$ with points $A(-2; -2)$ and $B(1; y)$, and a gradient $m = 2$. Calculate the value of $y$.

<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

**Solution:**
1. Substitute the known values into the gradient formula:
   $$2 = \frac{y - (-2)}{1 - (-2)}$$
2. Simplify the denominator:
   $$2 = \frac{y + 2}{3}$$
3. Multiply both sides by $3$:
   $$2(3) = y + 2$$
   $$6 = y + 2$$
4. Solve for $y$:
   $$y = 6 - 2$$
   $$y = 4$$

### Analytical Geometry: Perpendicular Lines

#### Key Concept: Perpendicular Gradients
When two lines are perpendicular, the product of their gradients is $-1$. If line $AB$ is perpendicular to line $CD$, then:
$$m_{AB} \times m_{CD} = -1$$
Or, expressed as a formula to find the gradient of a perpendicular line:
$$m_{AB} = \frac{-1}{m_{CD}}$$

---

The mid-point theorem states that the line joining the mid-points of two sides of a triangle is parallel to the third side and equal to half the length of the third side. To prove this, we must show that $MN \parallel BC$ and $MN = \frac{1}{2}BC$.

$$m_{BC} = \frac{-2 - (-2)}{3 - (-5)} = 0$$
$$m_{MN} = \frac{1 - 1}{-4 - 0} = 0$$

### Analytical Geometry: Properties of Quadrilaterals and Linear Functions

#### 1. Determining if a Quadrilateral is a Rhombus
To determine if a quadrilateral $ABCD$ is a rhombus, we check if the diagonals intersect at right angles. Two lines are perpendicular if the product of their gradients is $-1$ ($m_1 \times m_2 = -1$).

**Worked Example:**
Given the gradients of diagonals $AC$ and $BD$:
$$m_{AC} = -\frac{15}{7}$$
$$m_{BD} = \frac{1}{3}$$

**Calculation:**
$$m_{AC} \times m_{BD} = -\frac{15}{7} \times \frac{1}{3}$$
$$= -\frac{15}{21} \neq -1$$

**Conclusion:**
$ABCD$ is not a rhombus because the diagonals do not intersect at right angles.

#### 2. Calculating Distance (Length of a Line Segment)
The distance $d$ between two points $(x_1; y_1)$ and $(x_2; y_2)$ is calculated using the distance formula:
$$d = \sqrt{(x_1 - x_2)^2 + (y_1 - y_2)^2}$$

**Worked Example: Find the length of $AB$**
Given points $A(-3; 6)$ and $B(5; 0)$:
$$AB = \sqrt{(-3 - 5)^2 + (6 - 0)^2}$$
$$AB = \sqrt{(-8)^2 + (6)^2}$$
$$AB = \sqrt{64 + 36}$$
$$AB = \sqrt{100}$$
$$AB = 10$$

### Analytical Geometry: Perpendicular Lines, Collinearity, and Mid-points

#### 1. Perpendicular Lines
When two lines are perpendicular, the product of their gradients is $-1$. If line $g(x)$ is perpendicular to $f(x)$, then:
$$m_{g(x)} \times m_{f(x)} = -1$$

**Worked Example: Finding the value of $b$**
Given a line $g(x)$ passing through $U$ and perpendicular to $f(x)$, where $V(b; 4)$ lies on $g(x)$:

#### c) Proving Parallel Lines ($AB \parallel MN$)
To prove that two lines are parallel, show that their gradients are equal ($m_{AB} = m_{MN}$).

**Step 1: Find the coordinates of $A$ using the Midpoint Formula**
The midpoint formula is $M = \left( \frac{x_1 + x_2}{2}; \frac{y_1 + y_2}{2} \right)$.
Given midpoint $M_{OA} = (-1, 4)$ and origin $(0,0)$:

$$(-1, 4) = \left( \frac{x_A + 0}{2}; \frac{y_A + 0}{2} \right)$$

*   For $x$: $-1 = \frac{x_A}{2} \implies x_A = -2$
*   For $y$: $4 = \frac{y_A}{2} \implies y_A = 8$
*   Therefore, $A = (-2; 8)$

**Step 2: Find the coordinates of $B$**
Given midpoint $M_{OB} = (5, 2)$ and origin $(0,0)$:

$$(5, 2) = \left( \frac{x_B + 0}{2}; \frac{y_B + 0}{2} \right)$$

*   For $x$: $5 = \frac{x_B}{2} \implies x_B = 10$
*   For $y$: $2 = \frac{y_B}{2} \implies y_B = 4$
*   Therefore, $B = (10; 4)$

**Step 3: Calculate the gradient of $AB$**
Using the gradient formula $m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$:

$$m_{AB} = \frac{4 - 8}{10 - (-2)}$$
$$m_{AB} = \frac{-4}{12}$$
$$m_{AB} = -\frac{1}{3}$$

**Conclusion:**
Since $m_{AB} = -\frac{1}{3}$ and $m_{MN} = -\frac{1}{3}$, then $m_{AB} = m_{MN}$.
Therefore, $AB \parallel MN$.
