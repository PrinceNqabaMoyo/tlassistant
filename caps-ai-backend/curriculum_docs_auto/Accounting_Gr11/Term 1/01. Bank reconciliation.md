# Term 1 Topic 3: Bank Reconciliation

## What is Bank Reconciliation?
Bank reconciliation is a vital internal control procedure. It involves comparing the business's internal cash records against the information provided by the bank to ensure they match.

### Comparison of Records
| Records of the Business | Records of the Bank |
| :--- | :--- |
| Cash Receipts Journal (CRJ) | Bank Statement |
| Cash Payments Journal (CPJ) | |
| Bank Account (General Ledger) | |

### Why do differences occur?
Differences between the business records and the bank statement usually arise due to:
1. **Timing differences:** Transactions recorded by one party but not yet processed by the other.
2. **Errors:** Mistakes made either by the business or the bank when recording transactions.

---

## Reasons for Bank Reconciliation
Performing a bank reconciliation serves several key business functions:
* **Verification:** Confirms the actual amount of cash available in the bank account.
* **Control:** Enables the business to monitor and manage cash flow effectively.
* **Accuracy:** Provides an opportunity to identify and eliminate errors on the bank statement.
* **Security:** Acts as a detective control to uncover irregularities or fraudulent activity.

---

## Procedure for Bank Reconciliation
To perform a bank reconciliation, follow these systematic steps:

1. **Review Previous Records:** Compare the bank reconciliation statement from the previous month with the current bank statement. Identify and mark all differences (items that do not appear in both sets of records).
2. **Verify Receipts:** Compare the amounts in the bank column of the **Cash Receipts Journal (CRJ)** with the deposits (credits) shown on the bank statement. Mark any discrepancies.
3. **Verify Payments:** Compare the amounts in the bank column of the **Cash Payments Journal (CPJ)** with the payments (debits) shown on the bank statement. Mark any discrepancies.
4. **Investigate Outstanding Items:** Examine items that were marked on the previous bank reconciliation statement. Make necessary corrections or carry them forward to the current bank reconciliation statement if they remain outstanding.

# Term 1 Topic 3: Bank Reconciliation

## The Bank Reconciliation Process
To ensure the business records match the bank's records, follow these steps:

1. **Bank Statement Items:** Record differences or items marked on the bank statement in the business's cash journals (if they relate to the business) or in the bank reconciliation statement (if the bank needs to make corrections).
2. **Cash Journal Items:** Record differences or items marked in the cash journals in the bank reconciliation statement, or correct them directly in the cash journals.
3. **General Ledger:** Post the new totals from the updated cash journals to the Bank account in the General Ledger.
4. **Verification:** The final balance of the Bank account in the General Ledger must agree with the balance shown on the bank reconciliation statement.

> **Note:** The business compiles the bank reconciliation statement, not the bank. This statement assists the business in tracking information that the bank has not yet processed.

---

## Possible Entries in Cash Journals and Reconciliation

### 1. Cash Receipts Journal (CRJ)
These entries increase the bank balance and are recorded in the CRJ:
* Direct deposits into the current bank account.
* Receipts previously omitted or not recorded.
* Cancellation of cheques.
* Correction of payments (if the amount originally recorded in the Cash Payments Journal was too high).
* Interest received from the bank that was not previously recorded.

### 2. Cash Payments Journal (CPJ)
These entries decrease the bank balance and are recorded in the CPJ:
* Stop orders or debit orders paid by the bank but not yet recorded by the business.
* Bank charges not yet recorded.
* Interest on overdraft not yet recorded.
* Cheques received from debtors that have been dishonoured (RD).
* Correction of payments (if the amount originally recorded in the Cash Payments Journal was too low).
* Electronic fund transfers (EFTs) not yet recorded.
* Payments previously omitted or not recorded.

### 3. Bank Reconciliation Statement
These items are recorded on the bank reconciliation statement because they represent timing differences or bank errors:
* **Outstanding deposits:** Deposits made by the business but not yet recorded by the bank.
* **Outstanding cheques:** Cheques issued by the business but not yet presented to the bank for payment.
* **Bank errors:** Corrections of mistakes made by the bank.

# Term 1 Topic 3: Bank Reconciliation

## No Entry Procedures
Certain transactions do not require an immediate entry in the business's books. Follow these guidelines:

*   **Post-dated cheques from debtors:**
    1. Record the cheque in the post-dated cheque received register.
    2. Keep the cheque in a safe place until the date on the cheque.
    3. Issue a receipt on the date shown on the cheque.
    4. Record the cheque in the Cash Receipts Journal (CRJ).
    5. Deposit the cheque.
*   **Outstanding items:** No entry is required when outstanding deposits or outstanding cheques from a previous bank reconciliation statement appear on the current month’s bank statement, as these were already accounted for in the previous period.

### Key Concepts for Reconciliation

*   **Outstanding Deposits:** Amounts recorded in the business's Cash Receipts Journal (CRJ) but not yet processed by the bank.
*   **Outstanding Cheques:** Cheques issued and recorded in the Cash Payments Journal (CPJ) but not yet presented to the bank for payment.
*   **Dishonoured Cheques:** Cheques previously deposited but returned by the bank due to issues such as lack of funds, incorrect signatures, or stale dates.
*   **Bank Charges/Interest:** Fees or interest amounts appearing on the bank statement that must be recorded in the business's journals to update the bank account balance.
*   **Errors:** Discrepancies between the amount recorded in the journals and the amount processed by the bank (e.g., Item 12).

### Key Concepts & Notes

*   **Outstanding Deposits:** Amounts recorded in the business's CRJ but not yet processed by the bank by the end of the month. These appear as additions in the Bank Reconciliation Statement.
*   **Outstanding Cheques:** Cheques issued by the business (recorded in the CPJ) that have not yet been presented to the bank for payment. These appear as subtractions in the Bank Reconciliation Statement.
*   **Direct Deposits:** Payments made directly into the business bank account by third parties (e.g., rent). These must be recorded in the CRJ.
*   **Debit/Stop Orders & Bank Charges:** Automatic deductions processed by the bank. These must be recorded in the CPJ.
*   **Dishonoured Cheques:** Cheques previously received and recorded as receipts that the bank refuses to pay due to insufficient funds. These must be reversed in the CPJ.
*   **Post-dated Cheques:** Cheques dated for a future month. These are not recognized in the current month's bank reconciliation or journals.
