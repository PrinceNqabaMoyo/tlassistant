# Term 3 Topic 3: Cost Accounting

## 1. Introduction to concepts

### 1.1 Introduction
Enterprises are categorized based on their primary business activity:
*   **Trading Enterprise:** Buys and sells manufactured products at a profit. They deal with "purchased and sold trading stock."
*   **Service Enterprise:** Uses human skills to provide a service to the community.
*   **Manufacturing Enterprise:** Manufactures finished products from raw materials.

### 1.2 Cost concepts

| CONCEPT | EXPLANATION |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Cost** | The monetary value needed to manufacture a product or to render a service. |
| **Fixed costs** | Costs that remain the same regardless of the amount of products produced. |
| **Variable costs** | Costs that change as the amount of products produced changes. |
| **Direct costs** | Costs that can be directly linked to specific products, the production process, or a section. |
| **Indirect costs** | Costs that cannot be directly linked to a specific product or activity; they are part of the manufacturing process and are usually referred to as **overheads**. |
| **Labour costs** | The total amount paid to workers who are directly or indirectly involved in the manufacturing of products (includes worker contributions). |
| **Direct labour costs** | Remuneration paid to employees who are directly involved in the manufacturing of a product. |
| **Indirect labour costs** | Remuneration paid to employees who are not directly involved in the manufacturing of a product. |

## Key Concepts and Definitions

| Concept | Definition |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Direct material costs** | Costs of all raw materials and parts used in the production process that form part of the final product. |
| **Indirect material costs** | Materials and parts used in small quantities that form part of indirect costs. It is not possible to determine the exact amount used for individual products. |
| **Work-in-process** | Incomplete products. As units are manufactured, there will always be units at various stages of completion (e.g., 1% - 99%) at the end of a period. |
| **Completed products** | Units that are 100% complete and ready to be sold. |
| **Cost of sales** | The cost associated with completed units that have been sold. |

## Cost Formulas

The following mathematical relationships are used to determine production costs:

*   **Total costs:**
    $$\text{Total costs} = \text{Fixed costs} + \text{Variable costs}$$

*   **Primary costs:**
    $$\text{Primary costs} = \text{Direct material} + \text{Direct labour}$$

*   **Manufacturing costs:**
    $$\text{Manufacturing costs} = \text{Primary costs} + \text{Overheads}$$

## Activity 2: Manufacturing Costs for Thembi's Bags

Thembi manufactures designer bags. The following table outlines the costs incurred in the manufacturing process:

| Item | Quantity/Usage | Cost Rate |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Material | $0,75\text{m}$ | $R24$ per metre |
| Cotton | $50\text{m}$ | $R10$ per $1000\text{m}$ |
| Webbing (handle) | $0,75\text{m}$ | $R20$ per metre |
| Beads (decoration) | $48$ units | $R7$ per dozen |
| Labour | $2,5$ hours | $R15$ per hour |
| Rent (garage) | Monthly | $R1\,500$ per month |
| Cleaning material | Monthly | $R50$ per month |
| Electricity | Monthly | $R200$ per month |

---

## Questions

### 2.1 What is Thembi's Total direct material cost per item?

To calculate the direct material cost, sum the costs of the specific materials used per bag:
*   **Material:** $0,75\text{m} \times R24 = R18,00$
*   **Cotton:** $(\frac{50\text{m}}{1000\text{m}}) \times R10 = R0,50$
*   **Webbing:** $0,75\text{m} \times R20 = R15,00$
*   **Beads:** $(\frac{48}{12}) \times R7 = R28,00$

**Total Direct Material Cost:** $R18,00 + R0,50 + R15,00 + R28,00 = R61,50$

### 2.2 What is Thembi's Total direct labour cost per item?

*   **Labour:** $2,5\text{ hours} \times R15/\text{hour} = R37,50$

**Total Direct Labour Cost:** $R37,50$

### 2.3 What is Thembi's Total primary cost per item?

Primary cost (or Prime cost) is the sum of direct material costs and direct labour costs.

*   **Formula:** $\text{Primary Cost} = \text{Direct Material} + \text{Direct Labour}$
*   **Calculation:** $R61,50 + R37,50 = R99,00$

**Total Primary Cost:** $R99,00$

### 2.4 What is the total overhead cost involved in making the bags?

Overhead costs are the indirect costs associated with the manufacturing process (fixed monthly costs).

*   **Rent:** $R1\,500$
*   **Cleaning material:** $R50$
*   **Electricity:** $R200$

**Total Overhead Cost:** $R1\,500 + R50 + R200 = R1\,750$

## 2.5 Fixed Costs vs. Variable Costs
*   **Fixed Costs:** These are costs that remain constant regardless of the level of production or sales volume (e.g., factory rent, insurance, or salaries of permanent staff).
*   **Variable Costs:** These are costs that change in direct proportion to the level of production or sales volume (e.g., raw materials, direct labour, or packaging).

## 2.6 Calculation of Total Profit
To calculate the total profit for 100 bags, use the following formula:
$$\text{Total Profit} = (\text{Selling Price} \times \text{Quantity}) - (\text{Total Fixed Costs} + \text{Total Variable Costs})$$

Given:
*   Quantity = $100$
*   Selling Price = $R150$

$$\text{Total Revenue} = 100 \times R150 = R15\,000$$
$$\text{Total Profit} = R15\,000 - \text{Total Costs}$$

# Term 3 Topic 3: Cost Accounting

## 1. Concepts and Definitions

In manufacturing, costs are classified based on their relationship to the production process. Understanding these categories is essential for accurate product costing.

### Cost Classifications
*   **Direct Material Costs:** Raw materials that become a physical part of the finished product and can be easily traced to a specific unit (e.g., denim fabric).
*   **Direct Labour Costs:** Wages paid to employees who are directly involved in the physical manufacturing of the product (e.g., machinists).
*   **Indirect Material Costs:** Materials used in the factory that are necessary for production but are not part of the final product or are too insignificant to trace (e.g., cotton for sewing machines).
*   **Indirect Labour Costs:** Wages or salaries of factory staff who support production but do not work directly on the product (e.g., cleaners, quality checkers).
*   **Fixed Costs:** Expenses that remain constant regardless of the number of units produced (e.g., rent, insurance).
*   **Variable Costs:** Expenses that change in direct proportion to the level of production output (e.g., raw materials).

## 3. Conceptual Understanding: Fixed vs. Variable Costs

**Task:** Explain the difference between Variable cost and Fixed cost in manufacturing.

### Fixed Cost
Fixed costs are business expenses that do not change with the level of production or sales volume. These costs must be paid regardless of whether the factory produces one unit or one thousand units. Examples include factory rent, insurance premiums, and management salaries.

### Variable Cost
Variable costs are expenses that fluctuate in direct proportion to the volume of goods produced. If production increases, variable costs increase; if production decreases, variable costs decrease. These costs are directly tied to the manufacturing process, such as the cost of raw materials (e.g., denim, zips) and piece-rate labour.
