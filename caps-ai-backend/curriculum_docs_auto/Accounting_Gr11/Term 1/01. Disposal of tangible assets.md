# Term 1 Topic 4: Fixed/Tangible Assets

## What is an asset?
Assets are resources that the business possesses and controls. They are expected to bring future economic benefits to the business.

### Classification of Assets

| Non-current assets | Current assets |
| :--- | :--- |
| These are long-lasting resources (possessions of the business) like property, buildings, vehicles, and equipment. | These are assets consisting of cash or that can be converted into cash in the short term (less than a year). |
| **Fixed/Tangible assets** | **Financial assets** | |
| • Land and buildings | • Fixed assets | • Stock |
| • Vehicle | • Investments | • Trade and other receivables/Debtors |
| • Equipment | • Shares in companies | • Cash and cash equivalents |

---

## Fixed Assets
The business uses fixed assets to generate income. Some fixed assets have a limited useful life and must be replaced after a certain number of years. The cost of the fixed asset is distributed over its useful life by means of **depreciation**.

### Key Concepts

#### 1. Cost Price
The cost price of an asset is the original price paid for the asset. This includes any costs incurred in acquiring the asset, such as:
* Transport costs
* Registration costs
* Installation fees

#### 2. Depreciation
Depreciation is the allocation of the value of an asset over its useful life. It serves two main purposes:
* It indicates how much of the value of the asset has been 'used' to generate income in a specific period.
* It indicates which part of the cost of the asset can be used as a tax-deductible expense.

**Methods of calculating depreciation:**
* **Fixed percentage on cost price:** Also known as the straight-line method or fixed amount method.
* **Diminished balance method:** Also known as the percentage on carrying value method.

## Key Concepts

### Accumulated Depreciation
This represents the total depreciation written off on a specific asset up to a specific date, or the total value of the asset that has been "used up."

### Carrying Value
The carrying value is the remaining value of an asset that has not yet been written off as an expense. It is calculated using the following formula:

$$\text{Carrying Value} = \text{Cost Price} - \text{Accumulated Depreciation}$$

*Note: The minimum carrying value to be used in financial records is R1.*

### Asset Register
An asset register is a detailed record of each fixed asset owned by a business. It is essential for:
* Tracking the correct value of each asset.
* Maintaining internal control over assets.
* Making informed decisions regarding the economic value of assets.

# Term 1 Topic 4: Fixed/Tangible Assets

## Disposal of a Fixed Asset

When an asset no longer provides economic benefit to a business, it is disposed of to prevent unnecessary costs. Assets may be sold, withdrawn by the owner, traded in, or donated.

### Required Information for Disposal
To record the disposal of an asset, the following information is required:
1. **Cost price:** The original purchase price of the asset.
2. **Accumulated depreciation:** The total depreciation calculated up to the date of disposal.
3. **Disposal value:** The amount received (or value assigned) when the asset is sold, traded in, or withdrawn.
4. **Profit or Loss:** The difference between the carrying value and the disposal value.

---

## Example 2: Financial Year Ends 30 June
A computer was purchased on 1 July 20.2 for $R8\,000$. The table below illustrates the profit on the sale of the asset based on different disposal dates, assuming a depreciation rate of $25\%$ per annum.

| | Sold 1 July 20.3 for R7 000 | Sold 1 January 20.4 for R7 000 | Sold 30 June 20.4 for R7 000 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cost price** | $8\,000$ | $8\,000$ | $8\,000$ |
| **Accumulated depreciation** | $2\,000$ $(25\% \times 8\,000)$ | $3\,000$ $[2\,000 + (25\% \times 8\,000 \times \frac{6}{12})]$ | $4\,000$ $[2\,000 + (25\% \times 8\,000)]$ |
| **Carrying value** | $6\,000$ $(8\,000 - 2\,000)$ | $5\,000$ $(8\,000 - 3\,000)$ | $4\,000$ $(8\,000 - 4\,000)$ |
| **Sold for** | $7\,000$ | $7\,000$ | $7\,000$ |
| **Profit on sale of asset** | $1\,000$ $(7\,000 - 6\,000)$ | $2\,000$ $(7\,000 - 5\,000)$ | $3\,000$ $(7\,000 - 4\,000)$ |

## Example 4: Disposal of Asset
**Scenario:** A computer is purchased on 1 July 20.5 for $R8\,000$. Depreciation is $25\%$ per annum on cost. The computer is sold for $R7\,000$ cash on 1 January 20.7.

### Calculation of Profit/Loss
1. **Cost Price:** $R8\,000$
2. **Accumulated Depreciation:**
   * Year 1 (1 July 20.5 – 30 June 20.6): $8\,000 \times 25\% = 2\,000$
   * Year 2 (1 July 20.6 – 1 Jan 20.7): $8\,000 \times 25\% \times \frac{6}{12} = 1\,000$
   * Total: $2\,000 + 1\,000 = 3\,000$
3. **Carrying Value:** $8\,000 - 3\,000 = 5\,000$
4. **Profit on Sale:** $7\,000 - 5\,000 = 2\,000$

### Disposal Journal Entries
| Transaction | Subsidiary book | Account to debit | Account to credit | Amount |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Transfer of cost price | General Journal | Asset disposal | Equipment | $8\,000$ |

| Transfer of accum. dep. | General Journal | Accumulated depreciation | Asset disposal | $3\,000$ ($2\,000 + 1\,000$) |
| Entry for sales | Cash Receipts Journal | Bank | Asset disposal | $7\,000$ |
| Entry for profit | General Journal | Asset disposal | Profit on sale of asset | $2\,000$ ($7\,000 - 5\,000$) |

### Summary of Disposal Methods
| Transaction | Subsidiary book | Account to debit | Account to credit |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Entry for cash sales | Cash Receipts Journal | Bank | Asset disposal |
| Entry for credit sales | General Journal | Debtors control | Asset disposal |
| Entry for trade-in | General Journal | Creditors control | Asset disposal |
| Entry for drawings | General Journal | Drawings | Asset disposal |
| Entry for a donation | General Journal | Donations | Asset disposal |

# Term 1 Topic 4: Fixed/Tangible Assets

## 1. Concept: The Fixed Asset Note
The Fixed Asset Note is a supplementary schedule to the financial statements that provides a detailed breakdown of the movement in the carrying value of assets during the financial year.

### Key Formulas
*   **Carrying Value:** $\text{Cost Price} - \text{Accumulated Depreciation} = \text{Carrying Value}$
*   **Movement Calculation:** $\text{Opening Carrying Value} + \text{Additions} - \text{Disposals} - \text{Depreciation} = \text{Closing Carrying Value}$
*   **Closing Cost Price:** $\text{Opening Cost} + \text{Additions} - \text{Disposals} = \text{Closing Cost}$

## 3. Mathematical Formulas

### Diminishing Balance Method (Carrying Value)
Depreciation is calculated on the book value of the asset at the start of the financial year:
$$\text{Depreciation} = \text{Carrying Value} \times \text{Rate}$$

### Cost Price Method (Straight Line)
Depreciation is calculated on the original purchase price of the asset:
$$\text{Depreciation} = \text{Cost Price} \times \text{Rate}$$

### Calculations for Accumulated Depreciation on Vehicles

*   **Depreciation on vehicle sold:**
    $15\% \times (70\,000 - 46\,000) \times \frac{11}{12} = 3\,300$

The following table illustrates the movement of fixed assets for the financial year.

| **Movements** | | | | |
| Additions | 440 000 | 360 000 | 160 000 | 960 000 |
| Disposals (carrying value) | 0 | – | (34 816) | (34 816) |
| Depreciation | 0 | (95 000) | (31 624) | (126 624) |
| **Carrying value at end of year** | **920 000** | **620 000** | **360 000** | **1 900 000** |

#### Calculations

**Depreciation on vehicles for year:**
*   Old: $25\% \times 260\,000 = 65\,000$
*   New: $25\% \times 360\,000 \times \frac{4}{12} = 30\,000$
*   Total: $95\,000$

**Depreciation on equipment for year:**
*   Remaining: $20\% \times [(280\,000 - 80\,000) - (136\,640 - 39\,040)] = 20\,480$
*   New on 1 Dec. 20.8: $20\% \times 70\,000 \times \frac{3}{12} = 3\,500$
*   New on 1 Feb. 20.9: $20\% \times (86\,000 + 4\,000) \times \frac{1}{12} = 1\,500$
*   Total: $6\,144 + 20\,480 + 3\,500 + 1\,500 = 31\,624$

**Asset Balances:**
*   Additions to equipment: $70\,000 + 86\,000 + 4\,000 = 160\,000$
*   Equipment on 28 Feb. 20.9: $280\,000 - 80\,000 + 70\,000 + 86\,000 + 4\,000 = 360\,000$
*   Accumulated depreciation on equipment, 28 Feb. 20.9: $136\,640 + 31\,624 - 45\,184 = 123\,080$
