# Term 2 Topic 2: Non-profit organisations (NPOs)/Clubs

## Accounting for Clubs and NPOs
Non-profit organisations operate with the primary purpose of providing a service to their members or the community. The management committee is responsible for managing the organisation's resources. Standard accounting principles and concepts remain applicable to these entities.

### Key Concepts for NPO Accounting
*   **Membership Fees:** These are the primary source of income for most clubs. They are treated as income in the Income Statement. Amounts received in advance are treated as current liabilities, while amounts in arrears are treated as current assets.
*   **Trading Accounts (e.g., Athletic Vests):** Clubs often sell items to members. The accounting for these items follows the perpetual inventory system or a trading account approach where:
    *   **Purchases** are debited to the inventory/trading account.
    *   **Sales** are credited to the trading account.
    *   **Profit/Loss** is determined by comparing the cost of goods sold against the sales revenue, usually transferred to the Income and Expenditure Statement.

# Term 2 Topic 2: Non-profit Organisations/Clubs

## Key Concepts and Adjustments

In the accounting for non-profit organisations, specific adjustments are required at the end of the financial year regarding membership fees, inventory control, and honorariums.

### 1. Membership Fees
*   **Bad Debts:** Membership fees outstanding from the previous financial year ($20.2$) that are deemed uncollectable must be written off. The names of these members must be removed from the members' register.
*   **Outstanding Fees:** Membership fees for the current year ($20.3$) that remain unpaid must be recorded as outstanding.

### 2. Treasurer's Honorarium/Owed Amounts
*   If the organisation owes money to the treasurer (e.g., for expenses or honorarium), and the treasurer owes membership fees, a "set-off" may be granted. The amount owed to the treasurer is used to settle their outstanding membership fees for the upcoming year ($20.3$).

### 3. Inventory Control (Tracksuits)
*   Management must perform a physical stock take to verify the quantity of tracksuits on hand. Discrepancies between the physical count and the accounting records suggest potential theft or loss, requiring an adjustment to the stock account.

### 4.4 Debt-Equity Ratio Analysis
The debt-equity ratio is used to assess the financial leverage of an entity.

**Calculation:**
$$\text{Ratio} = 753\,140 : (740\,000 + 320\,000 + 49\,808 - 21\,700)$$
$$\text{Ratio} = 753\,140 : 1\,088\,108$$
$$\text{Ratio} \approx 0,69 : 1$$

**Interpretation:**
The ratio is relatively high. This may have a negative impact on future loan negotiations, depending on the magnitude of the loan requested.
