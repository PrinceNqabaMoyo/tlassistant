# Term 3 Topic 2: Analysis of Financial Statements

## 1. Introduction to Concepts

The analysis and interpretation of financial statements is an evaluation process used to assess the current and previous financial positions and performance results of an enterprise.

A **ratio** is the mathematical relationship between two different figures, expressed in simple terms. To be useful, ratios must represent meaningful relationships and be compared against benchmarks or comparative figures from previous periods.

### The Aims of Financial Analysis
The primary objectives of analyzing financial statements are to determine:

| Objective | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **1.1 Profitability** | A measure of business success by comparing profit made against the amount sold or invested. It also helps identify potential cost control issues. |
| **1.2 Return** | Determines if the investors (owners) are receiving a reasonable return on the capital they have invested in the business. |
| **1.3 Solvency** | The ability of the business to meet its financial obligations on time and its capacity to pay off long-term debts. |

## Calculating Gross Profit Percentages

Gross profit can be expressed as a percentage of either **Sales (Turnover)** or **Cost of Sales**.

### 1. Gross Profit as a Percentage of Sales
To calculate the gross profit percentage based on sales, use the following formula:

$$\frac{\text{Gross Profit}}{\text{Sales}} \times \frac{100}{1} = \text{Percentage}$$

**Example:**
If Gross Profit is R750 and Sales are R3 750:
$$\frac{750}{3 750} \times \frac{100}{1} = 20\%$$

### 2. Gross Profit as a Percentage of Cost of Sales
To calculate the gross profit percentage based on cost of sales, use the following formula:

$$\frac{\text{Gross Profit}}{\text{Cost of Sales}} \times \frac{100}{1} = \text{Percentage}$$

**Example:**
If Gross Profit is R750 and Cost of Sales is R3 000:
$$\frac{750}{3 000} \times \frac{100}{1} = 25\%$$

---

## Key Concept: Relationship between Mark-up and Margin
A profit mark-up of **20% on sales** is equivalent to a profit mark-up of **25% on cost of sales**. If a business maintains this mark-up consistently, these percentages will remain constant in the final Income Statement.

### Worked Example: Income Statement Analysis
Consider the following financial data for a business:

| Item | Amount (R) |
| :--- | :--- |
| Sales (240 articles) | 900 000 |
| Less: Cost of sales | (720 000) |
| **Gross profit** | **180 000** |

#### Analysis of the above data:

**Gross profit expressed as a percentage of sales:**
$$\frac{180 000}{900 000} \times \frac{100}{1} = 20\%$$

**Gross profit expressed as a percentage of cost of sales:**
$$\frac{180 000}{720 000} \times \frac{100}{1} = 25\%$$

# Term 3 Topic 2: Analysis of Financial Statements

## Key Definitions
*   **Turnover:** $\text{Sales} - \text{Debtors' Allowances}$
*   **Gross Profit:** $\text{Turnover} - \text{Cost of Sales}$

---

## Financial Ratios

### a) Percentage Gross Profit on Turnover
This ratio represents the gross profit of a financial year as a percentage of the net sales (turnover). If a business maintains a constant mark-up policy, this percentage should remain consistent.

**Formula:**
$$\text{Percentage gross profit on turnover} = \frac{\text{Gross profit}}{\text{Turnover}} \times 100$$

**Reasons for variation in this ratio:**
*   Theft of inventory.
*   Incorrect sales calculations.
*   Trade discounts allowed to customers.

### b) Percentage Gross Profit on Cost of Sales
This ratio determines whether the purchasing policy and the storage of inventory are being applied successfully. A decrease in this percentage compared to the previous year may indicate theft in the warehouse.

**Formula:**
$$\text{Percentage gross profit on cost of sales} = \frac{\text{Gross profit}}{\text{Cost of sales}} \times 100$$

### c) Percentage Operating Profit on Turnover
This ratio represents the operating profit as a percentage of turnover. It accounts for income and expenses before interest is considered. It is used to evaluate how effectively business expenses are being managed.

**Formula:**
$$\text{Percentage operating profit on turnover} = \frac{\text{Operating profit}}{\text{Turnover}} \times 100$$

*   **Note:** A lower percentage for operating expenses relative to turnover indicates better management of business expenses.

## Profitability Ratios (Continued)

### d) Net profit on turnover
This ratio represents the net profit for a financial period as a percentage of sales.

**Formula:**
$$\text{Net profit on turnover} = \frac{\text{Net profit}}{\text{Turnover}} \times \frac{100}{1}$$

*   **Analysis:** This percentage should ideally be $\frac{1}{3}$ of the percentage gross profit on turnover.
*   **Interpretation:** If the percentage differs significantly from the gross profit on turnover, it indicates that expenses are too high. ### e) Total expenses as a percentage of turnover
This ratio shows the portion of sales used to cover operating expenses.

**Formula:**
$$\text{Total expenses as a percentage of turnover} = \frac{\text{Operating expenses}}{\text{Turnover}} \times \frac{100}{1}$$

*   **Analysis:** This relates directly to cost control.
*   **Interpretation:** Compare this figure to the previous year. The percentage should ideally remain the same or decrease. A lower percentage indicates better management of expenses.

## 3.2 Liquidity

Liquidity refers to the ability of a business to meet its short-term obligations (current liabilities) using available funds (current assets).

### a) Current ratio
The current ratio measures the extent to which current assets cover current liabilities.

**Formula:**
$$\text{Current ratio} = \text{Current assets} : \text{Current liabilities}$$

*   **Calculation:** The current assets are divided by the current liabilities to express the ratio as $x : 1$.
*   **Interpretation:** The higher the ratio, the greater the business's ability to meet its short-term obligations.
*   **Example:** If the ratio is $2 : 1$, it means there are current assets of $R2$ available for every $R1$ of current liabilities.

## b) Acid Test Ratio
The acid test ratio is a measure of liquidity that excludes inventories, as these cannot always be immediately converted into cash.

### Formula
$$\text{Acid test ratio} = (\text{Current assets} - \text{Inventories}) : \text{Current liabilities}$$

### Key Notes
*   **Inventories:** Includes trading stock and consumable stores on hand. Both must be deducted from current assets.
*   **Interpretation:** The ratio is expressed as $x : 1$.
*   **Liquidity:** A business is considered liquid if the ratio is greater than $1$ (e.g., $1,2 : 1$). This means for every $R1$ of current liability, there is $R1,20$ of liquid assets available.

## 3.3 Solvency of the Business
Solvency refers to the ability of a business to meet its long-term financial obligations.

### Formula
$$\text{Solvency ratio} = \text{Total assets} : \text{Total liabilities}$$

### Components
*   $$\text{Total assets} = \text{Tangible assets} + \text{Fixed deposit} + \text{Current assets}$$
*   $$\text{Total liabilities} = \text{Non-current liabilities} + \text{Current liabilities}$$

### Status Definitions
*   **Solvent:** $\text{Total assets} > \text{Total liabilities}$
*   **Insolvent:** $\text{Total assets} < \text{Total liabilities}$
*   **Interpretation:** The ratio is expressed as $x : 1$. A business is considered solvent if the ratio is not lower than $1 : 1$.

# Term 3 Topic 2: Analysis of Financial Statements

## Borrowed Capital and Financial Risk
When a business borrows money, it must pay interest. Relying heavily on borrowed capital increases financial risk. If profits decline, the business must still repay the loan and interest, which can cause significant financial strain.

### The Debt-to-Equity Ratio
This ratio indicates the extent to which a business is financed by borrowed capital versus owner's equity. It helps stakeholders assess the level of risk the business has taken on.

#### Interpretation of Debt-to-Equity Ratios
*   **$1 : 1$**: Regarded as acceptable. For every $R1$ invested by owners, there is $R1$ of borrowed funds.
*   **$0,7 : 1$**: Considered low-risk (favourable gearing). The business relies more on equity than debt.
*   **$1,3 : 1$**: Considered high-risk (negative gearing). The business relies heavily on borrowed capital, which may lead to a poor credit rating.
*   **Lending Threshold**: Financial providers (banks) generally prefer to lend to businesses with a ratio between $0,5 : 1$ and $1 : 1$.

## 1. Liquidity Ratios
Liquidity ratios measure a business's ability to pay its short-term debts using its current assets.

### 1.2.1 Calculations for 2008
*   **Current Ratio:** Measures the ability to pay short-term liabilities with current assets.
    $$\text{Current Ratio} = \frac{\text{Current Assets}}{\text{Current Liabilities}}$$
*   **Acid Test Ratio:** Measures the ability to pay short-term liabilities with current assets excluding inventory (as inventory is not always easily converted to cash).
    $$\text{Acid Test Ratio} = \frac{\text{Current Assets} - \text{Inventory}}{\text{Current Liabilities}}$$

### 1.2.2 Interpretation
*   **Question:** Can the business pay its short-term debts? Briefly explain.
*   **Guidance:** Compare the calculated ratios against the industry norms (typically $2:1$ for Current Ratio and $1:1$ for Acid Test Ratio). If the ratio is above the norm, the business is generally liquid.
