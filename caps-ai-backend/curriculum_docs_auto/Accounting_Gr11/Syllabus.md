# Accounting Curriculum Specifications (CAPS)

## 1. Curriculum Overview
Accounting focuses on measuring performance and processing/communicating financial information about economic sectors. The discipline emphasizes ethical behavior, transparency, and accountability through the logical, systematic, and accurate recording of financial transactions, as well as the compilation, analysis, and interpretation of financial statements.

## 2. Content & Cognitive Scope
The curriculum is structured into three core fields: **Financial Accounting**, **Managerial Accounting**, and **Managing Resources**.

### Curriculum Weighting Matrix

| Field | Weighting | Topics |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Financial Accounting** | 50% – 60% | 1. Accounting concepts, 2. GAAP principles, 3. Bookkeeping, 4. Accounting equation, 5. Financial accounts and financial statements, 6. Salaries and wages, 7. Value-Added Tax, 8. Reconciliations |
| **Managerial Accounting** | 20% – 25% | 9. Cost accounting, 10. Budgeting |
| **Managing Resources** | 20% – 25% | 11. Indigenous bookkeeping systems, 12. Fixed assets, 13. Inventory, 14. Ethics, 15. Internal control |

## 3. Learning Outcomes
Upon completion of the curriculum, learners will be able to:
*   Record, analyze, and interpret financial and other relevant data to make informed decisions.
*   Present and communicate financial information effectively using generally accepted accounting practice in line with current developments and legislation.
*   Develop and demonstrate an understanding of fundamental accounting concepts.
*   Relate skills, knowledge, and values to real-world situations to ensure a balance between theory and practice, facilitating entry into the world of work or higher education.
*   Organize and manage personal finances and activities responsibly and effectively.

## 4. Fundamental Mathematical Framework
The core of the accounting equation, which underpins the recording of financial information, is defined as:

$$Assets = Equity + Liabilities$$

Where:
*   **Assets ($A$):** Resources controlled by the entity.
*   **Equity ($E$):** The residual interest in the assets of the entity after deducting all its liabilities.
*   **Liabilities ($L$):** Present obligations of the entity arising from past events.

### 4. Curriculum Specifications Summary
*Note: The provided source document focuses on the foundational requirements and resource specifications for the Accounting curriculum. Specific Annual Teaching Plans (ATP) and Assessment Matrices are defined in subsequent sections of the official CAPS document.*

#### Cognitive Scope & Depth
The curriculum is designed to progress through the following cognitive levels:
1.  **Knowledge:** Recall of accounting principles and definitions.
2.  **Routine Procedures:** Application of standard accounting entries and calculations.
3.  **Complex Procedures:** Integration of multiple accounting concepts to solve structured problems.
4.  **Problem Solving:** Application of principles to unfamiliar or complex business scenarios requiring critical analysis.

#### Mathematical Application
Accounting calculations must adhere to standard financial notation. Examples of core formulas utilized within the curriculum include:
*   **Gross Profit:** $\text{Sales} - \text{Cost of Sales} = \text{Gross Profit}$
*   **Net Profit:** $\text{Gross Profit} + \text{Other Income} - \text{Expenses} = \text{Net Profit}$
*   **Mark-up Percentage:** $\frac{\text{Gross Profit}}{\text{Cost of Sales}} \times 100 = \text{Mark-up \%}$

#### 3. MATHEMATICAL FORMULAS & CONCEPTS
The following mathematical frameworks are applied within the curriculum:

*   **Accounting Equation:**
    $$\text{Assets} = \text{Owner's Equity} + \text{Liabilities}$$
*   **Inventory Valuation Methods:**
    *   **FIFO (First-In-First-Out):** Valuation based on the assumption that the oldest stock is sold first.
    *   **Weighted Average Cost:**
        $$\text{Weighted Average Cost} = \frac{\text{Total Cost of Goods Available for Sale}}{\text{Total Units Available for Sale}}$$
*   **Cost Accounting:**
    $$\text{Unit Cost} = \frac{\text{Total Production Cost}}{\text{Number of Units Produced}}$$
*   **VAT Calculations:**
    $$\text{VAT Inclusive Amount} = \text{Exclusive Amount} \times (1 + \text{VAT Rate})$$
    $$\text{VAT Exclusive Amount} = \frac{\text{Inclusive Amount}}{1 + \text{VAT Rate}}$$

#### 4. Mathematics & Formulas

VAT calculations are governed by the current South African VAT rate ($r$).

*   **To calculate VAT on an exclusive amount:**
    $$\text{VAT Amount} = \text{Exclusive Amount} \times r$$
*   **To calculate the VAT-inclusive amount:**
    $$\text{Inclusive Amount} = \text{Exclusive Amount} \times (1 + r)$$
*   **To calculate the VAT component from an inclusive amount:**
    $$\text{VAT Amount} = \text{Inclusive Amount} \times \left( \frac{r}{1 + r} \right)$$
*   **To calculate the exclusive amount from an inclusive amount:**
    $$\text{Exclusive Amount} = \text{Inclusive Amount} \times \left( \frac{1}{1 + r} \right)$$

*(Note: $r$ represents the current VAT rate, e.g., $0.15$ for 15%)*

### Core Objectives:
*   **Learner Feedback:** Informing learners about their specific strengths, weaknesses, and overall progress.
*   **Stakeholder Decision-making:** Assisting teachers, parents, and relevant stakeholders in making informed decisions regarding the learning process and learner progression.

## 2. Assessment Categories

### A. Informal (Daily) Assessment
*   **Nature:** Planned teaching and learning activities integrated into daily classroom practice.
*   **Implementation:** Occurs at the beginning, during, or at the end of lessons.
*   **Methods:** Question and answer sessions, short written classwork, homework exercises, and open-book tests.
*   **Feedback Mechanism:** Provides immediate feedback to improve learning and teaching.
*   **Learner Involvement:** Encourages self-assessment and peer assessment to foster reflection on performance.
*   **Scaffolding:** Acts as a stepping stone toward formal Programme of Assessment (PoA) tasks.
*   **Recording & Promotion:** 
    *   Not formally recorded (unless at teacher discretion via simple checklists).
    *   **Not** taken into account for promotion or certification purposes.

### B. Formal Assessment
*   **Nature:** A systematic evaluation of learner progress within a grade and subject.
*   **Examples:** Projects, oral presentations, demonstrations, performances, tests, examinations, and practical tasks.
*   **Structure:** Forms part of a year-long formal **Programme of Assessment (PoA)**.
*   **Weighting:** All formal assessment tasks are school-based and weighted according to specific grade requirements.

### Assessment Specifications & Definitions

#### 1. Recording
Recording is the formal process of documenting a learner's performance level. Teachers must record actual raw marks against each specific assessment task using a standardized record sheet. These records serve two primary functions:
*   **Verification:** Tracking progress made by both teachers and learners throughout the teaching and learning cycle.
*   **Planning:** Monitoring learning gaps to inform future instructional planning and intervention.

#### 2. Reporting
Reporting is the process of communicating learner performance to stakeholders, including learners, parents, schools, district officials, employers, and tertiary institutions. 

*   **Standardization:** In accordance with CAPS policy for Grades R – 12, all reporting must be conducted in **percentages** against the subject-specific assessment scale.

#### 3. Mathematical Conversion Formulas

*   **Grade 11 Conversion:**

*   **Grade 12 Conversion:**
    *   Written Report: $50 \div 5 = 10$

### 2. Formal Assessment: Mandatory Project Specifications

In the FET (Further Education and Training) band, a project is a mandatory component of the Programme of Assessment.

#### Instructional Guidelines
*   **Guidance & Monitoring:** Teachers must provide necessary guidance prior to commencement and monitor progress throughout the project lifecycle.
*   **Classroom Integration:** Aspects of the project should be completed in class to facilitate teacher oversight.
*   **Transparency:** All assessment criteria must be discussed with learners and the assessment instrument (rubric/checklist) must be provided to learners alongside project instructions.
*   **Nature of Work:** Projects generally involve research, consolidation of information, and the preparation of a written document. In technical subjects (e.g., Computerised Accounting), the focus may shift to the development of specific software skills rather than traditional research.
*   **Timeline:** Projects are typically initiated towards the end of Term 1 for submission during Term 2.

#### Technical Formatting Requirements
*   **Structure:** Must include a cover page and a table of contents (where applicable).
*   **Content Layout:** Text must be divided into logical paragraphs or sections.
*   **Visuals:** Inclusion of pictures, photos, diagrams, and graphs is encouraged.
*   **Referencing:** All text references and bibliographies must be clearly indicated.
*   **Length:** The project should be between $4$ and $8$ typed pages (or $6$ to $10$ written pages), excluding the cover page, table of contents, graphs, and pictures.
*   **Typography:** Use font size $12$ and $1.5$ line spacing.
*   **Presentation:** The final document must be bound or stapled neatly.

### Assessment Task Specifications: Presentation, Case Study, and Report

This document outlines the instructional and assessment requirements for three core task types within the CAPS curriculum framework.

#### 1. Presentation
Presentations may be conducted in written or oral formats, provided that tangible evidence of the presentation is maintained for moderation.

**Instructional Guidelines:**
*   **Transparency:** All assessment criteria must be communicated to learners prior to the commencement of the task.
*   **Technology:** The use of electronic presentation tools is encouraged where resources permit.
*   **Structural Requirements:**
    *   Cover page and appropriate headings.
    *   Table of contents (where applicable).
    *   Logical organization of text into paragraphs or sections.
    *   Integration of visual aids (pictures, photos, diagrams, and graphs).
    *   Professional presentation (evidence must be bound or stapled).
    *   **Assessment Instrument:** The rubric or marking instrument must be provided to learners alongside the task instructions.

#### 2. Case Study
Case studies utilize real-life situations, problems, or incidents to facilitate the application of knowledge. Learners are required to adopt specific roles to interpret, analyze, and solve problems.

**Instructional Guidelines:**
*   **Source Material:** Utilize newspaper articles, magazine articles, and TV/radio presentations to ensure subject relevance and currency.
*   **Cognitive Scope:** Questions must span the cognitive hierarchy:
    *   **Lower-order:** Direct extraction of information/quotes.
    *   **Higher-order:** Analysis of comments and formulation of informed suggestions/decisions.
*   **Structural Requirements:**
    *   Cover page and appropriate headings.
    *   Table of contents (where applicable).
    *   Text divided into paragraphs or sections.
    *   Integration of visual aids (pictures, photos, diagrams, and graphs).
    *   Professional presentation (evidence must be bound or stapled).
    *   **Assessment Instrument:** The rubric or marking instrument must be provided to learners alongside the task instructions.

#### 3. Report
A report serves as the written evidence of a survey, analysis, or investigation. It is typically shorter in scope than a project and focuses on specific problem-solving scenarios (e.g., providing advice on business liquidity).

**Instructional Guidelines:**
*   **Focus:** Strong emphasis on problem-solving and, where applicable, consulting with subject matter experts.
*   **Structural Requirements:**
    *   Cover page and appropriate headings.
    *   Table of contents (where applicable).
