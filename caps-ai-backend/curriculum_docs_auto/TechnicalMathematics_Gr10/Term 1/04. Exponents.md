# 2 Exponents

## 2.1 Introduction

Exponential notation is a shorthand method for writing a number multiplied by itself multiple times. It is particularly useful in everyday life, science, engineering, and finance to represent very large or very small quantities cleanly and efficiently.

Exponents are also referred to as **indices** or **powers**. 

### Basic Exponential Notation

The general form of an exponential expression is:

$$\text{base} \leftarrow a^{n} \rightarrow \text{exponent or index}$$

For any real number $a$ and natural number $n$, we can write $a$ multiplied by itself $n$ times as:

$$a^n = \underbrace{a \times a \times a \times \cdots \times a}_{n \text{ times}} \quad (a \in \mathbb{R},\, n \in \mathbb{N})$$

---

### Core Identities and Rules

Remember the following fundamental identities involving exponents:

1. **Repeated Multiplication:** 
   $$a^n = a \times a \times a \times \cdots \times a \quad (n \text{ times}) \quad (a \in \mathbb{R},\, n \in \mathbb{N})$$

2. **Zero Exponent:** 
   $$a^0 = 1 \quad (a \neq 0 \text{ because } 0^0 \text{ is undefined})$$

3. **Negative Exponent:** 
   $$a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n} \quad \left(a \neq 0 \text{ because division by } 0 \text{ is undefined}\right)$$

4. **Reciprocal Negative Exponent:** 
   $$\frac{1}{a^{-n}} = a^n$$

## 2.2 Revision of exponent laws

There are several laws we can use to make working with exponential numbers easier. Some of these laws might have been done in earlier grades, but we list all the laws here for easy reference:

* $a^m \times a^n = a^{m+n}$
* $\frac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n}$
* $(ab)^n = a^n b^n$
* $\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^n = \frac{a^n}{b^n}$
* $(a^m)^n = a^{mn}$

where $a > 0, b > 0$ and $m, n \in \mathbb{R}$

## Worked Example 1: Applying the Exponential Laws

### Question

Simplify the following expressions:

1. $2^{3x} \times 2^{4x}$
2. $\frac{4x^3}{2x^5}$
3. $\frac{12p^2t^5}{3pt^3}$
4. $(3x)^2$
5. $(3^4 \times 5^2)^3$
6. $6p^0 \times (7p)^0$
7. $\left(\frac{2xp}{6x^2}\right)^3$
8. $(2^{-2})^{2x+1}$

---

### Solution

1. $2^{3x} \times 2^{4x} = 2^{3x+4x} = 2^{7x}$
2. $\frac{4x^3}{2x^5} = 2x^{3-5} = 2x^{-2} = \frac{2}{x^2}$
3. $\frac{12p^2t^5}{3pt^3} = 4p^{(2-1)}t^{(5-3)} = 4pt^2$
4. $(3x)^2 = 3^2x^2 = 9x^2$
5. $(3^4 \times 5^2)^3 = 3^{(4 \times 3)} \times 5^{(2 \times 3)} = 3^{12} \times 5^6$
6. $6p^0 \times (7p)^0 = 6(1) \times 1 = 6$
7. $\left(\frac{2xp}{6x^2}\right)^3 = \left(\frac{p}{3x}\right)^3 = \frac{p^3}{27x^3}$
8. $(2^{-2})^{2x+1} = 2^{-2(2x+1)} = 2^{-4x-2}$

## Worked Example 2: Exponential Expressions

### Question

Simplify:
$$\frac{2^{2n} \times 4^n \times 2}{16^n}$$

---

### Solution

#### Step 1: Change the bases to prime numbers
At first glance, it appears that we cannot simplify this expression. However, if we reduce the bases to prime bases, then we can apply the exponent laws.

$$\frac{2^{2n} \times 4^n \times 2}{16^n} = \frac{2^{2n} \times (2^2)^n \times 2^1}{(2^4)^n}$$

---

### Step 2: Simplify the exponents

$$\begin{aligned}
&= \frac{2^{2n} \times 2^{2n} \times 2^{1}}{2^{4n}} \\
&= \frac{2^{2n+2n+1}}{2^{4n}} \\
&= \frac{2^{4n+1}}{2^{4n}} \\
&= 2^{4n+1-(4n)} \\
&= 2
\end{aligned}$$

---

### Worked example 3: Exponential expressions

#### QUESTION

Simplify:

$$\frac{5^{2x-1} \cdot 9^{x-2}}{15^{2x-3}}$$

#### SOLUTION

##### Step 1: Change the bases to prime numbers

$$\begin{aligned}
\frac{5^{2x-1} \cdot 9^{x-2}}{15^{2x-3}} &= \frac{5^{2x-1} \cdot (3^{2})^{x-2}}{(5 \times 3)^{2x-3}} \\
&= \frac{5^{2x-1} \cdot 3^{2x-4}}{5^{2x-3} \cdot 3^{2x-3}}
\end{aligned}$$

##### Step 2: Subtract the exponents (same base)

$$\begin{aligned}
&= 5^{(2x-1)-(2x-3)} \times 3^{(2x-4)-(2x-3)} \\
&= 5^{2x-1-2x+3} \times 3^{2x-4-2x+3} \\
&= 5^{2} \times 3^{-1}
\end{aligned}$$

##### Step 3: Write the answer as a fraction

$$\begin{aligned}
&= \frac{5^{2}}{3} \\
&= \frac{25}{3}
\end{aligned}$$

---

**NOTE:**
When working with exponents, all the laws of operation for algebra apply.

**Worked example 4: Simplifying by taking out a common factor**

**QUESTION**
Simplify:
$$\frac{2^t - 2^{t-2}}{3 \cdot 2^t - 2^t}$$

**SOLUTION**

**Step 1: Simplify to a form that can be factorised**
For each of the exponent laws we can “undo” the law - in other words we can work backwards. For this expression we can reverse the multiplication law to write $2^{t-2}$ as $2^t \cdot 2^{-2}$.

$$\frac{2^t - 2^{t-2}}{3 \cdot 2^t - 2^t} = \frac{2^t - (2^t \cdot 2^{-2})}{3 \cdot 2^t - 2^t}$$

**Step 2: Take out a common factor**
$$= \frac{2^t(1 - 2^{-2})}{2^t(3 - 1)}$$

**Step 3: Cancel the common factor and simplify**
$$= \frac{1 - 2^{-2}}{3 - 1}$$
$$= \frac{1 - \frac{1}{4}}{2}$$
$$= \frac{3}{4} \times \frac{1}{2}$$
$$= \frac{3}{8}$$

**NOTE:**
When you have a fraction that has more than one term in the numerator or denominator, change to prime bases if necessary and then factorise.

**Worked example 5: Simplifying using difference of two squares**

**QUESTION**
Simplify:
$$\frac{9^x - 1}{3^x + 1}$$

---

## SOLUTION

### Step 1: Change the bases to prime numbers
$$\begin{aligned} \frac{9^x - 1}{3^x + 1} &= \frac{(3^2)^x - 1}{3^x + 1} \\ &= \frac{3^{2x} - 1}{3^x + 1} \quad \text{Recognise that } 3^{2x} = (3^x)^2 \end{aligned}$$

### Step 2: Factorise using the difference of squares
$$= \frac{(3^x - 1)(3^x + 1)}{3^x + 1}$$

### Step 3: Cancel the common factor and simplify
$$= 3^x - 1$$

## 2.3 Rational exponents

We can also apply the exponent laws to expressions with rational exponents.

### Worked example 6: Simplifying rational exponents

**QUESTION**

Simplify:
$$2x^{\frac{1}{2}} \times 4x^{-\frac{1}{2}}$$

**SOLUTION**

$$\begin{aligned}
2x^{\frac{1}{2}} \times 4x^{-\frac{1}{2}} &= 8x^{\frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{2}} \\
&= 8x^{0} \\
&= 8(1) \\
&= 8
\end{aligned}$$

---

### Worked example 7: Simplifying rational exponents

**QUESTION**

Simplify:
$$(0,008)^{\frac{1}{3}}$$

---

### SOLUTION

#### Step 1: Write as a fraction and simplify

$$\begin{aligned} (0,008)^{\frac{1}{3}} &= \left(\frac{8}{1000}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}} \\ &= \left(\frac{1}{125}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}} \\ &= \left(\frac{1}{5^3}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}} \\ &= \frac{1^{\frac{1}{3}}}{5^{\left(3 \cdot \frac{1}{3}\right)}} \\ &= \frac{1}{5} \end{aligned}$$

## 2.4 Exponential equations

EMAW

Exponential equations have the unknown variable in the exponent. Here are some examples:

$$3^{x+1} = 9$$

$$5^t + 3 \times 5^{t-1} = 400$$

If we can write a single term with the same base on each side of the equation, we can equate the exponents. This is one method to solve exponential equations.

**Important:** if $a > 0$ and $a \neq 1$ then:
If $a^x = a^y$, then $x = y$ (same base).

Also notice that if $a = 1$, then $x$ and $y$ can be different.

---

### Worked example 8: Equating exponents

**QUESTION**

Solve for $x$: $3^{x+1} = 9$.

**SOLUTION**

**Step 1: Change the bases to prime numbers**

$$3^{x+1} = 3^2$$

**Step 2: The bases are the same so we can equate exponents**

$$x + 1 = 2$$

$$\therefore x = 1$$

---

### Worked example 9: Equating exponents

**QUESTION**

Solve for $t$: $3^t = 1$.

**SOLUTION**

**Step 1: Solve for $t$**

We know from the exponent identities that $a^0 = 1$, therefore:

$$3^t = 1$$

$$3^t = 3^0$$

$$\therefore t = 0$$

#### Worked Example 10: Solving equations by taking out a common factor

**QUESTION**

Solve for $t$: $5^{t} + 3 \cdot 5^{t+1} = 400$.

**SOLUTION**

**Step 1: Rewrite the expression**

$$5^{t} + 3 \left(5^{t} \cdot 5\right) = 400$$

**Step 2: Take out a common factor**

$$5^{t} \left(1 + 3 \cdot 5\right) = 400$$

$$5^{t} \left(1 + 15\right) = 400$$

**Step 3: Simplify**

$$5^{t} \left(16\right) = 400$$

$$5^{t} = 25$$

**Step 4: Change the bases to prime numbers**

$$5^{t} = 5^{2}$$

**Step 5: The bases are the same so we can equate exponents**

$$\therefore t = 2$$

---

#### Worked Example 11: Solving equations by factorising a trinomial

**QUESTION**

Solve for $x$: 

$$3^{2x} - 80 \cdot 3^{x} - 81 = 0$$

**SOLUTION**

**Step 1: Factorise the trinomial**

$$\left(3^{x} - 81\right)\left(3^{x} + 1\right) = 0$$

---

### Step 2: Solve for $x$

$$3^{x} = 81 \quad \text{or} \quad 3^{x} = -1$$

However, $3^{x} = -1$ is undefined, so:

$$3^{x} = 81$$
$$3^{x} = 3^{4}$$
$$x = 4$$

**Therefore** $x = 4$.

---

### Worked example 12: Solving equations by factorising a trinomial

#### QUESTION
Solve for $p$:

$$p - 13p^{\frac{1}{2}} + 36 = 0$$

---

#### SOLUTION

**Step 1: Rewrite the equation**  
We notice that $\left(p^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{2} = p$ so we can rewrite the equation as:

$$\left(p^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{2} - 13p^{\frac{1}{2}} + 36 = 0$$

**Step 2: Factorise as a trinomial**

$$\left(p^{\frac{1}{2}} - 9\right)\left(p^{\frac{1}{2}} - 4\right) = 0$$

**Step 3: Solve to find both roots**

$$p^{\frac{1}{2}} - 9 = 0 \quad \text{or} \quad p^{\frac{1}{2}} - 4 = 0$$

$$p^{\frac{1}{2}} = 9 \quad \text{or} \quad p^{\frac{1}{2}} = 4$$

$$\left(p^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{2} = (9)^{2} \quad \left(p^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{2} = (4)^{2}$$

$$p = 81 \quad p = 16$$

**Therefore** $p = 81$ or $p = 16$.

---

### Worked example 13: Solving equations by factorisation

#### QUESTION
Solve for $x$:

$$2^{x} - 2^{4-x} = 0$$

---

## SOLUTION

### Step 1: Rewrite the equation
In order to get the equation into a form which we can factorise, we need to rewrite the equation:

$$2^{x} - 2^{4-x} = 0$$

$$2^{x} - 2^{4} \cdot 2^{-x} = 0$$

$$2^{x} - \frac{2^{4}}{2^{x}} = 0$$

Now eliminate the fraction by multiplying both sides of the equation by the denominator, $2^{x}$:

$$\left(2^{x} - \frac{2^{4}}{2^{x}}\right) \times 2^{x} = 0 \times 2^{x}$$

$$2^{2x} - 16 = 0$$

### Step 2: Factorise the equation
Now that we have rearranged the equation, we can see that we are left with a difference of two squares. Therefore:

$$2^{2x} - 16 = 0$$

$$(2^{x} - 4)(2^{x} + 4) = 0$$

$$2^{x} = 4 \quad \text{or} \quad 2^{x} \neq -4 \text{ (a positive integer with an exponent is always positive)}$$

$$2^{x} = 2^{2} = 4$$

$$x = 2$$

Therefore $x = 2$.

## 2.5 Summary

### Core Concepts

* **Exponential Notation:** Means writing a number as $a^n$, where $n$ is any natural number and $a$ is any real number.
  * $a$ is the **base**.
  * $n$ is the **exponent** or **index**.

### Definitions

* $a^n = \underbrace{a \times a \times \cdots \times a}_{n \text{ times}}$
* $a^0 = 1$, if $a \neq 0$
* $a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n}$, if $a \neq 0$
* $\frac{1}{a^{-n}} = a^n$, if $a \neq 0$

### The Laws of Exponents

| Law Name / Operation | Algebraic Formula |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Multiplying powers with the same base** | $a^m \times a^n = a^{m+n}$ |
| **Dividing powers with the same base** | $\frac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n}$ |
| **Power of a product** | $(ab)^n = a^n b^n$ |
| **Power of a quotient** | $\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^n = \frac{a^n}{b^n}$ |
| **Power of a power** | $(a^m)^n = a^{mn}$ |

### Problem-Solving Strategies

* **Simplifying Expressions:** When simplifying expressions with exponents, reduce the bases to **prime bases** or **factorise** where possible.
* **Solving Exponential Equations:** When solving equations with exponents, apply the rule that if $a^x = a^y$, then $x = y$; alternatively, factorise the expressions.

### 14. Simplify the expression:
$$\frac{2^{m+20}}{2^{m+20}}$$

**Solution:**
$$\begin{aligned}
\frac{2^{m+20}}{2^{m+20}} &= 2^{(m+20)-(m+20)} \\
&= 2^{m+20-m-20} \\
&= 2^{0} \\
&= 1
\end{aligned}$$

#### Problem 34

Simplify the expression:
$$\frac{16^x - 144^b}{4^x - 12^b}$$

**Solution:**

$$\begin{aligned}
\frac{16^x - 144^b}{4^x - 12^b} &= \frac{(4^2)^x - (12^2)^b}{4^x - 12^b} \\
&= \frac{(4^x)^2 - (12^b)^2}{4^x - 12^b} \\
&= \frac{(4^x - 12^b)(4^x + 12^b)}{4^x - 12^b} \\
&= 4^x + 12^b
\end{aligned}$$

---

#### Problem 35

Simplify the expression:
$$\frac{5^{2y-3} 2^{4y+4}}{10^{-5y+5}}$$

**Solution:**

$$\begin{aligned}
\frac{5^{2y-3} 2^{4y+4}}{10^{-5y+5}} &= \frac{5^{2y-3} \cdot 2^{4y+4}}{(5 \times 2)^{-5y+5}} \\
&= \frac{5^{2y-3} 2^{4y+4}}{5^{-5y+5} 2^{-5y+5}} \\
&= 5^{(2y-3) - (-5y+5)} \times 2^{(4y+4) - (-5y+5)} \\
&= 5^{7y-8} \times 2^{9y-1}
\end{aligned}$$

#### Problem 36

Simplify the expression:
$$\frac{6^4 \times 12^3 \times 4^5}{30^3 \times 3^6}$$

$$\begin{aligned}

&= 3^{4+3-3-6} \cdot 2^{4+6+10-3} \cdot 5^{-3} \\
&= \frac{2^{17}}{3^2 5^3}
\end{aligned}$$

## 2.4 Exponential Equations

Learners may find exponential equations much easier using the $k$-substitution method. 

### Worked Example: Solving using $k$-substitution

Solve the exponential equation:
$$2^x - 2^{4-x} = 0$$

**Solution:**

Using exponent laws to rewrite the second term:
$$2^x - 2^4 \cdot 2^{-x} = 0$$

$$\begin{aligned}
2^x - \frac{2^4}{2^x} &= 0
\end{aligned}$$

Let $k = 2^x$:
$$k - \frac{2^4}{k} = 0$$

Multiply the entire equation by $k$:
$$k^2 - 16 = 0$$

### Worked Examples: Exponential Equations

---

#### Example (Top of page)

**Problem:**
Solve for $t$:
$$2^{t} + 2^{t+2} = 40$$

**Solution:**
$$2^{t}(1 + 2^{2}) = 40$$
$$2^{t}(5) = 40$$
$$2^{t} = 8$$
$$2^{t} = 2^{3}$$
$$\therefore t = 3$$

---

#### Example k)

**Problem:**
Solve for $x$:
$$(7^{x} - 49)(3^{x} - 27) = 0$$

**Solution:**
$$(7^{x} - 49)(3^{x} - 27) = 0$$
$$(7^{x} - 7^{2})(3^{x} - 3^{3}) = 0$$
$$\therefore 7^{x} - 7^{2} = 0 \text{ or } 3^{x} - 3^{3} = 0$$
$$\therefore 7^{x} = 7^{2} \text{ or } 3^{x} = 3^{3}$$
$$\therefore x = 2 \text{ or } x = 3$$

---

#### Example l)

**Problem:**
Solve for $x$:
$$(2 \cdot 2^{x} - 16)(3^{x+1} - 9) = 0$$

**Solution:**
$$(2 \cdot 2^{x} - 16)(3^{x+1} - 9) = 0$$
$$(2^{x+1} - 2^{4})(3^{x+1} - 3^{2}) = 0$$
$$\therefore 2^{x+1} - 2^{4} = 0 \text{ or } 3^{x+1} - 3^{2} = 0$$
$$x + 1 = 4 \text{ or } x + 1 = 2$$
$$\therefore x = 3 \text{ or } x = 1$$

---

#### Example m)

**Problem:**
Solve for $x$:
$$(10^{x} - 1)(3^{x} - 81) = 0$$

**Solution:**
$$(10^{x} - 1)(3^{x} - 81) = 0$$
$$(10^{x} - 10^{0})(3^{x} - 3^{4}) = 0$$
$$\therefore 10^{x} - 10^{0} = 0 \text{ or } 3^{x} - 3^{4} = 0$$
$$\therefore x = 0 \text{ or } x = 4$$

---

#### Example n)

**Problem:**
Solve for $x$:
$$2 \times 5^{2-x} = 5 + 5^{x}$$

**Solution:**
$$2 \times 5^{2-x} = 5 + 5^{x}$$
$$2(5^{2})(5^{-x}) = 5 + 5^{x}$$
$$\frac{2(5^{2})}{5^{x}} - 5 - 5^{x} = 0$$
$$\left(\frac{50}{5^{x}}\right) \times 5^{x} - 5 \times 5^{x} - 5^{x} \times 5^{x} = 0$$
$$50 - 5(5^{x}) - (5^{x})^{2} = 0$$
$$(5^{x} - 5)(5^{x} + 10) = 0$$
$$5^{x} - 5 = 0 \text{ or } 5^{x} + 10 = 0$$
$$5^{x} = 5 \text{ or } 5^{x} = -10$$

### Solving Exponential Equations Using Trial and Error

When solving exponential equations where the base cannot easily be written with a common base on both sides, trial and error (numerical estimation) can be used to approximate the solution to a given number of decimal places.

---

### Worked Examples

#### Example 3
Use trial and error to find the value of $x$ correct to 2 decimal places:
$$2^x = 7$$

**Solution:**

1. **Find integer bounds for $x$:**
   $$2^2 = 4 \quad \text{and} \quad 2^3 = 8$$
   Since $4 < 7 < 8$, we know that:
   $$2 < x < 3$$
   Because $7$ is much closer to $8$ than to $4$, $x$ will be closer to $3$.

2. **Test values between 2 and 3:**
   * Test $x = 2{,}9$: 
     $$2^{2{,}9} = 7{,}464$$
   * Test $x = 2{,}8$: 
     $$2^{2{,}8} = 6{,}964$$
   * Test $x = 2{,}81$: 
     $$2^{2{,}81} = 7{,}01$$
   * Test $x = 2{,}805$: 
     $$2^{2{,}805} = 6{,}989$$
   * Test $x = 2{,}809$: 
     $$2^{2{,}809} = 7{,}007$$

3. **Conclusion:**
   Rounding to two decimal places:
   $$\therefore x \approx 2{,}81$$

---

#### Example 4
Use trial and error to find the value of $x$ correct to 2 decimal places:
$$5^x = 11$$

**Solution:**

1. **Find integer bounds for $x$:**
   $$5^1 = 5 \quad \text{and} \quad 5^2 = 25$$
   Since $5 < 11 < 25$, we know that:
   $$1 < x < 2$$

2. **Test values between 1 and 2:**
   * Test $x = 1{,}5$:

   * Test $x = 1{,}4$: 
     $$5^{1{,}4} = 9{,}51$$
   * Test $x = 1{,}45$: 
     $$5^{1{,}45} = 10{,}31$$
   * Test $x = 1{,}49$: 
     $$5^{1{,}49} = 11{,}001$$

3. **Conclusion:**
   Rounding to two decimal places:
   $$\therefore x \approx 1{,}49$$

\end{aligned}$$

6. Explain why the following statements are false:

a) $\frac{1}{a^{-1} + b^{-1}} = a + b$

**Solution:**
The sum of two powers of the same degree is not the power of the sum of the bases.

$$a + b = \frac{1}{(a + b)^{-1}} \neq \frac{1}{a^{-1} + b^{-1}}$$

b) $(a + b)^2 = a^2 + b^2$

**Solution:**
The sum of two powers of the same degree is not the power of the sum of the bases.

c) $(\frac{1}{a^2})^{\frac{1}{3}} = a^{\frac{2}{3}}$

**Solution:**
A negative sign is missing, when a power is moved from the denominator to the numerator, the sign of the exponent changes.
From the question we must note that $a \neq 0$.

$$\left(\frac{1}{a^2}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}} = (a^{-2})^{\frac{1}{3}}$$
$$= a^{-\frac{2}{3}}$$

### 7. Counting Digits in Exponential Expressions

### Exponential Equations

#### Worked Example: Verifying an Identity

Verify that the following equation holds true:

$$\frac{2^{n+1} + 2^{n}}{2^{n} - 2^{n-1}} = \frac{3^{n+1} + 3^{n}}{3^{n} - 3^{n-1}}$$

---

#### Solution Steps

##### 1. Simplify the Right-Hand Side (R.H.S.)

$$\text{R.H.S.} = \frac{3^{n+1} + 3^{n}}{3^{n} - 3^{n-1}}$$

Factor out the lowest power of the base ($3^{n}$) in the numerator and the denominator:

$$\text{R.H.S.} = \frac{3^{n}(3^{1} + 3^{0})}{3^{n}(3^{0} - 3^{-1})}$$

Cancel out $3^{n}$ from both the numerator and the denominator:

$$\text{R.H.S.} = \frac{3 + 1}{1 - \frac{1}{3}}$$

$$\text{R.H.S.} = \frac{4}{\frac{2}{3}}$$

$$\text{R.H.S.} = \frac{12}{2}$$

$$\text{R.H.S.} = 6$$

---

##### 2. Simplify the Left-Hand Side (L.H.S.)

$$\text{L.H.S.} = \frac{2^{n+1} + 2^{n}}{2^{n} - 2^{n-1}}$$

Factor out the lowest power of the base ($2^{n}$) in the numerator and the denominator:

$$\text{L.H.S.} = \frac{2^{n}(2^{1} + 2^{0})}{2^{n}(2^{0} - 2^{-1})}$$

Cancel out $2^{n}$ from both the numerator and the denominator:

$$\text{L.H.S.} = \frac{2 + 1}{1 - \frac{1}{2}}$$

$$\text{L.H.S.} = \frac{3}{\frac{1}{2}}$$

$$\text{L.H.S.} = 6$$

---

##### 3. Conclusion

$$\therefore \text{R.H.S.} = \text{L.H.S.}$$
