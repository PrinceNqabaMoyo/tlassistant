## DEFINITION: Budget

A budget is a plan of action to balance your expenditure with your income. It is usually displayed as a table of income items in one column and expenditure items in another. 

The last row of the budget shows the difference between income and expenditure:
* **Surplus:** When income exceeds expenditure (your income is more than your expenses).
* **Deficit or Shortfall:** When expenditure exceeds income (your expenses are more than your income).

Personal income is money that is paid to you, or that comes into your possession or bank account. Income can be paid to you in the form of a salary or wage for work undertaken, and it ranges from gifts or pocket money (e.g. from your parents) to bursaries or loans, savings, interest earned on your savings and income from inheritance.

There are three types of income: fixed, variable and occasional income.

* **Fixed** income is an amount of money a person receives, which does not change with time. Salaries and wages are examples of fixed income.
* **Variable** income is an amount of money a person receives that changes over time, or changes according to the situation. Commissions and interest on investments or savings are examples of variable income.
* **Occasional** income is when someone receives money from time to time. Examples of occasional income may include gifts (e.g. money for your birthday) or inheritance (e.g. if a family member passes away and leaves money to you in their will).

## Expenditure (EMG5F)

Personal expenditure is money that you spend. Expenditure can include living expenses (e.g. food, clothing, entertainment), accounts (e.g. water, electricity, telephone), fees (e.g. school fees), insurance (e.g. for a car or house), taxes and loan repayments (e.g. to pay off your store account).

As with expenditure, there are fixed, variable and occasional or unexpected expenses. Fixed expenses are the amounts of money a person spends, which do not change over time - for example rental for a flat. Variable expenses are amounts of money a person spends that do change over time. Groceries or electricity bills are examples of variable expenses. Occasional or unexpected expenses are sometimes expenses that you cannot plan for, for example a visit to the doctor, or repairs to your car if it breaks down. Some occasional expenses can be planned for, for example, annual car services.

## 8.3 Personal budgets, income and expenditure statements (EMG5G)

Drawing up a budget is a good way to plan your expenditure based on how much money you expect to receive. Budgets are often estimates, because you cannot easily include unexpected expenses in a budget. However, you can plan to save some money that you can use in the event of an unexpected expense. Budgeting includes normal monthly budgeting and budgeting for particular expenses, such as an expensive item that you want to buy, a project or a trip overseas.

---

## Monthly budgeting (EMG5H)

To produce a budget you need to estimate what your income and expenses should be for a given month. You might already know exactly how much your income is, if it is fixed.

Your budget should help you to get the most out of your money and will help to enable you to have enough money to cover all your expenses.

There are several things you should aim for in your personal budget:

* It should list all of the items that are needed and should try to anticipate unforeseen expenses.
* It should be realistic, so that you can keep to it.
* It should focus on the high priority items (essential items such as food and health care). If too much of the income is spent on non-essential items and not on savings, your budget is going to become problematic in the future.
* An ideal budget should include a plan to save money for the future, or to pay off debts to allow for savings in the following months.
* It should be balanced. If your income is less than your expenses, then you need to revise it until the two sides balance. If your income is more than your expenses, then you should plan to save the extra money.

## Lesson: Creating and Analyzing Personal Budgets

### Core Concepts & Procedures

A **budget** is a financial plan that lists expected income and expenses over a specific period. It helps individuals track their money, make informed decisions, and ensure they do not spend more than they have available.

* **Income:** Money received (e.g., from parents, savings, part-time jobs).
* **Expenses:** Money spent or paid out for goods and services (e.g., transport, food, accommodation).
* **Running Total of Money:** The net balance of funds available after accounting for income and expenses.

---

### Worked Scenario & Activity

**Problem Statement:**
Douglas wants to travel from Cape Town to Durban to visit his cousin. 
* His parents will give him $\text{R } 500$ towards the trip.
* He has $\text{R } 2000$ saved in his bank account.
* His uncle will give him a lift home, so he only needs to budget for the trip to Durban.
* He wants to have some spending money left over when he arrives.

He compares two travel options with Rainbow Buses:

* **Option 1:** Leave Saturday morning and travel straight to Durban. 
  * Trip cost: $\text{R } 1200$
  * Meals: $3 \text{ meals} \times \text{R } 30\text{ per meal} = \text{R } 90$

* **Option 2:** Leave Saturday morning and travel to Plettenberg Bay first.
  * Trip cost to Plettenberg Bay: $\text{R } 400$
  * Bus trip from Plettenberg Bay to Durban on Sunday morning: $\text{R } 500$
  * Accommodation on Saturday night: $\text{R } 200$
  * Extra meals: $3 \text{ extra meals} \times \text{R } 30\text{ each} = \text{R } 90$ *(Note: Option 2 also implies the same initial 3 meals as Option 1, or total meals needed for the journey must be accounted for).*

## 8.5 The importance of saving

**EMG5M**

Saving is a good way to prepare for unexpected costs, annual bills and important events. These could be essential or non-essential expenses such as medical costs, a trip, a party or car repairs and servicing. Also keep in mind that your circumstances can change very quickly, and you need to be prepared for this.

It is more important to repay debts before starting to save, because you need to pay interest on debts. Debts also tie up money that could be used for savings. When you are living on a strict budget there might be little or no spare money to set aside. However, your financial situation can get worse rapidly if there is no money set aside for unexpected expenses.

## Lesson: Personal Budgets and Decision Making

### Core Concepts

* **Budgeting:** A financial plan that lists expected income and expenses over a specific period. It helps individuals track their money and make informed financial decisions.
* **Income:** Money received (e.g., from parents, savings, or work). Income increases the running total of money available.
* **Expenses:** Money spent on goods or services (e.g., transport, food, accommodation). Expenses decrease the running total of money available.
* **Running Total:** The cumulative balance of money calculated after each item of income is added and each expense is subtracted.

---

l) Vodacom contract cell phone account:
   i. **Fixed component:** $\text{R } 135$ per month
   ii. **Variable component:** $\text{R } 0,80$ per minute of airtime used during peak time. An average of $100$ minutes of airtime per month is used during peak time. Off peak minutes are charged at a rate of $\text{R } 0,40$ per minute. An average of $200$ minutes per month is used during this time.

m) Telkom account:
   i. **Fixed component:** $\text{R } 400$ per month
   ii. **Variable component:** $\text{R } 0,50$ per minute during normal time. An average of $350$ minutes is spent each month on the phone during this time. Call more time is calculated at $\text{R } 7$ per night. The children use the phone an average of $20$ nights per month during this time.

n) Is the Philander family within budget? Explain your answer.

### Solution

a) $20 \times \text{R } 500 = \text{R } 10\ 000$

b) $\text{R } 2\ 500$

c) $\text{R } 5\ 500$

d) $\text{R } 2\ 500 + \text{R } 250 = \text{R } 2\ 750$

e) $\text{R } 800 + \text{R } 96 = \text{R } 896$

f) $\text{R } 1\ 200 \times 2 = \text{R } 2\ 400$

g) Taxi fare: $\text{R } 10 \text{ per day} \times 2 \text{ children} \times 20 \text{ days} = \text{R } 400$. 
   Petrol: $(20 \times 4 \text{ litres} \times \text{R } 10,50) + (10 \times 3 \text{ litres} \times \text{R } 10,50) = \text{R } 840 + \text{R } 315 = \text{R } 1\ 155$

h) Total salaries = $\text{R } 19\ 500$. $5\%$ of this is $\text{R } 975$.

i) $5\%$ of $\text{R } 9\ 500 = \text{R } 475$.

j) Total income = $\text{salaries} + \text{additional income} = \text{R } 19\ 500 + \text{R } 2\ 500 = \text{R } 22\ 000$. $5\%$ of this is $\text{R } 1\ 100$.

k) $(550 \times \text{R } 0,50) = \text{R } 275$

l) 
   i. $\text{R } 135$
   ii. $(100 \times \text{R } 0,80) + (200 \times \text{R } 0,40) = \text{R } 160$

m) 
   i. $\text{R } 400$
   ii. $(350 \times \text{R } 0,50) + (\text{R } 7 \times 20) = \text{R } 315$.

n) The total for fixed expenses is $\text{R } 12\ 456$. The total for variable expenses is $\text{R } 5\ 630$. So the total for all expenses is $\text{R } 18\ 086$. The total income for the household is $\text{R } 22\ 000$, so yes - they are within budget, because their income is greater than their total expenditure and they have a surplus of money.
