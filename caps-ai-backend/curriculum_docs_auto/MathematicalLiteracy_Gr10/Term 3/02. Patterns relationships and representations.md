### Continuous and Discrete Graphs

In mathematics, the nature of the data determines how a graph is drawn:

*   **Discrete Values:** These are values that can only be whole numbers (e.g., the number of people). When drawing graphs for discrete values, points are connected by **dotted lines** because the values between the whole numbers do not exist in the context of the data.
*   **Continuous Values:** These are values that can include decimal fractions (e.g., measurements like length, time, or temperature). When drawing graphs for continuous values, points are connected by **solid lines** to show that all values between the points are included.

# 2.2. Making Sense of Graphs That Tell a Story

## Key Concepts: Discrete vs. Continuous Data

Graphs are used to represent relationships between variables. Depending on the nature of the data, graphs are categorized as either **discrete** or **continuous**.

### 1. Discrete Data
Discrete data consists of values that can only be counted as whole numbers. You cannot have a fraction of these items.

*   **Example:** The number of people on a bus. You cannot have $2.5$ people on a bus; therefore, the data points are individual and separate.
*   **Graph Representation:** Usually shown as a series of individual points (often connected by lines to show a trend, but the spaces between the points do not represent valid data).



![Textbook_MathematicalLiteracy_Gr10_Measurement-of-perimeter-and-area.pdf-0005-03.png](images/Textbook_MathematicalLiteracy_Gr10_Measurement-of-perimeter-and-area.pdf-0005-03.png)


### 2. Continuous Data
Continuous data consists of values that can be measured. These values can include fractions, decimals, and any point along a scale.

*   **Example:** Time and distance. A bus travels over a period of time and covers a distance. At any given second, the bus has traveled a specific distance, meaning there is a value for every possible point on the line.
*   **Graph Representation:** Shown as a solid, unbroken line because every point along that line represents a valid measurement.

| Feature | Discrete Data | Continuous Data |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nature** | Counted (Whole numbers) | Measured (Any value) |
| **Graph Style** | Individual points | Solid, unbroken line |
| **Example** | Number of passengers | Distance vs. Time |

### Dependent and Independent Variables

#### Definition: Variable
A **variable** is the quantity you are measuring or calculating.

---

#### Key Concepts

**1. Independent Variable**
An independent variable is a variable that stands alone and is not changed by the other variables you are trying to measure. 
*   *Example:* A person's age is an independent variable. Other factors (such as what they eat, how they go to school, or how much television they watch) do not change a person's age.

**2. Dependent Variable**
A dependent variable depends on other factors. 
*   *Example:* A test score is a dependent variable because it can change depending on several factors, such as how much you studied, how much sleep you got the night before, or how hungry you were when you took the test.

---

#### How to Identify Variables
To determine which variable is independent and which is dependent, place the names of the two variables into a sentence in a way that makes the most sense.

*   *Example:* "Time causes a change in distance travelled." 
    *   It is not possible for "distance travelled" to cause a change in "time."
    *   Therefore, **Time** is the independent variable and **Distance** is the dependent variable.

---

#### Plotting Variables on a Graph
When plotting graphs of variables, follow this standard convention:
*   **Horizontal axis ($x$-axis):** Independent variable.
*   **Vertical axis ($y$-axis):** Dependent variable.

# Touching the Axes

## Key Concepts
When interpreting graphs, understanding where the line intersects the axes provides critical information about the relationship between variables.

*   **Vertical Axis ($y$-axis) Intercept:** If the graph touches the vertical axis, it indicates that the quantity on the horizontal axis has reached $0$.
*   **Horizontal Axis ($x$-axis) Intercept:** If the graph touches the horizontal axis, it indicates that the quantity on the vertical axis has reached $0$.

> **NOTE:** 
> *   The **horizontal axis** is the $x$-axis, where we plot the **independent variable**.
> *   The **vertical axis** is the $y$-axis, where we plot the **dependent variable**.

### Plotting points on a grid

To plot points, we use an **ordered pair**. An ordered pair provides the exact position of a point on a Cartesian plane (grid).

*   **Definition:** An ordered pair is written as $(x; y)$, where:
    *   The first number is the **horizontal coordinate** ($x$-axis).
    *   The second number is the **vertical coordinate** ($y$-axis).

#### Method: How to plot the point $(5; 4)$

1.  **Start at the origin:** Begin at the point $(0; 0)$.
2.  **Move horizontally:** Move along the horizontal $x$-axis until you reach $5$.
3.  **Move vertically:** Move upwards until you are in line with $4$ on the vertical $y$-axis.
4.  **Mark the point:** Draw a dot where the grid lines cross at this position.

### Question

### Solution

1. The cost increases by $R\ 4$ as the number of boxes increases by $1$.
2. The cost is equal to $2$ plus $4$ times the number of boxes.
   $$\text{Cost} = 2 + (4 \times \text{number of boxes})$$

*   **Rule using variables:** If we let $c = \text{cost}$ and $b = \text{number of boxes}$, the formula is:
    $$c = 2 + 4b$$

### Dependent and Independent Variables
In mathematical patterns, variables are used to show relationships:
*   **Independent variable:** The input value (e.g., the number of boxes made, $b$).

### Writing a General Formula
To describe the value of a term in a sequence, we use the position of the term, denoted by $n$. Since $n$ can represent different positions, it is called a **variable**.

#### Example: Deriving a Formula
If a sequence follows the general formula $(10n) - 5$:
*   To find the 100th term, substitute $n = 100$:
    $$(10 \times 100) - 5 = 995$$

#### Simplifying Expressions
Sometimes a pattern can be written in different ways. For example, a pattern starting at 5 and increasing by 10 can be written as:
$$5 + [(n - 1) \times 10]$$

To simplify this expression:
1.  Distribute the 10: $5 + 10n - 10$
2.  Combine like terms: $10n - 5$

# 2. Patterns, Relationships and Representations

## 2.2 Making sense of graphs that tell a story

Graphs are visual representations of the relationship between two quantities. They allow us to interpret data and understand trends in a narrative format.

### Key Concepts in Graph Interpretation

To understand how a graph "tells a story," focus on the following features:

*   **Increasing:** The graph moves upwards from left to right, indicating that the dependent variable is growing as the independent variable increases.
*   **Decreasing:** The graph moves downwards from left to right, indicating that the dependent variable is shrinking.
*   **Constant:** The graph is a horizontal line, indicating that the dependent variable remains unchanged over a period of time.
*   **Gradients (Steepness):** The slope of the line indicates the rate of change. A steeper line means a faster rate of change.
*   **Maximum and Minimum Points:** These represent the highest and lowest values reached on the graph.

### Continuous vs. Discrete Graphs

*   **Continuous Graphs:** Represented by solid lines, showing a smooth, unbroken relationship between variables.
*   **Discrete Graphs:** Represented by dotted lines or individual points. The dots help distinguish between specific data points and emphasize the steepness between them, rather than implying a continuous connection.

### Solutions and Explanations

**a) Total Prize Money:**
The total amount to be shared is R 2 000.

**b) Effect of Group Size:**
As the number of people in the group increases, the amount of money each person receives decreases. This is because the fixed total (R 2 000) is being divided by a larger number.

**c) Type of Relationship:**
This is an **inverse proportion** relationship. In an inverse proportion, as one variable increases, the other variable decreases by the same factor. The product of the two variables remains constant:

*   For 8 people: $\frac{2000}{8} = 250$
*   For 10 people: $\frac{2000}{10} = 200$
*   For 50 people: $\frac{2000}{50} = 40$

*   Standard hire cost: R 30 per game.
*   Gamers Club membership: R 150 per year + R 15 per game.

**Exercises:**

**a) Cost for 10 games (non-member):**
Multiply the number of games by the cost per game:
$10 \times R 30 = R 300$

**b) Equation for non-member cost:**
Let $C$ be the total cost and $n$ be the number of games:
$$C = 30n$$

**c) Cost for 5 games (Gamers Club member):**
Membership fee + (Number of games $\times$ discounted rate):
$R 150 + (5 \times R 15) = R 150 + R 75 = R 225$

**d) Equation for Gamers Club member cost:**
Let $C$ be the total cost and $n$ be the number of games:
$$C = 150 + 15n$$

### Mathematical Relationships: Independent and Dependent Variables

#### Key Concepts
*   **Independent Variable:** The variable that is changed or controlled in a scientific experiment or mathematical relationship to test the effects on the dependent variable. In this context, it is the number of games hired.
*   **Dependent Variable:** The variable being tested and measured. Its value "depends" on the independent variable. In this context, it is the total cost of hiring the games.
*   **Slope:** In a linear relationship, the slope represents the rate of change. A positive slope indicates that as the independent variable increases, the dependent variable also increases.

---

#### Exercises and Solutions

**e) In the relationship between costs and the number of games hired, which is the independent variable? Explain.**
*   **Solution:** The number of games hired is the independent variable. This is because the total amount Thomas pays depends on how many games he chooses to hire.

**f) Which is the dependent variable?**
*   **Solution:** The cost of hiring the games is the dependent variable.

**g) Would a graph for this relationship have a positive or a negative slope? Give a reason for your answer.**
*   **Solution:** The slope is positive. This is because the total cost increases as you hire more games.

#### Summary of Mathematical Models
Based on the provided solutions, the cost structures can be represented by the following formulas:

1.  **Simple Cost Calculation:**
    $$\text{Cost} = R30 \times \text{number of games}$$

2.  **Fixed + Variable Cost Calculation:**
    $$\text{Cost} = R150 + (R15 \times \text{number of games})$$
