## Key Concepts

### 1. Perimeter
The perimeter is the total distance around the outside of a two-dimensional shape. It is calculated by finding the sum of the lengths of all sides.

*   **Units:** Perimeter is measured in linear units (e.g., $mm$, $cm$, $m$, $km$).

### 2. Area
The area is the amount of surface covered by a two-dimensional shape. It represents the number of square units required to fill the interior of the shape.

*   **Units:** Area is measured in square units (e.g., $mm^2$, $cm^2$, $m^2$, $km^2$).

## 9.2 Measuring perimeter

> **DEFINITION: Perimeter**
> The total length of the outside of a shape or the continuous line forming the boundary of a closed geometric figure. Perimeter is measured in units of length such as $mm$, $cm$, $m$, or $km$.

In this section, we will find the perimeter of rectangles, squares, and triangles by measuring them directly, and the perimeter of a circle by estimation. We will not be using any formulae yet.

> **DEFINITION: Rectangle**

### Estimation and Direct Measurement of Perimeter (EMG5S)

#### Measuring Polygons
To measure the perimeter of a rectangle, a square, or a triangle, we simply measure the length of each side using a ruler and add up the lengths of all the sides to calculate the total perimeter.

#### Measuring Circles
To measure the perimeter of a circle, we use a piece of string:
1. Place the string along the outer edge (outline) of the circle.
2. Mark off the exact length required to go around the circle once.
3. Measure that length of string against a ruler to estimate the perimeter.

The perimeter of a circle is referred to as the **circumference**.

#### Definition: Circumference
The circumference is the perimeter of a circle, or the distance around the curved edge of a circle. Circumference is measured in standard units of length, such as:
* Millimetres ($\text{mm}$)
* Centimetres ($\text{cm}$)
* Metres ($\text{m}$)
* Kilometres ($\text{km}$)

#### Solution

**1. Measuring the perimeter on the diagram**
*   The length of the yard is $12\text{ cm}$.
*   The width of the yard is $10\text{ cm}$.
*   Since the backyard is a rectangle, opposite sides are equal in length.
*   The perimeter is the total length of the boundary of the yard:
    $$\text{Perimeter} = 12\text{ cm} + 12\text{ cm} + 10\text{ cm} + 10\text{ cm}$$

*   Calculate the actual perimeter:
    $$\text{Perimeter} = 44\text{ cm} \times 100$$
    $$\text{Perimeter} = 4400\text{ cm}$$
*   Convert the result to metres (since $100\text{ cm} = 1\text{ m}$):
    $$\text{Perimeter} = \frac{4400}{100}\text{ m}$$
    $$\text{Perimeter} = 44\text{ m}$$

### Core Concepts: Circles and Geometry

#### 1. Diameter
The diameter ($d$) of a circle is a straight line segment that passes through the centre of the circle, with both endpoints lying on the circle's edge.
*   **Formula:** $d = 2 \times r$ (where $r$ is the radius).

#### 2. Pi ($\pi$)
Pi ($\pi$) is a mathematical constant used to calculate the perimeter (circumference) and area of circles. While the value of $\pi$ is an infinite decimal ($3,141592645...$), for standard calculations, we use the approximation:
*   **Approximation:** $\pi \approx 3,142$

### Calculating Perimeter and Circumference

#### Review of Perimeter Calculations
The following solutions demonstrate how to calculate the perimeter of various shapes using standard formulas.

1. **Rectangular Backyard**
   Formula: $P = 2 \times \text{length} + 2 \times \text{width}$
   Calculation:
   $P = (2 \times 12\text{ cm}) + (2 \times 10\text{ cm})$
   $P = 24\text{ cm} + 20\text{ cm}$
   $P = 44\text{ cm}$

2. **Square Patio**
   Formula: $P = 4 \times \text{length}$
   Calculation:
   $P = 4 \times 5\text{ cm}$
   $P = 20\text{ cm}$
   Conversion: $20\text{ cm} \times 10 = 200\text{ mm}$

3. **Triangular Garden**
   Formula: $P = \text{side}_1 + \text{side}_2 + \text{side}_3$
   Calculation:
   $P = 4\text{ cm} + 5,5\text{ cm} + 6,8\text{ cm}$
   $P = 16,3\text{ cm}$

4. **Circular Table**
   Formula: $C = \pi \times \text{diameter}$
   Calculation:
   $C = \pi \times 1,5\text{ cm}$
   $C = 3,142 \times 1,5\text{ cm}$
   $C = 4,713\text{ cm}$
   Rounding: $C \approx 4,7\text{ cm}$

5. **Comparison of Methods**
   When measuring the circumference of a circle using physical tools like string and a ruler, results may vary (e.g., $4,5\text{ cm}$ or $5\text{ cm}$). Using the mathematical formula $C = \pi \times d$ provides a more accurate result ($4,7\text{ cm}$) than physical estimation.

### Worked Examples: Calculating Perimeter and Dimensions

#### Example 1: Circular Shapes
**b) Calculating Circumference**
Given a diameter of $40\text{ cm}$:
$$\text{Circumference} = \pi \times \text{diameter}$$
$$\text{Circumference} = 3,142 \times 40\text{ cm}$$
$$\text{Circumference} = 125,68\text{ cm} \approx 125,7\text{ cm}$$

**c) Perimeter of a Semicircle**
To find the perimeter of half of the larger circle:
$$\frac{125,7\text{ cm}}{2} = 62,85\text{ cm}$$

**d) Width of an Annulus (Yellow Area)**
$$\text{Width} = \text{radius of large circle} - \text{radius of small circle}$$
$$\text{Width} = \frac{40}{2}\text{ cm} - 5\text{ cm}$$
$$\text{Width} = 20\text{ cm} - 5\text{ cm} = 15\text{ cm}$$

---

#### Example 2: Composite Shapes (Square and Triangle)
To calculate the perimeter of a complex shape consisting of a square and a triangle, sum the outer boundaries:
$$\text{Perimeter} = 3 \times (\text{outer side of square}) + 2 \times (\text{outer side of triangle})$$

**Conversion to metres:**
*   $300\text{ mm} = 0,3\text{ m}$
*   $50\text{ cm} = 0,5\text{ m}$
*   $0,4\text{ m}$ (already in metres)

**Calculation:**
$$\text{Perimeter} = (3 \times 0,3\text{ m}) + 0,5\text{ m} + 0,4\text{ m}$$
$$\text{Perimeter} = 0,9\text{ m} + 0,9\text{ m} = 1,8\text{ m}$$

---

#### Example 3: Irregular Polygons
To calculate the perimeter of an irregular shape, ensure all units are converted to the same base unit (metres) before summing the sides.

**Calculation:**
$$\text{Perimeter} = 3\text{ m} + 2\text{ m} + 4\text{ m} + 300\text{ cm} + (4\text{ m} + 3\text{ m}) + (300\text{ cm} + 2\text{ m})$$
$$\text{Perimeter} = 3\text{ m} + 2\text{ m} + 4\text{ m} + 3\text{ m} + 7\text{ m} + 5\text{ m}$$
$$\text{Perimeter} = 26\text{ m}$$

# 9.3 Measuring Area

### Definition: Area
Area is the size of a surface or plane, or the amount of space inside the boundary of a flat (2-dimensional) object such as a square, triangle, or circle.

Area is measured in square units, such as:
*   $\text{mm}^2$
*   $\text{cm}^2$
*   $\text{m}^2$
*   $\text{km}^2$

---

### Estimating Area
We can estimate area without using any formulae by placing a square grid over the area we wish to measure. If we know the size of the squares, we can count how many squares are filled by the area.

*   **For some shapes:** This method is easy and accurate (it provides the same result as using a formula).
*   **For other shapes:** It becomes harder to estimate accurately if the shape does not fit neatly into the square grid.

---

When a shape is drawn on a grid where each square represents a specific unit of area (e.g., $1\text{ cm}^2$), you can calculate or estimate the area by counting the squares.

#### Worked Examples: Calculating Area using Formulae

To find the area of standard geometric shapes, we use specific mathematical formulae:

**a) Area of a Rectangle**
$$\text{Area} = \text{length} \times \text{width}$$
$$\text{Area} = 5\text{ cm} \times 6\text{ cm} = 30\text{ cm}^2$$

**b) Area of a Triangle**
$$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{perpendicular height}$$
$$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} \times 4\text{ cm} \times 12\text{ cm} = 24\text{ cm}^2$$

**c) Area of a Circle**
$$\text{Area} = \pi \times \text{radius}^2$$
$$\text{Area} = 3,142 \times (1\text{ cm})^2$$
$$\text{Area} = 3,142 \times 1\text{ cm}^2 = 3,142\text{ cm}^2$$

---

#### Comparison: Estimation vs. Calculation

When comparing grid-based estimation to formula-based calculation, consider the following:

| Shape | Estimation Method | Formula Method | Observation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Rectangle** | Grid | $30\text{ cm}^2$ | Results are identical as the shape fits perfectly into the grid. |
| **Triangle** | $24,5\text{ cm}^2$ | $24\text{ cm}^2$ | Estimation is less accurate due to partial blocks. |
| **Circle** | $3\text{ cm}^2$ | $3,142\text{ cm}^2$ | Formula provides a more precise result. |

**Key Takeaway:** Using formulae to calculate area is more accurate than estimating with a grid. It is also more efficient for triangles and circles.

---

#### Strategy for Complex Shapes
When dealing with irregular or complex shapes, follow this process:
1.  **Decomposition:** Break the complex object down into smaller, simpler shapes (e.g., rectangles, triangles).
2.  **Calculation:** Calculate the area of each individual smaller shape using the appropriate formula.
3.  **Summation:** Add the individual areas together to find the total area of the complex object.

### Calculating Area and Cost

#### Example 1: Composite Area and Unit Cost

### Problem 5: Paving a Patio

**Scenario:** The Dlamini family is re-paving a patio with dimensions $6\text{ m}$ by $8\text{ m}$ using cobblestones that are $10\text{ cm}$ by $10\text{ cm}$.

#### a) Calculating the number of cobblestones needed
1. **Convert units:** $10\text{ cm} = 0,1\text{ m}$.
2. **Calculate area of the patio:** $6\text{ m} \times 8\text{ m} = 48\text{ m}^2$.
3. **Calculate area of one cobblestone:** $0,1\text{ m} \times 0,1\text{ m} = 0,01\text{ m}^2$.
4. **Calculate total stones:** $\frac{48\text{ m}^2}{0,01\text{ m}^2} = 4800$ cobblestones.

#### b) Calculating the number of batches
Cobblestones are sold in batches of $200$.
$$\text{Batches needed} = \frac{4800}{200} = 24 \text{ batches}$$

#### c) Calculating the total cost
If one batch costs $\text{R } 129,99$:
$$\text{Total cost} = 24 \times \text{R } 129,99 = \text{R } 3119,76$$

### Geometry and Cost Calculations: Swimming Pools

This section provides worked solutions for calculating the perimeter, area, and associated costs for different swimming pool shapes.



![Textbook_MathematicalLiteracy_Gr10_Measurement-of-perimeter-and-area.pdf-0035-18.png](images/Textbook_MathematicalLiteracy_Gr10_Measurement-of-perimeter-and-area.pdf-0035-18.png)


#### Worked Solutions

**a) Rectangular Pool**
*   **i. Perimeter of pool:** $2 \times (8\text{ m} + 4\text{ m}) = 24\text{ m}$
*   **ii. Length of fencing:** $2 \times (10\text{ m} + 6\text{ m}) = 32\text{ m}$
*   **iii. Area of pool:** $8\text{ m} \times 4\text{ m} = 32\text{ m}^2$
*   **iv. Cost of fence:** $\text{R } 250,00 \times 32\text{ m} = \text{R } 8000$
*   **v. Cost of netting:** $\text{R } 199,99 \times 32\text{ m}^2 = \text{R } 6399,68$

**b) Circular Pool**
*   **i. Perimeter of pool:** $2\pi r = 2 \times 3,142 \times 3,5 = 21,99\text{ m}$
*   **ii. Length of fencing:** $2\pi(4,5\text{ m}) = 28,28\text{ m}$
*   **iii. Area of pool:** $\pi r^2 = 3,142 \times (3,5\text{ m})^2 = 38,49\text{ m}^2$
*   **iv. Cost of fence:** $\text{R } 250,00 \times 28,28\text{ m} = \text{R } 7070$
*   **v. Cost of netting:** $\text{R } 199,99 \times 38,49\text{ m}^2 = \text{R } 7697,62$

**c) Capsule-Shaped Pool**
*   **i. Perimeter of pool:** $2(\text{perimeter of semi-circles}) + \text{length of 2 rectangular sides}$
    $= (\text{perimeter one circle with radius } 2\text{ m}) + 2(7\text{ m})$
    $= 2\pi(2\text{ m}) + 14\text{ m} = 12,568\text{ m} + 14\text{ m} = 26,57\text{ m}$
*   **ii. Length of fencing:** $2(\text{perimeter of semi-circles}) + \text{length of 2 rectangular sides}$
    $= (\text{perimeter one circle with radius } 4\text{ m}) + 2(7\text{ m})$
    $= 2\pi(4\text{ m}) + 14\text{ m} = 25,136\text{ m} + 14\text{ m} = 39,14\text{ m}$
*   **iii. Area of pool:** $\text{Area rectangle} + 2(\text{area semi-circles}) = \text{Area rectangle} + (\text{area circle radius } 2\text{ m})$
    $= (7\text{ m} \times 4\text{ m}) + (\pi(2)^2) = 28\text{ m}^2 + 12,568\text{ m}^2 = 40,57\text{ m}^2$
*   **iv. Cost of fence:** $\text{R } 25,00 \times 39,14\text{ m} = \text{R } 9785$
*   **v. Cost of netting:** $\text{R } 199,99 \times 40,57\text{ m}^2 = \text{R } 8113,59$

#### Exercise 8: Triangular Garden and Paving
<!-- DIAGRAM_PLACEHOLDER: diagram -->

**Questions:**
a) What is the area of the vegetable garden in metres squared?
b) What is the area of the paving, in metres squared? (excluding the vegetable garden!)
c) If the paving is going to cost $\text{R } 24,65 \text{ per metre squared}$, how much will the total cost of the paving be?
d) Lebo wants to build a fence around her garden. To do this, she needs to calculate the perimeter of the triangular garden. Is this possible with the information provided in the diagram? Explain your answer.

**Solutions:**

**a) Area of the vegetable garden:**
The garden is a right-angled triangle with a base of $2,4 \text{ m}$ and a height of $90 \text{ cm}$ ($0,9 \text{ m}$).
$$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height}$$
$$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} \times (2,4 \text{ m}) \times (0,9 \text{ m}) = 1,08 \text{ m}^2$$

**b) Area of the paving:**
First, calculate the total area of the rectangular plot:
*   Total width = $1,2 \text{ m} + 2,4 \text{ m} + 1,2 \text{ m} = 4,8 \text{ m}$
*   Total height = $80 \text{ cm} + 90 \text{ cm} + 80 \text{ cm} = 250 \text{ cm} = 2,5 \text{ m}$
*   Total Area = $4,8 \text{ m} \times 2,5 \text{ m} = 12 \text{ m}^2$

$$\text{Area of paving} = \text{Total Area} - \text{Garden Area}$$
$$\text{Area of paving} = 12 \text{ m}^2 - 1,08 \text{ m}^2 = 10,92 \text{ m}^2$$

**c) Total cost of paving:**
$$\text{Cost} = 10,92 \text{ m}^2 \times \text{R } 24,65/\text{m}^2 = \text{R } 269,18$$

**d) Perimeter calculation:**
No, it is not possible. To calculate the perimeter of a triangle, you need the lengths of all three sides. We only know the base ($2,4 \text{ m}$) and the height ($0,9 \text{ m}$). We would need to use the Pythagorean theorem to find the length of the hypotenuse (the slanted side), which is not provided.
